# History Bible — Elyndor

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — established July 2026
> **Authority:** This document defines the canonical four-age framework of world history. All future lore, quest design, dungeon content, artifact descriptions, and regional history must align with this framework. History should explain the present — ancient ruins, artifacts, political grudges, and religious tensions all trace back to these ages.
> **Perspective:** Written from the view of a scholar with access to surviving records. The further back one looks, the less reliable the sources become. Much of the First Age is mythologized rather than recorded.
> **Cross-references:** [`elyndor/history/the_corruption.md`](the_corruption.md) · [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](../../docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md) · [`docs/GAME_BIBLE.md`](../../docs/GAME_BIBLE.md)

## Overview

The history of Elyndor is divided into **four ages**, each with a distinct identity, defining events, and long-term consequences that shape the world players enter.

| Age | Name | Theme | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | The Age of Creation | Sacrifice | Mythologized; partially recorded |
| **Second** | The Age of Kingdoms | Hope | Recorded; reliable |
| **Third** | The Age of Wars | Pride | Recorded; within living memory of oldest races |
| **Fourth** | The Age of Shadows | Choice | Present day; still being written |

## The First Age — The Age of Creation

> *"When gods still walked among mortals."*

This is the mythical beginning of recorded history — the era when the world was still being shaped and the forces that determined everything afterward were set in motion.

### What Is Known

- The Ancient God was active in the world; its influence directly shaped the physical world and the first races
- Dragons were common during this age; they were not yet the rare creatures they are today
- Magic flowed more freely; the laws governing magical practice were different from those of later ages
- The first civilizations began forming — early predecessors to the kingdoms of the Second Age
- The gods walked among mortals directly, before their withdrawal

### The Imprisonment

At some point during the First Age, one member of the Divine Chorus — now known only as **The Forgotten Eighth** — became changed by forces she had encountered beyond the boundaries of Creation. She had gone searching for what cannot be found: a way to undo the death of a mortal she had loved. The search exposed her to things outside Creation entirely. By the time the Chorus intervened, she was no longer the being they had known.

The rest of the Divine Chorus imprisoned her. Not as punishment. Not as victory. As the only means they could find to prevent what she had brought back from destroying what they had spent the entirety of creation maintaining.

What followed was a confrontation of such scale that it reshaped continents and fundamentally altered the nature of magic. The Chorus mourned what they had to do. They have never stopped loving their sister.

The gods withdrew from directly walking among mortals following this event. The dragons, whose involvement in the imprisonment came at enormous cost, became increasingly rare — a decline that continued through subsequent ages.

Full account of the Eighth, the imprisonment, and its aftermath: [`elyndor/history/the_eighth.md`](the_eighth.md)

### The Great Forgetting

Following the imprisonment, the Eighth's existence was gradually erased from the historical record. Some records were intentionally sealed. Others were lost through time, war, and civilizational collapse. The Age of Wars destroyed much of what might have preserved the truth.

The result: modern civilizations genuinely believe the Divine Chorus has always had seven members. The number eight does not appear in mainstream theology. Evidence that it was once different survives only in the oldest ruins, forgotten temples, and ancient records that few can access and fewer can read.

**The Great Forgetting is the reason the player begins the campaign without knowing the truth.** The revelation that the Chorus had an eighth member — and that the source of the Corruption is grief — is the campaign's spine.

### What Remains Deliberately Unknown

> ⚠️ The following details are **intentional mysteries** — do not define them without explicit canonical direction:

- The Eighth's true name (erased; the campaign's deepest mystery)
- Her elemental stewardship within the Chorus
- The identity of the mortal she loved
- Who among the Chorus performed the sealing's specific components
- The precise mechanics of the prison
- Whether the seal can be repaired
- What happens if she fully awakens
- Exactly when during the First Age this occurred

**Now established as canonical** (not mysteries):
- The Ancient God is The Forgotten Eighth — a former member of the Divine Chorus
- She was imprisoned by the rest of the Chorus because of what she had become after searching beyond Creation
- The imprisonment was a tragedy, not a victory; the Chorus mourned and continues to mourn
- The Corruption amplifies virtue past the point where it remains itself; it is not simple evil

These mysteries are part of the game's central story and will be revealed — or not — through deliberate narrative design, not background documentation.

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **The prison** | The god's prison beneath the world; its weakening is the central threat of the game |
| **Legendary artifacts** | Objects from this age that survived are extraordinarily powerful and almost always dangerous |
| **Ancient ruins** | Civilizations that existed before or during the imprisonment were destroyed or transformed; their ruins are common exploration destinations |
| **Lost civilizations** | Several peoples who existed in the First Age do not have surviving descendants |
| **Dragon rarity** | Dragons became rare following the imprisonment event; those that remain are ancient |
| **Changed magic** | The imprisonment changed the nature of magic; magic-users of later ages work within these altered laws without fully understanding why they exist |
| **The Great Forgetting** | The Eighth's existence was erased from history over generations; modern civilizations believe the Chorus has always had seven members; discovering the truth is the campaign's central revelation arc |

**Theme:** Sacrifice — the founding act of the world's current order required a cost that is still being paid.

**Lesson:** Unity can overcome even impossible darkness — but the victory reshapes everything that came after.

## The Second Age — The Age of Kingdoms

> *"An era of hope, discovery, and prosperity."*

Following the imprisonment of the Ancient God and the withdrawal of the gods, civilization entered a period of expansion and optimism. The existential threat appeared to be over. The world's peoples built on what remained.

### What Happened

- Major kingdoms were established across the continent
- The Capital Province became the political center of the emerging civilization
- The Iron Peaks became the heart of dwarven civilization and culture
- The Great Forest flourished; elven communities deepened their relationship with the land
- Sol Kareth emerged as a center of trade and learning, its position on ancient routes making it wealthy
- The Mages Guild was founded — formal magical education and research became institutionalized
- Major religions expanded their reach and established their organizational forms

Most people during this age believed the darkness of the First Age had been defeated permanently. The idea that the god's prison might one day weaken was not seriously entertained.

### What This Age Built

The Second Age created the foundations that the Third Age would damage and the Fourth Age inherits:

- The modern kingdoms and their political relationships
- The trade routes connecting major regions
- Universities, libraries, and centers of learning
- The major temples and religious hierarchies
- Formal magical education and Guild infrastructure

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Modern kingdoms** | Their borders, institutions, and political cultures trace back to the Second Age |
| **Trade infrastructure** | The routes connecting the continent were established during this age |
| **Educational institutions** | Universities, libraries, and the Guild's founding belong to this era |
| **Religious organization** | The major religious orders took their current institutional form during the Second Age |

**Theme:** Hope — the world believed the worst was behind it.

**Lesson:** Peace and prosperity must be actively protected. The Second Age's greatest failure was its assumption that safety was permanent.

## The Third Age — The Age of Wars

> *"When ambition replaced unity."*

The alliances that had held kingdoms together eventually fractured. What had been built during the Second Age became something to fight over. The Third Age is a period of centuries-long conflict, collapse, and slow, partially-successful rebuilding.

### What Happened

- The great alliances of the Second Age collapsed under the weight of territorial ambition, resource competition, and accumulated grievances
- Kingdoms fought one another for centuries; borders changed repeatedly; cities were destroyed
- Knowledge was lost — libraries burned, scholars died, entire fields of study were abandoned or forgotten
- The Vampire Houses, which had operated quietly during the Second Age, recognized the opportunity that war and political chaos provided and quietly expanded their influence and territory
- Goblin tribes, pressured and marginalized during the Second Age, became more organized in response to the increased violence around them
- The Corruption began its slow return during the later years of this age — unnoticed, because the world had too many immediate crises to track patterns of subtle magical deterioration

### Talos

Talos served during the **latter years of the Third Age**. His military experience — the discipline, the pragmatism, the exhaustion with institutions that claim to represent order while serving only themselves — was forged in this period. The Age of Wars shaped his worldview before he lost faith in the government he served. The Capital Province he defended was itself a product of everything the Third Age had corroded.

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Modern politics** | Most current political tensions, noble rivalries, and territorial disputes trace back to conflicts that began in the Third Age |
| **Ruined fortresses** | Military structures from the Age of Wars are common exploration destinations; many were abandoned mid-conflict and never reclaimed |
| **Battlefields** | Major battle sites from the Third Age have become changed places — some are simply scarred; some, affected by the Corruption's early return, have become something stranger |
| **Noble rivalries** | Families that fought on opposing sides during the Age of Wars have not forgotten it; these grudges shape Capital Province politics |
| **Fractured alliances** | The multilateral cooperation of the Second Age does not exist in the Fourth Age; its absence is felt |
| **Vampire expansion** | The Houses used the chaos of the Third Age to establish positions of influence they did not have before |

**Theme:** Pride — the Third Age was not inevitable. It resulted from the failure of powerful peoples to value what they shared over what they wanted separately.

**Lesson:** Division can destroy what previous generations built at enormous cost.

## The Fourth Age — The Age of Shadows

> *"The darkness returns unnoticed."*

This is the present. The campaigns of Project Dungeon Keeper take place in the earliest days of the Fourth Age.

### The State of the World

The Third Age's wars eventually wound down — not through victory, but through exhaustion. Trade has resumed. Kingdoms are rebuilding. Most people believe the worst is behind them. The institutions damaged during the Age of Wars are slowly reconstituting.

They are wrong about the worst being behind them.

The Corruption has been spreading since the later years of the Third Age, and it is accelerating now that the god's prison is genuinely weakening. Ancient ruins are becoming active. Monsters that were manageable during the late Third Age are now more aggressive, more numerous, and in some cases changed in ways that do not match any existing record. Dark magic is resurging in ways that the Mages Guild is only beginning to document.

Only a handful of scholars — and fewer still outside scholarly circles — recognize that these are not separate problems but symptoms of a single cause.

The player begins their journey here.

### The Player's Role

The Fourth Age is still being written. The player's choices will determine how history remembers this era — whether the Corruption was contained, how much was lost, which factions survived, and what kind of world exists when this age ends.

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Still being written** | No legacy is confirmed; it is determined by campaign events and player choices |

**Theme:** Choice — history shapes the future but does not determine it. The decisions made during the Fourth Age will define what the Fifth Age inherits, if there is one.

**Lesson:** Still being learned.

## History Design Philosophy

History in Elyndor exists to **explain the present**.

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| **Ancient ruins exist for a reason** | Every significant ruin has a historical origin; it was built by someone, for a purpose, during one of the ages |
| **Artifacts have history** | Objects of power from earlier ages carry the weight of what they were used for and what their creation cost |
| **Kingdoms remember old wars** | Political tensions, territorial disputes, and noble rivalries trace back to the Third Age's conflicts |
| **Cultures interpret history differently** | The Iron Peaks dwarves, the Great Forest elves, the Capital Province nobles, and the Frozen Highlands clans all experienced and remember these ages differently |
| **History enriches exploration** | Players who know history will understand what they find; players who do not will still find it, but they will understand it less |
