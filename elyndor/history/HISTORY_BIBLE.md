# History Bible — Elyndor

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — established July 2026  
> **Authority:** This document defines the canonical four-age framework of world history. All future lore, quest design, dungeon content, artifact descriptions, and regional history must align with this framework. History should explain the present — ancient ruins, artifacts, political grudges, and religious tensions all trace back to these ages.  
> **Canonical framework:** Four Ages — Age of Awakening, Age of Harmony, Age of Sundering, Age of Restoration. The Long Decline is the tragic final chapter of the Age of Harmony, not a separate age. The game begins near the end of the Age of Restoration.  
> **Perspective:** Written from the view of a scholar with access to surviving records. The further back one looks, the less reliable the sources become. Much of the Age of Awakening is mythologized rather than recorded.

## Overview

The history of Elyndor is divided into **four ages**, each with a distinct identity, defining events, and long-term consequences that shape the world players enter.

| Age | Name | Theme | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | The Age of Awakening | Birth | Mythologized; known through archaeology and oral tradition |
| **II** | The Age of Harmony | Flourishing | Recorded; filtered through post-Sundering reconstruction |
| **III** | The Age of Sundering | Collapse | Recorded but incomplete; much deliberately obscured |
| **IV** | The Age of Restoration | Recovery | Present day; still being written |

> **The Long Decline** is not a separate age. It is the tragic final chapter of the Age of Harmony — the centuries-long deterioration that preceded the Sundering. It is a recognized period of historical scholarship; scholars may reference it by name. It is not a fifth age.

---

## Age I — The Age of Awakening

> *"Before memory. Before language. Before gods were named — and then, slowly, the world began to fill."*

This is the mythical beginning — the era when the world was being shaped and the forces that determined everything afterward were set in motion. What is known comes from archaeology, geological evidence, and the oldest surviving oral traditions. The further back one looks in this age, the less reliable the sources become.

### What Is Known

- The Divine Chorus comes into being — eight members; Primordial Energy flows and stabilizes; physical reality takes its current form
- The first intelligent races emerge; their earliest existence is inferred rather than recorded
- Dragons were present and active during this age in numbers not seen since
- Magic flowed more directly from its source; the laws governing magical practice were different from those of later ages
- First writing systems develop; first cities and organized communities form
- **The First Temple is established** — the oldest surviving temple to the Chorus; the structure beneath it already exists when the temple is constructed above it
- First schools of magic develop — the initial systematic attempts to understand and teach magical practice
- The Chorus stewards existence; mortals begin the long process of learning what existence is

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ancient ruins** | Structures from the Age of Awakening are among the oldest on the continent; their purpose is often unclear |
| **First Temple** | The oldest surviving religious structure; the sealed foundation beneath it predates the temple itself |
| **Dragon presence** | Dragons were common during this age; the decline to their current rarity begins in the Age of Sundering |
| **Primordial magic** | Magic in its most direct form was accessible during this age; what modern practitioners work with is the altered architecture of a later era |
| **Mythologized history** | The early Age of Awakening is known primarily through myth; separating historical fact from accumulated narrative requires significant scholarly work |

**Theme:** Birth — the world and everything in it is still discovering what it is.

## Age II — The Age of Harmony

> *"When the world believed it had found what it had been building toward — and it was right, for a time."*

The Age of Harmony is the high-water mark of mortal civilization. A genuine golden era of prosperity, cross-regional cooperation, and intellectual achievement that has not been equaled since. It is also the age that contains the seed of everything that destroyed it.

### The Golden Era

- **The First Empire rises** — the continent's disparate kingdoms and peoples unified under a single political structure for the first and only time in recorded history; built through genuine advantages that made membership more valuable than independence; see [`docs/lore/civilization/`](../../docs/lore/civilization/) for the full canonical account of the First Empire, the First Council, and the fall
- **The Great Library founded** — the continent's central repository of knowledge, established to preserve what had been discovered against the next catastrophe; see [`docs/lore/GREAT_LIBRARY.md`](../../docs/lore/GREAT_LIBRARY.md)
- **Universities and centers of learning flourish** — formal institutions of general scholarship emerge; the tradition of systematic secular learning takes root
- **Trade routes connect civilizations** — the empire's infrastructure connected regions in ways they had never been connected before; the routes that still exist in the Age of Restoration trace back to this era
- **Magic reaches its greatest advancement** — the combination of literacy, institutional memory, and sustained peace produced an era of discovery across multiple fields simultaneously
- The major religious orders established their organizational forms during this age
- The Mages Guild's foundational precursors develop during this period

### The Meeting That Changed Everything

During the Age of Harmony, the Forgotten Eighth — the eighth member of the Divine Chorus — spent extended time among mortals. She met one particular ordinary person. That relationship is the hinge on which all of subsequent history turns.

The mortal was not remarkable. Not a ruler, not a mage, not a figure of historical significance. They possessed no power except who they were: their kindness, their humor, their capacity for appreciating ordinary existence. They lived an ordinary life and died an ordinary death.

The Eighth could not accept it.

Full account: [`elyndor/history/the_eighth.md`](the_eighth.md)

### The Long Decline — Final Chapter of the Age of Harmony

> *"Nobody noticed the world ending. That is how it always works."*

The Long Decline is not a separate age. It is the tragic final chapter of the Age of Harmony — the slow accumulation of consequences from a choice that could not be undone.

- **The Eighth's Corruption spreads slowly** — as her search beyond Creation exposed her to forces outside the world's design, traces of those forces began bleeding back into Elyndor; the early Corruption was subtle: elevated aggression in wildlife, unusual magical instability, communities becoming something worse
- **The Chorus spends centuries attempting to save the Eighth** — the most important detail absent from surviving historical records; the Chorus did not immediately imprison their sister; they tried to reach her; they tried to help her; the Long Decline is, in part, the duration of that attempt
- **Civilizations collapse** — the empire of the Age of Harmony did not survive its final centuries intact; the interconnected systems that made it prosperous made it vulnerable to cascade failure; as the Corruption amplified existing tensions, the alliances holding the empire together frayed, then snapped
- The mortal eventually sings the Final Verse — the specific moment that closes the Age of Harmony's golden era

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **The Great Library** | Founded in this age; damaged in the Sundering; rebuilt in the Age of Restoration; portions of its collection remain sealed |
| **Trade infrastructure** | The continent's major routes trace back to Age of Harmony construction |
| **Educational tradition** | Universities and the Guild's precursors were established during this age |
| **The half-remembered golden era** | Most people in the Age of Restoration know the Age of Harmony only as a time of greatness they have not recovered; they do not know what ended it |
| **The Long Decline's hidden cause** | The collapse of the Age of Harmony is attributed in mainstream history to political and economic factors; the Corruption's role — and the Chorus's centuries-long attempt to prevent the Sundering — is not in any accessible record |

**Theme:** Flourishing — and the tragedy of watching something irreplaceable destroy itself from within.

## Age III — The Age of Sundering

> *"The hardest decision ever made. The one they have never stopped mourning."*

**The Sundering** is the canonical name for the greatest catastrophe in recorded history — the event in which the Divine Chorus imprisoned their sister. "The Imprisonment" is acceptable as a descriptive phrase in scholarly writing. The Great Forgetting is a separate decision made in the aftermath, distinct from the Sundering itself.

### What Happened

- **The Eighth imprisoned** — the Divine Chorus, having exhausted every alternative, imprisoned the Forgotten Eighth; the prison was built with grief rather than hatred; they have not stopped loving their sister since
- **Reality fractures** — in places where the Eighth's research had reached furthest, the fabric of the world showed strain that surviving records describe only metaphorically, because the scholars writing those records had no framework for what they were observing
- **Continents permanently scarred** — the confrontation reshaped geography; mountain ranges shifted; coastlines changed; places where the confrontation was most intense still bear marks that no natural process accounts for
- **Magic permanently altered** — before the Sundering, Primordial Magic flowed as a unified force; the imprisonment fractured it into seven distinct elemental aspects; this is why modern practitioners work with elements rather than raw Primordial Energy
- **Dragons decline significantly** — their involvement in the age's events came at enormous cost; those that remain in the Age of Restoration are ancient and rare
- **The Great Forgetting follows** — in the Sundering's wake, the Chorus allowed the Eighth's existence to be erased from history; some records were intentionally sealed; others were lost through the civilizational collapse that followed; whether this was mercy, self-protection, grief, or something beyond mortal characterization is one of the campaign's unresolved questions
- **History deliberately obscured** — much of the record from this period was lost, destroyed, or deliberately sealed; what was reconstructed afterward was filtered through the perspectives of those doing the reconstructing

### What Remains Deliberately Unknown

> ⚠️ The following details are **intentional mysteries** — do not define them without explicit canonical direction:

- The Eighth's true name (erased; the campaign's deepest mystery)
- Her elemental stewardship within the Chorus
- The identity of the mortal she loved
- Who among the Chorus performed the sealing's specific components
- The precise mechanics of the prison
- Whether the seal can be repaired
- What happens if she fully awakens
- Exactly when within the Age of Harmony the meeting occurred

**Now established as canonical** (not mysteries):
- The imprisoned god is The Forgotten Eighth — a former member of the Divine Chorus
- She was imprisoned by the rest of the Chorus because of what she had become after searching beyond Creation
- The imprisonment was a tragedy, not a victory; the Chorus mourned and continues to mourn
- The Corruption amplifies virtue past the point where it remains itself; it is not simple evil

These mysteries are part of the game's central story and will be revealed — or not — through deliberate narrative design, not background documentation.

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **The prison** | The god's prison beneath the world; its weakening is the central threat of the campaign |
| **Altered magic** | Modern practitioners work within the elemental architecture created by the Sundering; most have no knowledge that it was different before |
| **Legendary artifacts** | Objects from this age that survived are extraordinarily powerful and almost always dangerous |
| **Ancient ruins** | Structures from the Age of Harmony that survived into the Sundering were often destroyed or transformed; their ruins are common exploration destinations |
| **The Great Forgetting** | The Eighth's existence was erased from history across the Age of Sundering and into the Age of Restoration; modern civilizations genuinely believe the Chorus has always had seven members |
| **Dragon rarity** | Dragons became rare following the Sundering; those that remain are ancient |

**Theme:** Collapse — the founding act of the world's current order required a cost that is still being paid.

## Age IV — The Age of Restoration

> *"Rebuilding on ground that no one fully understood had shifted."*

After the Sundering, the world did what it always does: it rebuilt. The Age of Restoration is the modern age — the civilization players encounter is the product of this age's reconstruction. It spans from the immediate aftermath of the Sundering to the present day.

### Early Restoration — Rebuilding and Conflict

The alliances that had existed during the Age of Harmony were gone. What replaced them was a period of territorial competition, shifting power, and slowly reestablishing political structures:

- New kingdoms emerge, organized around the geography the Sundering had permanently altered
- The continent's major kingdoms establish and contest their current borders
- Knowledge is partially recovered — libraries rebuilt, scholars trained, but much that was lost during the Long Decline and the Sundering cannot be recovered
- Magic stabilizes into its current elemental form, different from what it was before the Sundering
- **The Vampire Houses** recognize the opportunity that the post-Sundering instability provides and quietly expand their influence and territory; they use the political chaos of the era's early centuries to establish positions they did not hold before
- Goblin tribes, pressured and marginalized during this period, become more organized in response to the increased tension around them

### Later Restoration — Stabilization and the Present

The rebuilding eventually stabilizes:

- The Imperial Capital becomes the intellectual heart of civilization
- The Great Library is rebuilt; portions of its collection are sealed or classified
- Universities are reestablished; the Mages Guild takes its current institutional form
- Major trade routes reconnect; the political map of the continent settles into its current form
- The seven-member theology of the Divine Chorus becomes unquestioned truth
- Most political tensions, noble rivalries, and territorial disputes players encounter trace back to conflicts within the Age of Restoration's earlier centuries

**The Corruption's return:** The Corruption has been spreading since the Long Decline, and it is accelerating as the god's prison weakens. Ancient ruins are becoming active. Only a handful of scholars — and fewer still outside scholarly circles — recognize that these are not separate problems but symptoms of a single cause.

### Talos

Talos served during the **middle centuries of the Age of Restoration** — a period when the Capital Province's institutions had stabilized enough to field a professional military, but not yet reached the level of political calcification he eventually lost faith in. The discipline, pragmatism, and exhaustion with institutions that claim to represent order while serving only themselves — all of it was forged during this period. The Capital Province he defended was itself a product of everything the Age of Restoration had built and corroded.

See [`docs/heroes/TALOS.md`](../../docs/heroes/TALOS.md)

### The Game Begins Near the End of This Age

The player is not chosen. They are not the subject of prophecy. They are someone who arrived at this moment in history — with the companions, the capability, and the circumstance to do something about it.

**What they do, and what it costs, will determine how the Age of Restoration is remembered — and whether a fifth age follows it.**

### Legacy

| Legacy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Still being written** | No legacy is confirmed; it is determined by campaign events and player choices |
| **Modern kingdoms** | Their borders, institutions, and political cultures were established during this age |
| **Vampire influence** | The Houses used the early chaos of the Age of Restoration to establish positions of influence they did not hold before |
| **Noble rivalries** | Families and factions that were on opposing sides during the Age of Restoration's earlier conflicts have not forgotten it |
| **Fractured alliances** | The multilateral cooperation of the Age of Harmony has not returned; its absence shapes every political interaction |

**Theme:** Recovery — and the question of whether what is recovered will be enough.

## History Design Philosophy

History in Elyndor exists to **explain the present**.

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| **Ancient ruins exist for a reason** | Every significant ruin has a historical origin; it was built by someone, for a purpose, during one of the ages |
| **Artifacts have history** | Objects of power from earlier ages carry the weight of what they were used for and what their creation cost |
| **Factions remember old conflicts** | Political tensions, territorial disputes, and rivalries trace back to events within the Age of Restoration |
| **Cultures interpret history differently** | The Iron Peaks dwarves, the Great Forest elves, the Capital Province nobles, and the Frozen Highlands clans all experienced and remember these ages differently |
| **History enriches exploration** | Players who know history will understand what they find; players who do not will still find it — they will understand it less |
| **The most useful question** | For any location, artifact, or institution: *what was this during the Age of Harmony, and what happened to it during the Long Decline or the Sundering?* |
