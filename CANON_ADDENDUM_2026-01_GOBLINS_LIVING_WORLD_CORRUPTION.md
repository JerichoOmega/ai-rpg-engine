## 1. Goblin Civilization — Final Canon

### Three Tribes Are the Only Goblin Civilizations

The three established tribes remain the only major goblin civilizations:

- **Stonefang**
- **Mossroot**
- **Ashfire**

**No fourth tribe exists.** Any hostile or "evil" goblins encountered throughout the game are **Corrupted Goblins** — former members of one of the three tribes who have fallen to the Corruption.

The Corruption is not a race, nation, or civilization. It is a force that infects all races equally.

---

## 2. Goblin Identity

The defining characteristic of goblins is **adaptability**.

- Humans are versatile.
- Goblins are adaptive.
- Give goblins enough time, and they can build a thriving civilization in almost any environment.

### Shared Cultural Values

- Family
- Community
- Contribution
- Adaptability
- Respect earned through service rather than status
- Deep respect for elders and experience
- Practicality (waste very little)
- Leaving the tribe stronger than they found it

### Shared Proverb

> *"Leave the tribe stronger than you found it."*

This philosophy applies equally to warriors, builders, healers, hunters, parents, blacksmiths, merchants, and leaders. **Everyone contributes.**

---

## 3. Council of Elders

Every goblin tribe has a **Council of Elders**.

Goblins believe that **wisdom is earned through experience.** Only elders are eligible to serve on a council because they have lived through war, peace, hardship, prosperity, and sacrifice.

**Leadership is responsibility, not privilege.**

Each tribe expresses this differently:

| Tribe | Council Role | Daily Leadership |
|---|---|---|
| **Mossroot** | The Council governs directly. Consensus is preferred. | Council-led (no separate chieftain role) |
| **Stonefang** | The Council selects and advises the Chieftain. The Chieftain may be removed if they fail the tribe. | Chieftain (Warchief Grakkor, per existing canon) |
| **Ashfire** | The Council preserves traditions and safeguards the tribe's long-term future. | Warchief leads daily affairs (Warchief Skarra, per existing canon) |

### Shared Saying

> *"The young carry our future. The elders carry our memory. A tribe without either cannot endure."*

---

## 4. Living World Philosophy — Core Design Pillar

**The world exists independently of the player.**

Villages should feel alive whether the player is present or not. NPCs should have everyday lives beyond quests.

### Examples

- Families eating together
- Children playing games
- Apprentices learning trades
- Blacksmiths working
- Farmers harvesting crops
- Hunters returning home
- Merchants unloading goods
- Elders discussing village matters
- Guards changing shifts
- Storytelling around evening fires

The player should feel like they are **visiting a living civilization**, not entering a static quest hub.

---

## 5. Small Stories Matter

Not every quest should involve saving the world. Many quests should involve helping ordinary citizens.

### Examples

- A child loses a treasured keepsake.
- An apprentice damages an important tool.
- Two neighbors argue over farmland.
- A hunter's companion goes missing.
- A merchant needs an escort.
- A family requests help preparing for a festival.

These smaller stories create **emotional attachment to the world.**

---

## 6. Time Matters

Settlements should feel different depending on the time of day.

### Morning
- Preparing for work
- Lighting fires
- Hunters leaving
- Markets opening

### Afternoon
- Shops busy
- Construction
- Farming
- Crafting

### Evening
- Families gathering
- Storytelling
- Music
- Meals

### Night
- Quiet streets
- Guards patrolling
- Lanterns lit
- Most citizens asleep

---

## 7. Goblin Villages — Living Civilization

When players enter a goblin settlement they should immediately understand that goblins are a true civilization.

They should witness everyday life:

- Children playing.
- Parents teaching.
- Craftsmen working.
- Neighbors talking.
- Elders debating.
- Musicians performing.
- Animals wandering.
- People simply living.

The player should feel that **life was happening before they arrived and will continue after they leave.**

---

## 8. The Corruption — Universal Threat

The Corruption is the central existential threat to the world.

**It does not discriminate.** It infects:

- Humans
- Goblins
- Elves
- Dwarves
- Beastfolk
- Any future intelligent race

**It has no civilization of its own. It cannot create. It only twists, consumes, and destroys what civilizations have built.**

### Canonical Quote

> *"No race is born Corrupted. Every Corrupted was once someone who chose… or was broken into choosing."*

This philosophy influences future lore, quest design, NPC writing, settlements, and worldbuilding across every civilization.
