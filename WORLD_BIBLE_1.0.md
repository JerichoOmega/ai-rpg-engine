> **Canon Status:** ✅ CANONICAL — established February 2026.
> **Purpose:** The concise master reference. The first document any contributor should read. Not a replacement for detailed lore files; an *overview* that explains the *soul* of Elyndor.
> **Reading time:** 20 minutes. Every section below points to deeper reference documents.

---

## 1. What Elyndor Is

Elyndor is an offline-first, AI-enhanced role-playing world in transition to a Godot prototype. It is a living continent shaped by five civilizations, one ongoing existential threat (the Corruption), one pantheon (the Divine Chorus), one canonical mystery (the Forgotten Eighth), and hundreds of individual choices that add up — over centuries — to what the world has become.

Elyndor is not a world where heroes save gods. **It is a world where mortals decide what the gods refuse to decide.**

---

## 2. Core Themes

- **Choice defines identity, not birth.** No race is born evil. No civilization is fated. Every intelligent being is judged by what they do.
- **Balance is survival.** Every virtue can save. Every virtue, isolated, can destroy.
- **The world remembers.** What is done leaves traces. What is preserved outlasts its keepers. The past is not gone — it is underfoot.
- **Wonder is worth protecting.** Some questions are meant to remain unanswered. Some mysteries make the world larger by refusing resolution.
- **Nothing meaningful is wasted.** Ideas, characters, cultures, and choices are preserved, repurposed, or remembered — never discarded.

---

## 3. Design Philosophy

### 3.1 Living World
The world exists whether the player is in it or not. Civilizations govern themselves, factions pursue their own goals, seasons turn, and the Corruption spreads by its own logic. The player *joins* a running story; they do not *summon* one.
See: `LIVING_WORLD_DESIGN_PILLAR.md`

### 3.2 Choice vs. Nature
No intelligent people are born evil. No race is a monolith. Every named enemy has a story that made them what they became. The player judges *actions*, not *categories*.
See: goblin canon (D-04b), vampire canon (V-1 through V-6), Divine Chorus (D-04c).

### 3.3 The Corruption
The Corruption is not a nation, race, or cult. **It is imbalance.** It amplifies what is already present in a person or civilization until one voice drowns out the rest. It does not create new evil — it isolates old virtue from its balancing virtues.
See: `elyndor/history/the_corruption.md`; vampire Withering (V-2); goblin Corruption Response Protocol (`GOBLIN_CULTURE.md` §19).

### 3.4 The Divine Chorus
Eight Voices, each a fundamental truth about existence. No Voice is the Chorus alone. Gods never physically appear. Living the virtue matters more than reciting the prayer.
See: `DIVINE_CHORUS.md`, `COMMON_PRAYERS_AND_BLESSINGS.md`, `RELIGIOUS_SYMBOLS_AND_ICONOGRAPHY.md`.

### 3.5 Primordial Magic
The Sundering fractured Primordial Magic into seven elemental aspects (Fire, Water, Earth, Air, Life, Death, Time). Modern mages study these aspects. The complete pre-Sundering First Song is inaccessible — except to Eleanor's Harmonic Soul, whose canonical arc engages with it.
See: `elyndor/magic/MAGIC_BIBLE.md`; D-02, D-04.

### 3.6 Preserving Wonder
Do not explain what should be discovered. Do not answer what should be debated. Do not resolve what makes the world larger by remaining unresolved.
See: `PRESERVING_WONDER_DESIGN_DIRECTIVE.md`.

## 4. Major Civilizations

### 4.1 Goblins — Three Tribes, One Contribution
Adaptive civilization built on **contribution, community, and leaving the tribe stronger than you found it**. Three tribes: Stonefang (warriors), Mossroot (listeners), Ashfire (reforgers). Full culture doc: `GOBLIN_CULTURE.md`.

### 4.2 Vampires — Three Houses, One Lost Civilization
The Three Houses are the **surviving arguments** of a collapsed vampire civilization. Vetharis (control), Drakmor (strength), Soleth (memory). Each is one philosophical answer to "how should vampire civilization endure." Full canon: `docs/world/vampire_houses.md` + V-1 through V-6.

### 4.3 Humans — Cities, Kingdoms, Institutions
The most politically visible civilization. Home of the Capital Province, the Solari Covenant, the Mages Guild, the Adventurers Guild, and the machinery of continent-scale governance — much of which the Corruption is currently destabilizing.
See: `docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`, `docs/lore/IMPERIAL_CAPITAL.md`.

### 4.4 Dwarves — Iron Peaks Craftsmen
Deep-dwelling, oath-bound, patient. Craft-first culture; institutional memory measured in centuries. Traders with goblins (Stonefang particularly).
See: `docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`.

### 4.5 Elves, Gnomes, Beastfolk, Halflings, Orcs
- **Elves** — long-lived, forest and grove cultures; often Veridan/Solenne/Noctis emphasis.
- **Gnomes** — inventors and archivists; the Great Library's Sealed Archive is gnomish-tended.
- **Beastfolk** — canonized as canonical peoples in A6; culture to be authored later.
- **Halflings** — canonized in A6; culture to be authored later.
- **Orcs** — canonical (Ragash is orc per D-03); goblin-adjacent warrior traditions.

## 6. Legacy Questlines

Each civilization has (or will have) a **Legacy Questline** — a long-arc storyline expressing that civilization's central philosophical question through play.

- **Goblins:** unity across the three tribes. *"Can civilization grow from its own history?"*
- **Vampires:** *"Can vampires create something new without repeating the mistakes that destroyed what came before?"* (per V-1)
- **Humans:** (to be authored) likely around the Corruption's institutional destabilization
- **Dwarves:** (to be authored) around inherited oath and inherited debt
- **Elves, Gnomes, Beastfolk, Halflings, Orcs:** to be authored per civilization pass

Framework doc: `LEGACY_CIVILIZATION_QUESTLINES.md`.

## 7. Companion Philosophy

Every companion carries a **Core Wound** — an inherited or lived imbalance they are wrestling toward resolution. Their arcs are not "power-up" arcs; they are *balance* arcs. Some companions reach balance. Some do not. Both outcomes are valid.

The player influences companion arcs through *choice*, not through *stat*. There is no companion approval meter in the traditional sense; there is only what the player did, what the companion witnessed, and how the companion updates.

---

## 8. Narrative Pillars

1. **Living World** — the world runs without the player.
2. **Choice vs. Nature** — actions define beings, not categories.
3. **The Corruption as Imbalance** — no external evil is being imported; existing imbalance is being amplified.
4. **The Chorus as Balance** — every virtue is necessary; no virtue is sufficient.
5. **Preserving Wonder** — some mysteries stay unanswered.
6. **Nothing Meaningful Is Wasted** — every idea is preserved, repurposed, or remembered.
7. **The Player is a Voice** — one voice among many; not the world's chosen; not the world's answer; not the world's savior. The world existed before them and will exist after them.

---

## 9. Canon Writing Standards

- **Reuse before invent.** Consult `LEGACY_CHARACTER_RESERVE.md` before creating new named characters.
- **Preserve before overwrite.** Existing canon is a shared resource; contradictions are resolved via reframing whenever possible (see D-04c's Herald resolution as the model).
- **Rule before write.** Major decisions go through `FINAL_CANON_DECISIONS.md` first. Documents follow rulings; documents do not create rulings.
- **Discover, don't explain.** Preserving Wonder governs all new content. If the player *could* discover it through play, do not spell it out in a doc.
- **Cross-reference generously.** Every new doc links to at least three existing docs. Elyndor is a network of interlocking canon, not a stack of parallel files.
- **Respect the Silence.** The Forgotten Eighth's identity, what lies beyond the Final Verse, and the Schism's exact events are permanent mysteries.

## 10. What Makes Elyndor Unique

Elyndor is not the first fantasy world to have:
- Multiple pantheons
- Fallen civilizations
- Long-lived vampire houses
- Goblin cultures
- A creeping cosmic threat

Elyndor is one of the few worlds that treats **balance itself as the theological foundation**. The Divine Chorus is not eight gods squabbling over dominion — it is eight truths that only work together. The Corruption is not a demon — it is the isolation of one truth from the others. Every civilization is a partial answer to how one lives with the fact that no single truth is enough. Every companion is one attempt to find their own balance. Every player choice is a small vote on which voice speaks loudest today.

Elyndor is not a world you save. It is a world you live in. Then you leave it to those who come after.

**Leave it stronger than you found it.**
