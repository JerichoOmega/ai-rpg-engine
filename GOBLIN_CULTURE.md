## 1. Core Identity

Goblins are one of the intelligent peoples of Elyndor — a genuine civilization, not chaotic vermin, not a monolithic threat. The defining characteristic of goblins is **adaptability**.

- Humans are versatile.
- Goblins are adaptive.

Given time and opportunity, goblins can build a thriving civilization almost anywhere. What outsiders often mistake for chaos is often improvisation under constraint — solving problems with what is at hand.

Goblins are non-playable but fully realized. Their culture is distinct, their institutions are old, and their peoples deserve to be treated with the same respect as any other civilization in the world.

---

## 2. Shared Values

Every goblin tribe shares these cultural values:

- **Family**
- **Community**
- **Contribution**
- **Adaptability**
- **Respect earned through service** — never assumed by status
- **Deep respect for elders and experience**
- **Practicality** — waste very little
- **Leaving the tribe stronger than you found it**

Every goblin — warrior, builder, healer, hunter, parent, blacksmith, merchant, leader — is expected to contribute to the survival and future of the tribe. Contribution is not measured by heroism. A parent raising a capable child, an elder mediating a dispute, a smith rebuilding a broken tool — all are equally recognized.

---

## 3. The Three Tribes

The three established tribes are the **only** major goblin civilizations. There is no fourth tribe. Any hostile or "evil" goblins encountered are **Corrupted Goblins** — former members of one of the three tribes who have fallen to the Corruption. The Corruption is not a nation, race, or tribe.

### Stonefang — Warrior-Tradesmen Culture

- **Territory:** Rugged highland edges of The Frontier; defensible ridges and stone outcrops.
- **Voice in the world:** Loud, direct, proud of craft, quick to negotiate, quicker to fight when negotiation fails.
- **Signature:** Every warrior earns a second name through action. Names are *given* by the tribe, not chosen. *Grakkor* is a name earned.
- **Named leader:** **Warchief Grakkor** (Council-selected Chieftain — see §5).

### Mossroot — Listening Culture

- **Territory:** Deep-forest interior; Corruption's early ecological warning zone.
- **Voice in the world:** Quiet, observational, conflict-averse without being pacifist. Speak in careful sentences and pay attention to what others don't say.
- **Signature:** No cairns for the dead — instead, a specific hardwood sapling is planted for each. Old Mossroot territory contains groves that can be dated by tree age — a walking map of the tribe's losses.
- **Named leader:** **Elder Vess** — the Council's most senior member; typically speaks on the Council's behalf when outsiders are present.

### Ashfire — Reforging Culture

- **Territory:** Newly-claimed lands and old ruins reclaimed after collapse. Growing rapidly.
- **Voice in the world:** Deliberate, formal, oath-bound. Every task is announced before it begins.
- **Signature:** Every Ashfire settlement contains a *Preserving Room* — a Council chamber holding objects deliberately not-yet-decided (an artifact from a lost ally, an unfinished letter, a broken tool). The Council debates them once per season and preserves what the tribe cannot afford to forget.
- **Named leader:** **Warchief Skarra** (leads daily affairs alongside the preservation-focused Council — see §5).

---

## 4. Government

Every goblin tribe is governed through a **Council of Elders**. The tribes differ in *how* the Council governs, not in whether it exists.

Leadership is **responsibility, not privilege**. To rule is to owe. A Council member's authority is inseparable from what they are willing to sacrifice for the tribe.

---

## 5. Council of Elders

Goblins believe **wisdom comes from experience**. Only elders may serve on a council because they have lived through war, peace, hardship, prosperity, and sacrifice.

### Mossroot — Council Governs Directly

- The Council itself is the leadership.
- Consensus is preferred whenever possible.
- Elder Vess speaks first on external matters but does not decide alone.
- Council debates happen at a stone table so long that no one sits at its head. Physical geometry enforces consensus.

### Stonefang — Council Selects and Advises the Chieftain

- The Chieftain (Warchief Grakkor) makes daily decisions.
- The Council may **remove** the Chieftain if the tribe suffers under their leadership.
- Each removed Chieftain leaves behind a stone in the Council chamber inscribed with the reason for their removal — a living history of Stonefang leadership.

### Ashfire — Warchief Leads; Council Preserves

- The Warchief (Skarra) leads the tribe's daily affairs and outward-facing expansion.
- The Council preserves traditions and safeguards the tribe's long-term future.
- Warchief–Council debates are public but formal; conducted in call-and-response cadence where the Warchief proposes and the Council names a *cost*. If the Warchief can accept the cost, the proposal proceeds.
- This structure is the source of Ashfire's most productive internal tension: the Warchief pushes outward; the Council asks what expansion costs. Deep Ashfire reputation may allow players to witness this tension play out in real Council chambers.

## 6. Religion & Spirituality

Goblins are not highly religious in the traditional fantasy sense. They believe the world itself teaches wisdom through experience. Their spirituality is centered on three things:

- **The First Hearth** — community.
- **The Ancestors** — memory.
- **The Living World** — stewardship.

The gods (the Divine Chorus) are respected but not worshipped daily. Instead, goblins believe:

> *"The gods gave us the world. Our ancestors teach us how to keep it."*

### Shamans

Shamans are a **recognized role** in goblin society, but they are not political leaders and they are not part of the governing hierarchy. Every tribe has shamans, but each tribe views and uses them differently.

- **Stonefang** — Usually one or two respected shamans. They bless warriors, preserve oral history, officiate funerals, and advise the Chieftain and Council of Elders when spiritual or cultural matters arise.
- **Mossroot** — Multiple shamans are common. They act as healers, naturalists, storytellers, and keepers of the tribe's relationship with the land. Spiritual wisdom is considered something that grows naturally through service.
- **Ashfire** — Shamans maintain the First Hearth, oversee ceremonies such as the Ash Oath, preserve sacred traditions, and advise both the Warchief and Council of Elders. They are guardians of the tribe's spiritual continuity rather than political authority.

Shamans **never outrank** the Council of Elders. Their authority comes from wisdom and respect, not law. A council may ignore a shaman's advice, but doing so is usually considered a serious decision because shamans are regarded as the keepers of tradition, memory, and spiritual health.

> **In short:** Elders govern the tribe. Shamans guide its spirit.

---

## 7. Death & Funerals

Death is never hidden. Every goblin attends funerals if able. Each tribe honors the dead differently.

- **Stonefang** — Raises a **memorial stone** carved with the goblin's greatest contribution.
- **Mossroot** — Plants a **hardwood tree**. Every forest around a Mossroot settlement slowly becomes a living history of its people. (See §3 — Mossroot territory's grove-map of losses.)
- **Ashfire** — Returns the **ashes to the First Hearth fire**. They believe every life strengthens the tribe's flame.

**Shared belief:** *"No contribution is ever truly lost."*

**Corruption-related death:** A goblin who dies resisting the Corruption is honored as **one who made their final contribution to the tribe**. (See §16.)

---

## 8. Marriage & Family

Families are the foundation of goblin society. Marriage is viewed as a **partnership of service** rather than status.

- Large extended families are common. Households commonly span three or four generations under one roof.
- Grandparents help raise children while parents work; children are surrounded by elders as a matter of course.
- Meals are shared. Nobody eats alone in a goblin household except by illness or choice.
- Orphans are adopted by the tribe. There is **no stigma around adoption**. A child belongs to the tribe as much as to their parents. A household may include children from lost or scattered tribes, or from other races entirely. Belonging is *earned by presence*, not by blood.

---

## 9. Childhood

Children carry only their first name until adolescence.

- Children are encouraged to explore.
- Failure is expected. Mistakes are teaching opportunities.
- Every adult has permission — and responsibility — to teach.
- A child may receive guidance from hunters, blacksmiths, elders, cooks, builders, and healers. **Everyone helps raise the next generation.**

Play mirrors adult work:
- Young **Stonefang** children play at negotiation and craft-competition.
- Young **Mossroot** children play at silent observation and listening games.
- Young **Ashfire** children play at oath-swearing and formal debate.

Play is cultural muscle memory. What a goblin child practices at seven, they will do at seventy.

---

## 10. Coming of Age — The First Contribution

Adolescence ends when a goblin completes their **First Contribution** — a genuine act that improves the tribe. This principle is **universal**; each tribe expresses the First Contribution through its own established rite.

Examples of qualifying contributions (any tribe):
- Forging a useful tool
- Protecting another goblin
- Growing food
- Building part of a home
- Healing someone
- Crafting clothing
- Leading a successful hunt

The contribution must genuinely improve the tribe. Only then is the youth considered an adult.

### Tribe-Specific Rites (the form the First Contribution takes)

- **Stonefang** — the youth earns a *second name* through their First Contribution, witnessed by the tribe. The name is announced by an elder, not chosen by the youth.
- **Mossroot** — the youth walks the **Silence Watch** alone for the first time. When they return, if they can name three things that have changed since the previous dawn that no one else noticed, they are considered adult. (The Silence Watch itself counts as a contribution — protective observation of the tribe's territory.)
- **Ashfire** — the **Naming Night**. Before the Naming Night is granted, the adolescent must complete a First Contribution recognized by an elder. Once the Contribution stands, the adolescent stands before the Council and speaks a name they would carry. Any elder may object. A rejected name must wait a full year before being tried again. Some Ashfire adults are still called by their child-name at forty.

---

## 11. Respect for Elders

Elders are not retired. They remain active members of the community until they cannot be — teaching, mediating, keeping oral history, serving on Council, supervising apprentices, resolving disputes.

An elder's word carries weight not because of age alone but because of the accumulated debts and gifts that made them elders. A young goblin who ignores an elder is not simply rude — they are declaring themselves indifferent to what the tribe has already survived.

When an elder dies, their Council seat is left empty for a mourning period. In Ashfire the seat may remain empty for a full season; in Mossroot until the Council reaches consensus on a successor; in Stonefang until the Chieftain formally requests the seat be refilled. Empty seats are visible — the community sees the loss until the loss is answered.

---

## 12. Community Expectations

- Every goblin is expected to contribute according to their capacity.
- Contribution is not judged by scale. A parent's contribution and a warrior's contribution are equally recognized.
- Failure to contribute without cause is a private matter first (Council or elder speaks with the individual) and a public matter only if unresolved.
- Debts within the tribe are formally negotiated — see the **Trial of Debts** (§27).

---

## 13. Daily Life

Time-of-day rhythms differ by tribe:

- **Stonefang mornings** are loud — warriors sparring, smiths at anvils, Council in session.
- **Mossroot mornings** are quiet — the Silence Watch returning, morning meal eaten without speech, work begins only after the last watcher returns.
- **Ashfire mornings** are deliberate — every task is announced before beginning, the Warchief hears the day's plan, work starts on a bell.
- **All three tribes at evening:** stories. Children join the adults. Elders speak first. Silence between stories is respected.
- **All three tribes at night:** the perimeter is walked in shifts, even in peace. This is not a defensive measure — it is a *presence* measure. The land is not left alone.

---

## 14. Food & Hospitality

Goblin food is hearty and practical. **Waste is frowned upon.** Each tribe specializes.

- **Stonefang** — smoked meats, root vegetables, stews, hard breads.
- **Mossroot** — fish, herbs, wild fruits, mushrooms, garden vegetables.
- **Ashfire** — roasted meats, spiced dishes, flatbreads, grilled vegetables, fermented drinks.

### Hospitality

Goblins are cautious of outsiders but not cruel to them.

- A visitor is offered water at the settlement edge before crossing further. Refusing water is refusing hospitality — a serious act.
- A visitor invited to a hearth is not asked their business until after they have eaten.
- A visitor who eats with a goblin family shares a temporary tie to that family. Harming them afterward is an offense to the household, not just the guest.
- **Guests always receive the first serving.**
- **Enemies who parley are still fed.** Enemies under truce are fed. This is not weakness; it is discipline.

---

## 15. Art

Goblin art celebrates **contribution**. Statues are rare. Instead they create:

- Carvings
- Tapestries
- Decorated tools
- Murals
- Pottery

Objects used every day are made beautiful. **Beauty should serve purpose.**

---

## 16. Music

Each tribe develops its own sound.

- **Stonefang** — deep drums; marching songs; forge rhythms.
- **Mossroot** — flutes; whistles; nature-inspired melodies.
- **Ashfire** — chanting; choirs; ceremonial horns.

Story songs preserve history. (Chroniclers — see §22 — often work alongside singers.)

---

## 17. Humor

Goblins enjoy cleverness. Popular jokes involve:

- Exaggerated hunting stories
- Playful rivalries
- Harmless pranks
- Self-deprecating humor

**Laughing at yourself is considered healthy.**

---

## 18. Clothing

Practical first. Decorative second.

- **Stonefang** — heavy leather; stone jewelry; metal clasps.
- **Mossroot** — plant fibers; dyed cloth; feathers; wood carvings.
- **Ashfire** — layered fabrics; decorative stitching; bronze ornaments.

---

## 19. Justice

Justice aims to **restore the community**. Punishment exists. **Revenge does not.**

**Minor crimes:**
- Restitution.
- Community service.
- Trial of Debts (see §27).

**Major crimes:**
- Council hearing.
- Temporary exile.
- Permanent exile (rare).

### Corruption-Related Crimes — Canonical Protocol

Goblins believe the Corruption is both an **affliction** and an **existential threat**. Their first duty is always to protect the community. Their second duty is to save the afflicted if there is still hope.

Every confirmed case follows the same general process across all three tribes:

1. **Immediate isolation** to protect the tribe.
2. **Assessment** by shamans, healers, and the Council of Elders.
3. **Treatment or cleansing** is attempted whenever there is a reasonable chance of success.
4. If recovery is impossible and the individual becomes an imminent danger, the Council authorizes **a merciful death or permanent containment**, depending on the circumstances.

No goblin is abandoned simply because they become Corrupted. Likewise, no goblin is allowed to endanger the entire tribe. This reflects one of goblin society's deepest beliefs:

> *"We do not give up on our own. But neither do we sacrifice the many for the one."*

**Questline surface:** escorting a Corrupted goblin to a sacred healing site; debating whether someone is still saveable; searching for a rare cure before time runs out; deciding between containment and mercy when hope fades.

---

## 20. Economy

- Every adult contributes. Children learn trades early.
- Goblin economies are craft-first and **barter-primary** within the tribe; coin is used mainly for outside trade.
- Every tribe supplies its own basic needs (food, shelter, tools, weapons) before engaging in outside trade.
- Trade between tribes is common. Trade with non-goblins is common:
  - **Stonefang** trades iron goods and warrior services.
  - **Mossroot** trades knowledge of the wild, herbs, and observational scouting.
  - **Ashfire** trades reclaimed materials and its unusual craftwork (rebuilt objects that outsiders often find beautiful precisely because they are second-life).
- Craftsmanship is a source of pride. **Poor-quality work reflects poorly on the maker.**
- Debts within the tribe are formal; debts between tribes are recorded; debts to outsiders are honored strictly.

---

## 21. Education

Education is **lifelong**.

Children learn:
- Reading
- History
- Survival
- Craftsmanship
- Cooperation

Apprenticeships begin young. **Knowledge belongs to the tribe.**

---

## 22. Craftsmanship

Tools carry names. A working hammer, plow, or fishing net in a goblin village bears a small marking indicating who first made it. Some tools have been rebuilt by four generations of the same family — the *name* persists across each rebuilding, so that the tool is functionally continuous even though no original part remains.

This is not sentimental. It is practical philosophy: a craft object is a *continuation*, not a possession. To break a named tool is to owe its lineage a rebuild.

---

## 23. Settlement Philosophy

Goblin settlements are built in **layers over centuries**. Every settlement shows three or four generations of construction. The oldest building is usually the Council chamber (or its equivalent — Mossroot's Long Table, Stonefang's Stones chamber, Ashfire's Preserving Room). The newest building is always a workspace or teaching space.

Every goblin settlement has a **First Hearth** — the fire that started the community. Even in tribes that have moved several times, the First Hearth's ashes travel with them. Extinguishing the First Hearth is unthinkable; it is tended in rotating shifts, generation to generation.

When a player enters a goblin settlement, they should see:
- Children playing.
- Parents teaching.
- Craftsmen working.
- Neighbors talking.
- Elders debating.
- Musicians performing.
- Animals wandering.
- People simply living.

The settlement should feel like it was alive before the player arrived and will continue after they leave.

---

## 24. Architecture

- **Stonefang** architecture is stone and iron — reinforced, defensive without being paranoid, showing repairs over centuries. Old sections of Stonefang halls carry the maker's-marks of long-dead smiths.
- **Mossroot** architecture is timber and earth — settlements grow around trees rather than clearing them. A Mossroot great-hall may include living trees as structural columns.
- **Ashfire** architecture is reclaimed and layered — old ruins re-used, new construction visibly built atop older stone. Ashfire settlements often display the seams where new work meets old work as a matter of pride.

All three tribes share **maker's-marks on structural elements** — walls, beams, hearths carry the identifying signs of the goblins who built them. Some walls hold the marks of goblin *and* gnome *and* even dwarven artisans — evidence of civilizations trading craft across centuries.

---

## 25. History and Oral Traditions

Goblin history is oral, layered, and shared. Each tribe holds a **Chronicler** — an older goblin who preserves memory. Chroniclers differ:

- **Stonefang** chroniclers are blunt and factual. Dates, names, debts.
- **Mossroot** chroniclers are elliptical and lyrical. What was seen, what was not said, what changed.
- **Ashfire** chroniclers are deliberate and probing. Why a decision was made, what it cost, whether it would be made again.

Chroniclers train apprentices for decades. When a chronicler dies, the apprentice recites the last unfinished passage of the tribe's history at the funeral. If they cannot, another chronicler from a neighboring tribe may be requested — a rare but not unprecedented inter-tribe act of trust.

---

## 26. Relationships with Other Peoples

- **Humans** — Respectful. Sometimes cautious. Potential allies.
- **Dwarves** — Mutual respect. Frequent trade. Healthy rivalry between craftsmen.
- **Elves** — Admiration mixed with frustration. Goblins think elves sometimes overcomplicate simple problems.
- **Beastfolk** — Kindred spirits. Shared respect for nature.
- **Gnomes** — Highly respected inventors. Goblins enjoy collaborating with them. *(This is the natural cultural groundwork for Corwin's long-standing familiarity with Mossroot — see §32.)*
- **Halflings** — Warm friends. Both cultures value family and hospitality.
- **Orcs** — Respected warriors. Old disagreements exist but are not defining. *(This grounds Ragash's native fluency in goblin tribal dynamics — see §32.)*
- **Vampires** — Viewed as tragic enemies. Not hated because of what they are; opposed because of what many choose to become.

### Canonical Position on Vampires (Nuance)

The world's central philosophy — *no intelligent race is born evil; individuals are judged by their choices* — applies here. However:

- The established vampire houses remain **dangerous, expansionist, and primary antagonists**. Most vampires the player encounters will be enemies because of the choices they have made and the societies they support.
- **Rare exceptions exist:** a vampire who rejects their house; a vampire living in isolation to avoid harming others; a vampire temporarily cooperating against a greater threat such as the Corruption; a vampire struggling against their nature rather than embracing it.
- These are **exceptional, not common**. They reinforce the world's philosophy while preserving the vampire houses as major antagonistic powers.

Goblins therefore view vampires this way:

> *"We do not hate them for what they are. We oppose those who choose to prey upon others."*

---

## 27. Relationship with the Corruption

Goblins are not naturally aligned with the Corruption — no race is. But the Mossroot are the continent's earliest ecological indicators of Corruption spread, because their culture is built on noticing what others miss. Silent zones in the forest, hollow game, wildlife behavior shifts — Mossroot hunters have documented these for generations, often without knowing what they were seeing.

**Corrupted Goblins** are former members of any of the three tribes who have fallen to the Corruption. They are not a separate people. They were once someone's child, someone's smith, someone's Council candidate. When goblins face Corrupted Goblins in battle, they face people they may have known.

The canonical framing: *"No race is born Corrupted. Every Corrupted was once someone who chose… or was broken into choosing."*

Cultural response to Corruption:
- **Mossroot** grieve. Corrupted Mossroot goblins are still remembered by name; their planting-trees are tended even after they are lost.
- **Stonefang** confront. Corrupted Stonefang goblins are pursued formally; the tribe considers itself in debt to them until the debt is settled.
- **Ashfire** contain. Corrupted Ashfire goblins are recorded in the Preserving Room. What they were before is not forgotten.

**Institutional protocol** for handling suspected Corruption cases is defined in §19 (Justice — Corruption-Related Crimes).

## 28. Shared Sayings and Proverbs

- *"Leave the tribe stronger than you found it."* — universal.
- *"The young carry our future. The elders carry our memory. A tribe without either cannot endure."* — universal.
- *"The gods gave us the world. Our ancestors teach us how to keep it."* — universal (spiritual).
- *"No contribution is ever truly lost."* — universal (funeral).
- *"We do not give up on our own. But neither do we sacrifice the many for the one."* — universal (Corruption).
- *"We do not hate them for what they are. We oppose those who choose to prey upon others."* — universal (vampires).
- *"I am fuel and forge both. What I burn for is what I am."* — Ashfire (closing line of the Ash Oath).
- *"Debts are how we remember."* — Stonefang.
- *"Listen twice; speak once."* — Mossroot.

## 29. Traditions, Festivals, and Holidays

### First Hearth Day (universal)

Celebrates the founding of each settlement. Every settlement observes its own date. The First Hearth burns hotter than usual; every adult adds a piece of fuel personally over the course of the day.

### First Hearth Tending (universal, ongoing)

Every settlement's First Hearth is tended in rotating shifts by every adult in the community across the year. The rotation is a public schedule. Missing a shift is not a small matter; it is a public failure to contribute. *(This is the year-round discipline; First Hearth Day is the annual celebration.)*

### The Contribution Feast (universal)

Once a year, every goblin brings something they *made* — not something they hunted or acquired. Children bring first-attempts. Elders bring lifetime crafts. The tribe eats together and gifts are exchanged by *pairing* rather than by rank. No one gives to the most respected; everyone gives to a specific someone by name.

### Ancestors' Night (universal)

Stories of previous generations are told. Children ask questions. **No work is done after sunset.** Chroniclers preside; shamans lead the invocation of names.

### Renewal Day (universal)

Homes repaired. Tools restored. Community service. **Everyone helps someone else.** The oldest households are visited first; the newest last.

### The Trial of Debts — Stonefang

When a Stonefang goblin harms another (accidental or otherwise), they must formally negotiate a **debt** — a service, a gift, a season's labor. Refusing the trial is worse than the original harm. Debts, once satisfied, are recorded in the Council chamber; unresolved debts are equally recorded.

### The Silence Watch — Mossroot

Every dawn, a Mossroot goblin walks the perimeter of the tribe's territory alone, in silence, listening for what has changed since the previous dawn. The duty rotates through every adult, including the Council. What is heard is reported at the morning meal.

### The Ash Oath — Ashfire

New Ashfire members swear an oath at a hearth that includes ashes from the tribe's founding fire. The oath ends: *"I am fuel and forge both. What I burn for is what I am."* The Ash Oath is renewed publicly by every Ashfire adult once every seven years.

### The Turning — Mossroot

Held the night when the first frost is *sensed* rather than measured. Mossroot goblins burn autumn's last flowers and plant next spring's seeds in the same fire pit's ashes.

### The Reckoning — Stonefang

Every three years the Council opens the Chieftain's Stones chamber and lets any tribemate raise a public grievance against the current Chieftain. Most years, nothing is raised. When something is raised, the tribe listens.

### The Naming Night — Ashfire

See §10 Coming of Age.

---

## 30. Symbols

Each tribe has its own emblem. All goblins, however, recognize these **four shared symbols**:

- **The Hearth** — home.
- **The Ember** — hope.
- **The Hand** — contribution.
- **The Tree** — growth.

Together they symbolize: **Home. Hope. Contribution. Growth.**

These symbols appear woven into tapestries, carved into doorframes, and stitched into travel gear. Even Corrupted goblin sites often retain traces of them — a small tragedy the tribes note but do not remove.

---

## 31. Final Cultural Principle

> Goblins believe a good life is measured not by power, wealth, or glory, but by what you leave behind for those who come after you. Every tool forged, every child taught, every home built, every field planted, and every sacrifice made is another ember added to the fire that will warm future generations. To a goblin, immortality is not living forever — it is leaving the tribe stronger than you found it.

## 32. Companion & Cross-Cultural Notes

- **Corwin ↔ Mossroot:** Corwin is the Gnome Ranger canonized in D-03. His work as a Corruption investigator likely brought him into contact with Mossroot hunters years before the game begins. Elder Vess may already know his name. Additionally, goblin-gnome cultural affinity (§26) means Corwin is treated as a **respected collaborator on sight** in Mossroot territory, not merely tolerated. This is *the* natural first-goblin-encounter for a Corwin-Origin playthrough.
- **The Community Healer ↔ displaced goblins:** Her refugee settlement (per D-03) may include Corrupted-Goblin survivors' family members, orphaned goblin children, or exiles rejected by their own tribes. Undefined territory; worth authoring later.
- **Ragash ↔ tribal familiarity:** Ragash's Orc-tribal upbringing means she reads goblin tribal structure natively. She recognizes Council dynamics without explanation. She is often the first companion to grasp why a negotiation is going the way it is. §26 confirms this is *culturally grounded*, not personality alone: goblins and orcs share centuries of neighboring civilization.
