# RELIGIOUS_SYMBOLS_AND_ICONOGRAPHY.md — The Visual Language of the Chorus

> **Canon Status:** ✅ CANONICAL — established February 2026.
> **Purpose:** The definitive visual reference for Elyndor's religious aesthetics. Every temple, banner, priestly vestment, prayer bead, sacred plant, holy metal, incense, seasonal decoration, and iconographic motif is grounded here.
> **Design principle:** the Divine Chorus is *encountered* through its aesthetics before it is *understood* through its theology. What a person *sees* at a temple should teach them something about the Voice honored there before they read a single text.

---

## 1. The Eight Symbols

The core canonical symbols per `DIVINE_CHORUS.md` §3. These appear woven, carved, painted, embroidered, and worn throughout Elyndor.

| Voice | Symbol | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurelion** | Rising sun behind a mountain | Half-sun ascending; the mountain's silhouette dark below; simple, always oriented so the sun rises to the viewer's right. |
| **Kaelos** | Hammer on anvil | Squared and grounded; the hammer resting, not raised — Kaelos is strength at peace with itself. |
| **Elyra** | Eternal hearth | A stylized hearth with three visible flames; often shown with two hands warming above it. |
| **Veridan** | Great tree with exposed roots | The roots are drawn as elaborately as the branches; a Veridan symbol without visible roots is considered incomplete. |
| **Solenne** | Open book | Two pages visible; no text shown, or (in scholarly variants) a single line in an unreadable script. |
| **Tyras** | Balanced scales | Perfectly level; a symbol drawn with the scales tipped is a warning, not a devotion. |
| **Lyssara** | Open hand holding a seed | Palm upward; the seed small enough that it could be missed by an inattentive viewer. |
| **Noctis** | Crescent moon with stars | Seven visible stars (never eight — the Forgotten's star is deliberately absent). |

### The Eightfold Ring

The full Chorus displayed together forms **the Eightfold Ring** — the eight symbols arranged in a circle, equidistant, no symbol at the top. Common above temple doorways, on formal vestments, and on documents of unusual solemnity. In some traditions, the Ring includes a small unfilled space between two symbols — a visual acknowledgment of the Forgotten Eighth's place.

### The Silence

A blank space in an otherwise-complete iconographic display represents the Forgotten Eighth. Never filled. Never labeled. Its position within the Ring is not standardized — different traditions place it between different Voices, based on which Voice they believe was Forgotten.

## 2. Temple Banners

Banners hang inside and outside temples to indicate which Voice(s) the temple emphasizes.

- **Solid-color banners** display sacred colors (see §5) without symbols; used at festivals.
- **Symbol banners** display the Voice's icon on a background of the sacred color.
- **Ring banners** display the Eightfold Ring; used for shared festivals and multi-Voice occasions.
- **Herald banners** display a Herald's ceremonial mark alongside the Voice they serve (see §11); used at tradition-specific temples (Solari, Old Ways, Ancestors' Path, Veiled Order).

Banners are hand-woven where possible. A machine-woven banner is not disrespectful, but a hand-woven one carries additional weight because the weaver's labor is itself a Lyssara offering.

## 3. Clergy Clothing

Priesthood vestments vary by tradition, but common elements recur:

- **Robes** in the Voice's sacred color(s); floor-length in formal settings, knee-length in daily temple work.
- **A stole or sash** bearing the Voice's symbol; worn diagonally from left shoulder to right hip.
- **A small pendant** with the Voice's symbol; worn under the robe, against the skin; never displayed publicly.
- **No jewelry beyond function** — a Solenne priest may wear a reading-lens on a chain; a Kaelos priest may wear a small hammer-pendant that is actually usable. Decoration for its own sake is discouraged across all traditions.
- **Bare feet or simple sandals** inside temple spaces; the earth is holy in most traditions.

Priests do not wear vestments outside temple duties. In daily life, they dress as ordinary members of their community. This is a canonical humility.

### Vestment Colors by Voice

| Voice | Primary Robe Color | Accent Color |
|---|---|---|
| Aurelion | Pale gold | Soft rose |
| Kaelos | Deep iron-grey | Ember-red |
| Elyra | Warm terracotta | Cream |
| Veridan | Forest green | Root-brown |
| Solenne | Dark blue | Cream (for stole) |
| Tyras | White | Black (for balance) |
| Lyssara | Sun-yellow | Earth-brown |
| Noctis | Deep indigo | Silver |

## 4. Pilgrim Tokens

Pilgrims carry small tokens marking their journey.

- **The Circle of Eight pilgrim** carries a small wooden ring with all eight symbols burned into it; each symbol is darkened as the pilgrim visits that Voice's temple.
- **Herald Path pilgrims** carry seven small clay disks, one per Herald; each is stamped with the temple's mark when visited.
- **Silent Vigil tokens** — a single black bead, given at Noctis temples to those completing the seven-night vigil; often woven into a bracelet along with a family member's hair.
- **First Hearth pilgrims (goblin)** carry a fragment of ash from another settlement's First Hearth; brought home to mingle with their own.

Tokens are personal, not ceremonial. Losing one is not a religious failure — it is a small grief.

## 5. Prayer Beads

Prayer beads are used privately, not in group worship.

- **The Eight Beads** — a string of eight beads, one per Voice, each in the Voice's sacred color. Held one at a time while reflecting on that Voice's Core Truth. A common personal-devotion object across every civilization.
- **The Herald Beads** — a string of seven beads plus one Void bead (unmarked, representing the Forgotten). Common in scholarly and Ancestors' Path traditions.
- **The Long Chain** — Solenne-specific; hundreds of small beads, each representing a name or memory the holder is trying to keep alive. Chains grow across a lifetime; when the owner dies, the chain is passed on or buried with them.
- **The Single Bead** — worn on a cord around the neck; represents the Voice the wearer most needs to remember today. Chosen fresh each morning in some traditions.

## 6. Sacred Colors

Sacred colors per Voice, as displayed in banners, vestments, and iconography. Combinations across Voices are used for pairings (Aurelion + Elyra, etc.).

| Voice | Primary | Secondary | Occasion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aurelion** | Pale gold | Soft rose | Dawn; births; new beginnings |
| **Kaelos** | Iron-grey | Ember-red | Forge; war; protection |
| **Elyra** | Terracotta | Cream | Home; welcome; harvest |
| **Veridan** | Forest green | Root-brown | Growth; birth; recovery |
| **Solenne** | Dark blue | Cream | Study; funerals; memorials |
| **Tyras** | White | Black | Courts; oaths; judgment |
| **Lyssara** | Sun-yellow | Earth-brown | Craft; gift; harvest |
| **Noctis** | Deep indigo | Silver | Death; night; reflection |

**Undyed cloth** (natural wool, linen, or cotton) is universally acceptable for personal devotion and is preferred by many humble worshippers regardless of tradition. It represents the Chorus as whole, unemphasized.

## 7. Sacred Flowers

Every Voice has one or more associated blooms; used at weddings, funerals, festivals, and altar offerings.

- **Aurelion** — the **dawn-lily**, a white bloom that opens only at first light and closes by mid-morning; often planted around eastern temple entrances.
- **Kaelos** — the **iron-thistle**, a purple thistle that grows in stony ground where nothing else survives; used in warrior funerals and forge festivals.
- **Elyra** — the **hearth-marigold**, a warm-orange flower that blooms across the growing season; woven into wedding crowns and hospitality wreaths.
- **Veridan** — the **first-shoot**, a small green sprig from any species — a symbol of new growth generally, not one species; used in birth and planting rituals.
- **Solenne** — the **ink-poppy**, a deep blue poppy whose crushed petals were once used for scholarly ink; still symbolically associated with writing and study.
- **Tyras** — the **balanced-lily**, a white flower with two symmetrical petals; used at judicial ceremonies and oath-swearings.
- **Lyssara** — the **gift-clover**, a four-lobed clover-relative; given as tokens between neighbors on Contribution and Giving days.
- **Noctis** — the **night-star**, a white flower with five-pointed petals that opens after sunset; grown in Noctis temple gardens and laid on graves.

## 8. Sacred Animals

Not worshipped, but respected as symbolic of specific Voices.

- **Aurelion** — the **dawn-lark**; its first song of the day is considered a small blessing.
- **Kaelos** — the **anvil-hound**, a heavy-boned working dog bred in dwarven and Stonefang communities; loyal, patient, protective.
- **Elyra** — the **hearth-cat**; a domestic cat, especially one that adopts a household without being invited. Cats that choose you are considered Elyra's small mercies.
- **Veridan** — the **root-hare**, any wild hare or rabbit — associated with cycles and adaptation.
- **Solenne** — the **thread-owl**, a small owl associated with libraries; often seen in Solenne temples where it hunts vermin among the archives.
- **Tyras** — the **scale-crane**, a long-legged wading bird that stands utterly still until it acts; symbol of judicial patience.
- **Lyssara** — the **gift-bee**; every honey-jar carries an implicit Lyssara blessing.
- **Noctis** — the **veil-moth**, a large pale moth that emerges only at dusk; found in Noctis temple gardens and at graves.

## 9. Holy Metals

Metals used in ceremonial objects, worn as pendants, and forged into relics.

- **Kaelos** — iron (raw, honest) and steel (forged, refined). Kaelos symbols are almost never gold.
- **Aurelion** — pale gold; associated with dawn and hope.
- **Elyra** — copper (warm to the touch); associated with hearths.
- **Veridan** — no primary metal; wood is Veridan's material. Where metal is required, unrefined silver.
- **Solenne** — bronze (used for library plaques and record-vessels); considered dignified without ostentation.
- **Tyras** — silver (bright, honest, tarnishes visibly and must be maintained — the metaphor is intentional).
- **Lyssara** — every metal, especially recycled or remade metal. A Lyssara offering is often a tool made from an older, broken tool.
- **Noctis** — pewter or lead; heavy, cool, dignified.

**Gold in general** is treated with caution across traditions — it is Aurelion's specifically, and using it for other Voices risks theological narrowing. Elaborate gilded temples are considered gauche by every tradition except (unofficially) the wealthiest Solari Covenant institutions, which are quietly criticized for it.

## 10. Incense and Ceremonial Candles

Incense is used to mark the transition from ordinary to sacred space, and to signal which Voice is being honored.

- **Aurelion** — citrus and morning-honey; used at dawn services.
- **Kaelos** — cedar and forge-smoke; often literal charcoal smoke, treated as sacred.
- **Elyra** — cinnamon and baking bread; some Elyra temples burn no incense at all and instead ensure the kitchen scents reach the sanctuary.
- **Veridan** — pine, moss, wet earth; often unlit at all — the scent comes from living plants inside the temple.
- **Solenne** — old paper, ink, and a specific reading-oil that Solenne temples make themselves.
- **Tyras** — sage and clean water; the water is not burned but visibly present.
- **Lyssara** — beeswax candles; the light itself is the offering.
- **Noctis** — myrrh and cold stone; often no incense — silence is Noctis's atmosphere.

### Ceremonial Candles

- **Colors** match the Voice's sacred color.
- **Number** matters. One candle for private devotion; three for household observance; seven at Herald services; eight (in a ring) for full-Chorus rites; the seventh candle in a Noctis service is left unlit to represent the Forgotten.
- **The Silent Candle** — a candle lit but not placed on the altar; placed instead on the empty seat at the Silent Feast (per `DIVINE_CHORUS.md` §11). It is allowed to burn out unattended.

## 11. Herald Iconography

The seven canonical Heralds retain their existing iconographic identities, preserved from prior canon (`elyndor/magic/MAGIC_BIBLE.md`, `docs/world/religions.md`). Herald imagery is *subordinate to and points beyond* the Voice's symbol.

| Herald | Iconographic Element | Voice Served |
|---|---|---|
| **Solaryn** — First Flame | A single upright flame; often paired with Kaelos's hammer-on-anvil to signal the Herald's relationship to the Voice | Kaelos |
| **Sylvara** — Deep Root | A downward-reaching root system; often paired with Veridan's great tree | Veridan |
| **Morvel** — Patient Witness | An unblinking eye; often paired with Noctis's crescent | Noctis |
| **Eldris** — Long Thread | A woven thread across generations; often paired with Solenne's open book | Solenne |
| **Tharok** — Unbroken Foundation | A single squared stone; paired with either Kaelos or Veridan depending on tradition (see D-04c §5.3) | *Debated* |
| **Neressa** — Endless Tide | A cresting wave; paired with either Tyras or Elyra depending on tradition | *Debated* |
| **Zephyros** — Wandering Wind | A curved gust-line; paired with either Lyssara or Aurelion depending on tradition | *Debated* |

**Convention:** when a Herald icon is displayed *without* a Voice pairing, it is a signal that the temple or tradition is either (a) uncertain of the Herald's Voice-association, (b) deliberately preserving the theological debate, or (c) confusing Herald with Voice (a common institutional error).

## 12. Marriage Symbols

Wedding iconography draws primarily on **Elyra** (hearth) and **Lyssara** (gift), with a third Voice chosen by the couple.

- **The Two Hands** — clasped hands, one from each partner; the primary symbol of a marriage.
- **The Shared Flame** — a single candle lit from two smaller candles; used in the ceremony itself and preserved (unlit, on a shelf) in the couple's home afterward.
- **The Gift Braid** — a woven cord combining threads from each partner's family; worn around the wrist by both partners on the wedding day.
- **The Hearth-Mark** — a symbol carved into the couple's first shared threshold; different per culture.
- **Goblin marriage symbol:** two hands joined around a small tool — the Two Hands / Shared Tool motif — literalizing the Doctrine of Partnership as Service.

---

## 13. Funeral Symbols

Funeral iconography draws primarily on **Noctis** (peace at the threshold) and **Solenne** (memory).

- **The Crescent and the Book** — Noctis's moon paired with Solenne's book; the universal funerary combination.
- **The Empty Seat** — a chair left at every meal for a set period after the death; universal grief practice across most cultures.
- **The Woven Name** — the deceased's name embroidered into a cloth kept by the family; unrolled at anniversaries.
- **Goblin funeral symbols** vary by tribe:
  - **Stonefang** — the memorial stone bears the deceased's name and greatest contribution
  - **Mossroot** — the planted tree carries a small carved marker at its base
  - **Ashfire** — the deceased's name is added to the First Hearth's List of Contributions, kept by the Chronicler
- **Veiled Order funerary marks** — a small unadorned indigo ribbon worn by the bereaved for one full year.

## 14. Seasonal Decorations

Temple and household decorations shift with the year's rhythm.

- **First Light (winter solstice):** unlit candles arranged so they can be lit sequentially through the vigil; pale gold cloth over dark surfaces.
- **The Forge Day (midsummer):** iron and steel objects displayed; anvils and hammers polished and shown.
- **Hearth Week (autumn):** open doors marked with terracotta cloth; hearths kept burning through the seven days.
- **The Green Turning (spring):** first-shoots gathered and displayed; green ribbons on doorways.
- **The Reading (winter):** open books on public display; ink-poppy blooms on library windows.
- **The Weighing (autumn):** balanced-lily wreaths at courthouses and civic buildings.
- **The Giving (pre-hard-season):** every household displays something they intend to give away.
- **The Long Night (autumn/late-year):** indigo cloth; night-star flowers on graves; silver polished.
- **The Silent Feast:** the empty seat prepared with white cloth, empty plate, unlit candle, and a fresh flower whose species is not standardized (each tradition chooses).

## 15. Temple Architecture Motifs

Architectural motifs distinctive to each Voice's temples (per `DIVINE_CHORUS.md` §3).

- **Aurelion temples:** east-facing atria, high ceilings for sunrise light, no permanent flame.
- **Kaelos temples:** central working forge, squared architecture, tool-hung walls.
- **Elyra temples:** low ceilings, warm materials, no closed doors, long benches for strangers.
- **Veridan temples:** built around living trees, moss and ivy welcomed, partially open roofs.
- **Solenne temples:** long reading-hall geometry, low green-shaded lamps, visible scriptorium corner.
- **Tyras temples:** symmetric halls, two doors instead of one, central visible scales.
- **Lyssara temples:** half-workshop; tables for making; no altar; gift-walls.
- **Noctis temples:** underground or partially so, cool stone, deliberately dim, silence-by-expectation.

### Universal Elements

Regardless of Voice:
- **A threshold stone** at every temple entrance; visitors touch it before entering. Worn smooth by generations.
- **A List of Names** somewhere within — of those who died in this temple's service, of local worthies, of everyone the community wishes to remember. Never displayed prominently; discovered by attentive visitors.
- **An open bench** where any person may sit without being asked their business. In Elyra temples this is the main feature; in others it is smaller but present.
