## What a Legacy Civilization Questline Is

A **Legacy Questline** is an optional, multi-stage story arc that permanently changes the future of a civilization. Not a main-story quest — the game can be completed without ever touching one. But a player who invests in a civilization's Legacy Questline reshapes that civilization's place in the world for the remainder of the campaign and beyond.

Legacy Questlines are the deepest expression of the project's core philosophies:

- **Living World** — the world continues to move; the player helps shape which direction it moves.
- **No Chosen One** — the player never rules a civilization; the civilization solves its own problems.
- **Civilization vs. Corruption** — the Corruption is the universal threat that all civilizations must reckon with.
- **History Belongs to the People Who Live in the World** — the player creates opportunities; civilizations decide their own futures.

---

## Required Characteristics

Every Legacy Questline must satisfy all of the following:

### 1. Multi-Stage

Not a single quest. A questline unfolds across multiple stages — usually beginning as unrelated tribal, faction, or regional content that only reveals its larger shape late in the arc. Players should not know they are on a Legacy Questline until well into it.

### 2. Optional

The main story is completable without any Legacy Questline. A player who never engages with a civilization's flagship arc misses the outcome — but is not blocked from completing the game.

### 3. Requires Long-Term Investment

Legacy Questlines cannot be rushed. They require the player to earn trust with a civilization across a meaningful stretch of the game. Reputation, completed sub-quests, respected traditions, and demonstrated care all matter.

### 4. Permanently Alters the World

Completion produces persistent, visible world-state changes: new alliances, new institutions, new representation, altered trade, altered Corruption resistance, new NPCs, altered dialogue across other civilizations. Failure also permanently alters the world — history takes a different path.

### 5. Never Places the Player in Charge

The player is never crowned, elected, or installed as the leader of any civilization. The player is a catalyst. Civilizations decide their own outcomes.

### 6. Civilizations Solve Their Own Problems

The player's role is to create conditions under which a civilization can move forward. The civilization does the actual work — the vote, the ceremony, the reconciliation, the choice.

### 7. Success and Failure Through Sustained Choices

No arbitrary fail state. No single dialogue can close the questline. Failure occurs only through **sustained neglect** — the player never showing up, never earning trust, never engaging with the civilization until events overtake them.

Success is earned across many hours; failure is earned by absence.

## Universal Structure

Every Legacy Questline follows the same five-stage shape. Individual questlines fill this shape with civilization-specific content.

### Stage 1 — Seeds (Act 1–2)

Foreshadowing begins early. NPCs, chroniclers, environmental storytelling, and small quests plant references to the civilization's unresolved historical wound, unfinished institution, or unhealed division. The player has no way of knowing these are connected.

### Stage 2 — Trust (Act 2)

The player completes individual quests within the civilization's sub-groups (tribes, houses, orders, guilds — whatever structure that civilization has). Trust is earned in pieces. The player learns the civilization's factions individually.

### Stage 3 — Recognition (Act 2 late / Act 3 early)

The player recognizes — or an NPC helps them recognize — that the sub-groups face a shared problem no single sub-group can solve. The Corruption is often the forcing function. This stage is when the Legacy Questline reveals itself.

### Stage 4 — The Convocation (Act 3 mid)

The civilization's sub-groups meet, debate, and choose a shared response. The player is present but does not decide. The civilization decides.

### Stage 5 — The New Order (Act 3 late / Act 4 / post-campaign)

The world reflects the choice. New institutions, new representation, new alliances, new environmental storytelling. Or — if the civilization failed to reach the moment — history remembers what could have been.

---

## Universal Failure Model (per owner directive)

**A Legacy Questline can only fail through sustained neglect.** Never through a single dialogue, persuasion check, or isolated mistake.

The failure trigger is a compound condition specific to each civilization. Recommended shape:

1. The player has not completed the civilization's sub-group questlines by a defined story milestone.
2. Corruption or an equivalent civilization-specific pressure has reached a threshold where the shared response is no longer viable.
3. A key leader (an elder, a monarch, a matriarch, a chieftain) has died, been Corrupted, or been removed.

If all three hold, the questline closes. NPCs reference the missed opportunity for the rest of the campaign — with sorrow, not accusation. History moved on.

---

## Universal Success Model

Success requires sustained engagement. Every Legacy Questline should require some form of:

- Positive reputation with the civilization's sub-groups.
- Completion of the civilization's sub-group questlines.
- One resolved long-standing internal issue.
- Respect shown for the civilization's traditions.

When these are met, the culminating event (the Convocation) becomes possible.

## The Five Confirmed Legacy Questlines (Working Titles)

Names preserved unless future lore requires adjustment.

### 1. The Council of Embers — Goblins

**Reference implementation.** See `CANDIDATE_QUESTLINE_COUNCIL_OF_EMBERS.md`.

Three tribes (Stonefang, Mossroot, Ashfire) form a permanent inter-tribal council after a failed first attempt generations ago. Sub-groups: the three tribes. Convocation: the Fourth Flame Ceremony. New Order: goblin representation at the Capital + mixed patrols + reopened trade.

### 2. The Reclamation of Khaz-Dur — Dwarves

Working concept: an ancient dwarven hall — abandoned during the Sundering — is reclaimed. Sub-groups likely: rival dwarven clans or houses, each with claim on the hall. Convocation: the descent ceremony that reopens the deepest halls. New Order: dwarves reclaim a piece of their pre-Sundering heritage; gnome archivists gain access to records lost for an age; the Iron Peaks gain a new political center.

### 3. The Renewal of the Elder Grove — Elves

Working concept: an ancient elven grove — where inherited names were once returned — has fallen dormant. Sub-groups likely: elven communities that hold different pieces of the ceremony's original knowledge. Convocation: the ceremony that returns a specific inherited name to a specific newborn, restoring the practice. New Order: elven cultural continuity reasserts itself; Talos's disillusionment finds context.

### 4. The Crown's Reckoning — Humans

Working concept: the Kingdom's Crown faces a reckoning for something long buried in its history. Sub-groups likely: the Capital's institutions (Royal Government, Military, Mages Guild, Universities, Great Library, religious traditions). Convocation: an event at the Capital where the Kingdom formally acknowledges what was done. New Order: Kingdom politics reshape; power redistributes; Talos's reasons for leaving military service may be canonized publicly.

### 5. The Gathering of the Great Hunt — Beastfolk

Working concept: the semi-migratory beastfolk kin-groups converge at a shared site for the first time in generations. Sub-groups: the individual kin-groups. Convocation: the Great Hunt itself — not a hunt for prey but a ceremonial pursuit of something the kin-groups have refused to name. New Order: beastfolk emerge from the periphery of the world's politics; new seasonal settlements become permanent; a beastfolk voice is added to inter-civilization diplomacy.

## Design Constraints Common to All Five

- **The Corruption is always the forcing function.** Each Legacy Questline exists because the civilization cannot ignore the universal threat forever.
- **No single-race solution.** Each questline should include at least one moment where the civilization must accept help, information, or presence from outside their own kind. This reinforces the theme that the Corruption is universal.
- **Companion resonance.** Every companion should have a natural role in at least two Legacy Questlines. Talos: The Crown's Reckoning + The Renewal of the Elder Grove. Ragash: The Council of Embers + The Gathering of the Great Hunt. Eleanor: The Renewal of the Elder Grove + The Reclamation of Khaz-Dur. Torren: The Reclamation of Khaz-Dur + The Crown's Reckoning. Ronan: The Gathering of the Great Hunt + The Crown's Reckoning. Corwin: The Council of Embers + The Gathering of the Great Hunt. Community Healer: any questline touching refugee stakes.
- **Never merge civilizations.** Each Legacy Questline ends with civilizations more themselves, not less.
- **Small stories inside big stories.** Every stage of every Legacy Questline should contain at least one Small Story per Living World principle — an ordinary person with an ordinary problem within the larger frame.

---

## Player Feeling — The North Star

By the time the player has helped one civilization through its Legacy Questline, they should feel: *"I did not save that people. I helped them save themselves."*

By the time the player has helped several, they should feel: *"The world is different because I was in it — but it does not belong to me."*

This is the difference between the chosen-one fantasy and this project's philosophy. Every Legacy Questline exists to teach that distinction through gameplay.
