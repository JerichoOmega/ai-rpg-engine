# LEGACY_CHARACTER_RESERVE.md — Elyndor's Living Character Archive

> **Canon Status:** Established February 2026 as **official worldbuilding policy**.
> **Purpose:** A permanent repository of retired, replaced, and superseded characters preserved for future repurposing into the living world.
> **This is not a graveyard.** It is a pool of future NPCs, historical figures, mentors, villains, scholars, merchants, quest givers, explorers, and legends waiting to be reused.
> **Cross-references:** `FINAL_CANON_DECISIONS.md` (see Policy P-01) · `PRESERVING_WONDER_DESIGN_DIRECTIVE.md` · `LIVING_WORLD_DESIGN_PILLAR.md`

---

## 1. Philosophy

Two guiding principles govern this document:

> **"Nothing meaningful is wasted."**

> **"Whenever practical, legacy characters should be repurposed before being removed from canon."**

The goal is to preserve good creative work while allowing the world to evolve.

Elyndor is not built from a single draft. It is built from **layers of thinking**, each of which produced characters, ideas, names, and roles. When a character is replaced or superseded, the strongest question to ask is not *"can we delete this?"* but *"can this character still enrich the world in another role?"*

Only if the answer is genuinely *"no"* — after real consideration — should a character be archived without future plans. Even then, they belong in the **Reserved** section below, not deleted, because a character with no immediate role may become the perfect fit for a future civilization, questline, or historical mystery.

The Living World grows from its own history. This document is that history's back-office.

## 2. Character Reserve Format (Template)

Every entry in this document uses the following template. When retiring or replacing a character, create a new entry using this format.

```
### [Original Name]

**Original Role:** [What they were in prior canon]
**Original Source:** [Which document(s) originally referenced them; approximate date]
**Current Canon Status:** [Superseded by / Retired / Reserved]
**Why the Role Changed:** [Brief explanation]

**Personality Summary:** [2–4 sentences]
**Core Themes:** [The philosophical or emotional themes the character embodied]
**Memorable Traits:** [Specific images, gestures, phrases, or details that made them distinctive]

**Potential Future Uses:**
- [Suggested repurpose 1]
- [Suggested repurpose 2]
- [Suggested repurpose 3]

**Suggested Civilizations:** [Where they might best fit — may differ from their original placement]
**Quest Potential:** [Types of quests they could naturally anchor]
**Historical Potential:** [Whether they work as a historical or legendary figure]
**Recommended Priority:** [P1 = strongly reusable soon / P2 = when opportunity arises / P3 = long-term reserve]
**Notes:** [Any additional context, warnings, or design considerations]
```

## 3. Categories

Legacy characters are organized by their **best future use**, not by their original role. A former ruler may be best repurposed as a scholar; a former merchant may be best remembered as a legend. The category reflects what the world needs from them going forward.

### Former Leaders
Previous rulers, retired chiefs, former guildmasters, past council members, previous generals — characters whose leadership role has been superseded but whose weight of authority can still enrich the world through their memory, their surviving decisions, or their retirement.

### Historical Figures
Founders, explorers, inventors, scholars, diplomats, heroes, artists, architects, saints — characters best positioned as **remembered rather than encountered**. They shape the world through what they did before the campaign begins.

### Supporting NPCs
Merchants, smiths, innkeepers, hunters, guards, teachers, healers, farmers, council members, craftsmen — characters best used as **encounters at settlement, quest-adjacent presence, or texture**. Ordinary lives that make the world feel populated.

### Quest Characters
Characters who can anchor one or more side quests through their specific situation, moral position, or unresolved history.

### Organization Founders
Creators of guilds, military orders, schools, libraries, religious organizations, trade companies. Their names may appear on institutions the player interacts with even if they themselves are long gone.

### Legends
Characters remembered only through **books, ruins, journals, statues, songs, folklore**. Their canonical existence is *documentary* — the player never meets them, only their traces.

### Reserved
Characters with strong concepts that currently have no natural place. Do not delete them. Leave them available for future inspiration when a new civilization, questline, or historical mystery may find the perfect fit.

#### Serath Vetharis *("Lord Commander Serath Vetharis")*

**Original Role:** Lord of House Vetharis
**Original Source:** `docs/lore/world/WORLD_FOUNDATION_SUMMARY.md` (legacy summary file); appears nowhere in primary canon
**Current Canon Status:** Superseded by **Lord Cassiel Vetharis** in `docs/world/vampire_houses.md`
**Why the Role Changed:** During the 2026 vampire canon development, Vetharis's leader was fully authored as Cassiel — a patient political operator whose current cover identity is Aldric Hourne. Serath's name and title did not survive that development.

**Personality Summary:** The title *"Lord Commander"* implies a more militarized, hierarchical, and openly-authoritative vampire than the current Vetharis philosophy accommodates. Serath suggests an **earlier era** of the House when leadership was less hidden — before Vetharis fully committed to the doctrine that "power held openly is power with a target on it" (per V-1).

**Core Themes:** Command; authority as visible presence; the transition from open hierarchy to hidden influence.

**Memorable Traits:** The formal military title "Lord Commander" — unusual for modern Vetharis, whose leaders carefully avoid announcing themselves.

**Potential Future Uses:**
- **Historical Vetharis figure** — the last "Lord Commander" of the House before it reformed toward its current subtle approach. His portrait or crest may hang in a Vetharis Loyalist Core meeting-place, referenced obliquely by Cassiel when discussing the House's philosophical evolution.
- **A Schism-era Vetharis figure** — per V-4, the Schism was a shared historical event that reformed all three Houses on the question of consent. Serath could be canonized as the Vetharis leader who initiated the House's reform toward "the Considered Offer" doctrine after witnessing the Schism's consequences.
- **A cautionary name** — a Vetharis whose openness of command produced disasters that taught the House to hide.

**Suggested Civilizations:** House Vetharis (historical).
**Quest Potential:** A questline uncovering Vetharis's pre-current-doctrine history — old journals, sealed correspondence, a portrait whose subject the current House does not want identified.
**Historical Potential:** Very strong. Fits V-1's "surviving arguments" frame — Serath is a name from the argument that lost.
**Recommended Priority:** **P1** — the philosophical fit with V-1 and V-4 is unusually clean.
**Notes:** Do not resurrect Serath as a current character. His value is in the *before* he represents.

#### Kira Drakmor *("Warlord Kira Drakmor")*

**Original Role:** Lord of House Drakmor
**Original Source:** `docs/lore/world/WORLD_FOUNDATION_SUMMARY.md`
**Current Canon Status:** Superseded by **Lady Morreth Drakmor** in `docs/world/vampire_houses.md`
**Why the Role Changed:** During the 2026 vampire canon development, Drakmor's leader was fully authored as Morreth — a six-century-old war-lord who regards war as a craft. Kira did not survive that development.

**Personality Summary:** The title *"Warlord"* fits Drakmor's philosophy well. Kira likely represented an **earlier interpretation** of Drakmor's leadership — possibly a more openly-brutal or expansionist version — before Morreth's more disciplined "force as craft" approach became canonical.

**Core Themes:** Conquest; the discipline of violence; the difference between warlord and war-craftsman.

**Memorable Traits:** The plain "Warlord" title — Morreth is "Lady," which is more restrained. Kira's title suggests a more forward-facing military presence.

**Potential Future Uses:**
- **Historical Drakmor figure** — a predecessor of Morreth, possibly the leader Morreth replaced or the sire who turned Morreth. Would give Morreth's six-century arc a specific point of origin.
- **A cautionary Old Guard figure** — an ancient Drakmor whose military doctrine failed in some historical campaign, teaching the House the difference between winning battles and winning campaigns (a criticism the current canon already levels at Morreth's blind spot — Kira could be canonized as the disaster that established that blind spot as *the House's inherited weakness*).
- **A frontier warlord whose name is remembered by mercenary companies** — old mercenary contracts, banners, or oaths may still reference her.
- **Morreth's sire** — a rich possibility. If Kira turned Morreth (V-4 canonicalized the sire/progeny formative bond), Kira would be Morreth's first teacher of Drakmor doctrine. Kira's own eventual fate — died in battle, retired to obscurity, entered the Withering, or fell to Corruption — would be one of the great unspoken threads of Morreth's psychology.

**Suggested Civilizations:** House Drakmor (historical), or independent frontier warlord history.
**Quest Potential:** A questline recovering artifacts, records, or veterans of one of Kira's campaigns — creating friction with the Old Guard's mythologized memory of the era.
**Historical Potential:** Very strong. The "Morreth's sire" angle in particular is a natural way to canonicalize her.
**Recommended Priority:** **P1** — especially if the sire angle is chosen; it would give V-4's sire/progeny doctrine an immediate load-bearing example at the top of vampire canon.
**Notes:** If Kira is designated as Morreth's sire, do not fully author her fate yet — leave it as one of the questions a high-Drakmor-reputation questline could uncover.

#### Mira Soleth *("Archivist-Queen Mira Soleth")*

**Original Role:** Lord of House Soleth
**Original Source:** `docs/lore/world/WORLD_FOUNDATION_SUMMARY.md`
**Current Canon Status:** Superseded by **The Archivist** (personal name unused for centuries) in `docs/world/vampire_houses.md`
**Why the Role Changed:** During the 2026 vampire canon development, Soleth's leader became a titular figure — the Archivist — deliberately without a personal name to reflect his centuries-long identity dissolution into his role. Mira Soleth did not survive that development.

**⚠ CRITICAL:** Mira Soleth is **NOT** the same person as **Mira Deln**, the canonical Soleth operative embedded in Sol Kareth's scholarly community (see `docs/world/vampire_houses.md` line 737). Mira Deln remains a fully-active canonical NPC. Do not conflate them. The Reserve's Mira Soleth entry refers only to the retired "Archivist-Queen" leadership figure; Mira Deln keeps her identity untouched.

**Personality Summary:** The title *"Archivist-Queen"* is fascinating — it combines Soleth's scholarly identity with a monarchical framing that the current canon has moved away from. Mira likely represented **an earlier Soleth model** in which the House's leader was more publicly and formally identified as a ruling figure, rather than the deliberately-diffuse "Archivist" title the modern House uses.

**Core Themes:** Sovereignty over memory; the transition from named authority to titular anonymity; the loss (or renunciation) of a personal name in service of a role.

**Memorable Traits:** The compound title "Archivist-Queen" — no modern Soleth would use it.

**Potential Future Uses:**
- **Historical Soleth figure — the last Archivist-Queen** — the Soleth leader before the Archivist. Perhaps she was the Archivist's own sire and predecessor. Perhaps her renunciation of her personal name established the modern practice.
- **A Schism-era figure** — per V-4, one interpretation of the Schism could be tied to Mira's era. The Archivist may have taken his role, and shed his personal name, *because* of what Mira presided over or witnessed.
- **A Preservationist icon** — Mira's memory may be one of the sacred figures in Preservationist internal culture; the Weaponizers may argue that her legacy has been misused.
- **The name in an archive** — Mira's writings, correspondence, or personal ledger may be objects the player recovers, without ever meeting her.

**Suggested Civilizations:** House Soleth (historical, Schism-era).
**Quest Potential:** A discovery of Mira's personal writings could be the anchor of a mid-to-late-game revelation questline about Soleth's origins — or about the Schism itself.
**Historical Potential:** Extremely strong. She is the natural named face of Soleth's pre-Archivist era.
**Recommended Priority:** **P1** — she is the most philosophically load-bearing legacy character on this list, because her existence gives the Archivist's title-as-identity a specific historical point of origin.
**Notes:** If Mira is canonized as the Archivist's sire and predecessor, this connects V-4's sire responsibility doctrine to the very top of Soleth's leadership — the Archivist's caution around action becomes, in part, the weight of his lifelong moral responsibility for what Mira may have done, or failed to do, or become.

#### Grak *("Chieftain Grak Stonefang")*

**Original Role:** Chieftain of the Stonefang goblin tribe
**Original Source:** `docs/lore/world/WORLD_FOUNDATION_SUMMARY.md`
**Current Canon Status:** Superseded by **Warchief Grakkor Stonefang** in `docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`
**Why the Role Changed:** During goblin canon development, the Stonefang Chieftain became Grakkor — a fuller name and a shift from "Chieftain" to "Warchief" as the tribal leadership title (though both remain in use; see `GOBLIN_CULTURE.md` §5). "Grak" did not survive that development.

**Personality Summary:** The short form "Grak" suggests either an **earlier draft name that grew into Grakkor** or **a distinct predecessor character** — a former Stonefang Chieftain from before Grakkor's rise.

**Core Themes:** Removed leadership; the Council's power to unseat a Chieftain; the weight of a name inscribed in stone.

**Memorable Traits:** Per `GOBLIN_CULTURE.md` §5: *"Each removed Chieftain leaves behind a stone in the Council chamber inscribed with the reason for their removal — a living history of Stonefang leadership."* Grak's stone would be one such marker.

**Potential Future Uses:**
- **A removed Stonefang Chieftain from before Grakkor** — his stone stands in the Council chamber with the inscription of why he was removed. His name is remembered, but his rule ended in failure. Grakkor may have been a young warrior when this happened; the memory of Grak's removal may be part of Grakkor's motivation.
- **A cautionary figure in Stonefang oral tradition** — chroniclers reference him when explaining the Council's power to hold a Chieftain accountable.
- **A player-discoverable historical thread** — a party with high Stonefang reputation may be shown the Council chamber; each stone tells a story; Grak's may be one of the most consequential.
- **The Chieftain before Varkk's grandfather** — a way to give the deputy Varkk (canonical, per `docs/quests/goblin_tribe_quests.md` line 23) family history connected to the tribe's leadership lineage.

**Suggested Civilizations:** Stonefang tribe (historical).
**Quest Potential:** A minor questline recovering something Grak lost that the Council later held against him — or seeking to understand why he was removed, which may cast a shadow on Grakkor's current leadership.
**Historical Potential:** Very strong. The Stonefang Council-chamber-stones system is already canonical and specifically designed to accommodate figures like Grak.
**Recommended Priority:** **P1** — the canonical mechanism to preserve him already exists in `GOBLIN_CULTURE.md` §5.
**Notes:** The exact reason for Grak's removal should be preserved as *discoverable* rather than fully authored — following the "Preserving Wonder" directive. The player learns why by visiting the chamber, not by reading an in-text explanation.

#### Ember *("Warchief Ember")*

**Original Role:** Warchief of the Ashfire goblin tribe
**Original Source:** `docs/lore/world/WORLD_FOUNDATION_SUMMARY.md`
**Current Canon Status:** Superseded by **Warchief Skarra Ashfire** in `docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`
**Why the Role Changed:** During goblin canon development, Ashfire's leader became Skarra — the young nation-builder who unified eight clans through demonstration rather than speeches (per `docs/world/goblin_tribes.md` line 149+). Ember did not survive that development.

**Personality Summary:** The name "Ember" carries strong Ashfire symbolic weight — the Ember is the goblin symbol of hope (per `GOBLIN_CULTURE.md` §30). A Warchief named Ember would have been named for or renamed in honor of that symbol, suggesting a **founding-era Ashfire figure** whose personal identity became inseparable from what she represented.

**Core Themes:** Founding; the moment of unification; the symbolic weight of a name.

**Memorable Traits:** The name itself. "Warchief Ember" is a name a tribe *tells stories about*, not a name a tribe *elects*.

**Potential Future Uses:**
- **The founding figure of Ashfire — pre-unification.** Ember could be canonized as one of the leaders of the eight pre-Skarra clans, or the first goblin who proposed the idea that would become Ashfire generations later. Skarra completed the unification; Ember may have started it.
- **A legendary Ashfire figure** whose name is invoked in the Ash Oath — "*in the memory of Ember*" — connecting the founding to the current ceremonial tradition.
- **A canonical origin for Ember Hollow** — the Sol Kareth geothermal cave complex named Ember Hollow (per `docs/world/GEOGRAPHY_LANDMARKS.md`) may have received its name from a legendary journey Ember made in her youth. This would give a canonical landmark a specific origin story without requiring new author-work.
- **A songs-and-oral-tradition figure** — Ashfire chroniclers reference her when explaining what the Ember symbol truly means. Songs. Story-lines invoked at Contribution Feasts.

**Suggested Civilizations:** Ashfire tribe (historical/legendary); potentially cross-referenced with Sol Kareth (Ember Hollow).
**Quest Potential:** A late-game questline uncovering the true story of the Ashfire's origins — Ember's journey, her death, or her disappearance. Given Skarra's canonical position (*"the Ashfire encompasses what came before rather than erasing it"* per `docs/world/goblin_tribes.md` line 176), Skarra would actively support this discovery.
**Historical Potential:** Extraordinarily strong. She is the single legacy character on this list with the most naturally-legendary framing.
**Recommended Priority:** **P1** — connects goblin canon, Ashfire symbolism, the Ash Oath, and potentially the Sol Kareth geography together with a single character.
**Notes:** Ember should probably remain **never-encountered**. Her value is entirely in the *memory* of her. The player learns her name from songs, from Cinderhold's founding stones, from a Fire-Speaker who invokes her at the unification anniversary. Fully author her only if a specific questline requires it — otherwise leave her mysterious per the Preserving Wonder directive.

## 6. Design Principles for Repurposing

When recommending new roles for a legacy character, apply these principles:

**Prefer creating:**
- Historical figures (remembered rather than encountered)
- Mentors (offer wisdom without being in current authority)
- Scholars (recorded voices in books and archives)
- Explorers (whose journeys mark maps)
- Retired leaders (whose decisions still echo)
- Merchants (recurring, low-stakes texture)
- Guildmasters (from prior generations)
- Quest NPCs (specific situations, specific stakes)
- Famous artisans (whose work still exists)
- Legendary adventurers (invoked in oral tradition)

**Avoid:**
- Immediately promoting every legacy character to a *current* ruling role. Current leadership should remain unique to characters designed for that purpose.
- Forcing a legacy character to remain in the same civilization if another placement is stronger. A retired Vetharis operative may make a better independent Mages Guild scholar than an artificial re-embedding in Vetharis.
- Over-authoring. The Preserving Wonder directive applies here too — legacy characters are strongest when the player *discovers* them, not when the text explains them.

## 7. Living World Integration

The Legacy Character Reserve is a **primary consultation resource** whenever the world needs:

- New NPCs
- Side quest characters
- Historical figures
- Books, journals, statues, songs, folklore
- Mentors and teachers
- Recurring merchants
- Guild members
- Civilization-specific personalities

**Rule of first consultation:** before inventing a new named character, check the Reserve. If a legacy character can serve the need, use them. This lets Elyndor grow from its own history rather than from an ever-expanding roll of new names.

## 8. Future Development Workflow

Whenever a major character is replaced during development:

1. **Evaluate whether they can be repurposed.**
   - Do they have a strong concept?
   - Would the world be diminished by their absence?
   - Is there a plausible current or historical role for them?

2. **If yes:** move them into the Legacy Character Reserve with recommended future roles. Use the template in §2.

3. **If not immediately:** place them in the **Reserved** section (§5.7) for future consideration.

4. **Characters should only be permanently removed if they genuinely cannot strengthen the world** — after real consideration.

5. **Update `FINAL_CANON_DECISIONS.md`** if the retirement or repurposing intersects with any active canon lock decision (rare but possible).
