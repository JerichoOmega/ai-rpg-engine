# PROJECT DUNGEON KEEPER
## CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Project Dungeon Keeper is a handcrafted tactical RPG with a dynamic AI Dungeon Master.

The game is built around:

- Tactical turn-based combat
- Memorable handcrafted companions
- Meaningful player choices
- Dynamic campaign variation
- A living world
- High replayability

Replayability should come from the world evolving differently each campaign rather than replacing the main story.

---

# MAIN STORY

Every campaign begins with the same central premise.

An ancient god remains imprisoned beneath the world.

Its prison has begun to weaken.

Its influence slowly spreads throughout the continent.

This influence is known as:

## The Corruption

The Corruption does NOT mind-control people.

Instead it amplifies what already exists.

Examples include:

- Greed
- Fear
- Hatred
- Pride
- Ambition
- Violence
- Despair

Examples within the world:

- Governments become increasingly corrupt.
- Noble houses betray one another.
- Forests slowly become twisted.
- Wildlife becomes more aggressive.
- Ancient monsters awaken.
- Cults begin hearing whispers.
- Magic becomes unstable.
- Long-forgotten ruins begin awakening.

People remain responsible for their own actions.

The Corruption simply accelerates darkness already present.

---

# CAMPAIGN PHILOSOPHY

Every campaign tells the same overarching story.

The ancient god is awakening.

What changes is how the world reacts.

The AI Dungeon Master dynamically changes:

- Companion availability
- Recruitment order
- Regional events
- Political conflicts
- World events
- Hidden dungeons
- Secret bosses
- Rare merchants
- Side quests
- Companion quests
- Random travel encounters
- Regional outcomes
- Town survival
- Faction relationships
- Consequences of player choices

Every campaign should feel like another telling of the same epic legend rather than an entirely different story.

---

# DUNGEON MASTER PHILOSOPHY

The AI Dungeon Master does NOT create an entirely new story.

Instead it personalizes each campaign.

It should:

- React to player decisions
- Select encounters appropriate for the current campaign
- Manage world events
- Control pacing
- Maintain lore consistency
- Create memorable emergent stories

The AI should always respect established lore.

Randomness should enhance storytelling rather than replace it.

---

# WORLD STRUCTURE

The world uses two exploration layers.

## Strategic Layer

Travel occurs on a continent map similar to Solasta.

Players travel between major locations.

Travel itself is gameplay.

Possible travel encounters include:

Combat

- Goblin camps
- Orc warbands
- Bandit ambushes
- Corrupted wildlife
- Vampire covens
- Werewolf packs
- Necromancer rituals
- Trolls
- Ancient monsters

Social

- Traveling merchants
- Refugees
- Pilgrims
- Wandering musicians
- Mercenary companies

Discovery

- Ancient ruins
- Hidden shrines
- Forgotten watchtowers
- Treasure maps
- Secret caves
- World bosses

Companion Events

Examples:

Talos recognizes an old battlefield.

Eleanor senses magical disturbances.

Ragash's hounds detect hidden trails.

Ronan hears distant howls.

Steven discovers hidden mine entrances.

Travel should never feel like simply moving from Point A to Point B.

---

## Regional Exploration

Upon arrival, players enter handcrafted explorable regions.

Regions contain:

- Main quests
- Side quests
- Companion quests
- Hidden dungeons
- Secret bosses
- Treasure
- Environmental storytelling
- Crafting resources
- Optional encounters

Every region should have its own identity.

# WORLD ENCOUNTERS

Not every encounter should immediately become combat.

Many encounters should present choices.

Example:

A goblin camp is discovered.

Possible options:

- Attack
- Scout
- Sneak through
- Wait until night
- Avoid entirely

Player decisions should create future consequences.

Ignoring encounters should sometimes allow threats to grow.

---

# INTELLIGENT FACTIONS

Not every intelligent creature is automatically evil.

Examples include:

- Goblins
- Orcs
- Vampires
- Werewolves
- Witches
- Necromancers
- Cults
- Mercenary companies

Each faction should have:

- Goals
- Territory
- Leadership
- Politics
- Internal conflicts

Diplomacy should sometimes be possible.

---

# VAMPIRES

Vampires are one of the world's major supernatural factions.

They should never feel like generic enemies.

Possible forms include:

- Ancient vampire lords
- Vampire nobles
- Newly turned vampires
- Feral vampires
- Secret vampire covens

Campaigns may feature very different levels of vampire activity.

Some campaigns may barely feature them.

Others may revolve around stopping an expanding vampire threat.

---

# HERO PHILOSOPHY

Every companion should feel handcrafted.

Mechanics should emerge naturally from personality.

Each companion should possess:

- Unique gameplay
- Unique dialogue
- Unique visuals
- Personal quest
- Relationship dynamics
- Emotional growth

No companion should feel interchangeable.

---

# CORE WOUND PHILOSOPHY

Every companion has one defining emotional wound.

This wound shapes:

- Personality
- Dialogue
- Combat style
- Relationships
- Character growth

Examples:

Talos

Lost faith in governments after years of war.

Eleanor

Believes the world is good and trusts too easily.

Ragash

Rejected by her own people.

Ronan

Believes his curse makes him dangerous to others.

Steven

His mysterious identity defines his journey.
