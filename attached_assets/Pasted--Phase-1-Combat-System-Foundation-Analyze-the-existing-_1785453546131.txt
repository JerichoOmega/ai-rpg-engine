# Combat Philosophy

Combat is a turn-based tactical system fought on square grids within fully 3D isometric environments.

The design philosophy emphasizes:

- Tactical positioning
- Meaningful decision making
- Environmental awareness
- Team synergy
- Readable combat
- Flexible player choice

Combat should reward planning rather than speed.

---

# Battlefield

## Grid

Combat uses square tiles.

Movement occurs from tile to tile.

---

## Movement

Heroes may:

- Move before acting.
- Act before moving.
- Split movement before and after actions as long as movement remains.

Movement should feel flexible rather than restrictive.

---

# Action Economy

Every hero receives the following each turn:

## Movement

Movement Points used for movement across the battlefield.

---

## Action Point Pool (AP)

Action Points are used for offensive and tactical abilities.

Examples include:

- Weapon attacks
- Offensive spells
- Combat abilities
- Dash
- Combat interactions
- Special class abilities

Different abilities may consume different amounts of AP.

---

## Support Action

Each hero receives one dedicated Support Action every turn.

Support Actions are separate from AP and cannot be converted into Action Points.

Support Actions include examples such as:

- Drinking potions
- Applying buffs
- Healing allies
- Reviving downed allies
- Using support abilities
- Other utility-focused actions

Support Actions exist to encourage strategic utility instead of pure offense.

---

## Reactions

Each hero may have one Reaction available each round.

Examples include:

- Opportunity attacks
- Shield blocks
- Counter attacks
- Protecting nearby allies
- Future class-specific reactions

Reactions refresh each round.

---

# Initiative

Combat uses an initiative system inspired by the design philosophy of modern tactical RPGs.

Each combatant has an Initiative value.

Heroes whose turns occur consecutively within the initiative order may be activated in whichever order the player chooses before initiative proceeds to the opposing side.

This system encourages tactical combinations while maintaining initiative-based gameplay.

---

# Facing

Combat includes directional facing.

Front

Side

Rear

Facing influences combat mechanics including:

- Shield protection
- Defensive bonuses
- Flanking
- Back attacks

Positioning should remain an important tactical consideration.

---

# Downed System

When a hero reaches zero health:

- They become Downed.
- They cannot act.
- Allies may revive them during combat.

If combat ends while a hero remains Downed:

- They automatically survive.
- They stand after combat.
- They recover with very low health (approximately 1 HP or another low value determined during balancing).
- They enter a Critical Condition and should be healed before the next battle.

---

# Death

Death is separate from being Downed.

A hero only dies through special circumstances, such as:

- Execution while Downed
- Special enemy abilities
- Story events
- Other intentional gameplay mechanics

Dead heroes cannot be revived through normal healing.

They must be restored using the world's resurrection mechanics.

Death should remain uncommon and meaningful.

---

# Party System

The player controls four active heroes during combat.

Additional recruited heroes remain outside the active party.

Players may freely swap party members outside combat through the party management interface.

Party swapping is disabled during combat.

---

# Recruitment Philosophy

Not every hero is guaranteed to appear in every campaign.

Recruitment opportunities may vary between playthroughs to increase replayability.

Future campaigns should feel different based on available companions and player decisions.

---

# Design Goals

Combat should prioritize:

- Tactical positioning
- Team synergy
- Strategic flexibility
- Readability
- Player creativity
- Replayability

The system should remain approachable while offering meaningful tactical depth.
