Project Evolution Plan – Transition to a Living World Simulation

Objective

The goal of this update is not to add random features.

The goal is to continue evolving the project from an AI-assisted RPG into a persistent world simulation with an intelligent Dungeon Master.

The AI should become one subsystem of the engine—not the engine itself.

Every system should prioritize consistency, persistence, and simulation over generation.

⸻

Core Design Philosophy

The player should never feel like they are chatting with an AI.

Instead, they should feel like they are exploring a world that continues to exist whether they are present or not.

The AI’s responsibility is to interpret and narrate the current world state—not invent a new reality every response.

Before generating any narrative, the AI should always consult existing game state.

Priority order:

1. World State
2. Rules
3. Existing Lore
4. NPC Memory
5. Quest State
6. AI Creativity

Creativity should never override established facts.

⸻

Phase 1 – Deep NPC Memory

Expand NPC persistence.

Each NPC should permanently remember:

* conversations
* promises
* favors
* insults
* crimes witnessed
* gifts received
* friendships
* rivalries
* reputation
* family relationships
* previous encounters
* player choices affecting them

NPC memories should decay only when appropriate rather than resetting.

NPC behavior should naturally evolve from accumulated memories.

⸻

Phase 2 – Living World Simulation

The world should continue progressing while the player explores elsewhere.

Implement simulation systems for:

* settlements
* kingdoms
* factions
* religion
* trade
* migration
* weather
* food production
* wars
* diplomacy
* corruption spread
* cult expansion

Time should continue moving regardless of player location.

Major events should occur naturally.

⸻

Phase 3 – Dynamic Factions

Expand faction simulation.

Every faction should maintain:

* leadership
* goals
* territory
* military strength
* wealth
* diplomatic relationships
* religious beliefs
* public reputation
* alliances
* rivalries

Factions should actively pursue objectives without waiting for player interaction.

⸻

Phase 4 – Consequence Engine

Player actions should ripple throughout the world.

Examples:

* Crimes spread through rumor networks.
* Heroes become famous.
* Villages remember aid.
* Merchants adjust prices.
* Nobles react politically.
* Religious groups respond.
* Kingdoms change policies.
* NPC opinions evolve.

The player should frequently encounter delayed consequences hours later.

⸻

Phase 5 – World Calendar

Implement a persistent calendar.

Track:

* seasons
* holidays
* harvests
* festivals
* birthdays
* coronations
* elections
* migrations
* wars
* religious events

NPC schedules should depend on time.

Seasonal events should naturally influence gameplay.

⸻

Phase 6 – Character Goal System

Every significant NPC should possess ongoing goals.

Goals may include:

* acquiring wealth
* revenge
* political power
* protecting family
* religious devotion
* exploration
* trade
* conquest
* research

NPC decisions should arise from these motivations rather than random AI generation.

⸻

Phase 7 – Rumor & Information Network

Implement an information propagation system.

News should spread gradually based on:

* distance
* trade routes
* travelers
* messengers
* bards
* merchants
* political censorship

Rumors may become distorted over distance.

Different settlements may know different versions of the same event.

⸻

Phase 8 – Director AI

Separate narrative generation from world simulation.

Create a Director layer responsible for:

* pacing
* dramatic tension
* foreshadowing
* callbacks
* emotional highs and lows
* encounter timing

The Director should never modify world facts.

Its responsibility is presentation rather than simulation.

⸻

Phase 9 – Lore Validation Layer

Before every AI response, verify consistency.

Validate:

* locations
* living/deceased NPCs
* historical events
* religions
* factions
* timelines
* inventories
* quests
* relationships

If generated content conflicts with existing lore, revise it before presenting it to the player.

⸻

Phase 10 – World State First

Refactor AI prompts so they prioritize structured simulation.

The AI should receive:

Current World State

↓

Current NPC States

↓

Faction States

↓

Quest Progress

↓

Recent Memories

↓

Player Context

↓

Narrative Request

The AI should interpret the simulation—not replace it.

⸻

Long-Term Goal

The finished project should feel less like an AI storyteller and more like a living tabletop campaign run by an exceptional Dungeon Master.

Players should remember the game because:

* the world remembers them,
* NPCs change over time,
* factions pursue their own agendas,
* consequences persist,
* and every playthrough creates a unique history grounded in consistent simulation.

⸻

Implementation Requirements

* Integrate with existing architecture whenever possible.
* Reuse current managers (world_state, memory_engine, faction_manager, relationship_manager, etc.) instead of duplicating functionality.
* Document all new systems in the Development Bible.
* Keep systems modular and extensible.
* Maintain backward compatibility with existing saves where feasible.
