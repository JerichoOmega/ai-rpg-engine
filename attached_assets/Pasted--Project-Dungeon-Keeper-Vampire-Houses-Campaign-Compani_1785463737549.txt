# VAMPIRE HOUSE PHILOSOPHY

The Vampire Houses are one of the world's primary supernatural powers.

They are NOT misunderstood heroes.

They are fundamentally one of the world's major evil factions.

However...

They are intelligent civilizations with their own politics, goals, rivalries, and philosophies.

The player may interact with them.

The player may negotiate with them.

The player may temporarily cooperate with them.

The player may even make temporary alliances when facing greater threats.

This should never imply that the Vampire Houses are morally good.

Their goals ultimately serve themselves.

They often manipulate others for power, influence, knowledge, or survival.

---

# THREE MAJOR VAMPIRE HOUSES

The world contains three major Vampire Houses.

Their names, histories, and leaders will be developed later.

Current design philosophy:

• Each house occupies a different region of the continent.

• One house secretly operates within the Capital, manipulating politics and noble families from the shadows.

• The remaining two houses should naturally fit other regions of the world.

Each house should possess:

- Unique leadership
- Unique philosophy
- Political goals
- Territory
- Internal politics
- Rivalries with other houses

The houses should compete with one another as often as they cooperate.

---

# PLAYER INTERACTION

Players should be able to interact with Vampire Houses in multiple ways.

Examples include:

- Negotiation
- Temporary alliances
- Information gathering
- Political agreements
- Hostile confrontations
- Infiltration
- Deception

Combat should remain an option, but not the only option.

Every interaction should carry meaningful consequences.

Helping one Vampire House may anger another.

Other factions may lose trust in the player.

Companions may react differently depending on the player's choices.

---

# COMPANION REACTIONS

Each companion should possess their own opinions regarding the Vampire Houses.

## Talos

Talos deeply distrusts all Vampire Houses.

His military experience has taught him that vampires rarely act without hidden motives.

He will only tolerate cooperation if innocent lives are immediately at risk.

---

## Eleanor

Eleanor believes every situation deserves understanding before violence.

She initially hopes diplomacy may save lives.

As the story progresses, she slowly realizes that some Vampire Lords cannot be redeemed.

---

## Ragash

Ragash judges individuals by their actions rather than appearances.

She is willing to negotiate if it protects innocent people or her companions.

She never fully trusts vampires.

---

## Ronan

Because Ronan understands what it feels like to be feared because of what he is, he does not immediately condemn every vampire.

However...

He has no patience for vampires who willingly prey upon innocent people.

He understands the difference between struggling against one's nature and embracing cruelty.

---

# DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Villains are not always immediate enemies.

Enemies are not always fought immediately.

Players may cooperate with morally evil factions when circumstances demand it.

Such decisions should always produce meaningful long-term consequences.

The world should remember these decisions.

---

# CAMPAIGN-SPECIFIC COMPANION RELATIONSHIPS

Dynamic Campaign Story Arcs should also create unique companion interactions that only occur during those campaigns.

These interactions should deepen relationships and provide additional replay value.

---

# THE FRACTURED CIRCLE

If Eleanor and Ronan are both active companions during this campaign...

Additional dialogue, camp conversations, and story events become available.

As Eleanor watches respected members of the Mages Guild succumb to the Corruption...

Ronan becomes one of her greatest emotional supports.

Earlier in the story...

Eleanor helped Ronan fight despair during his struggle with lycanthropy.

During this campaign...

Their roles reverse.

Ronan encourages Eleanor not to lose hope.

He reminds her that those who have fallen were once good people.

He understands what it feels like to fear losing yourself.

This campaign demonstrates Ronan's emotional growth.

He is no longer only someone who needs help.

He has become someone capable of helping others.

---

# RAGASH & ELEANOR

Throughout the main story...

Ragash gradually realizes why Talos is so protective of Eleanor.

Initially...

She assumes Talos simply sees Eleanor as inexperienced.

Over time...

She comes to understand Eleanor's true importance.

Eleanor is the emotional heart of the party.

She inspires hope.

She sees goodness in others.

She reminds everyone why they continue fighting.

Eventually...

Ragash becomes just as protective of Eleanor as Talos.

Not because Eleanor is weak...

But because she recognizes that losing Eleanor would break something within the entire group.

---

# RAGASH & RONAN

Ragash and Ronan gradually develop one of the strongest friendships within the party.

Because Ragash has spent much of her life isolated from her own people...

She understands what it feels like to feel like an outsider.

Her experience raising and training hounds has also taught her that fear and instinct do not define a person's worth.

She sees Ronan as a man struggling against his curse rather than a monster.

She never judges him for his condition.

She becomes one of the first companions Ronan truly trusts.

Alongside Eleanor...

Ragash becomes one of the most important companions in Ronan's personal quest.

Together...

Eleanor offers hope through knowledge.

Ragash offers acceptance through compassion.

---

# PARTY PHILOSOPHY

The companions should gradually become a family.

Relationships should continuously evolve.

Friendships should deepen.

Trust should grow.

Disagreements should naturally occur.

Dynamic Campaign Story Arcs should strengthen different relationships depending on which story arc is active.

This ensures that different campaigns produce different emotional experiences for the player.

---

# DESIGN GOAL

Project Dungeon Keeper should feel like a handcrafted tabletop RPG campaign.

The player's choices matter.

Companion relationships matter.

Faction relationships matter.

Every campaign should tell the same epic story...

But the emotional journey should feel different depending on the world's evolving events and the companions present throughout that playthrough.

These additions should become canonical and be integrated into the Game Bible, Hero Bible, Companion Relationship documentation, World Bible, and AI Dungeon Master documentation.
