This documentation establishes the rules all current and future companions must follow.

⸻

Companion Philosophy

Companions are people, not combat units.

Every companion has:

* Personal goals
* Beliefs
* Strengths
* Weaknesses
* Friendships
* Opinions
* Fears
* A life outside the player

The player is not the center of every companion’s life.

Companions travel together because circumstances and shared goals bring them together.

⸻

Party Structure

Canonical party system:

* Six permanent companions.
* Four active party members.
* Companion swapping occurs only in civilization and designated safe locations.
* Inactive companions remain part of the larger expedition narratively.

⸻

Three Companion Pillars

Every companion must have three distinct identities.

1. Combat Identity

How they fight.

Example:

* Elemental Mage
* Construct Engineer
* Guardian
* Ranger
* Rogue
* Healer

⸻

2. Expedition Identity

How they contribute outside combat.

Examples:

Eleanor

* Magical scholar
* Arcane expert
* Identifies magical anomalies
* Preserves magical reagents

Torren

* Master blacksmith
* Salvage expert
* Engineering knowledge
* Equipment efficiency

Ranger

* Hunter
* Scout
* Survivalist
* Finds food
* Locates herbs
* Tracks creatures
* Detects hidden paths

Healer

* Medic
* Herbalist
* Disease expert
* Medical knowledge

Rogue

* Trap detection
* Lockpicking
* Hidden caches
* Secret passages

Talos

* Structural knowledge
* Defensive planning
* Hazard recognition
* Safe routes

Every companion should contribute meaningfully during exploration.

⸻

3. Narrative Identity

Every companion brings a unique worldview.

They should answer:

“What does this person believe the world needs more of?”

Examples:

* Understanding
* Duty
* Compassion
* Freedom
* Craftsmanship
* Patience

Their dialogue and approval are based on these beliefs.

⸻

Companion Approval

Approval is principle-based.

It is not a morality system.

Companions respond according to their values.

Approval unlocks:

* Additional Journey Conversations
* Companion banter
* Personal quests
* Optional scenes
* Romance (where applicable)

Companions rarely leave the party.

Leaving only occurs after repeated violations of their deeply held beliefs through major player decisions.

⸻

Journey Conversations

There is no traditional camp system.

Companion development occurs while traveling.

Journey Conversations trigger naturally based upon:

* Region
* Story progress
* Companion combinations
* Previous conversations
* Trust
* Major events

Travel becomes the primary location for character growth.

⸻

Personal Quests

Personal quests unlock naturally through:

* Trust
* Shared experiences
* Journey Conversations
* Story progression

They should reveal:

* Hidden burdens
* Past failures
* Personal dreams
* Unresolved conflicts

These quests exist to deepen characters rather than simply provide combat encounters.

⸻

Companion Relationships

Companions interact with each other independently of the player.

Relationships include:

* Friendship
* Respect
* Rivalry
* Mentorship
* Romance
* Professional admiration

Not every companion relationship involves the player.

⸻

Companion Progression Philosophy

Each companion possesses one fixed combat identity.

Players customize how that companion performs their role rather than changing who they are.

Companions never change classes.

Instead, every companion chooses one of two permanent specialization paths.

These choices reflect differing philosophies of combat rather than entirely new identities.

Design Principles

Every companion should feel valuable in:

* Combat
* Exploration
* Storytelling

No companion exists solely for damage output.

Every companion should make the player feel that bringing them along changes the journey in meaningful ways.

⸻

Future Companion Rule

Before any new companion is approved, it must clearly define:

* Combat Identity
* Expedition Identity
* Narrative Identity
* Two Specialization Paths
* Companion Passive Ability
* Approval Philosophy
* Personal Quest Theme

Only companions that satisfy these requirements should be added to the game.

⸻

Developer Note

The goal of the companion system is to create believable traveling companions rather than interchangeable RPG party members.

Players should remember companions because of:

* their personalities,
* their conversations,
* their relationships,
* their practical contributions to the expedition,
* and the unique philosophies they bring to both combat and the world around them.

Every companion should feel like someone whose absence would noticeably change both the gameplay experience and the story.
