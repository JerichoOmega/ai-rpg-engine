Design Philosophy

Avoid traditional fantasy clichés.

Vampires are not a single kingdom.

They are ancient noble bloodlines scattered across the continent.

Some openly participate in politics.

Some live in isolation.

Some have nearly disappeared.

Some actively try to help civilization endure.

Others believe civilization is already doomed.

⸻

Origins

The exact origin of vampirism is unknown.

Several competing theories exist:

* An ancient magical affliction.
* A forgotten experiment from the Age of Harmony.
* A curse dating to the Long Decline.
* A condition tied to primordial magic.

No theory has been conclusively proven.

The truth should remain a long-term mystery.

⸻

Society

Vampires organize themselves into Houses.

Each House operates independently.

There is no Vampire King.

No global vampire empire.

Instead:

* alliances
* rivalries
* political marriages
* ancient feuds

shape vampire society.

⸻

Relationship with Civilization

Most citizens never knowingly meet a vampire.

Many nobles suspect certain Houses possess unusually long family histories.

Rumors exist.

Proof is rare.

Some Houses actively support civilization.

Others manipulate events from the shadows.

Most avoid unnecessary attention.

⸻

Relationship with the Divine Chorus

The Divine Chorus does not hunt vampires.

Nor do they endorse them.

Vampires are considered mortal beings living under unusual circumstances.

They are judged by their choices rather than their condition.

⸻

Relationship with the Long Decline

Every House has its own philosophy.

Some seek to preserve civilization.

Some believe survival requires ruthless pragmatism.

Some believe the world is approaching an inevitable end.

This ideological divide creates conflict between Houses.

⸻

Example Houses

House Valenor

Known for diplomacy and scholarship.

Maintains hidden archives rivaling those of the Great Library.

Prefers negotiation over violence.

⸻

House Draven

Old military lineage.

Produces renowned generals.

Believes order is maintained through strength.

⸻

House Seraphe

Mystics and philosophers.

Study ancient magic and the Final Verse.

Some members quietly investigate evidence related to the Forgotten Eighth.

⸻

House Morcant

Political manipulators.

Experts in diplomacy and espionage.

Maintain extensive intelligence networks.

⸻

House Kaelor

Lives in remote mountain estates.

Known for strict honor.

Rarely involved in continental politics.

⸻

House Noctis

One of the oldest surviving bloodlines.

Its origins are almost completely lost.

Even other vampires know little about them.

⸻

Internal Politics

Vampire Houses regularly debate:

* Whether to reveal themselves.
* Whether to assist civilization.
* Whether immortality is a blessing or burden.
* Whether the Long Decline can truly be stopped.

These debates should shape quests and relationships.

⸻

Gameplay Role

Vampires are uncommon.

Most players may encounter only a handful during a playthrough.

When encountered they should feel important.

They are:

* quest givers
* historians
* political figures
* scholars
* warriors
* antagonists
* allies

Never simply “high-level enemies.”

⸻

Culture

Each House possesses:

* Heraldry
* Motto
* Formal traditions
* Codes of conduct
* Family histories
* Estate architecture
* Distinct clothing styles

Their culture should resemble ancient aristocracy rather than gothic horror.

⸻

Moral Philosophy

Avoid portraying vampires as universally evil or heroic.

Some Houses genuinely preserve communities.

Others exploit them.

Some struggle against their hunger.

Others embrace it.

Every House should possess internal disagreement.

⸻

World State Integration

The World State should influence vampire society.

If civilization weakens:

* Isolationist Houses gain influence.
* Predatory factions become bolder.
* Ancient rivalries intensify.

If civilization remains resilient:

* Cooperative Houses become more active.
* Alliances with human institutions increase.
* Ancient knowledge is more readily shared.

⸻

Quest Integration

Vampire Houses should support quests involving:

* political intrigue
* ancient history
* family disputes
* forgotten bloodlines
* relic recovery
* moral dilemmas
* diplomacy

Avoid simple “kill the vampire lord” quests.

Instead, emphasize difficult choices, conflicting loyalties, and the burden of centuries of history.

⸻

Developer Notes

Vampires should reinforce the world’s central themes.

They are living witnesses to history.

Some remember the First Empire.

Some knew individuals who lived during the Age of Harmony.

Their greatest value is not their supernatural abilities, but the perspective that comes from centuries of watching civilizations rise and fall.

They should feel like another thread in the tapestry of the world—deeply connected to its history, politics, and philosophy—rather than a genre trope inserted from outside the setting.
