Preparation happens in civilization.

Adventure happens in the wilderness.

The player should rarely feel forced into unnecessary menu management or repetitive maintenance systems.

⸻

Safe Areas

Cities, towns, inns, guild halls, forts, and other secure locations act as preparation hubs.

Players can:

* Shop
* Upgrade equipment
* Accept quests
* Change party members
* Talk with NPCs
* Advance companion storylines
* Learn recipes
* Learn alchemy formulas

These locations represent civilization.

⸻

Crafting Philosophy

Crafting is intentionally lightweight.

Players may craft outside of combat, regardless of location, provided they possess the required ingredients.

Crafting is unavailable only when:

* actively in combat
* being pursued during scripted events
* participating in time-sensitive story sequences

There is no requirement to locate crafting stations or camps.

The assumption is that the party takes a few moments to prepare items.

⸻

Cooking

Cooking serves as an optional support mechanic.

Food is not required for survival.

There are:

* no hunger systems
* no starvation mechanics
* no mandatory meals

Food serves two primary purposes:

* moderate healing outside combat
* temporary stat bonuses

Food should never replace potions.

Instead:

Potions = emergency recovery

Food = preparation and sustained adventuring

Regional recipes should reinforce worldbuilding and local culture.

⸻

Alchemy

Alchemy follows the same philosophy.

Players may craft potions whenever they are not in combat.

Requirements:

* learned formula
* required ingredients

Potion crafting should be immediate and intuitive.

No crafting stations are required.

⸻

Companion Passive Skills

Companions contribute outside combat.

Examples include:

Torren

* improved smithing efficiency

Healer

* stronger crafted potions

Ranger

* increased herb gathering

Eleanor

* preserves rare magical reagents

Future companions should provide exploration and crafting benefits that reflect their personalities and professions.

These bonuses should be meaningful without forcing specific party compositions.

⸻

Party Structure

Canonical party system:

* six permanent companions
* four active party members

Companions may be swapped in civilization and other designated safe locations.

Inactive companions remain part of the broader expedition narratively.

⸻

Journey Conversations

There is no traditional camp system similar to Baldur’s Gate 3 or Solasta.

Instead, the game uses Journey Conversations.

These occur naturally while:

* traveling
* exploring
* leaving towns
* entering new regions
* completing quests

Companions converse while the player continues moving.

Journey Conversations exist to:

* build relationships
* reveal character history
* unlock personal quests
* create companion banter
* develop romances

Travel itself becomes the primary storytelling space.

⸻

Companion Approval

Approval is based upon personal principles rather than morality.

Each companion evaluates decisions according to their own beliefs.

Approval unlocks:

* additional conversations
* deeper relationships
* companion quests
* optional scenes
* romance (where applicable)

Companions generally do not abandon the party unless the player repeatedly violates their core values through significant actions.

⸻

Quest Philosophy

Every quest should matter.

Avoid generic filler objectives.

Before approving a quest, it should ideally satisfy most of the following:

* teaches something about the world
* develops a character
* presents an interesting decision
* changes the player’s understanding
* leaves a lasting consequence

Players should remember quests because of their stories rather than their rewards.

Quest design should draw inspiration from The Witcher 3, emphasizing memorable narratives and meaningful consequences over repetitive objectives.

⸻

World State System

One of the game’s core mechanics.

This is not a morality system.

It tracks the overall condition of civilization as the player progresses.

Helping people…

Protecting knowledge…

Resolving conflicts…

Preserving communities…

All contribute toward slowing the Long Decline.

The corruption cannot be permanently stopped.

However, the player can strengthen civilization’s ability to endure it.

This buys the world time.

Helping the world matters.

Ignoring suffering allows the decline to advance more quickly.

⸻

World State Visibility

World State is not displayed constantly.

Instead, it exists within the Journal.

Players must intentionally view it.

Avoid visible morality meters.

Avoid percentages.

Avoid alignment systems.

Instead use descriptive assessments such as:

* Flourishing
* Stable
* Holding
* Strained
* Declining
* Fading
* On the Brink

World State should feel observational rather than mechanical.

⸻

It expands naturally as the player discovers the world.

Major sections include:

Regional observations.

Scholar assessments.

Knowledge preserved.

Major historical developments.

Character Menu

Separate from the Journal.

Contains:

* attributes
* equipment
* resistances
* weapon proficiencies
* active effects
* character progression

This menu answers:

“Who am I?”

⸻

Party Menu

Contains:

* all companions
* active party selection
* biographies
* trust levels
* personal quest progression

This menu answers:

“Who is traveling with me?”

⸻

Inventory

Organized into:

* Weapons
* Armor
* Accessories
* Consumables
* Crafting Materials
* Quest Items
* Valuables

Inventory remains clean and intuitive.

⸻

Statistics

Statistics are intentionally separated from World State.

Examples include:

* enemies defeated
* quests completed
* distance traveled
* gold earned
* deaths
* potions crafted
* meals cooked
* treasures discovered
* hours played

Statistics answer:

“What have I accomplished?”

World State answers:

“What has happened to the world?”

These systems should never overlap.

⸻

UI Philosophy

Adopt a two-layer menu system.

Quick Access Wheel

Accessible during gameplay.

Contains:

* Inventory
* Journal
* Map
* Quests
* Party
* Crafting
* Return

These are the most frequently accessed gameplay menus.

⸻

Pause Menu

Contains:

* Character
* Statistics
* Settings
* Save
* Load
* Accessibility
* Return to Title

This menu manages the player rather than the world.

⸻

Design Philosophy

Every system should reinforce the central themes of the game.

Knowledge grows.

Relationships deepen.

Civilization endures.

Small acts matter.

The player is not the Chosen One.

The player changes history through countless ordinary decisions that collectively leave the world stronger than they found it.

The goal is for every gameplay system—from crafting and companion conversations to the Journal and World State—to support that philosophy through mechanics, not just dialogue or lore
