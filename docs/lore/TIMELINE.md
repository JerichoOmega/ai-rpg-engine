# Timeline — Concise Developer Reference

This document is a condensed chronological reference. It covers the major turning points of each age for use in development — quest design, codex entries, NPC dialogue, academic texts, and historical references.

For fuller treatment of each age, see `HISTORY_BIBLE.md`.

---

## Before History

**The Architects are present.** No records. No witnesses. Evidence only: structures, materials, geometries that cannot be explained by any civilization that came after. The Chorus does not pre-exist this period. Whatever the Architects were, they were here before the gods.

---

## Age of Awakening
*The First Song. The world becomes.*

- The Divine Chorus comes into being — eight members
- The First Song is sung; Primordial Energy flows; physical reality takes its current form
- The first mortal races awaken
- No religion, no writing, no cities — only the raw world and the people beginning to inhabit it
- The Chorus stewards existence; mortals begin the long process of learning what existence is

**Key fact for developers:** No mortal civilization exists yet. No temples, no Great Library, no politics. This age is pre-history.

---

## Age of Discovery
*Civilization begins.*

- First writing systems developed independently across multiple regions
- First cities and organized communities
- First formal religions and temples, including the **First Temple** (oldest surviving temple to the Chorus)
- First schools of magic — the initial understanding that Primordial Energy can be studied and directed
- Trade routes and early political structures

**Key fact for developers:** The First Temple is built in this age. The structure beneath it already exists when the temple is constructed above it.

---

## Age of Unity
*The great empire. The meeting that changes everything.*

- The continent's greatest empire rises — not through conquest alone but through genuine political unity
- The **Great Library** is founded in the Imperial Capital
- Roads, standardized scholarship, continent-spanning trade
- The eighth member of the Chorus meets the mortal she will come to love
- The Chorus is whole; the world is arguably at its best

**Key fact for developers:** This is the age the world still half-remembers as a golden era. It ended from within — not from external attack.

---

## The Long Decline
*The Chorus tries to save her.*

- The Eighth's corruption spreads slowly — Love → Grief → Obsession → Justification → Hatred
- The Chorus spends centuries disagreeing about what is happening and how to respond
- Individual members attempt to reach her; none succeed
- The world does not yet know. The empire is already weakening but mortals attribute this to political causes
- The Eighth's Stage IV arrival: *"It had to be done."* This is when corruption truly takes root
- Stage V: she concludes the Chorus chose law over love and therefore betrayed her first

**Key fact for developers:** The Long Decline is not a sudden fall. It is a slow deterioration that the Chorus watched and could not stop. This is the most emotionally important period in the Chorus's history.

---

## The Sundering
*The Eighth is imprisoned. The world is permanently altered.*

- The Chorus, having exhausted every alternative, imprison the Eighth
- The event scars the physical world — the shape of continents, the behavior of magic, and the balance of Primordial Energy are permanently changed
- The **Great Forgetting** follows: the systematic erasure of the Eighth's existence from mortal memory
- The empire does not survive this period intact
- Written records of the Eighth disappear, are destroyed, or are sealed in archives whose existence is not public knowledge

**Key fact for developers:** The Sundering is the canonical name for this event. "The Imprisonment" is acceptable as a descriptive phrase in scholarly writing. The Great Forgetting is a separate decision from the Sundering itself — the Chorus chose to erase her, and the moral weight of that choice is unresolved.

---

## Age of Restoration
*Modern civilization rebuilds.*

- The empire is gone; smaller kingdoms rise in its place
- The seven-member theology becomes unquestioned truth — no living person remembers the Eighth, and the Great Forgetting has removed her from the accessible record
- The Great Library survives, though portions of its collection are now sealed or classified
- New religious traditions, new trade networks, new political structures
- Magic stabilizes into its current form — different from what it was before the Sundering

**Key fact for developers:** This is the period that feels like "normal history" to most people in the world. Most of what players encounter as established civilization was built in this age.

---

## Present Age
*History appears understood. It is not.*

- A thousand years of relative stability
- The Corruption associated with the Eighth is re-emerging — subtle at first, then less so
- The prison that holds the Eighth is weakening
- The gaps in the historical record are being noticed by a small number of scholars
- Players enter the story here

**Key fact for developers:** The Present Age feels stable on the surface. Underneath, every institution in the world is built on a foundation that has a missing piece. The campaign is about what happens when that piece starts to come back.

## Key Dates (Relative)

| Reference Point | Approximate Era |
|---|---|
| First Temple construction | Age of Discovery |
| Great Library founded | Age of Unity |
| The meeting (Eighth and the mortal she loved) | Age of Unity |
| Stage IV — justification takes root | The Long Decline |
| The Sundering | End of the Long Decline |
| Great Forgetting | Immediately following the Sundering |
| Modern kingdoms established | Age of Restoration |
| Present Age begins | ~1,000 years before campaign |

No absolute dates are assigned. This is intentional — it allows flexibility for regional histories and prevents inconsistencies between different content creators.
