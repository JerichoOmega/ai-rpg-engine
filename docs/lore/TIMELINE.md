# Timeline — Concise Developer Reference

This document is a condensed chronological reference. It covers the major turning points of each age for use in development — quest design, codex entries, NPC dialogue, academic texts, and historical references.

For fuller treatment of each age, see `HISTORY_BIBLE.md`.

> **Canonical framework:** Four Ages. The Long Decline is the tragic final chapter of the Age of Harmony, not a separate age. The game begins near the end of the Age of Restoration.

---

## Before History

**The Architects are present.** No records. No witnesses. Evidence only: structures, materials, geometries that cannot be explained by any civilization that came after. The Chorus does not pre-exist this period. Whatever the Architects were, they were here before the gods.

---

## Age I — Age of Awakening
*The First Song. The world becomes. Civilization begins.*

- The Divine Chorus comes into being — eight members
- The First Song is sung; Primordial Energy flows and stabilizes; physical reality takes its current form
- The first intelligent races emerge
- The first writing systems develop independently across multiple regions
- First cities and organized communities form
- The **First Temple** is established — the oldest surviving temple to the Chorus; the structure beneath it already exists when the temple is constructed above it
- First schools of magic — the initial understanding that Primordial Energy can be studied and directed
- The foundations of magic, culture, and civilization are laid

**Key fact for developers:** This age spans pre-history through the earliest organized civilizations. It covers everything from the world's first moments through the establishment of formal religion, writing, cities, and early magic. No great empire yet. No Great Library. Only the raw world and the peoples beginning to understand it.

---

## Age II — Age of Harmony
*The greatest civilization. The meeting that changes everything. The beginning of the end.*

- The First Empire rises — not through conquest alone but through genuine political unity
- The **Great Library** is founded in the Imperial Capital
- Universities and centers of learning flourish
- Trade routes connect civilizations continent-wide
- Magic reaches its greatest advancement
- Scientific and cultural achievement reaches heights not equaled since
- The Forgotten Eighth — the eighth member of the Divine Chorus — meets an ordinary mortal she will come to love
- The mortal lives an ordinary life and dies an ordinary death
- The Eighth cannot accept it; her search beyond Creation begins

**The Long Decline — final chapter of the Age of Harmony:**
- The Eighth's Corruption spreads slowly as her search exposes Elyndor to forces outside the world's design
- The Chorus spends centuries disagreeing about what is happening and how to respond
- The world does not yet know; the empire weakens but mortals attribute this to political causes
- Individual Chorus members attempt to reach her; none succeed
- Reality begins breaking in places where the Eighth's research has reached furthest
- The alliances holding the empire together fray, then snap

**Key fact for developers:** The Age of Harmony is the world's golden era — and the tragedy is that it fell from within. The Long Decline is not a separate age; it is the final act of the Age of Harmony, the period when the greatest civilization in history quietly destroyed itself. This is the age the world still half-remembers as a golden era. Writers should understand: the Long Decline is the most emotionally important period in the Chorus's history. They watched it happen and could not stop it.

---

## Age III — Age of Sundering
*The greatest catastrophe in recorded history. The world is permanently altered.*

- The Eighth's Corruption spreads beyond recovery
- Reality itself fractures in regions most exposed to her research
- Civilizations collapse under the cascade of the empire's failure
- The Divine Chorus, having exhausted every alternative, imprisons the Forgotten Eighth
- The Sundering is not quiet — the confrontation permanently reshapes the continent; mountain ranges shift, coastlines change
- Magic is permanently altered: the unified Primordial Energy fractures into seven elemental aspects; this is why modern mages work with elements rather than raw Primordial Energy
- The **Great Forgetting** follows: the systematic erasure of the Eighth's existence from mortal memory — a second, separate decision made in the aftermath of the imprisonment
- Much of history is deliberately obscured or lost during the aftermath

**Key fact for developers:** The Sundering is the event everything before it was building toward and everything after it is a consequence of. "The Sundering" is the canonical name. "The Imprisonment" is acceptable as a descriptive phrase in scholarly writing. The Great Forgetting is a separate decision from the Sundering itself — the Chorus chose to erase their sister, and the moral weight of that choice is unresolved. The prison built with grief rather than hatred.

---

## Age IV — Age of Restoration
*Modern civilization rebuilds. History appears understood. It is not.*

- New kingdoms emerge — the empire of the Age of Harmony does not return; smaller kingdoms organized around the Sundering's altered geography take its place
- Modern cultures and institutions develop
- The Imperial Capital becomes the intellectual heart of civilization
- The Great Library survives and is rebuilt; portions of its collection remain sealed or classified
- Trade, religion, scholarship, and political structures are reestablished
- The seven-member theology of the Divine Chorus becomes unquestioned truth — the Great Forgetting, accumulated across centuries, has erased all mainstream knowledge of the Eighth
- Records reconstructed from the Sundering's aftermath are filtered through the interests of those doing the reconstructing
- Magic stabilizes into its current elemental form
- **The game begins near the end of this age**

**Key fact for developers:** This is the period that feels like "normal history" to most people in the world. Most of what players encounter as established civilization was built in this age. The Corruption associated with the Eighth is re-emerging — subtle at first, then less so. The prison that holds the Eighth is weakening. The gaps in the historical record are being noticed by a small number of scholars. Players enter the story here. What they do, and what it costs, will determine how the Age of Restoration is remembered.

## Clan Ironhall — Historical Anchors
Cross-referenced entries anchoring **[Clan Ironhall](../world/factions/clan_ironhall.md)**
(a dwarven craft-honour clan of the Iron Peaks) within the world chronology. These
**connect existing canon rather than inventing new world history**; exact eras/dates
use `_TBD_` where not yet canon.

### Founding — Baelor Ironhall *(deep past — era `_TBD_`)*
- **Baelor Ironhall** — *The First Builder / The Stonewright / The First Master* —
  **founds Clan Ironhall** and establishes the Ironhall philosophy,
  *"Truth is the first measurement."*
- He **forges the Founder's Compass** (the clan's living historical record), builds
  the **earliest Ironhall Great Forge**, and **begins the lineage of Clan Masters.**
- **Chronological placement:** the deep past of dwarven craft — plausibly spanning
  the Age of Awakening's *first cities and organized communities* into the great
  building era of the Age of Harmony. **Exact age/date: `_TBD_`** (dwarven clan
  founding predates most surviving human records). Full canon:
  [`clan_ironhall.md`](../world/factions/clan_ironhall.md#baelor-ironhall--the-first-builder).

### The Broken Oath — Durgan Ironhall *(Age of Restoration; date `_TBD_`)*
- **Durgan Ironhall**, **Lord Warden of the Vault**, swore an unbreakable oath to
  guard a relic. During a **catastrophic disaster** he chose to **save the
  kingdom's people over protecting the relic.**
- He **accepted public disgrace rather than reveal the High King's sealed command**
  that had released him from the oath (the King died before it could be made
  public). **Clan Ironhall fell from honour**; the disgrace was carved into the
  **Hall of Names.**
- **Chronological placement:** the era of modern kingdoms and a **High King** — the
  **Age of Restoration**, **generations before** the campaign present. Exact date:
  `_TBD_`. This is a **separate catastrophe from the Sundering** and must not be
  conflated with it. Full canon:
  [`clan_ironhall.md`](../world/factions/clan_ironhall.md#historical-truth--durgan-and-the-broken-oath)
  · quest: [`the_empty_pedestal.md`](../quests/the_empty_pedestal.md).

### Restoration of Honour — Torren Ironhall *(near the end of the Age of Restoration — campaign present)*
- **Torren Ironhall uncovers the historical truth**; the **High King's sealed
  letter is revealed.**
- **High Thane Borik Stoneheart restores Clan Ironhall's honour**; the disgrace is
  **formally removed from the Hall of Names.**
- Torren becomes the **first Ironhall Master in generations to use the Ironhall
  Master's Seal**, and engraves his name and lesson in the Founder's Compass —
  beginning the next generation of the clan.
- **Chronological placement:** the campaign present (**near the end of the Age of
  Restoration**), during/after the companion quest
  [`The Empty Pedestal`](../quests/the_empty_pedestal.md).
