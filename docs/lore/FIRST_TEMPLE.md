# The First Temple

The First Temple is the oldest surviving temple dedicated to the Divine Chorus.

It stands in the Imperial Capital's Ecclesiastical District, at the center of the city's religious quarter. Every major religious tradition in the world acknowledges the First Temple as significant, even those whose theology diverges sharply from Chorus worship. It predates them all.

---

## History

The First Temple was built during the **Age of Discovery** — the era when mortal civilizations first developed writing, organized religion, and formal institutions. It is, as far as any record confirms, the earliest deliberate act of worship toward the Chorus.

Who built it is disputed. The civilization that constructed it did not survive to the present age in any recognizable form. What they knew about the Chorus — and why they built here, in this location, rather than anywhere else — has not been recovered.

The building standing today is not the original structure. It has been rebuilt, expanded, damaged, restored, and consecrated again across multiple ages of history. What has not changed is its location, its orientation, and what lies beneath it.

---

## The Structure Beneath

Below the main temple complex, accessible through a sealed passage in the lowest level of the foundation crypts, lies a structure that predates the temple above it.

It predates the Age of Discovery.

It may predate mortal civilization entirely.

The architecture does not match any known building tradition. The materials have not been identified — they are not stone, not metal, not any substance catalogued in the Great Library's materials archive. The geometry of the interior spaces follows principles that scholars of architecture and mathematics describe as *suggesting* a different understanding of physical space rather than *demonstrating* one.

The Chorus has studied this structure. They have not explained it.

---

## What the Chorus Has Said

Almost nothing.

Individual Chorus scholars — mortals who have devoted their lives to interpreting the Chorus's communications through their traditions, texts, and observable behavior — note that the Chorus does not avoid the question of the structure beneath the First Temple. They are silent about it. These are not the same thing. Avoidance implies knowledge being withheld. Silence, in this context, implies something closer to the Chorus being at the edge of what they understand.

The most senior Veiled Archivists, who have had the longest access to the Restricted and Sealed collections of the Great Library, report this consistently: the Chorus's silence on the sub-temple structure is different in quality from their silence on other questions they decline to answer.

---

## Access

The sealed passage is permanently closed.

It has been sealed by ecclesiastical authority, by the Mages Guild, and — according to records in the Great Library's Restricted Collection — by the Chorus themselves, though the mechanism and date of that sealing are not publicly documented.

No living person has been inside the structure beneath the First Temple in recorded history. If anyone entered it before the sealing, those records do not exist in any accessible archive.

---

## Design Principle

**The mystery beneath the First Temple is never resolved.**

Players may learn it exists. They may learn the Chorus cannot explain it. They may encounter fragments of evidence — references in old texts, notes from scholars who got close, architectural echoes in other Architect-adjacent locations around the world.

The passage remains sealed. What lies below is never fully described. No quest grants access to the interior. No revelation explains what the structure is or what it means.

This is not a locked door with a key somewhere in the world. This is a boundary that the game does not cross.
