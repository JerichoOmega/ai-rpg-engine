## The Core Principles

These principles govern all First Empire content. They are not optional narrative flavoring — they are canonical constraints.

**The Empire was not a utopia.**  
It experienced political disagreement, economic challenges, cultural differences, regional rivalries, and legislative debates. Its greatness came from its ability to unite many peoples under a common purpose rather than erase their differences. Writers must portray this complexity; a flawless First Empire is a historically inaccurate one.

**No single race was the founder or sole architect.**  
The First Empire was built together by Humans, Elves, Dwarves, Orcs, Halflings, and Gnomes — each contributing their own strengths. Any content that frames one race as the originating force behind the Empire is incorrect.

**Its collapse was not a political failure.**  
The First Council's institutions remained fundamentally sound at the end. The Empire fell because the natural laws of reality themselves were unraveling during the Long Decline. Writers must not portray the collapse as the result of political corruption, internal faction conflict, or governance failure.

**Its defining legacy was preservation.**  
When the Council understood that collapse was unavoidable, it ordered the greatest preservation effort in recorded history. This choice — to save what could be saved rather than fight what could not be stopped — is the moral core of the First Empire's legacy. It is the philosophical origin of the Order of Archivists and the Great Library's founding mission.

## How These Documents Relate to the Campaign

Players should not arrive in the Age of Restoration knowing any of this. The First Empire's sophistication, the First Council's ideals, and the tragedy of its final years are discoveries — uncovered gradually through ruins, fragments, scholarly research, and the pieces of the world that survived.

The sequence of discovery:
1. Physical evidence — ruins and artifacts whose craftsmanship and scale imply a civilization beyond anything modern kingdoms have achieved
2. Textual evidence — fragments, copies, and secondhand references that begin to suggest what that civilization was
3. The Council's philosophy — glimpses of how it governed, what it valued, and how it faced the end
4. The preservation decision — the moment players understand that what they have been using throughout the campaign is a deliberate inheritance

This sequence should develop naturally through gameplay. These documents exist so that writers can build evidence of it consistently, not so that exposition can deliver it all at once.
