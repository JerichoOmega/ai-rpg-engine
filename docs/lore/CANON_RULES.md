# Canon Rules

These are the immutable design principles of the game. They cannot be overridden by quest content, dialogue, NPC dialogue, codex entries, systems design, or any other content unless a deliberate, documented revision is made to this file.

When in doubt about whether a design choice is allowed, check this document first.

---

## World Philosophy

**There is no chosen one.**
No character is selected by fate, prophecy, or divine will to save the world. Heroes emerge through accumulated choices and consequences — not because they were destined to.

**There are no chosen races.**
No race is inherently superior, divinely favored, or cosmically significant. Every civilization rises or falls through its own decisions, not through any quality of birth.

**There are no divine bloodlines.**
Power, nobility, and leadership are not inherited through divine ancestry. A character cannot be special because of who their parents were in relation to the gods.

**Heroes are created through their choices.**
A character becomes significant through what they decide to do, what they sacrifice, and what they refuse to compromise — not through who they are at the start.

**History belongs to mortals.**
The gods do not write history. Mortals do. The Chorus stewards the forces that make mortal life possible; what mortals do with that life is entirely their own.

**Every civilization rises or falls through its own decisions.**
The Chorus does not guide empires, protect kingdoms, or intervene in political outcomes. The consequences of mortal choices — good and catastrophic — belong entirely to mortals.

## The Divine Chorus

The Divine Chorus preserves existence — not history.

**They do not:**
- Choose rulers or dynasties
- Manipulate wars or political outcomes
- Select individuals for divine purposes
- Issue prophecies naming heroes
- Intervene in mortal governance of any kind
- Communicate personal divine instructions to individuals

**They do:**
- Preserve Primordial Energy and cosmic balance
- Steward the Final Verse (the passage of souls beyond mortal existence)
- Maintain the stability of reality itself

**They intervene directly only when reality itself is threatened.**
The Forgotten Eighth is the single defining historical example. It took centuries of the Chorus attempting every alternative before they acted — and the cost of their intervention (the Great Forgetting, the Sundering) shaped the world for a thousand years afterward.

**Free will is sacred to the Chorus.** They believe that removing mortal agency — even in service of better outcomes — would ultimately destroy what civilization is. Mortals must own both their triumphs and their failures.

> **For all content creators:** No NPC, quest, or codex entry may credibly claim that the Chorus sent a vision with specific instructions, chose a character for a purpose, or intervened in a political outcome. If such a claim appears in content, it is either the character's mistaken belief, deliberate manipulation by another party, or a Corruption-adjacent phenomenon — never literal truth.

## The Architects

The Architects predate the Divine Chorus. Even the Chorus does not fully understand them.

**They never appear.**
**They never speak.**
**They never become enemies.**
**Their origin is never confirmed.**
**Their existence is intentionally mysterious.**

Evidence of the Architects exists in the world as archaeological curiosities — structures that do not match any known civilization, materials that cannot be explained by known craft, geometries that imply an understanding of existence that predates mortal thought.

The greatest concentration of this evidence lies beneath the First Temple.

**Developers must never over-explain the Architects.** Their mystery is not a puzzle to be solved — it is a permanent feature of the world. Any content that moves toward a definitive explanation of who or what the Architects were violates this principle.

## The Forgotten Eighth

The Eighth is not to be named. She is referred to as the Forgotten Eighth, the Absent One, or the goddess of a specific domain only in contexts where that domain is relevant.

Her story is not a villain's origin story. It is a tragedy about love, grief, and the slow corruption of a being who genuinely believed she was right.

**The Five Stages of her fall are canon:**
1. Love
2. Grief
3. Obsession
4. Justification *(the true root — "It had to be done" is when corruption takes hold)*
5. Hatred *(she believes the Chorus betrayed her first: "They chose law over love")*

**Stage IV is the most important design principle of her arc:** Corruption did not overwrite her from outside. It grew from the compromises she chose and then justified. This is what makes her sympathetic and what makes her dangerous.
