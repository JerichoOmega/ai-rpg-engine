# The Architects

The Architects predate the Divine Chorus.

That sentence contains nearly everything that can be said about them with certainty.

---

## What Is Known

Something built the world before the Chorus existed to steward it. Evidence of that something persists in the form of structures, materials, and geometries scattered across the world — none of which match any known civilization, and none of which the Chorus can fully explain.

This evidence is sparse and indirect. No Architect has ever been seen. No Architect has ever spoken. No text attributed to the Architects exists — only texts written by later civilizations attempting to interpret what they found. Those interpretations are frequently wrong, occasionally inspired, and never definitive.

The greatest concentration of Architect evidence lies beneath the **First Temple** in the Imperial Capital. The structure beneath the Temple predates the Age of Discovery — which is to say, it predates written records, organized religion, and possibly mortal civilization itself. The Chorus has studied it for as long as the Temple has stood above it. They have not resolved what it is.

---

## What the Architects Are Not

They are not gods.
They are not enemies.
They are not sleeping and waiting to return.
They are not the creators of mortal life.
They are not the source of the Corruption.

None of these claims appear in any canonical document. Any content that assigns one of these identities to the Architects — definitively, as fact — violates the design principle below.

---

## The Central Design Principle

**The Architects must never be over-explained.**

Their mystery is not a puzzle waiting for a late-game revelation. It is a permanent feature of the world.

The world is older than the Chorus. Something was here before the gods. That something left traces that even the gods cannot interpret. This is the complete statement. It does not resolve into an answer.

Content that approaches a definitive explanation of who the Architects were, what they wanted, or what their structures mean is stepping outside canonical bounds. The correct approach is to deepen the mystery — more evidence, more unanswered questions, more evidence that the questions are older than anyone asking them — never to resolve it.

---

## How to Use the Architects in Content

**Archaeological evidence only.** Players may find structures, carvings, materials, or phenomena that clearly predate any known civilization. These discoveries should raise questions, not provide answers.

**Scholar interpretations, not facts.** Historians, archivists, and academics may have theories. Those theories should be interesting, contradictory, and unverifiable. No NPC speaks with authority about the Architects.

**The First Temple is the epicenter.** If content involves the Architects directly, it should connect to the sealed lower structure beneath the First Temple. This is where the evidence is densest and most unsettling — and access is sealed.

**The Chorus's silence is itself significant.** When players learn that even the gods cannot explain what is beneath the First Temple, that discovery should land with weight. Use it sparingly.

---

## What Developers Must Never Do

- Give the Architects a name for themselves
- Confirm a purpose or goal they had
- Make them active in any era of history
- Make them antagonists, allies, or neutrals the player can interact with
- Resolve the mystery beneath the First Temple
- Have a member of the Chorus explain the Architects definitively
- Have any NPC explain the Architects definitively

The Architects are the world's oldest unanswered question. That is their role. That is their value. The moment they are explained, that value is gone.
