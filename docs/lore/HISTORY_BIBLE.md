## Core Principles

These are immutable rules governing how history works in this world. All content must respect them.

- **History belongs to mortals.** The Divine Chorus preserves existence, not history. They do not author civilizations or determine their outcomes.
- **There are no chosen races.** No people is divinely selected for greatness, destiny, or favor above others.
- **There is no chosen one.** No individual is prophesied or destined to save the world. Heroes become heroes through circumstance, sacrifice, and decisions — not destiny.
- **There are no divine bloodlines.** Mortal lineage carries no inherited divine mandate. Power derived from ancestry is political, not cosmic.
- **Kingdoms rise and fall because of mortal choices.** The Chorus does not build empires or destroy them. Civilizations are entirely the work and responsibility of the peoples who created them.
- **The Chorus only intervenes when the stability of existence itself is threatened.** The Forgotten Eighth's fall is the defining historical example of such intervention — the only event in recorded history that warranted direct Chorus action.

> These principles are not widely known in-world. They are design constraints. No piece of content should feature prophecy, chosen heroes, divine bloodlines, or Chorus intervention in mortal politics without explicitly violating canon.

## How History Works in Elyndor

History in this world is **incomplete, contested, and deliberately buried in places**.

Most people alive today know a simplified version of what happened. Scholars know more, but not all scholars agree, and the oldest records are fragmentary. The forces that shaped the world most profoundly — the imprisonment of the Eighth, the Great Forgetting — are not in any mainstream history text. They have been erased too thoroughly for ordinary scholarship to recover.

> **For writers and the AI DM:** History should always feel like it has more to it than any character knows. Ruins exist for reasons. Artifacts carry weight. The player who learns history will understand the world; the player who does not will still encounter it — they just will not know what they are looking at.

The further back one looks, the less reliable the sources become. The Age of Awakening is known only through archaeology, myth, and geological record. The Age of Unity is recorded but filtered through the politics of those who wrote it. The Long Decline was documented by people who were surviving it and were not always honest about what they saw. The Present Age is still being written.

## The Seven Ages — Overview

| Age | Theme | What Defined It |
|---|---|---|
| **Age of Awakening** | Beginning | The Chorus awakens; the first intelligent races emerge; no religion yet |
| **Age of Discovery** | Foundation | Writing, cities, temples, the first schools of magic |
| **Age of Unity** | Civilization | The great empire; the Great Library founded; roads, trade, knowledge — and the meeting that changed everything |
| **The Long Decline** | Unraveling | The Corruption spreads slowly; the Chorus spends centuries attempting to save the Eighth |
| **The Sundering** | Fracture | The Eighth imprisoned; continents scarred; magic permanently altered |

| **Present Age** | Choice | The beginning of the game; history appears understood but is actually incomplete |

## The Age of Awakening

> *"Before memory. Before language. Before gods were named."*

This is the world's earliest period — predating recorded history entirely. What is known comes from archaeology, geological evidence, and the oldest surviving oral traditions, most of which have passed through so many retellings that separating fact from myth requires scholarly work that is ongoing and contested.

### What Happened

- **The First Song** — the earliest surviving mythological account of the world's origin. The Chorus is not yet named in these records. They are described only as presences, forces, the shape of the world before mortal eyes existed to see it.
- **The Divine Chorus awakens** — or, more precisely, the Chorus begins to be understood as a coherent group of distinct stewards. Earlier accounts describe elemental forces without personality or relationship; the Age of Awakening marks the period when those forces became, to emerging mortal minds, recognizably *someone*.
- **The first intelligent races emerge** — the oldest peoples take their initial form. Their earliest existence is not documented; it is inferred.
- **No organized religion** — mortals are aware of the divine forces around them, but no formal tradition, temple, or theology exists yet. The relationship between mortal and divine is immediate, unmediated, and not yet institutionalized.

## The Age of Discovery

> *"The first mark on stone. The first word written down."*

Civilization begins.

### What Happened

- **Writing** — the first systems for recording language appear. The written record begins here, fragmented and incomplete as it is.
- **First cities** — permanent settlements of scale, organized enough to be called cities rather than camps or villages. The architectural signatures of this age are recognizable in certain First Age ruins.
- **First temples** — mortal relationship with the Divine Chorus becomes formalized. Shrines become structures. Individual devotion becomes communal practice.
- **First schools of magic** — Primordial Magic has always been accessible to those with natural affinity, but the Age of Discovery sees the first systematic attempts to understand, transmit, and teach magical practice. The foundations of what will eventually become the Mages Guild begin here.

## The Age of Unity

> *"When the world believed it had found what it had been building toward."*

The Age of Unity is the high-water mark of pre-Sundering civilization — a period of genuine prosperity, cross-regional cooperation, and intellectual achievement that has not been equaled since.

### What Happened

- **The first great empire** — the continent's disparate kingdoms and peoples unified under a single political structure for the first and, so far, only time in recorded history. The empire was not built through conquest alone; it was built through the accumulation of genuine advantages that made membership more valuable than independence.
- **The Great Library founded** — civilization recognized that its institutional memory required physical infrastructure. The Great Library was established as the continent's central repository of knowledge: a place where what had been discovered could be preserved against the next catastrophe. See [`docs/lore/GREAT_LIBRARY.md`](GREAT_LIBRARY.md).
- **Universities established** — formal institutions of general scholarship, distinct from the magical schools, emerge during this period. The tradition of systematic secular learning takes root.
- **Roads and trade** — the empire's infrastructure connected regions in ways they had never been connected before. The trade routes that still exist in the Fourth Age trace back to Age of Unity construction.
- **Scientific advancement** — the combination of literacy, institutional memory, trade-connected knowledge exchange, and peace produced an era of discovery across multiple fields simultaneously.

### The Meeting That Changed Everything

During the Age of Unity, the Forgotten Eighth — the eighth member of the Divine Chorus — spent extended time among mortals. She met one particular ordinary person. That relationship is the hinge on which all of subsequent history turns.

The mortal was not remarkable. Not a ruler, not a mage, not a figure of historical significance. They possessed no power except who they were: their kindness, their humor, their capacity for appreciating ordinary existence. They lived an ordinary life and died an ordinary death.

The Eighth could not accept it.

Full account: [`elyndor/history/the_eighth.md`](../../elyndor/history/the_eighth.md)

## The Long Decline

> *"Nobody noticed the world ending. That is how it always works."*

The Long Decline is not a single catastrophe. It is the slow accumulation of consequences from a choice that could not be undone.

### What Happened

- **The Corruption spreads slowly** — as the Eighth's search beyond Creation exposed her to forces outside the world's design, traces of those forces began bleeding back into Elyndor. The early Corruption was subtle: elevated aggression in wildlife, unusual magical instability, communities that were merely fearful becoming something worse. No one connected these symptoms to a common cause.
- **Reality begins breaking** — in places where the Eighth's research had reached furthest, the fabric of the world showed strain. Ancient First Age sites report phenomena that surviving records describe only metaphorically, because the scholars writing those records had no framework for what they were observing.
- **Civilizations collapse** — the Age of Unity's empire did not survive the Long Decline intact. The interconnected systems that made the empire prosperous also made it vulnerable to cascade failure. As the Corruption amplified existing tensions — political, economic, cultural — the alliances holding the empire together frayed, then snapped.
- **The Chorus spends centuries attempting to save the Eighth** — this is the detail most absent from surviving historical records, and the most important. The Chorus did not immediately imprison their sister. They tried to reach her. They tried to help her. The Long Decline is, in part, the duration of that attempt — centuries of the Chorus searching for another way while the world deteriorated beneath them.

## The Sundering

> *"The hardest decision ever made. The one they have never stopped mourning."*

**The Sundering** is the canonical name for the event that ended the Long Decline and permanently altered the world.

### What Happened

- **The Eighth imprisoned** — the Divine Chorus, having exhausted every alternative they could find, imprisoned the Forgotten Eighth. The prison was built with grief rather than hatred. They have not stopped loving their sister since.
- **Continents permanently scarred** — the Sundering was not quiet. The confrontation between the full strength of the Divine Chorus and the Eighth — who had, by this point, carried forces from beyond Creation back into the world — reshaped geography. Mountain ranges shifted. Coastlines changed. Places where the confrontation was most intense still bear marks that no natural process accounts for.
- **Magic permanently altered** — before the Sundering, Primordial Magic flowed as a unified force. The imprisonment fractured it into seven distinct elemental aspects. This is why modern mages work with elements rather than raw Primordial Energy: they are working within the architecture of the aftermath.
- **History forever changed** — in the Sundering's wake, the Chorus made a second decision: they allowed the Eighth's existence to be erased from history. Whether this was mercy, self-protection, the deepest grief, or something that cannot be characterized in mortal terms — is one of the campaign's unresolved questions.

The Sundering is the event that everything before it was building toward and everything after it is a consequence of.

## The Age of Restoration

> *"Rebuilding on ground that no one fully understood had shifted."*

After the Sundering, the world did what it always does: it rebuilt.

### What Happened

- **Modern kingdoms rise** — the current political map of the continent traces to the Age of Restoration. The empire of the Age of Unity did not return. What replaced it was a collection of smaller kingdoms, shaped by the geography the Sundering had permanently altered, organized around the resources and defensible positions that the new landscape provided.
- **Records reconstructed** — scholars and archivists worked to recover what the Long Decline and the Sundering had destroyed. Most of it could not be recovered. What was reconstructed was filtered through the perspectives of the people doing the reconstructing, who had their own political and theological interests.
- **Knowledge partially lost** — entire fields of study that had flourished during the Age of Unity no longer had practitioners. Languages died. Magical traditions that predated the elemental fracturing were either adapted to the new framework or disappeared. The Great Library of the Age of Unity had been badly damaged; what survived became the foundation of the reconstructed institution.
- **People genuinely believe there have always been seven Divine Chorus members** — the Great Forgetting was not a single event. It was the gradual accumulation of erasure across the Age of Restoration and beyond: records lost, records sealed, traditions that no longer quite fit a seven-member theology quietly revised. By the end of the Age of Restoration, the knowledge that the Chorus had ever had eight members existed only in the sealed collections of institutions that did not discuss their sealed collections.

## The Present Age

> *"History appears understood. It is not."*

This is where the player begins.

The Age of Restoration's rebuilding eventually stabilized. Trade is established. Kingdoms have borders. The major institutions — the Mages Guild, the religious orders, the universities, the Great Library — are functioning. Most people believe the world is recovering from a difficult past and moving toward something better.

They are working from an incomplete picture.

The Corruption has been spreading since the Long Decline, and it is accelerating now that the prison weakens past the point where the acceleration is visible — if you know where to look. Ancient ruins are becoming active. The world is changing in ways that do not fit the explanations currently available.

The player is not chosen. They are not the subject of prophecy. They are someone who arrived at this moment in history — with the companions, the capability, and the circumstance to do something about it.

**What they do, and what it costs, will determine how history remembers the Present Age.**

## What History Means for Writers

Every ruin was built by someone. Every abandoned temple had worshippers. Every crumbling fortress was once considered permanent.

The most useful question a writer can ask about any location, artifact, or institution: *what was this during the Age of Unity, and what happened to it during the Long Decline or the Sundering?*

The answer will almost always suggest something worth using.

**What must remain unanswered:** The Eighth's true name, her elemental stewardship, the identity of the mortal she loved — these are campaign revelations, not background details. They should feel absent from the world's knowledge because they are absent. Scholars who have noticed the gaps treat them as archival failures, not as mysteries with knowable answers.

**No prophecy. No chosen ones. No divine bloodlines.** Any content that features these elements is in direct conflict with the Core Principles at the top of this document.
