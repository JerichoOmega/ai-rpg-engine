# History Bible — Lore Reference

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]  
> **Authority:** This is the canonical source of truth for the world's historical framework. All future story, dialogue, quests, worldbuilding, and gameplay systems must remain consistent with this document.  
> **Canonical framework:** Four Ages — Age of Awakening, Age of Harmony, Age of Sundering, Age of Restoration. The Long Decline is the tragic final chapter of the Age of Harmony, not a separate age. The game begins near the end of the Age of Restoration.

## Core Principles

These are immutable rules governing how history works in this world. All content must respect them.

- **History belongs to mortals.** The Divine Chorus preserves existence, not history. They do not author civilizations or determine their outcomes.
- **There are no chosen races.** No people is divinely selected for greatness, destiny, or favor above others.
- **There is no chosen one.** No individual is prophesied or destined to save the world. Heroes become heroes through circumstance, sacrifice, and decisions — not destiny.
- **There are no divine bloodlines.** Mortal lineage carries no inherited divine mandate. Power derived from ancestry is political, not cosmic.
- **Kingdoms rise and fall because of mortal choices.** The Chorus does not build empires or destroy them. Civilizations are entirely the work and responsibility of the peoples who created them.
- **The Chorus only intervenes when the stability of existence itself is threatened.** The Forgotten Eighth's fall is the defining historical example of such intervention — the only event in recorded history that warranted direct Chorus action.

> These principles are not widely known in-world. They are design constraints. No piece of content should feature prophecy, chosen heroes, divine bloodlines, or Chorus intervention in mortal politics without explicitly violating canon.

## How History Works in Elyndor

History in this world is **incomplete, contested, and deliberately buried in places**.

Most people alive today know a simplified version of what happened. Scholars know more, but not all scholars agree, and the oldest records are fragmentary. The forces that shaped the world most profoundly — the imprisonment of the Eighth, the Great Forgetting — are not in any mainstream history text. They have been erased too thoroughly for ordinary scholarship to recover.

> **For writers and the AI DM:** History should always feel like it has more to it than any character knows. Ruins exist for reasons. Artifacts carry weight. The player who learns history will understand the world; the player who does not will still encounter it — they just will not know what they are looking at.

The further back one looks, the less reliable the sources become. The Age of Awakening is known only through archaeology, myth, and geological record. The Age of Harmony is recorded but filtered through the politics of those who wrote it. The Long Decline — the final chapter of the Age of Harmony — was documented by people who were surviving it and were not always honest about what they saw. The Age of Restoration is still being written.

## The Four Ages — Overview

| Age | Theme | What Defined It |
|---|---|---|
| **Age of Awakening** | Beginning | The Chorus awakens; the first intelligent races emerge; writing, cities, and temples develop; foundations of magic and civilization are laid |
| **Age of Harmony** | Flourishing | The First Empire and the Great Library; the greatest civilization; the meeting that changed everything; *(Long Decline: the tragic final chapter)* |
| **Age of Sundering** | Collapse | The Eighth imprisoned; continents scarred; magic permanently altered; history deliberately obscured |
| **Age of Restoration** | Recovery | Modern kingdoms rise; records reconstructed; the Great Forgetting takes hold; the game begins near the end of this age |

> **The Long Decline** is not an age of its own. It is the final chapter of the Age of Harmony — the period when the greatest civilization in history quietly destroyed itself from within. Content that references "the Long Decline" as a historical period is accurate; content that lists it as a fifth age is not.

## Age I — The Age of Awakening

> *"Before memory. Before language. Before gods were named — and then, slowly, the world began to fill."*

This age spans the world's earliest period through the rise of its first organized civilizations. What is known comes from archaeology, geological evidence, and the oldest surviving oral traditions, most of which have passed through so many retellings that separating fact from myth requires scholarly work that is ongoing and contested.

### What Happened

- **The First Song** — the earliest surviving mythological account of the world's origin. The Chorus is not yet named in these records. They are described only as presences, forces, the shape of the world before mortal eyes existed to see it.
- **The Divine Chorus awakens** — the eight stewards come into being; Primordial Energy begins stabilizing; physical reality takes its current form.
- **The first intelligent races emerge** — the oldest peoples take their initial form. Their earliest existence is not documented; it is inferred from geological and archaeological evidence.
- **Primordial Energy stabilizes** — what will eventually be understood as magic settles into a form mortals can begin to interact with.
- **First writing** — the first systems for recording language appear. The written record begins here, fragmented and incomplete as it is.
- **First cities** — permanent settlements of scale, organized enough to be called cities rather than camps or villages.
- **The First Temple is established** — the oldest surviving temple to the Divine Chorus; mortal relationship with the Chorus becomes formalized. The structure beneath the temple already exists when it is constructed above it. See [`docs/lore/FIRST_TEMPLE.md`](FIRST_TEMPLE.md).
- **First schools of magic** — Primordial Magic has always been accessible to those with natural affinity, but this age sees the first systematic attempts to understand, transmit, and teach magical practice.

**Key fact for developers:** This age is long and its early periods are pre-history. No great empire, no Great Library, no continent-spanning politics. The Chorus stewards existence; mortals begin the long process of learning what existence is. By the age's end, the foundation of everything that follows has been laid.

## Age II — The Age of Harmony

> *"When the world believed it had found what it had been building toward."*

The Age of Harmony is the high-water mark of mortal civilization — a genuine golden era of prosperity, cross-regional cooperation, and intellectual achievement that has not been equaled since. It is also the age that contains the seed of everything that destroyed it.

### The Golden Era

- **The First Empire rises** — the continent's disparate kingdoms and peoples unified under a single political structure for the first and only time in recorded history. The empire was built through the accumulation of genuine advantages that made membership more valuable than independence.
- **The Great Library founded** — civilization recognized that its institutional memory required physical infrastructure. The Great Library was established as the continent's central repository of knowledge: a place where what had been discovered could be preserved against the next catastrophe. See [`docs/lore/GREAT_LIBRARY.md`](GREAT_LIBRARY.md).
- **Universities established** — formal institutions of general scholarship, distinct from the magical schools, emerge during this period. The tradition of systematic secular learning takes root.
- **Trade routes connect civilizations** — the empire's infrastructure connected regions in ways they had never been connected before. The trade routes that still exist today trace back to Age of Harmony construction.
- **Magic reaches its greatest advancement** — the combination of literacy, institutional memory, and peace produced an era of discovery that modern mages study from fragments.

### The Meeting That Changed Everything

During the Age of Harmony, the Forgotten Eighth — the eighth member of the Divine Chorus — spent extended time among mortals. She met one particular ordinary person. That relationship is the hinge on which all of subsequent history turns.

The mortal was not remarkable. Not a ruler, not a mage, not a figure of historical significance. They possessed no power except who they were: their kindness, their humor, their capacity for appreciating ordinary existence. They lived an ordinary life and died an ordinary death.

The Eighth could not accept it.

Full account: [`elyndor/history/the_eighth.md`](../../elyndor/history/the_eighth.md)

### The Long Decline — Final Chapter of the Age of Harmony

> *"Nobody noticed the world ending. That is how it always works."*

The Long Decline is not a separate age. It is the tragic final chapter of the Age of Harmony — the slow accumulation of consequences from a choice that could not be undone.

- **The Eighth's Corruption spreads slowly** — as her search beyond Creation exposed her to forces outside the world's design, traces of those forces began bleeding back into Elyndor. The early Corruption was subtle: elevated aggression in wildlife, unusual magical instability, communities that were merely fearful becoming something worse.
- **The Chorus spends centuries attempting to save the Eighth** — this is the detail most absent from surviving historical records, and the most important. The Chorus did not immediately imprison their sister. They tried to reach her. They tried to help her. The Long Decline is, in part, the duration of that attempt.
- **The mortal sings the Final Verse** — the specific moment that closes the Age of Harmony's golden era. The circumstances are part of what the campaign may reveal.
- **Civilizations collapse** — the empire of the Age of Harmony did not survive its final centuries intact. The interconnected systems that made it prosperous made it vulnerable to cascade failure. As the Corruption amplified existing tensions, the alliances holding the empire together frayed, then snapped.

**Key fact for developers:** The Long Decline is the most emotionally important period in the Chorus's history. They watched it happen and could not stop it. This is the period writers should understand most deeply — because everything the Corruption does in the campaign echoes what it did here, and every Chorus member still carries the memory of those centuries.

## Age III — The Age of Sundering

> *"The hardest decision ever made. The one they have never stopped mourning."*

**The Sundering** is the canonical name for the greatest catastrophe in recorded history. "The Imprisonment" is acceptable as a descriptive phrase in scholarly writing. The Great Forgetting is a separate decision made in the aftermath — distinct from the Sundering itself.

### What Happened

- **The Eighth imprisoned** — the Divine Chorus, having exhausted every alternative they could find, imprisoned the Forgotten Eighth. The prison was built with grief rather than hatred. They have not stopped loving their sister since.
- **Reality fractures** — in places where the Eighth's research had reached furthest, the fabric of the world showed strain that surviving records describe only metaphorically, because the scholars writing those records had no framework for what they were observing.
- **Continents permanently scarred** — the confrontation between the full strength of the Divine Chorus and the Eighth — who had, by this point, carried forces from beyond Creation back into the world — reshaped geography. Mountain ranges shifted. Coastlines changed. Places where the confrontation was most intense still bear marks that no natural process accounts for.
- **Magic permanently altered** — before the Sundering, Primordial Magic flowed as a unified force. The imprisonment fractured it into seven distinct elemental aspects. This is why modern mages work with elements rather than raw Primordial Energy: they are working within the architecture of the aftermath.
- **The Great Forgetting follows** — in the Sundering's wake, the Chorus made a second decision: they allowed the Eighth's existence to be erased from history. Whether this was mercy, self-protection, the deepest grief, or something that cannot be characterized in mortal terms — is one of the campaign's unresolved questions.
- **History deliberately obscured** — much of the historical record from this period was lost, destroyed, or deliberately sealed. What was reconstructed afterward was filtered through the perspectives of those doing the reconstructing.

## Age IV — The Age of Restoration

> *"Rebuilding on ground that no one fully understood had shifted."*

After the Sundering, the world did what it always does: it rebuilt. The Age of Restoration is the modern age — the civilization players encounter is the product of this age's reconstruction.

### What Happened

- **New kingdoms emerge** — the empire of the Age of Harmony did not return. What replaced it was a collection of smaller kingdoms, shaped by the geography the Sundering had permanently altered, organized around the resources and defensible positions that the new landscape provided.
- **Modern cultures develop** — new religious traditions, new trade networks, new political structures. The cultures players encounter in the campaign were formed in this age.
- **The Imperial Capital becomes the intellectual heart of civilization** — the Great Library survives, though portions of its collection are now sealed or classified. The universities are reestablished. The Capital becomes the continent's center of scholarship and governance. See [`docs/lore/IMPERIAL_CAPITAL.md`](IMPERIAL_CAPITAL.md).
- **Records reconstructed** — scholars and archivists worked to recover what the Long Decline and the Sundering had destroyed. Most of it could not be recovered. What was reconstructed was filtered through the perspectives of the people doing the reconstructing, who had their own political and theological interests.
- **Knowledge partially lost** — entire fields of study that had flourished during the Age of Harmony no longer had practitioners. Languages died. Magical traditions that predated the elemental fracturing were either adapted to the new framework or disappeared.
- **Most people genuinely believe there have always been seven members of the Divine Chorus** — the Great Forgetting was not a single event. It was the gradual accumulation of erasure across the Age of Restoration and beyond: records lost, records sealed, traditions that no longer quite fit a seven-member theology quietly revised.

### The Game Begins Near the End of This Age

The Age of Restoration's rebuilding eventually stabilized. Trade is established. Kingdoms have borders. The major institutions — the Mages Guild, the religious orders, the universities, the Great Library — are functioning. Most people believe the world is recovering from a difficult past and moving toward something better.

They are working from an incomplete picture.

The Corruption has been spreading since the Long Decline, and it is accelerating now that the prison weakens past the point where the acceleration is visible — if you know where to look. Ancient ruins are becoming active. The world is changing in ways that do not fit the explanations currently available.

The player is not chosen. They are not the subject of prophecy. They are someone who arrived at this moment in history — with the companions, the capability, and the circumstance to do something about it.

**What they do, and what it costs, will determine how the Age of Restoration is remembered.**

## What History Means for Writers

Every ruin was built by someone. Every abandoned temple had worshippers. Every crumbling fortress was once considered permanent.

The most useful question a writer can ask about any location, artifact, or institution: *what was this during the Age of Harmony, and what happened to it during the Long Decline or the Sundering?*

The answer will almost always suggest something worth using.

**What must remain unanswered:** The Eighth's true name, her elemental stewardship, the identity of the mortal she loved — these are campaign revelations, not background details. They should feel absent from the world's knowledge because they are absent. Scholars who have noticed the gaps treat them as archival failures, not as mysteries with knowable answers.

**No prophecy. No chosen ones. No divine bloodlines.** Any content that features these elements is in direct conflict with the Core Principles at the top of this document.

**On the Long Decline:** Characters in the world who know history may refer to the Long Decline by name — it is a recognized period of historical scholarship, even if its causes are misattributed. What they do not know is that the Long Decline was the Corruption's first full expression in mortal civilization, or that the Chorus's attempts to save the Eighth were the invisible story running beneath the political and military history they study.
