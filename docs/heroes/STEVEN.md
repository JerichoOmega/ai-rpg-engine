## Hero Summary

Steven is a Human Miner who does not speak. He carries a bright blue pickaxe that is too distinctive to be coincidence. He is a rare companion — not every campaign will include him, and some players will finish a full run without ever meeting him. Those who do find him will find someone who solves problems in ways that shouldn't work, notices things that shouldn't be there, and knows things about himself that he has not told anyone.

He is the most mysterious member of the confirmed cast, deliberately. What he is not is hollow. His silence is not absence — it is presence of a very particular kind. The mystery is the character.

---

## Core Identity

> Steven does not fight because of a cause, or a wound, or a philosophy.
> He is here, and things around him get handled.
> Everything about his gameplay should reinforce this: he finds the path, solves the problem, and offers no explanation.

## Core Wound

**His identity and mysterious past define his emotional journey — what he knows about himself that others do not.**

Steven's Core Wound is intentionally not yet fully defined. This is a structural choice: his mystery is the story engine. Defining the wound prematurely would collapse the narrative space his arc requires.

What is confirmed:
- He knows something about himself or his past that no other character knows
- This knowledge shapes how he moves through the world
- The player's journey with Steven is, in part, the process of learning what it is

What it is: ⚠️ **NOT YET DEFINED** — to be established when his personal arc is authored.

## Personality

### Public Personality
- **Silent** — does not speak; communicates through gesture, expression, and action
- **Observant** — misses nothing; watches everything
- **Practical** — solves problems simply; no flourish
- **Unassuming** — easy to overlook; that may be intentional

### Private Personality

⚠️ **NOT YET FULLY DEFINED** — Steven's private face is the campaign's reveal. What he is underneath the silence will emerge through his personal arc.

What can be inferred from confirmed canon:
- He is not indifferent — he chooses to help, which implies investment
- His silence is a condition, not a performance — he is not withholding to be dramatic
- He appears to have made peace with something; whether that peace is genuine or practiced is unknown

### The Contrast

The contrast between Steven's unassuming presence and what he actually knows — or is — is the entire point of him. Players who dismiss him as background texture will be wrong. When the contrast reveals itself, it should reframe everything that came before.

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
Human, unremarkable at first glance. Average build. Nothing about his physical appearance demands attention, which makes him easy to overlook in a group — and easy to forget in a room.

### Equipment
The blue pickaxe is his primary weapon and the most immediately distinctive thing about him. It is bright enough to be remembered. It should not exist, in the sense that a miner carrying a pickaxe that vivid into dangerous situations implies either obliviousness or absolute confidence. Steven does not appear to be oblivious.

### Recognizable Personal Detail
The blue pickaxe. It is his signature item and the visual anchor of his character. It should remain consistently distinctive — not dark, not worn into grey. Bright blue. The color is the detail.

### Silhouette
Smaller and quieter than the rest of the party. In a lineup of the five heroes, Steven's silhouette should be the one that makes a player ask "and who is that?" The answer, eventually, should surprise them.

## Combat Identity

Steven is the party's **hidden variable** — a companion whose combat effectiveness depends on things that are not obvious about him, deployed in situations that other party members cannot handle the same way.

His Master Miner ability defines his non-combat utility: finding passages, identifying structural weaknesses, accessing areas others cannot reach. In combat, his pickaxe is more effective than it should be, in ways that will make more sense when his past does.

> Steven works, consistently and without explanation. The question of *why* he works so well is the character.

## Unique Passive — *(Name not yet defined)*

> *No other companion may possess this passive.*

**Working direction:** Steven's passive should connect to his perception and his hidden knowledge — possibly something that lets him interact with the environment in ways other characters cannot, or a passive that reflects an unusual resilience or awareness tied to his unrevealed past.

**Design rationale:** His Core Wound is about what he knows that others don't. The passive should make that gap mechanically real without revealing what it is. Players should feel that Steven knows something — they should not yet know what.

⚠️ **NOT YET DEFINED** — Exact mechanic and name to be established alongside his arc.

---

## Signature Ability — Master Miner

Steven's defining ability is environmental — his capacity to find, access, and use spaces and structures that other characters walk past.

**What it does:**
- Identifies hidden passages, structural weaknesses, and concealed areas
- Interacts with terrain in ways other companions cannot
- In combat: may interact with the battlefield environment itself (collapsing structures, forcing passage, creating new tactical options)

**Design intent:** Master Miner should feel like Steven knowing something about the space that no one else does. Not magical — practical. He has been somewhere like this before, or he understands something about how things are built that gives him an edge.

### Theme

⚠️ **NOT YET DEFINED** — Steven's personal quest is the revelation of his Core Wound and the question of what he does with what is revealed. The shape of the quest depends on what his past contains.

### Player Influence

⚠️ **NOT YET DEFINED** — The resolutions available to the player will emerge from the authored arc.

- The personal quest is the campaign's primary vehicle for revealing who Steven is
- The revelation should reframe his earlier behavior — things he did or did not do should make more sense afterward
- The tone should feel earned rather than shocking; players should feel they had the pieces and did not assemble them, not that the reveal came from nowhere

### Emotional Core

Steven's arc is about what someone does with a past they carry alone. Whether that means disclosure, acceptance, resolution, or something more complicated is ⚠️ **NOT YET DEFINED**.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Steven does not speak. His "dialogue" is expressed through:
- Gestures
- Expression
- Action (doing something rather than saying something)
- The reactions of others to what he communicates

### Tone
Difficult to read at first. Becomes clearer as the player learns his non-verbal register.

### Vocabulary
N/A in the conventional sense. What Steven communicates through other means should have a consistent logic to it — he is not randomly expressive; his communication has a grammar, even without words.

### Humor
⚠️ NOT YET FULLY DEFINED — Whether and how Steven expresses humor should emerge from his arc and his non-verbal register. Something is there; what exactly it looks like requires more character development.

### Emotional Tendencies
Contained. What he feels surfaces in what he does, not what he says. A companion who has learned to read him can tell when something matters to him.

### What He Does Not Say
Everything. This is not evasion — it is condition. The question his character asks is: what does communication mean when words are not available, and what does it say about the other characters that some of them learn to hear him anyway?

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Steven is the reason the party finds what was never supposed to be found."**

His environmental awareness and Master Miner ability open paths, reveal secrets, and create options that no other companion provides. Campaigns that include him play differently at a structural level.

---

## Rare Companion Note

Steven does not appear in every campaign. His rarity is intentional — it makes encountering him feel like a discovery rather than a given. Campaigns that include him should feel meaningfully different from those that do not, both in terms of what is accessible (Master Miner) and in terms of the questions his presence raises.

The first time a player meets him in one campaign and then does not find him in the next, they should wonder why.
