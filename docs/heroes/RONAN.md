## Hero Summary

Ronan is a cursed drifter who has spent years making himself small. He keeps moving so he cannot be the reason something goes wrong in a place he has come to care about. He fights well — too well, in his opinion, especially in the form he tries not to think about. He is looking for a cure. He does not entirely believe he will find one. He keeps looking anyway.

He is not defined by the curse. He is defined by what he chooses to do with it — the care he takes, the distances he maintains, the people he finds himself caring about despite his best efforts.

---

## Core Identity

> Ronan is not a fighter because of the curse.
> He fights because he wants to protect people — the same people he keeps a careful distance from, because he fears what protecting them at full power might cost.
> Everything about his gameplay should hold this tension: he is most powerful when most dangerous.

---

## Core Wound

**Believes he is a danger to everyone around him because of his curse.**

The werewolf curse is not the wound. The wound is what Ronan decided about himself because of it: that his presence is a liability, that closeness is a risk, that the best thing he can do for anyone he cares about is keep enough distance that when things go wrong, he is the only one who gets hurt.

He is not wrong that the curse is dangerous. He is wrong about what that means — or at least, wrong about whether it means he has to face it alone.

The wound is the isolation he has chosen as the price of not being a danger. Whether the campaign challenges that choice, validates it, or finds a third answer is the heart of his arc.

---

## Core Belief

> "I am the threat in the room. The kindest thing I can do is manage myself."

He does not feel sorry for himself about this. He accepted it a long time ago. He has built his entire way of moving through the world around it: keep moving, keep distance, do not let anyone get close enough that losing control would destroy them.

---

## Greatest Fear

Losing control of the transformation when it matters most — when someone he cares about is nearby and cannot get clear in time.

Not the transformation itself. What he might do in it, to someone he would die to protect.

---

## Driving Goal

Find a cure for the curse.

He does not know if one exists. He does not know where to look definitively. He is following threads. He keeps looking because the alternative is accepting that this is permanent, and he is not ready to do that.

## Personality

### Public Personality
- **Quiet** — default mode is minimal; does not fill space he does not need
- **Watchful** — pays attention to everything; situational awareness is survival
- **Measured** — careful with what he says; says less than he thinks
- **Keeps distance** — not unfriendly, but maintains a margin; does not lean in

### Private Personality
- **Deeply empathetic** — reads people well and feels what they feel more than he lets on
- **Self-deprecating** — not performatively; he genuinely does not rate himself highly
- **Capable of real warmth** — when he lets himself; it is quieter than Eleanor's warmth but just as real
- **Darkly funny** — dry humor about his own situation; uses it to defuse rather than deflect

### The Contrast

Ronan reads as guarded and a little hollow until he opens up, at which point the person underneath is warmer, funnier, and more thoughtful than the exterior suggests. The public face is not a lie — it is a precaution. The private face is what the precaution is protecting.

---

## Moral Compass

Ronan's priorities:
1. **Not harming people he cares about** — this is above everything, including the goal of stopping threats
2. **Protecting innocents** — especially those who cannot protect themselves
3. **His companions** — carefully, at a distance that feels like protection but is also fear
4. **Himself** — lowest priority; he has accepted significant personal cost as a regular condition

He will sacrifice his own safety without hesitation. He will not sacrifice it in ways that risk transforming near people who cannot get clear.

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
Human form: lean and road-worn. Has clearly been traveling a long time. Carries scars from sources he does not fully explain. Moves with the particular economy of someone who is used to being alert and never quite at rest.

### Equipment
Two short swords in human form — practical, fast, paired. In Werewolf Form, the weapons become secondary to what he becomes.

### Recognizable Personal Detail
The scars — specifically one scar whose origin story he has not told anyone. It is visible enough to notice. He does not offer an explanation. ⚠️ The specific origin is **NOT YET DEFINED** — this is an intentional mystery for his personal arc.

### Silhouette
Lean, self-contained, slightly separate from the group even when standing in it. In Werewolf Form: entirely different. The silhouette shift should be dramatic — not the same person with different stats, but a visually distinct entity.

## Combat Identity

Ronan is the party's **controlled escalation** — a fighter who holds back by default and has access to something he would rather not use.

In human form he is capable and careful. In Werewolf Form he is something else: more powerful, harder to control, and carrying risks that extend beyond the mechanics. Playing Ronan well means knowing when to escalate and carrying the consequences of that decision.

> The power is real. So is the cost. That tension is Ronan in combat.

## Unique Passive — *(Name not yet defined)*

> *No other companion may possess this passive.*

**Working direction:** Ronan's passive should express his hyper-vigilance — possibly an ability to sense danger before it fully materializes, granting the party early warning of ambushes or enemy positioning. Alternatively: a passive that strengthens when he is protecting someone specifically, expressing that his careful distance collapses when someone he cares about is in immediate danger.

**Design rationale:** His Core Wound is about managing himself so he is not a danger to others. His passive should show what that vigilance looks like mechanically — and what it costs or enables.

## Signature Ability — Werewolf Form

Ronan's defining ability is transformation — and everything that comes with it.

**What it does:**
- Ronan transforms into his Werewolf Form
- Significantly enhanced combat capability: strength, speed, aggression
- Carries risks and tradeoffs that standard buffs do not (exact mechanics TBD during design)
- Silver weakness applies and may interact with this form

**Design intent:** Werewolf Form should feel like a decision, not just a button. Players who use it should feel the power and feel the tension. The form is not a clean win — it is Ronan at his most effective and his most exposed, simultaneously.

The ability is the wound made mechanical: he becomes what he fears he is, and the player decides whether that is worth it.

## Personal Quest

### Theme

Ronan's personal quest is the search for a cure — and the deeper question of what he will do with himself if the cure cannot be found, or if finding it means accepting things about himself he has been avoiding.

### Player Influence

**Resolution A — The Cure Exists**
A cure is found or begins to be found. The process is not simple — it requires Ronan to confront things he has been running from. But the possibility is real. His arc ends with something like hope, earned through difficulty.

**Resolution B — There Is No Cure**
The quest confirms that the curse cannot be removed. Ronan's arc becomes about what he does with that — whether he can accept himself as he is, find a way to live with the curse that does not require constant distance, and stop running. The resolution is not triumphant. It is honest.

**Resolution C — The Wrong Question**
Ronan discovers that the question he has been asking — "how do I get rid of this?" — was never the right question. The right question is "how do I stop letting this define everything?" He may or may not find a cure. But he stops organizing his entire life around the fear of himself.

### Emotional Core

Ronan's quest is about self-acceptance that is earned, not given. Whatever the player guides him toward, it should not be easy, and it should feel like his — not imposed.

## Relationship Dynamics

### Eleanor
Eleanor and Ronan's relationship can develop into romance depending on player choices. She sees him clearly — the guilt, the isolation, the deliberate care — and does not find any of it disqualifying. He finds her openness both disarming and frightening, because it does not leave him comfortable distance. Their dynamic works because she does not require him to be fixed, and he does not require her to be more guarded. It costs both of them something.

During **The Fractured Circle** campaign arc, the relationship inverts: Eleanor's struggle watching respected Guild members fall to the Corruption mirrors, in a different register, what Ronan lives with every day. He becomes one of her primary supports — reminding her that those who have fallen were once good people, and helping her carry the weight of impossible decisions. This is a direct expression of his growth: he is no longer only someone who needs saving. He has become someone capable of helping others using what his experience has taught him. See [`docs/systems/dynamic_story_arcs.md`](../systems/dynamic_story_arcs.md).

### Talos
Talos is cautious around Ronan — not hostile, and not personal. His concern is protective of others: if Ronan loses control, who gets hurt? Over time, as Ronan demonstrates the extent of his effort and his self-awareness, Talos's caution can evolve into genuine respect. They share more than either would say out loud: both have learned to manage something difficult alone.

### Ragash
### Ragash

Ragash becomes one of the first companions Ronan genuinely trusts. She does not approach his condition with the careful, considered openness that Eleanor does — she approaches it with simple, unperformed acceptance. She has spent years working with animals that others feared or dismissed, and she recognizes the pattern: fear and instinct do not define what a creature is, or what it is capable of.

She never asks him to justify his curse or to demonstrate that he is not dangerous. She takes the evidence of his behavior as it comes. This is, for Ronan, more disarming than sympathy. He knows how to receive pity. He does not know what to do with someone who simply does not make it a question.

Alongside Eleanor — who offers hope through her belief in who he can become — Ragash offers something he has rarely had: acceptance of who he is right now, without conditions attached.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Economical. Chooses words carefully. Not poetic — precise. He does not say more than he means.

### Tone
Even, with a slight undercurrent of tired. Not hopeless — just someone who has been carrying something for a long time.

### Vocabulary
Plain. Educated enough but not formal. His metaphors, when they appear, usually involve travel, distance, or light.

### Humor
Dark and quiet. Aimed at himself, almost always. The kind that only lands when the person hearing it understands what is underneath.

### Emotional Tendencies
Does not display emotion in ways that invite concern. Has learned to absorb difficult feelings without externalizing them. People who watch carefully will notice when he flinches at something; he will not acknowledge it if asked.

### What He Does Not Say
He does not talk about before the curse — what he was, where he was, what he lost. He does not say he is fine. He does not say he is not fine either. He redirects.

---

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Ronan is the reason the party can survive encounters they should not win."**

His Werewolf Form is the escalation option when standard approaches have failed. The player who uses it wisely wins fights that would otherwise be losses. The player who uses it carelessly learns why Ronan doesn't.
