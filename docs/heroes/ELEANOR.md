## Hero Summary

Eleanor is a young Human Mage with a gift so rare the Mages Guild has never quite known what to do with her. She can wield multiple elements simultaneously — something that should be impossible, or at least deeply inadvisable. She joined the Guild to support her family and prove that her gift is real, not a liability. She believes in people the way some people believe in gods: completely, sometimes at great cost. She is the warmest person in any room she walks into, and the one most likely to be hurt by it.

---

## Core Identity

> Eleanor is not a mage because she wants power.
> She became a mage because she was born with something extraordinary and chose to do something good with it.
> Everything about her gameplay should reinforce that her magic is an expression of who she is — and that who she is comes with a cost.

---

## Core Wound

**Naive optimism that leaves her vulnerable to betrayal.**

Eleanor sees the best in people. This is not performed optimism — she genuinely believes that most people, given the chance, will do the right thing. She extends trust quickly. She forgives readily. She assumes good intent.

This has hurt her before. It will hurt her again.

The wound is not cynicism waiting to happen. Eleanor's arc is not "learning to stop trusting people." It is learning to love people clearly — to see them as they are, not only as she hopes they will be — without losing the warmth that makes her worth knowing in the first place.

---

## Core Belief

> "People are trying their best. Most of them, most of the time."

She believes this with real conviction. It is not naivety as stupidity — she has seen enough hardship to know the world is difficult. She believes people are struggling, not malicious. The betrayals she has experienced have not yet broken this belief. They have only wounded it.

---

## Greatest Fear

Being the reason something good was destroyed.

Not failure in the abstract. The specific fear of trusting someone, vouching for someone, believing in someone — and being wrong in a way that costs other people dearly. She can bear being hurt herself. She is not sure she can bear causing harm through her own openness.

---

## Driving Goal

Prove that her gift is a gift — not a danger — and use it to do something that matters.

Support her family. Advance in the Mages Guild. Show that someone who wields multiple elements at once does not have to become a disaster.

She is trying to be worthy of the extraordinary thing she was born with.

## Personality

### Public Personality
- **Warm and open** — her default is friendliness; she does not perform approachability, she simply is approachable
- **Earnest** — says what she means; does not have a calculating register
- **Enthusiastic** — genuinely excited by magic, by exploration, by people; her energy is real, not performed
- **Occasionally oblivious** — her optimism can make her miss things that more guarded people would catch

### Private Personality
- **Quietly anxious** — aware that her power is unusual and that unusual things make people nervous
- **Deeply family-oriented** — her family is her anchor; thoughts of them surface when she is scared or homesick
- **Stubborn when it counts** — her warmth can read as softness; it is not; she will not abandon something she believes in
- **Needs to be needed** — finds meaning in being useful; struggles when she cannot help

### The Contrast

Eleanor's public warmth is genuine, not armored — which is precisely what makes her vulnerable. What she guards privately is the anxiety that her power might one day make her a burden rather than an asset, and the specific loneliness of being extraordinary in ways that set you apart from people you love.

---

## Moral Compass

Eleanor's priorities:
1. **The people she loves** — family, companions
2. **Innocent people** — especially those without protection
3. **The Mages Guild** — her institution, her career, her aspiration
4. **Abstract ideals** — justice, order; she believes in these, but they yield to people

She will not follow an order she believes will hurt innocent people. She will not abandon a companion to preserve an institution. She will, however, forgive an institution that has wronged her if she believes it can still do good — more readily than she probably should.

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
Young — visibly so. Carries herself with more confidence than her age might suggest, because she has spent years learning to take up space in rooms full of people who doubted her.

### Equipment
Light armor or mage robes. Her focus — staff, wand, orb, or crystal — is her primary weapon. The focus should feel like a natural extension of her rather than something imposed on her.

## Combat Identity

Eleanor is the party's **force multiplier** — specifically through magic that no one else could safely use.

She does not play it safe. Her Rare Elemental Casting lets her combine elements in ways that are more powerful than standard single-element spells and structurally less stable. Managing that instability is the core of playing Eleanor well. The risk is the point. She is not designed to be the party's safety net — she is designed to be its escalation.

> Her combat role and her character are the same thing: she does something extraordinary that comes with a cost she has to manage in real time.

## Signature Ability — Rare Elemental Casting

Eleanor's defining ability is her capacity to wield **multiple elements simultaneously**.

**What it does:**
- Combines two or more elemental types in a single spell
- More powerful than standard single-element casting
- Carries an instability tradeoff — combining elements creates risk as well as power

**Design intent:** This ability should feel like Eleanor at her most herself — brilliant and a little dangerous. Players who master it are rewarded with spectacular results; players who push it too far discover why the Guild was nervous about her.

The instability is not a punishment. It is the character.

## Personal Quest

### Theme

Eleanor's personal quest challenges her belief that people, given the chance, will do the right thing. The specific shape of this challenge: she extends trust to someone or something during the campaign, and that trust is tested — or broken.

### Player Influence

**Resolution A — The Cost of Clarity**
Eleanor's trust is betrayed in a way she cannot rationalize away. She emerges from it changed: still warm, but seeing people more clearly. She learns that loving someone well sometimes means seeing them honestly rather than hopefully. She does not become cynical. She becomes discerning.

**Resolution B — Vindicated**
Her faith in someone is challenged and ultimately proven right — not because she was naive, but because she saw something in that person that no one else believed in, and she was correct. This resolution should feel earned, not convenient.

**Resolution C — The Third Thing**
Eleanor discovers that the question is not "should I trust people?" but "what do I do with the hurt when trust goes wrong?" She finds a way to grieve what was broken without letting it close her off. The wound is not healed — it is metabolized.

### Emotional Core

Eleanor's quest is not about defeating an enemy. It is about what she does with being wrong about someone she believed in. Whatever resolution the player guides her toward, it should leave her changed — but recognizable.

## Relationship Dynamics

### Talos
Talos trusts Eleanor more quickly than he trusts most people. He recognizes her youth and genuine goodness, not as weaknesses but as things worth protecting. He does not become her guardian — she has not asked for that — but he becomes quietly, fiercely attentive. She may not always notice. He does not point it out.

### Ronan
Eleanor and Ronan's relationship can develop into romance depending on player choices. She sees him clearly — the guilt, the isolation, the genuine effort he makes to protect others from himself — and finds those qualities compelling rather than frightening. She is not naive about the curse. She simply believes the person matters more than the condition.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Natural rhythm; sentences come easily. Not performative — she does not think about how she sounds. She is simply talking.

### Tone
Warm and direct. Can shift to careful and precise when she is explaining something magical. Humor comes naturally and often unexpectedly.

### Vocabulary
Educated but not formal. Guild training has given her a technical vocabulary she uses when it fits and sets aside when it doesn't.

### Humor
Genuine and quick. Not used as armor — she laughs because things are actually funny.

### Emotional Tendencies
Expressive. Does not hide when she is sad or scared, though she tries not to burden others with it. More likely to ask someone else how they are feeling than to say she is struggling.

### What She Does Not Say
She avoids expressing doubt about herself in ways that invite reassurance. She does not want to be managed. She would rather struggle quietly than be handled.

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Eleanor is the reason the party can do things they shouldn't be able to do."**

Her Rare Elemental Casting opens options that no single-element mage could access. The instability that comes with it is the price — and the drama — of playing her.
