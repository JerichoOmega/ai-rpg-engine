## Hero Summary

Torren is a Master Blacksmith who carries his forging hammer everywhere — not as a weapon first, but as a tool. He is the party's craftsman, its repairer of broken things, and, in quiet ways he never announces, its optimist. He does not believe the world will fix itself. He believes it can be fixed, by people willing to do the work.

In a game about resisting corruption — about preserving identity against something that twists and consumes — Torren is the companion who answers the Corruption's central philosophy not with defiance but with craft.

The Ancient God whispers: *"Everything breaks."*

Torren's response: *"Then we rebuild."*

---

## Core Identity

> Torren is not optimistic because he has not seen loss.
> He is optimistic because he has seen loss and chose restoration anyway.
> Everything about his gameplay should reinforce this: he builds, repairs, supports, and endures.

---

## Core Wound

He once refused to give up on something that could not be saved. He held on past the point where letting go would have protected others — because he believed, with genuine conviction, that it was still repairable. The cost of that conviction was borne by people who trusted his judgment.

The philosophy he carries now — *"broken things aren't worthless; they simply require someone willing to rebuild them"* — is not naïveté. It is a response to having learned the hard way that the rebuilding must be real, not just hoped for. He tries now. He tries with his hands, not just his belief. And he has learned to recognize when something cannot be saved — even if it takes him longer than most to reach that conclusion.

**What remains unresolved:** What specifically could not be saved, and whether the people who bore the cost of his conviction have forgiven him.

⚠️ **The specific personal arc is not yet authored** — the wound's expression and resolution will be established when his quest is written.

---

## Core Belief

> "Broken things aren't worthless. They simply require someone willing to rebuild them."

He applies this to weapons, armor, relics, buildings, and people with equal sincerity. This is not a metaphor for him — he genuinely does not distinguish. Fixing a cracked breastplate and helping a companion who is falling apart require the same fundamental commitment: stay present, do the work, do not give up before the evidence justifies it.

---

## Greatest Fear

Being wrong again in the same way. Believing something can be rebuilt when it cannot, and having others pay for that mistake.

---

## Driving Goal

To leave the world in better condition than he found it — specifically, concretely, piece by piece. Not in the abstract. Through the work of his hands.

## Personality

### Public Personality
- **Calm** — not unfeeling; calm by practice and disposition
- **Patient** — does not rush; understands that good work takes the time it takes
- **Reliable** — the companion who does what he said he would do
- **Humble** — does not announce his work; the evidence is the announcement
- **Quietly humorous** — his humor is dry, warm, and usually arrives after a pause

### Private Personality
- **Carries something heavy** — a failure he has not spoken about in detail
- **Prefers action to conversation** — when something is wrong, he reaches for tools first
- **Needs the work** — his crafting is not just a role; it is how he manages what he carries
- **Deeply invested in the party** — he would not say so; the evidence is in what he repairs without being asked

### The Contrast

Torren's public calm is genuine but incomplete. He has made peace with most of what he carries; there is still one piece he has not resolved. The party will likely notice this before he acknowledges it.

---

## Moral Compass

Torren's priorities:
1. **The people around him** — practical protection; keeping the party functional
2. **The larger world** — what can be preserved, built, or restored for more than just those present
3. **The principle** — his code is not complicated: do not give up before it is time

He will not walk away from something repairable. He will not pretend something is repairable when it is not. The discipline required to know the difference is the work of his life.

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
Broad-shouldered. The build of someone who has been at a forge for decades. Hands that show the work — calloused, scarred, burn-marked. Nothing about his appearance is decorative. Everything has been used.

### Equipment
His forging hammer is his primary weapon — a warhammer that is also a craftsman's tool. It has been repaired more than once. He carries a portable forge kit and tools that should be too heavy to travel with but somehow always travel with him.

### Recognizable Personal Detail — The Cold Clasp

Torren carries a **heat-warped iron clasp** — a hinged fastening about the size of his palm, the kind forged to hold something bigger than itself shut. It has been through fire hot enough to run the metal: the hinge is fused, the plate is bowed, and one corner has slagged into a shape no smith would sign. The workmanship underneath the damage is visible to anyone who knows what to look for. It was made well. It failed anyway.

He keeps it in his tool roll — not with his personal effects, but in the slot beside his finest hammer, where a working smith keeps a pending job. That placement is the entire statement: to Torren it is not a memorial. It is work he has not finished.

**The detail that defines it:** Torren repairs everything. Cracked breastplates, strangers' gate hinges, companions' boot buckles no one asked him to touch. The clasp is the one broken thing he owns that he has never repaired. At camp, some nights, he takes it out, turns it over in the firelight, sets it on the anvil — and then puts it back in the roll and picks up someone else's gear instead. He has never once put a tool to it. The party will notice this long before anyone understands it.

If asked about it directly, he answers the way he answers everything — plainly, and no further than the question: *"It's from something I couldn't save."* He does not elaborate. He does not lie. He puts it away.

**What it anchors:** the clasp is the physical form of his Core Wound — the thing he refused to let go of past the point where letting go would have protected others. What the clasp held shut, and who bore the cost when it didn't, is deliberately not defined here; that belongs to his personal arc. Writers should treat the clasp exactly as Torren does: present, visible, unexplained. Whether repairing it — or finally choosing not to — becomes part of his arc's resolution is a decision reserved for the arc itself.

**Usage guidance:** the clasp should appear the way Talos's bead bracelet appears — naturally and recurrently, never as a set piece. Campfire scenes are its home: the sound of the tool roll opening, the pause, the roll closing. Companions may notice and react in character (Ragash would recognize an object that carries the record of what it survived; Ronan would recognize the one thing someone keeps and won't touch). No one should force the conversation. Torren hasn't.

### Silhouette
Solid. Grounded. In a lineup of the five heroes, Torren's silhouette is the one that looks like it cannot be moved easily. He occupies space the way load-bearing walls occupy space.

## Combat Identity

Torren is the party's **battlefield shaper** — not the most spectacular combatant, but the one whose presence changes the geometry of every fight.

His attacks are deliberate and powerful rather than agile. He does not chase targets. He changes the battlefield so that others can fight better — by creating cover, reinforcing defenses, placing constructs that control terrain, and wearing down opponents through sustained heavy strikes.

> His combat role and his character are the same thing: he builds what endures, and he does not move from it.

## Signature Ability — Field Forge

Torren's defining ability is his capacity to **construct during combat** — building practical, handcrafted structures that reshape the battlefield rather than summoning magical creatures or deploying technology.

**What it does:**
- Creates functional battlefield structures during combat
- Structures persist across turns and affect positioning, movement, and combat geometry
- Everything feels handcrafted by a legendary smith — not magical, not technological

**Available constructs (examples):**

| Construct | Effect |
|---|---|
| Defensive Barricade | Creates cover for adjacent allies |
| Spike Barrier | Zone denial; enemy movement costs increase through area |
| Forge Beacon | Persistent buff aura for allies within range |
| Supply Cache | Allows adjacent allies to restore consumables |
| Portable Anvil | Enables in-combat equipment repair |
| Reinforced Wall | Permanent terrain modification for the encounter |
| Defensive Tower | Creates elevated position for ranged allies |
| Ballista | Long-range damage emplacement; persistent threat in zone |

**Design intent:** Every construct should feel like something Torren made with his hands in the middle of a fight — impressive because of craft, not magic. Limitations on what he can build should feel like craft constraints, not arbitrary rules.

## Skill Trees

Torren's three specialization paths offer meaningfully different playstyles. Players cannot unlock everything. Every investment should matter. No build should invalidate another.

### Forge Master — Ally Enhancement

**Focus:** Supporting the party through equipment reinforcement and ongoing repairs.

| Skill | Effect |
|---|---|
| Sharpen Weapon | Temporary attack bonus for target ally |
| Temper Edge | Makes ally's next attack armor-piercing |
| Reinforce Armor | Temporary defense bonus for target ally |
| Battle Repairs | Restores armor durability mid-combat |
| Forge Blessing | Party-wide equipment bonus before major encounters |
| Emergency Reinforcement | Reactive repair when an ally drops below health threshold |

**Playstyle:** Torren as force multiplier — the blacksmith who makes everyone else better equipped for what they face.

---

### Engineer — Battlefield Construction

**Focus:** Terrain manipulation and tactical positioning through constructs.

| Skill | Effect |
|---|---|
| Portable Barricade | Rapid cover deployment |
| Spike Barrier | Zone denial; enemy movement costs increase |
| Defensive Tower | Elevated position; ranged ally gains accuracy bonus |
| Ballista | Damage emplacement; persistent threat in zone |
| Supply Cache | Sustain tool; adjacent allies can resupply |
| Forge Beacon | Persistent buff aura within range |
| Cover Reinforcement | Strengthens existing constructs' durability |

**Playstyle:** Torren as architect — every fight is a construction problem, and the player who thinks spatially wins.

### War Smith — Hammer Mastery

**Focus:** Transforming Torren into a durable frontline fighter.

| Skill | Effect |
|---|---|

**Playstyle:** Torren as the weapon — the hammer the party puts where it needs to go.

## Personal Quest

### Theme

⚠️ **NOT YET AUTHORED** — Torren's personal quest will center on his Core Wound: the thing he once refused to release past the point where he should have, and the people who bore the cost of that refusal. The shape of the quest depends on what that was — and whether those people are still out there.

### What Is Known

- The quest will force Torren to confront the limits of his philosophy under conditions that test it most severely
- The Corruption will be involved — something that may not be rebuil by ordinary means
- Resolution options will involve what he decides about what restoration actually means: fixing the original thing, accepting the loss, or building something new from what remains
- His relationship with Ronan — the specific dynamic of "broken steel can be reforged" — should be directly engaged during this arc

### Emotional Core

Torren's quest is not about learning to believe in restoration. He already believes in it. It is about confronting the one thing his belief did not protect — and deciding whether that failure disproves his philosophy or deepens it.

## Relationship Dynamics

### Talos

Mutual respect built on discipline and craftsmanship. Talos respects Torren's dedication — the consistency, the absence of complaint, the quiet competence that does not need an audience. Torren recognizes Talos's duty in the same way he recognizes any craftsman who takes their work seriously: with acknowledgment rather than admiration. Neither announces this. Both notice it in the other. It does not require conversation.

### Eleanor

Eleanor understands magical harmony; Torren understands physical restoration. Their conversations tend toward comparison — what it looks like to repair a relic versus to understand why it broke, what healing a person shares with rebuilding a structure. Eleanor finds his perspective reaches conclusions her academic instinct sometimes misses. Torren finds her curiosity a genuinely different lens on problems he approaches from one direction.

### Ragash

Ragash helps Torren appreciate imperfect beauty — that things which have been damaged carry the record of what they have survived, and that record is not simply a flaw to be corrected. Torren teaches Ragash patience through craftsmanship — that some things require sustained, unhurried attention to come back right. Together they reinforce the idea that identity is built through experience rather than perfection.

### Ronan

Torren understands that Ronan believes himself broken. He does not treat this as a diagnosis to argue with. He treats it the same way he treats a cracked blade: as a fact about current condition that says nothing definitive about what is possible.

He does not try to fix Ronan. He reminds him — usually through action rather than argument — that being scarred is not the same as being beyond repair.

> *Ronan: "I'm broken."*  
> *Torren: "Broken steel can be reforged."*

This is not false comfort. Torren has seen things that could not be repaired. He also knows that the conclusion of *cannot be saved* should not be reached before the attempt has been made.

## Campfire Presence

Torren is almost always doing something when the party camps.

Typical scenes:
- Repairing damaged weapons and armor before anyone asks
- Teaching basic maintenance to younger or less experienced companions
- Quietly listening to conversations while his hands are occupied
- Offering a thought after a long pause — usually the one that was worth waiting for
- Maintaining equipment before everyone wakes
- Working beside the campfire late into the night; the sound of his tools becomes part of the camp's ambient texture

His craftsmanship is part of the game's atmosphere. Players who pay attention will notice that gear tends to be in better condition after a camp with Torren.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Speaks after listening. His sentences are shorter than most companions' and more considered. He has the unhurried quality of someone who has learned that rushing a weld weakens it.

### Tone
Warm without being effusive. Dry without being cold. He does not perform patience — he simply has it.

### Vocabulary
Craft-inflected. He reaches for forge and tool metaphors naturally, not affectedly. His frame of reference for most things is the workshop.

### Humor
Dry and genuine. Usually arrives after a pause, which is part of why it lands. He is not performing humor — something struck him as funny and he said so.

### Emotional Tendencies
Does not announce what he is feeling. The evidence is in what he does — which companion's equipment he quietly checks after a hard day, which conversations make him set his work down, which moments make his hands stop.

### What He Does Not Say
He does not discuss the failure in detail. He does not announce his philosophy. He does not claim to know that things will be fine. He says what he believes — *"broken things can be reforged"* — and he means it, and he does not need others to agree.

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Torren is the reason the party can hold a position no one expected them to hold."**

His Field Forge ability and Engineer skill tree allow the party to reshape combat geometry in ways no other companion provides. Campaigns built around him play differently at a structural level — more durable, more controlled, more capable of turning a losing position into a defensible one.

---

## Philosophical Position in the Roster

Each companion represents a different response to the Corruption's central assault: the claim that identity cannot be preserved against what corrupts it. Torren's answer is specific and practical.

The Corruption destroys. Torren builds. Where the Corruption consumes what has been made, Torren repairs it. Where the Corruption whispers that everything is lost, Torren's presence is the ongoing evidence that something can be done about it — that the correct response to a broken thing is not to discard it, but to put it back together.

This is not a position he argues. It is a position he demonstrates.

## The Forgotten Eighth — Revelation Response

When the party learns the truth about the Ancient God — that she was one of the Chorus, that she was sealed away by her family, that the prison is the greatest containment structure ever built — Torren approaches the revelation through the lens of craft, systems, and design.

He does not lead with blame. His first instinct is not *who was right* but *was there another way*.

He studies the prison itself. He considers what it would take to build something that could hold a god — the engineering, the intent, the decision-making about what level of permanence to commit to. He respects the craftsmanship of the solution while questioning whether containment was the only answer. He knows from his own work that the first solution that presents itself is not always the right one, and that a solution built under grief and urgency carries the shape of the moment it was made in.

He does not condemn the Chorus. He does not absolve them. He asks the question a builder asks: *given what they knew, given how much time they had, given what was at stake — did they make the best structure they could? And: was there a design they didn't think of?*

His companion response: *"They built something that would hold. I understand that instinct. I also know that 'hold' isn't always the same as 'solve.' What they built has been leaking for a thousand years. That's a design problem."*

This is not coldness. It is the way Torren processes grief — through the question of what could have been made differently, so that the next thing can be better.
