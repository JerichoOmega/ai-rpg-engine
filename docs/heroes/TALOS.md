## Hero Summary

Talos is an Elf Knight who became a soldier not because he wanted to fight, but because someone has to stand between innocent people and the horrors of the world. Decades of war have left him scarred — physically and emotionally. He trusts almost no one. He protects everyone.

His bead bracelet, gifted one bead at a time by children he has met across his travels, is the only decoration he wears. It represents exactly who he fights for.

---

## Core Identity

> Talos is not a warrior because he enjoys violence.  
> He became a warrior because someone has to stand between innocent people and the horrors of the world.  
> Everything about his gameplay should reinforce this philosophy.

He is a **protector by necessity** — not a glory-seeker, not a warrior-born. The violence in his life is the cost he accepted to keep others safe from it.

---

## Core Wound

**Lost faith in authority after years of war.**

Talos has spent years watching ordinary people suffer while governments, commanders, and institutions pursued their own agendas. He has followed orders he later regretted. He has seen good soldiers die for politics. He has watched leaders who claimed to protect their people use those same people as tools.

He does not hate governments because he is rebellious. He distrusts them because he has seen too clearly what they become.

---

## Core Belief

> "Every institution, given enough time and power, will eventually sacrifice the people it claims to protect."

He believes this not as cynicism, but as a conclusion drawn from experience. He does not enjoy being right about it.

---

## Greatest Fear

Being unable to protect someone who needed him.

Not death. Not pain. Not loneliness. The specific fear of standing there, trained and capable, and still failing to keep an innocent person safe.

---

## Driving Goal

Stand between innocent people and the horrors of the world.

Not revenge. Not justice in the abstract. Concrete, immediate protection of the people in front of him. His community. His companions. Strangers who have no one else.

---

## Personality

### Public Personality
Talos presents to the world as:
- **Stoic** — controlled expression; rarely shows what he feels until he trusts you
- **Disciplined** — methodical, deliberate; never acts on impulse in public
- **Blunt** — says what he means; no time for softening or politics
- **Slow to trust** — years of dealing with people who had their own agendas have made him deeply cautious

### Private Personality
Those who earn his trust discover:
- **Deeply kind** — especially with people who cannot protect themselves
- **Protective instinct** — will step in front of danger for someone he barely knows
- **Loves telling stories** — particularly to children; has an inexhaustible supply of adventure tales
- **Dry humor** — quiet, deadpan, appears without warning around people he trusts

### The Contrast

His public face is formidable. His private face is the reason he became a soldier. That contrast — the intimidating exterior protecting a genuine heart — is central to who he is.

---

## Moral Compass

Talos' loyalty, in order:

1. **Innocent people** — strangers, children, the vulnerable
2. **His companions** — once trust is earned, it is absolute
3. **His community** — the people around him, not an institution
4. **Government** — if and only if it has demonstrably earned it

His morality always prioritizes protecting people over following authority. He will disobey an order he believes is wrong. He has before.

---

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
- Tall and heavily built — decades of combat and training
- Numerous visible battle scars
- Heavy armor — full plate or equivalent; built for sustained engagement
- One-handed sword and shield as primary loadout

### The Bead Bracelet

The bead bracelet worn on his wrist is his most important visual detail.

Each bead was gifted to him by a child he met during his travels. It should remain **visible on his character model whenever possible** — worn over gauntlets if needed. It represents exactly who he fights to protect.

It is the only personal decoration he carries.

### Silhouette

Talos should be immediately recognizable at a glance: towering, heavy-armored, shield raised. The silhouette is a wall.

---

## Combat Identity

Talos is a **protector** — not a damage dealer.

He is designed around **enabling the rest of the party to survive**, not around dealing the highest damage numbers. Players who want to maximize Talos's effectiveness think about positioning, coverage, and who needs protecting this round — not about their own attack output.

> His combat role and his identity as a person are the same thing.

---

## Unique Passive — Guardian's Resolve

> *No other companion may possess this passive.*

**When adjacent to allies:**
- Nearby allies gain defensive bonuses
- Talos gains increased resistance to crowd control effects

Exact values determined during balancing.

**Design rationale:** Guardian's Resolve mechanically expresses Talos's identity. His presence physically makes the people around him safer. It rewards positioning him near the companions who need protection most.

---

## Signature Ability — Battlecry

Battlecry is Talos's defining move — the ability players most associate with him.

**What it does:**
- Inspires allies — grants stat boosts to the party
- Intimidates enemies — applies a debuff or unsettles enemy behavior
- Represents Talos as a battlefield leader, not just a frontline fighter

**Design intent:** Battlecry should feel like the moment Talos stops being a soldier and becomes the reason everyone else keeps fighting. It is a rallying tool, not a personal damage spike.

---

## Shield Stance Integration

Talos is the **primary showcase** for the Shield Stance system.

He is the definitive defensive hero. Every Shield Stance mechanic, from basic block to advanced reactions, should feel most natural when playing as Talos.

**Future talents and abilities may expand his Shield Stance into:**

| Ability | Design Direction |
|---|---|
| **Shield Wall** | Extend protection to adjacent allies |
| **Hold the Line** | Prevent enemy movement through his position |
| **Bulwark** | Significantly increased block threshold for one round |
| **Brace** | Prepare for a telegraphed heavy attack |
| **Guardian's Oath** | Story/battle ability; commits to protecting a named ally for a duration |

These are design directions, not implemented abilities. Exact mechanics defined during implementation.

## Personal Quest

### Theme

Talos's personal quest challenges his foundational belief that every government and institution inevitably becomes corrupt and fails the people it claims to protect.

### Player Influence

The player's choices can guide Talos toward one of three resolutions:

**Resolution A — Earned Trust**
Talos encounters a leader who genuinely does right by their people, even at personal cost. He does not fully abandon his skepticism — but he learns that his rule has exceptions. That some leaders deserve the trust he withheld.

**Resolution B — Confirmed Conviction**
The institutions in the story confirm every fear he has ever had. He was right. He ends the quest not bitter, but clearer: his path was always the correct one, and it always will be.

**Resolution C — A Third Way**
Talos finds that his question — trust institutions or reject them? — was the wrong question. True change begins at the level he has always operated: protecting one community at a time, building something small enough to hold. Neither naive faith in institutions nor endless opposition — a quieter, more personal kind of hope.

### Emotional Core

The quest is not about defeating a villain. It is about healing an emotional wound. Whatever resolution the player guides him toward, it should feel **earned** — not imposed.

## Relationship Dynamics

### Eleanor
Talos trusts Eleanor surprisingly quickly. He recognizes her innocence and optimism — not as weaknesses to exploit but as things worth protecting. Over the course of the story he becomes quietly, fiercely protective of her. He may never say it directly.

### Ragash
Mutual respect — the kind that grows between two people who recognize the same quality in each other without needing to say it. Both are willing to put themselves in harm's way for others. This shared foundation can, depending on player choices, develop into a slow-burn romance.

### Ronan
Talos remains cautious around Ronan — not personally, but practically. He does not fear who Ronan is. He fears what Ronan's curse could do to someone nearby if control slips. His concern is protective of others, not hostile toward Ronan. Over time, as Ronan demonstrates control and intent, this caution can evolve into genuine mutual respect.

### Steven
Talos and Steven share something unusual: a form of communication that does not require words. Talos does not try to fill Steven's silence, does not treat it as a problem to solve, and does not speak louder or more slowly as if that would help. He simply adjusts — treating Steven's gestures and expressions as a complete language, because they are. For Steven, this is rarer than it should be.

Whether this mutual ease deepens into something more — genuine understanding, a specific trust, a friendship built in the particular quiet they share — depends on the events of the campaign and the player's choices.

## Orphanages & Children

Every town Talos visits — every single one — he finds the orphanage.

He does not announce it. He does not explain it to the party. He simply goes. He greets the children at eye level — which requires the large, heavily armored elf to kneel. He tells them stories of grand adventures with enough detail to feel real and enough hope to feel possible. He has an inexhaustible supply. He always has time.

The bead bracelet tells the history of this habit. Each bead was gifted to him by a child he met during his travels — given willingly, remembered specifically. He knows where each one came from. He does not speak about the bracelet unless someone asks directly, and even then he answers plainly, without sentiment: *"One from each."*

This is the private Talos the public Talos protects. The intimidating exterior — the height, the scars, the full plate — is not armor against the world. It is armor for the people who need protecting. What it protects is someone who kneels to talk to children and wears their gifts on his wrist into every battle he fights.

> The contrast between his appearance and this detail is the character. It should appear naturally and frequently in gameplay — never as a narrative set-piece, always as a thing he simply does.

## Character Details

The following personal details are confirmed canon and should appear naturally throughout gameplay:

| Detail | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Loves wine** | A genuine pleasure he does not apologize for |
| **Favorite food: roasted duck** | Specific; he will mention it unprompted |
| **Always visits orphanages** | Every town, without exception; kneels to greet children; tells stories |
| **Loves telling stories** | Especially to children; has an inexhaustible supply |
| **Bead bracelet** | One bead from each child he has met on his travels; worn always; knows where each came from |
| **No racial prejudice** | Hard line; anyone who expresses it loses his respect immediately |

These details should **contrast with his intimidating appearance**. The large, scarred, armored soldier who kneels down to talk to children at eye level — that contrast is the character.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Deliberate. Chooses words with weight. Not verbose. Every sentence means something.

### Tone
Can shift between heartfelt and bitter depending on the conversation and who he is speaking with. Neither register is performed — both are genuine.

### Vocabulary
Direct. No decoration. No euphemism. He says what he means in the plainest words available.

### Humor
Dry. Deadpan. Appears without warning, usually one sentence, then gone. It only emerges around people he trusts.

### Emotional Tendencies
Does not hide his feelings — but chooses carefully when to express them. With strangers: controlled. With trusted companions: present.

### What He Does Not Say
He does not complain. He does not explain himself to people he does not respect. He does not ask for sympathy.

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Talos is the reason the rest of the party survives."**

Every design decision about his abilities, positioning, and passive should serve this sentence.
