## Hero Summary

Ragash is an Orc Houndmaster who found belonging in the one place her people never thought to look: with animals. Her hounds did not care what clan she came from, whether she fit the mold, or whether she proved herself by their standards. They came when she called. They stayed. She has built her entire identity around deserving that loyalty — and quietly, carefully, she is beginning to extend a version of it to people too.

She is blunt to the point of seeming cold. She is warm in ways that take time to see. She is proud enough to make everything harder than it needs to be, and honest enough that you never wonder where you stand with her.

---

## Core Identity

> Ragash is not a fighter because she is aggressive.
> She fights because the things she loves — her hounds, her chosen companions — are worth protecting, and she will not rely on anyone else to do it.
> Everything about her gameplay should express this: her power comes from what she has built and who she has committed to.

---

## Core Wound

**Rejected by her own people; found belonging with her hounds instead of other people.**

Ragash was part of an orc clan that had clear expectations of what she should be. She did not fit those expectations — not because she was weak, but because she was different in ways her people did not know how to value. The rejection was not dramatic. It was accumulated. A series of moments in which she understood, clearly, that she was on the outside of something she would never be invited inside.

She did not grieve it openly. She found something better.

Her hounds gave her unconditional belonging for the first time. She has organized her life around being worthy of it ever since. People, in general, have remained at a careful distance — valued when proven, trusted slowly, never taken for granted.

---

## Core Belief

> "You earn belonging. It is not given."

This applies to everything: her place in any group, the loyalty of her hounds, the respect of companions. She does not believe in unconditional acceptance from people — she believes in the kind that is built, tested, and demonstrated. She is suspicious of anyone who offers trust easily, because trust that costs nothing is worth nothing.

---

## Greatest Fear

Losing one of her hounds.

Not in abstract. The specific loss of an animal she raised, trained, and made promises to. Her hounds receive the openness she withholds from people — they are the place where she is not armored. Losing one of them would reach her in a way that nothing else can.

---

## Driving Goal

Find the rare mythical breed of hound she has heard legends of — a creature said to be nearly extinct, whose existence she cannot confirm and cannot stop thinking about.

Prove, on her own terms, that her way of doing things is worth something.

She is not trying to go home. She left. She is building something new.

---

## Personality

### Public Personality
- **Blunt** — says exactly what she means; no softening; no preamble
- **Proud** — does not apologize for who she is or how she operates
- **Self-contained** — does not need validation; visibly uncomfortable when asked to explain herself emotionally
- **Reliable** — shows up; does what she says she will do; no drama

### Private Personality
- **Deeply loyal** — once given, her commitment is absolute and does not waver
- **Observant of people** — pays more attention than she lets on; reads rooms well while appearing to ignore them
- **Gentle with her hounds** — a completely different register; patient, attentive, quietly devoted
- **Dry sense of humor** — surfaces rarely, without announcement, and usually lands perfectly

### The Contrast

Ragash's public face reads as indifference. It is not indifference — it is the careful management of how much she shows, because she has learned that showing too much is how you end up hurt. The person who emerges slowly is warmer, funnier, and more perceptive than most people expect. That gap is the character.

---

## Moral Compass

Ragash's priorities:
1. **Her hounds** — always; they come first
2. **Companions who have earned it** — absolute once given
3. **Herself and her own code** — she has a strong personal ethic that does not bend to authority
4. **Institutions and groups** — respected if they prove worthy; dismissed if they don't

She will not follow orders from someone who has not earned her respect, regardless of their rank. She will not abandon a companion for any institutional reason. She respects results and honesty; she has no patience for politics, performance, or pretense.

---

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
Built for endurance. Orc physique — strong, durable. She moves with the unhurried confidence of someone who is not trying to impress anyone. Scars from old hunts and older conflicts.

### Equipment
Longsword as primary weapon. Practical armor — heavy enough to take damage, not so heavy it slows her down. Nothing decorative. Everything functional.

### Recognizable Personal Detail
Her hounds. Wherever Ragash is, her hounds are nearby or recently departed. They are not just a combat mechanic — they are her companions, raised from pups, individually known to her. The way she interacts with them — quiet commands, familiar gestures, a different kind of attention — tells you everything about who she is.

### Silhouette
Grounded and solid. Not towering like Talos, but unmistakably capable. The hounds at or near her position are part of the silhouette. She should be recognizable as a unit with them.

---

## Combat Identity

Ragash is the party's **coordinated force** — a fighter whose effectiveness multiplies when her hounds are deployed correctly alongside her.

She is not a simple damage dealer. She is a tactician who has developed a precise working relationship with animals that respond to her as extensions of her own intent. Positioning matters. The hounds are not pets in combat — they are partners.

> Playing Ragash well means thinking about where the hounds are, not just where she is.

## Signature Ability — Hound Summon

Ragash calls her trained hounds into the battle.

**What it does:**
- Deploys one or more hounds to the battlefield
- Hounds act as independent units (attack, flank, intercept)
- Ragash and her hounds work as a coordinated system

**Design intent:** The Hound Summon should feel like Ragash calling on a relationship, not activating a tool. The hounds should feel like characters — specific, responsive, hers. Players should feel the weight of deploying them into danger.

## Personal Quest

### Theme

Ragash's personal quest is about the search for the rare mythical hound breed — and what that search reveals about what she is actually looking for. On the surface it is a hunter's quest. Underneath it is a question about belonging: whether she can find a place in the world that is entirely her own, built from what she values rather than what she was told to value.

### Player Influence

**Resolution A — Found**
The rare breed exists. She finds it, or finds proof of it, or makes contact with it. Whether she can bond with one, and what that means for her, is a moment of genuine arrival — she built something no one believed she could.

**Resolution B — The Hunt Is the Point**
The rare breed may not be findable. What Ragash discovers is that the search itself has shaped her: the places she went, the companions she trusted, the version of herself she built while looking. The destination mattered less than she thought.

**Resolution C — Belonging Is Already Here**
Through the events of the campaign, Ragash realizes that she has already built the belonging she was looking for — in her hounds, in her companions, in the party she joined for reasons she couldn't have articulated at the start. The mythical breed remains out there. She knows where to look next. But she is not alone in looking.

### Emotional Core

Ragash's quest is about whether someone who was told she did not belong can build belonging on her own terms. The answer should feel like hers — not given to her.

## Relationship Dynamics

### Talos
Mutual respect — deep and immediate, the kind that forms between two people who recognize the same quality in each other before they have words for it. Both Talos and Ragash are protectors who have learned not to rely on anyone. Both lead with action, not speech. Depending on player choices, this mutual recognition can develop into a slow-burn romance. Neither would say it first.

### Eleanor

Ragash gradually comes to understand why Talos is so protective of Eleanor. Initially she assumes it is because Talos sees Eleanor as inexperienced — someone who needs looking after until she finds her footing. Over time, through watching Eleanor with the party, she reaches a different conclusion.

Eleanor is the emotional heart of the group. She inspires hope. She sees goodness in people when they have stopped seeing it in themselves. She reminds everyone why they are still fighting. Ragash — who has spent much of her life without that kind of presence — recognizes its value in a way that is not sentimental but practical: losing Eleanor would break something within the party that could not be repaired.

Ragash becomes just as protective of Eleanor as Talos. Not because Eleanor is weak — she is not — but because Ragash understands what she provides and what it would cost to lose her.

### Ronan

Ragash and Ronan develop one of the strongest friendships in the party. The foundation is recognition: Ragash has spent most of her life as an outsider to her own people, and she understands viscerally what it is to feel like that. She does not need Ronan to explain his situation to her. She already knows what it looks like.

Her years raising and training hounds have also given her a particular understanding of fear and instinct — how those things shape behavior without defining worth. She sees Ronan as a man struggling against his curse, not as the curse itself. She never judges him for his condition. She does not treat it as something he needs to apologize for.

She becomes one of the first companions Ronan genuinely trusts. Alongside Eleanor — who offers hope through belief in his humanity — Ragash offers something different: acceptance through the absence of judgment. Eleanor sees who Ronan could be. Ragash accepts who he is now. Both matter to him, and in different ways.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Short sentences. Direct. Says what needs to be said and stops. Does not fill silence.

### Tone
Even. Rarely raised. More impact in quiet than in volume.

### Vocabulary
Plain. No decoration. The most efficient word for the meaning.

### Humor
Rare, dry, and precisely timed. The people who catch it value it more because of how rarely it appears.

### Emotional Tendencies
Does not display emotion in ways that invite response. Anger is controlled; grief is private; satisfaction is a nod. Those who know her well learn to read the small signals.

### What She Does Not Say
She does not talk about her clan unless asked, and even then says little. She does not explain why she trusts someone or why she has withdrawn trust — she simply acts on it. She does not ask for help. She finds a way or does without.

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Ragash is the reason the party always has eyes everywhere."**

Her hounds extend her reach beyond her own tile. The party fights differently with Ragash because the battlefield has more players on it.

## Companion Events — Goblin Trail Detection

These events trigger when the party is traveling in Frontier regions and Ragash's hounds detect goblin activity. Each event identifies a specific tribe based on the nature of the trail, provides actionable information that changes how the party can approach what lies ahead, and surfaces character texture that is otherwise unavailable.

**Trigger conditions:**
- Party is traveling in the Frontier
- Ragash is in the active party
- A goblin camp, raiding party, or significant trail is within range of the current path

**AI Director note:** These events are not random flavor. Each one changes the tactical or diplomatic situation. The AI Director should use the information provided to create a genuine branch — a different encounter outcome is possible because Ragash caught the trail. If the party ignores the information and proceeds directly, the encounter proceeds as it would have without the event.

---

### Event 1 — The Raiding Weight (Stonefang)

**When it triggers:** The party is moving through eastern Frontier territory, within range of an active Stonefang raiding party or the approach to Fang-Hold.

**What happens:**

One of Ragash's hounds — Dusk, the lead tracker — goes rigid mid-stride. Then drops low, nose working fast and low along the ground. Ragash raises a fist without turning around.

> "Stop."

She watches Dusk for a moment. Then she crouches beside the hound and examines the ground herself — compressed earth, a partial bootprint, something smeared against the base of a shrub. She stays there longer than the situation seems to require.

When she stands:

> "Stonefang. Raiding party. Eight, maybe ten. That trail is two, three hours old — they're moving west, toward the settlement road."
>
> "They're not hunting. They're carrying weight. Whatever they hit, they already hit it."

**What the party gains:**

- **Tactical advance warning:** The raiding party is ahead on the western road, loaded down with loot. Loaded fighters move slower and are less alert than a force moving to engage. The party has the option to intercept from an advantageous position, set up a roadblock, or let them pass.
- **Flanking route:** Ragash can read from the trail which direction the party came from and which direction they're heading. She can identify a parallel track through the brush that bypasses the road entirely — useful if the party wants to avoid the encounter or reach the settlement ahead of them.
- **Diplomatic leverage option:** If the party has or wants standing with the Stonefang, allowing a successful raiding party to pass without interference is a non-action that costs nothing and generates no enemies. If Grakkor's trackers later learn a party of adventurers encountered the trail and didn't interfere, that information has value.

**Ragash's read on the situation (player prompt):**

> "Your call. If they've already done their damage, there's nothing to stop. If you want to push them — we have position."

She does not advocate. She provides information and waits.

**Relationship note:** Ragash does not express sympathy for Stonefang raiders, but she does not perform anger about them either. She treats the trail as a tactical problem. Players who expect her to have an opinion about goblins will find she does not — not because she is indifferent, but because she has learned that treating any group as a uniform threat is how you stop reading situations accurately.

---

### Event 2 — The Quiet Passage (Mossroot)

**When it triggers:** The party is moving through the forest margin zones where the Great Forest meets the western Frontier, within range of Mossroot territory or a Mossroot seasonal camp.

**What happens:**

Ragash's hounds do something unusual — they do not alert. They go quiet. All three of them, almost simultaneously, tracking something with careful, contained attention rather than the sharp interest of a pursuit lock. Ragash notices before the party does.

> "Hold."

She watches her hounds for a long moment. Then she moves a few steps off the path, kneels, and examines a section of undergrowth. There is almost nothing to see. Almost.

> "Mossroot. Hunter trail — two, maybe three. Recent. An hour, less."

She straightens, and there's something in her expression that wasn't there a moment ago — not quite respect, but recognition.

> "They didn't want to be found. My hounds almost missed it."

**What the party gains:**

- **Safe passage intelligence:** Mossroot hunters who are not alerting to the party's presence are either unaware of them or have chosen not to engage. This is meaningful — if the Mossroot wanted to intercept the party, they would already have position. Their absence from the path is a form of permission.
- **Contact opportunity:** Ragash can identify approximately where the hunters went and can attempt to follow the trail to make contact rather than proceeding blind into Mossroot territory. This is the party's best window for a non-hostile first meeting with Mossroot — they have advance knowledge, they have not triggered a defensive response, and they are not blundering into sacred territory unannounced.
- **Territorial marker reading:** The way the trail is concealed tells Ragash something about where Mossroot hunters consider their core range to be. She can warn the party if they are approaching a boundary the Mossroot are likely to defend.

**The inter-species dynamic:**

This is the event where Ragash's orc status becomes relevant in a specific way. If the party pursues the trail to make contact:

> "Let me go first. Or don't go at all."

She does not explain unless pressed. If pressed:

> "They'll read me differently than they'll read you. I'm not from the civilization that's been contracting against them for thirty years. That's not nothing."

This is not a guarantee of a better reception — Ragash is not claiming kinship with goblins. She is making an accurate tactical observation about how her species will be interpreted differently than the human party members' species. The Mossroot's Elder Speaker Vess has noted in the goblin tribes documentation that she distrusts outsiders on principle, not out of hatred, and that genuine behavior matters more than performed respect. Ragash approaching alone or first signals that the party understands this distinction, which is itself a form of appropriate behavior.

**Relationship note:** This event is one of the clearest windows into how Ragash processes her own species and its relationship to the world's political landscape. She does not romanticize it. She uses it as information.

---

### Event 3 — The Spread Pattern (Ashfire)

**When it triggers:** The party is moving through central or southern Frontier territory within range of Ashfire patrols, or approaching Cinderhold from any direction.

**What happens:**

Ragash's hounds split without being commanded — Dusk holds the road, while the other two fan wide in opposite directions. Ragash watches them with an expression that shifts from attention to something more focused.

> "Wait here."

She follows Dusk for thirty seconds, then moves to check one of the others. When she returns, she's taking longer to formulate what she found.

> "Ashfire. But it's not a raiding party."
>
> "They're not moving together. Multiple groups, spread out, covering ground deliberately. This is a patrol pattern — someone thought this through."

**What the party gains:**

- **Structural intelligence:** The trail configuration tells Ragash that the party is inside a coordinated Ashfire patrol network, not near a single camp. This is qualitatively different from Stonefang or Mossroot encounters — it means the Ashfire know this territory systematically and are likely aware of the party's general position, even if not their exact location.
- **Tactical options:** The party can attempt to move between patrol paths using Ragash's hound-tracking to identify the gaps, or they can choose to let themselves be found — a deliberate approach rather than a stumbled-upon one, which changes the nature of any contact that follows.
- **Diplomatic framing:** If the party intends to seek contact with Warchief Skarra, being found by a patrol versus presenting themselves deliberately are very different first impressions. Skarra respects capability and intent. A party that navigated her patrol network and then chose to present themselves signals something different than a party that blundered through.

**The inter-species dynamic:**

This is the event that most directly engages with the Ragash/goblin inter-species material established in the goblin tribes document. After she identifies the pattern:

> "The Ashfire — their warchief — she runs her tribe the way someone runs a state, not a camp."

If a companion or the player asks what that means for them:

> "It means she notices things. That we're orc and human traveling together, that I'm running hounds in her territory — she'll have a read on that before anyone says a word."

She is not hopeful or pessimistic about this. She is accurate. Skarra's awareness of Ragash's situation — an outsider operating outside her people's expectations — is documented. Whether that awareness creates an opening depends on what happens next. Ragash is naming the variable, not predicting the outcome.

**Relationship note:** Ragash does not frame this as her and the goblins having common cause. She frames it as Skarra being someone who pays attention to the same kinds of things Ragash pays attention to — which is its own form of recognition, careful and precise.
