## Hero Summary

Ragash is an Orc Houndmaster who found belonging in the one place her people never thought to look: with animals. Her hounds did not care what clan she came from, whether she fit the mold, or whether she proved herself by their standards. They came when she called. They stayed. She has built her entire identity around deserving that loyalty — and quietly, carefully, she is beginning to extend a version of it to people too.

She is blunt to the point of seeming cold. She is warm in ways that take time to see. She is proud enough to make everything harder than it needs to be, and honest enough that you never wonder where you stand with her.

---

## Core Identity

> Ragash is not a fighter because she is aggressive.
> She fights because the things she loves — her hounds, her chosen companions — are worth protecting, and she will not rely on anyone else to do it.
> Everything about her gameplay should express this: her power comes from what she has built and who she has committed to.

---

## Core Wound

**Rejected by her own people; found belonging with her hounds instead of other people.**

Ragash was part of an orc clan that had clear expectations of what she should be. She did not fit those expectations — not because she was weak, but because she was different in ways her people did not know how to value. The rejection was not dramatic. It was accumulated. A series of moments in which she understood, clearly, that she was on the outside of something she would never be invited inside.

She did not grieve it openly. She found something better.

Her hounds gave her unconditional belonging for the first time. She has organized her life around being worthy of it ever since. People, in general, have remained at a careful distance — valued when proven, trusted slowly, never taken for granted.

---

## Core Belief

> "You earn belonging. It is not given."

This applies to everything: her place in any group, the loyalty of her hounds, the respect of companions. She does not believe in unconditional acceptance from people — she believes in the kind that is built, tested, and demonstrated. She is suspicious of anyone who offers trust easily, because trust that costs nothing is worth nothing.

---

## Greatest Fear

Losing one of her hounds.

Not in abstract. The specific loss of an animal she raised, trained, and made promises to. Her hounds receive the openness she withholds from people — they are the place where she is not armored. Losing one of them would reach her in a way that nothing else can.

---

## Driving Goal

Find the rare mythical breed of hound she has heard legends of — a creature said to be nearly extinct, whose existence she cannot confirm and cannot stop thinking about.

Prove, on her own terms, that her way of doing things is worth something.

She is not trying to go home. She left. She is building something new.

## Personality

### Public Personality
- **Blunt** — says exactly what she means; no softening; no preamble
- **Proud** — does not apologize for who she is or how she operates
- **Self-contained** — does not need validation; visibly uncomfortable when asked to explain herself emotionally
- **Reliable** — shows up; does what she says she will do; no drama

### Private Personality
- **Deeply loyal** — once given, her commitment is absolute and does not waver
- **Observant of people** — pays more attention than she lets on; reads rooms well while appearing to ignore them
- **Gentle with her hounds** — a completely different register; patient, attentive, quietly devoted
- **Dry sense of humor** — surfaces rarely, without announcement, and usually lands perfectly

### The Contrast

Ragash's public face reads as indifference. It is not indifference — it is the careful management of how much she shows, because she has learned that showing too much is how you end up hurt. The person who emerges slowly is warmer, funnier, and more perceptive than most people expect. That gap is the character.

---

## Moral Compass

Ragash's priorities:
1. **Her hounds** — always; they come first
2. **Companions who have earned it** — absolute once given
3. **Herself and her own code** — she has a strong personal ethic that does not bend to authority
4. **Institutions and groups** — respected if they prove worthy; dismissed if they don't

She will not follow orders from someone who has not earned her respect, regardless of their rank. She will not abandon a companion for any institutional reason. She respects results and honesty; she has no patience for politics, performance, or pretense.

## Visual Identity

### Appearance
Built for endurance. Orc physique — strong, durable. She moves with the unhurried confidence of someone who is not trying to impress anyone. Scars from old hunts and older conflicts.

### Equipment
Longsword as primary weapon. Practical armor — heavy enough to take damage, not so heavy it slows her down. Nothing decorative. Everything functional.

### Recognizable Personal Detail
Her hounds. Wherever Ragash is, her hounds are nearby or recently departed. They are not just a combat mechanic — they are her companions, raised from pups, individually known to her. The way she interacts with them — quiet commands, familiar gestures, a different kind of attention — tells you everything about who she is.

### Silhouette
Grounded and solid. Not towering like Talos, but unmistakably capable. The hounds at or near her position are part of the silhouette. She should be recognizable as a unit with them.

## Combat Identity

Ragash is the party's **coordinated force** — a fighter whose effectiveness multiplies when her hounds are deployed correctly alongside her.

She is not a simple damage dealer. She is a tactician who has developed a precise working relationship with animals that respond to her as extensions of her own intent. Positioning matters. The hounds are not pets in combat — they are partners.

> Playing Ragash well means thinking about where the hounds are, not just where she is.

## Signature Ability — Hound Summon

Ragash calls her trained hounds into the battle.

**What it does:**
- Deploys one or more hounds to the battlefield
- Hounds act as independent units (attack, flank, intercept)
- Ragash and her hounds work as a coordinated system

**Design intent:** The Hound Summon should feel like Ragash calling on a relationship, not activating a tool. The hounds should feel like characters — specific, responsive, hers. Players should feel the weight of deploying them into danger.

## Personal Quest

### Theme

Ragash's personal quest is about the search for the rare mythical hound breed — and what that search reveals about what she is actually looking for. On the surface it is a hunter's quest. Underneath it is a question about belonging: whether she can find a place in the world that is entirely her own, built from what she values rather than what she was told to value.

### Player Influence

**Resolution A — Found**
The rare breed exists. She finds it, or finds proof of it, or makes contact with it. Whether she can bond with one, and what that means for her, is a moment of genuine arrival — she built something no one believed she could.

**Resolution B — The Hunt Is the Point**
The rare breed may not be findable. What Ragash discovers is that the search itself has shaped her: the places she went, the companions she trusted, the version of herself she built while looking. The destination mattered less than she thought.

**Resolution C — Belonging Is Already Here**
Through the events of the campaign, Ragash realizes that she has already built the belonging she was looking for — in her hounds, in her companions, in the party she joined for reasons she couldn't have articulated at the start. The mythical breed remains out there. She knows where to look next. But she is not alone in looking.

### Emotional Core

Ragash's quest is about whether someone who was told she did not belong can build belonging on her own terms. The answer should feel like hers — not given to her.

## Voice & Dialogue

### Speaking Style
Short sentences. Direct. Says what needs to be said and stops. Does not fill silence.

### Tone
Even. Rarely raised. More impact in quiet than in volume.

### Vocabulary
Plain. No decoration. The most efficient word for the meaning.

### Humor
Rare, dry, and precisely timed. The people who catch it value it more because of how rarely it appears.

### Emotional Tendencies
Does not display emotion in ways that invite response. Anger is controlled; grief is private; satisfaction is a nod. Those who know her well learn to read the small signals.

### What She Does Not Say
She does not talk about her clan unless asked, and even then says little. She does not explain why she trusts someone or why she has withdrawn trust — she simply acts on it. She does not ask for help. She finds a way or does without.

## Gameplay Identity

> **"Ragash is the reason the party always has eyes everywhere."**

Her hounds extend her reach beyond her own tile. The party fights differently with Ragash because the battlefield has more players on it.
