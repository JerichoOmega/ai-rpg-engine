# The Hidden Pack

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — 2026-07-31.  
> **Authority:** This document defines the canonical identity, named members, internal factions, daily life, and encounter structure for the Hidden Pack — the nomadic werewolf community encountered during Ronan's personal quest. All future content involving the Hidden Pack must draw from or extend this document. The pack's existence is not common knowledge; they have survived by remaining unknown.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/heroes/RONAN.md`](../heroes/RONAN.md) · [`docs/heroes/RAGASH.md`](../heroes/RAGASH.md) · [`docs/heroes/TALOS.md`](../heroes/TALOS.md)

---

## Overview

The Hidden Pack is not a kingdom. It is not an organization. It is a small community of people living with the same condition who found each other and decided that surviving together was better than surviving alone.

There are currently eleven adults in the pack and one child born into it. Some were turned; some were born into it. Some have lived this way for decades; some arrived within the last few years. They move constantly through remote regions — the deep Frontier, the forest margins, the edges of the Iron Peaks and the Frozen Highlands. They do not stay in the same camp sites twice in the same season. Very few people outside the pack know they exist, which is exactly how they intend to remain.

What the pack is not: a cure, an answer, or a single community with a unified position on what their condition means. It contains people who have mastered the transformation and people who still struggle every day. It contains people who have made peace with their dual nature and people who never have. It contains, most relevantly for Ronan, a genuine argument — conducted with mutual respect, over years, with no resolution — about whether the pack should engage with the wider world or stay hidden forever.

**This is the design point of the Hidden Pack encounter:** it is not the answer to Ronan's question. It is proof that other people have faced the same question and survived making different choices. The encounter's emotional gift to Ronan is belonging — not resolution.

## Internal Factions

The pack's primary internal tension divides along a single axis: **isolation vs. coexistence**.

### The Isolationist Position

*Led by Horath; supported by roughly half the pack*

Position: The hidden life is the only sustainable life. Every attempt to engage with outside communities ends the same way — discovery, fear, and exile at best; violence at worst. The pack's survival over several generations proves that isolation works. Anything else is sentimentality in the face of evidence.

This is not a fearful position. It is an evidential one. The isolationists have accumulated thirty years of experience across multiple communities, and the pattern holds without exception. They are not wrong that this has always been how it ends. Their argument is simply that the sample size is sufficient and the conclusion should be accepted.

The isolationist faction is not hostile to outsiders by default — they are cautious, because caution has kept them alive. A party that arrives with Ronan, that demonstrates genuine respect for the pack's way of life and does not ask for things it has no right to, will be received as guests. Guests are treated well. They are also expected to leave.

### The Coexistence Position

*Led by Lyss; supported by roughly half the pack, minus Tae, who is too young to vote but counts as coexistence-adjacent*

Position: The hidden life is survival without living. The world is changing in ways the pack's isolation policy was not designed to account for. Not everywhere, not uniformly — but the assumption that every community outside would react with violence is no longer as certain as it was twenty years ago. Some communities have complexity. Some individuals are not defined by the fear their institutions carry.

This position is not naive. Lyss knows what happened to Horath. She has had her own encounters that confirmed everything the isolationists argue. Her position is not that coexistence is safe — it is that isolation is a slow extinction, and slow extinction is still extinction. If the pack never tests the world, it never learns whether the world has changed. And the pack is small enough that a single bad season of hunting could end it.

The coexistence faction is not trying to go public. The debate is about whether specific, careful, chosen contact — with vetted individuals or communities — could be possible without the outcomes the isolationists predict. Lyss has not found proof yet. She is still looking.

### How the Faction Tension Reads in Practice

The two positions have been debated for years and both sides know each other's arguments completely. When the party arrives, the argument does not stop — it becomes concrete. The party is a test case for the coexistence position and a risk for the isolationist position, and everyone in the camp knows it.

Writers should not resolve this tension during the encounter. The party's reception and what Ronan chooses to do can influence the debate's momentum — but the pack will still be arguing when the party leaves. That is accurate to who they are.

## Named Members

---

### Sera — Pack Elder

**Age:** Appears mid-forties; older in pack years  
**Origin:** Born into the pack (her mother was turned before her birth; Sera has never known another life)  
**Position:** Neither faction; the center

Sera did not ask to lead the pack. She was the person still standing when several older members died in the same bad winter, eleven years ago. She has led by consensus since then — she calls meetings, she hears arguments, she remembers what was decided and why. She does not cast a deciding vote when the factions are split. She has never had to.

She is the first person the party will speak to. She approaches them in human form, at a distance from the camp, alone. She does not introduce herself immediately. She watches the party for a moment — specifically, she watches how they stand, how they hold their weapons, and where their eyes go — and then she speaks.

She will spend the first conversation assessing the party's intentions without framing it as an assessment. Her questions are specific and practical: how did you find us (she needs to understand the vulnerability), how long have you known Ronan (she is reading the relationship), what do you want here (she asks this plainly, without apology). She is not hostile in any of this. She is simply someone who has kept eleven people alive for a long time by knowing who is and is not safe.

Her engagement with Ronan is different from her engagement with the party. With the party she is measured. With Ronan she is direct in a way that bypasses the usual caution — she recognizes immediately what she is looking at: a werewolf who has been running from himself for years. She has met this person many times in the people the pack has absorbed. She does not say this outright. She says something close to it.

**Her position on the isolation/coexistence debate:** She believes both sides are right about different things. Horath is right that the pattern has been consistent. Lyss is right that the pattern was set in a world that may be changing. She does not have a conclusion. She is waiting for more information. She finds the party's arrival potentially useful for this reason, which she acknowledges to Ronan privately.

**Voice:** Unhurried. She does not fill silence; she has made her peace with it. When she says something difficult — and she says difficult things — she says them as simply as she can. She does not perform anything.

**Talos connection:** Sera is the member Talos eventually respects most. He arrives skeptical of a community of werewolves by definition; he watches Sera manage the internal disagreements, protect her people, absorb the stress of the party's arrival without passing it downward. By the end of the encounter, he identifies her as someone who has done what he has done — held a line, absorbed the cost, protected the people behind her — but with fewer resources and less recognition, for longer than he has. He does not say this to her. She can read it in his behavior. She lets it be what it is.

### Horath — Voice of the Isolationists

**Age:** Appears late forties  
**Origin:** Turned approximately twenty years ago; ran with a human community for fifteen years before the encounter that ended that life  
**Position:** Isolationist faction leader

Horath is not bitter. He arrived at his position the way anyone arrives at a conclusion after evidence accumulates long enough: gradually, then completely. He would not describe himself as someone who gave up on the world. He would describe himself as someone who paid sufficient attention.

What happened: he was turned during a hunting accident in the mountains — the details he does not share, but the turn was not violent, and the creature that turned him was not malicious. He survived. He spent the next fifteen years building a life in a human village, managing the transformation carefully, contributing to the community, being a person there. He was known as reliable and skilled. He had friendships. He saved a child's life during a transformation — he was the only thing between the child and a collapsing structure, and he used the form to do it, and someone saw.

The hunting party assembled within three days. He did not fight them. He ran.

His argument: he is not an outlier. The pack has collected stories from everyone who has been with them more than a few years. The pattern does not vary. Some version of this is what always happens when a werewolf tries to build something in a human community. The sample size is sufficient.

When the party arrives, Horath's initial position is that their presence is a mistake. He is not impolite about this — he expresses it once, clearly, to Sera, and then accepts her decision to receive them. After that he is courteous and largely correct in his assessments. He will not pretend to be warmer than he is.

His engagement with Ronan is specific: he sees in Ronan the version of himself from twenty years ago. Someone still holding onto the possibility that things could be different. He does not try to take that away from Ronan. He does tell Ronan, when asked, exactly what happened. He tells it plainly, without flourish, including the parts that make the community he lost sound worth the loss — because it was. He does not regret the years. He regrets that they ended.

**His position on the coexistence debate:** He respects Lyss as a person. He does not believe she is wrong in principle. He believes she will eventually have the same story he has, and he hopes she does not. He does not argue with her in front of the party. He has already said everything he has to say.

**Voice:** Steady. He has debated this for years and the repetition has not made him impatient; it has made him precise. He finds the right words because he has had to find them many times. He is not cold — he is careful with his certainty because certainty used carelessly becomes cruelty.

**What Ronan hears in Horath:** The future Ronan is afraid of. Not a monster — a person who was careful and still lost everything he built. Horath's story is Ronan's fear made specific. The conversation between them is one of the encounter's most significant moments. Horath does not intend it to be devastating. He is simply telling the truth.

### Lyss — Voice of the Coexistence Advocates

**Age:** Appears late twenties  
**Origin:** Turned approximately six years ago; came to the pack after eighteen months surviving alone  
**Position:** Coexistence faction, most active voice

Lyss arrived in the pack after eighteen months of what she describes as "figuring out how not to die," which covers a period she does not discuss in detail except to say that she made more mistakes than she had time to account for. She was turned during an attack she survived when she probably should not have — something she is quietly matter-of-fact about. She found the pack through a chain of careful contacts she refuses to detail because protecting those contacts is more important than satisfying curiosity.

She has a brother named Coran in a city she has not seen in six years. She sends word through three intermediaries when she can. She knows he is alive. She does not know what he thinks happened to her.

Her position is not optimistic — it is specific. She is not arguing that the world is safe for people like them. She is arguing that the pack's isolation policy was set in a world that was less differentiated than it is now, and that the policy should be reevaluated with current evidence rather than evidence from twenty years ago. She has identified three communities in the Frontier that, based on everything she has been able to learn from a distance, might be worth an extremely careful approach. She has not made the approach. Horath's counterarguments are serious. She needs better evidence before she is willing to risk the pack on her read.

The party's arrival is useful to Lyss in a specific way: they are the first outsiders who have not reacted to the pack's existence with fear or hostility. She does not overinterpret this. She notes it. She will ask the party specific questions about the communities they have moved through — how people reacted to unusual information, what the capacity for nuance looked like in different regions. She is gathering data. She is transparent about this.

**Her relationship with Ragash:** Immediate and mutual. Lyss is direct in the same register Ragash is direct, and Ragash's presence with her hounds gives Lyss something concrete to engage with. She is genuinely interested in the hounds — not romantically or sentimentally, but practically: how does someone develop that kind of relationship with animals? What does it cost? What does it provide? These are not abstract questions for Lyss; they are questions she has been asking about herself and her own dual nature. The conversation that develops between Lyss and Ragash becomes one of the encounter's unexpected friendships. Ragash recognizes in Lyss someone who is building belonging rather than waiting for it to arrive, which is exactly what Ragash has been doing for years.

**Voice:** Direct. Occasionally impatient — not with people, but with the pace at which evidence accumulates and conclusions are allowed to shift. She is warm underneath the directness in ways that become visible quickly if the party is willing to talk to her rather than at her. She asks questions more than she makes statements.

**What Ronan sees in Lyss:** A version of what he might have been if he had found the pack earlier. Someone who has integrated the same condition into a life that is not defined by running from it. She has not resolved anything — she is not resolved — but she is building something, and she has people around her while she does it. The effect on Ronan is complicated: something between hope and grief.

### Davan — Oral Historian

**Age:** Appears mid-forties (turned in his mid-twenties; has been in the pack for about twenty years)  
**Origin:** Turned while traveling; found the pack after two years alone  
**Position:** Coexistence-adjacent, though he frames this as neutral

Davan spent his pre-turn life as a traveling physician — not formally credentialed, but practically trained. He arrived in the pack with medical knowledge that proved immediately useful. Over the following twenty years he has added to that knowledge a comprehensive documentation of what lycanthropy actually does, from the inside, based on two decades of direct observation across a community of eleven people.

He is the pack's oral historian. He maintains no written records — too dangerous — but he has an extraordinary memory and has structured his documentation in a way that can be passed on through conversation. He knows what each member of the pack knows about the transformation, what has worked for them, what hasn't, and why results vary. He knows the patterns. He has hypotheses about what controls the cycle and what makes it unpredictable. He is not a mage and cannot verify his hypotheses against magical theory — he can only observe behavior and note correlations.

Eleanor will find him within the first hour. He will respond to her with cautious interest — he has never met someone who could bridge between his empirical documentation and magical theory, and the possibility that his twenty years of data might make more sense when viewed through that lens is enough to override his caution about talking to outsiders. He will not volunteer information that could endanger the pack (locations of camps, methods the pack uses to stay hidden, anything that could be used by hunters). He will share anything that is strictly about the condition itself.

The conversation between Eleanor and Davan is one of the encounter's quieter significant moments. She learns things about lycanthropy that no scholar has documented. He learns that some of his hypotheses have theoretical backing he did not know about. They will both leave the conversation with more than they arrived with.

**Voice:** Methodical. He speaks the way someone who has spent twenty years organizing information speaks: precisely, in ordered sequence, with appropriate qualification. He is not cold — he is exact. When he talks about pack members he has watched through difficult transformations, there is a careful tenderness that the precision does not hide.

**His relationship with Ronan:** Davan is the person best positioned to tell Ronan what the pack knows about managing the transformation — what has worked, what hasn't, and why. He does this during a private conversation (Sera arranges it). He does not promise anything. He presents what he knows as what it is: observations from limited sample size, over limited time, with significant individual variance. Whether any of it helps Ronan depends on Ronan. He says this clearly. He then shares everything he has.

### Tae — Born into the Pack

**Age:** Seventeen  
**Origin:** Born into the pack; has never lived outside it  
**Position:** Too young to vote; Lyss-adjacent in instinct

Tae is the pack's only member who has no memory of life before the pack, no experience of turning, and no framework for understanding the outside world except through the stories the others tell. She was born to a member who died when Tae was four — Sera has raised her since, in the practical sense.

She is curious about the outside world in a way that makes Horath nervous, Lyss protective, and Sera quietly attentive. She has asked every member of the pack every question they will answer about where they came from and what the world was like before. She has run out of questions. She would like to see it herself.

When the party arrives, Tae's response is not fear or caution. It is interest. She approaches them — not immediately, but faster than Sera would have preferred — and her first questions are specific: not "who are you" or "why are you here" but "what's the largest city you've been to" and "is the sea actually that color."

Her interaction with Talos is the unexpected one. She approaches him without hesitation — she is not afraid of his size or his armor, because she has grown up around people who can become far more physically significant than any armored soldier. She asks him something specific about the world outside. His answer — which will be whatever the AI DM or writer constructs for the moment — should be honest and particular, because Talos does not condescend to anyone who asks genuine questions, and this is as genuine as it gets. The interaction is brief but specific. It is one of the moments where his private self (the person who kneels to talk to children, who has an inexhaustible supply of stories) is visible beneath the careful exterior.

**Voice:** Fast. She speaks quickly when she is interested, which is often. She is not trying to impress anyone — she is simply operating at her natural pace. She listens carefully when she wants information, which makes it more noticeable when she is bored.

**What she represents in the encounter:** The cost of isolation that Horath's faction does not account for. Tae has grown up safe. She has also grown up without the world. The pack's isolation policy has worked — and it has produced a seventeen-year-old who has never seen a city, never made a friend outside of eleven people, and is going to make a decision about that someday. Lyss is aware of this. Horath is aware of this. Neither of them has a clean answer.

## Daily Life

### Movement and Camp

The pack moves on a rolling schedule — never the same camp site twice within six months, never more than a week at any one site during summer, never more than three weeks in winter. Movement is not random; Sera plans routes based on seasonal game patterns, weather, and known threat vectors. They have been doing this long enough that the routes have a settled quality despite their constant change.

Travel: small groups of two or three in human form. Never the full pack together during daylight in open terrain. They communicate across distance using carved markers left at established waypoints — specific patterns the pack has developed over generations, indistinguishable from general trail markers to anyone who does not know what to look for.

### Camp Structure

A Hidden Pack camp is built for rapid departure. This is immediately visible to anyone who knows what to look for: gear is organized rather than scattered, fire is kept lower than comfort would require, every member maintains a sight line to at least two exits. The camp does not look military. It looks like people who have had a long time to practice disappearing and have gotten very good at it.

Members who are in difficult phases of the cycle — newer members, or established members during particularly hard periods — have a specific camp position that keeps them with sight lines and support without making them feel surveilled. The watch rotation is structured around this. Sera does not announce the rotation; everyone simply knows it.

### Transformation Management

Controlled members (Sera, Horath, and two others) can hold through a full lunar cycle without involuntary transformation and can call the form at will. They do not use this capability casually — there is an ethic in the pack about when transformation is appropriate, developed over years and influenced by the particular costs of living in close quarters with people who have the same capacity.

Members still learning have more limited control. The pack has developed a specific support structure for difficult nights: members who are stable stay close, not to prevent anything, but to be present if help is needed. Davan's documentation suggests that involuntary transformation is significantly more likely when someone is alone. The pack's practice addresses this not as a rule but as a standard of care — the same way one would check on someone who is ill.

### First Contact Protocol

The party does not find the camp. Sera finds the party.

When unknown individuals enter the territory the pack is moving through, scouts (Lyss and another member) locate them and report. Sera then makes the decision to engage or avoid. If she decides to engage, she approaches alone, in human form, at a significant distance from the camp. The camp moves to ready during this time without being told to.

The first conversation with outsiders never happens at the camp. It happens in neutral space. Sera determines whether further contact is appropriate before anyone is taken to where the pack actually is.

If Ronan is with the party, this protocol changes in one specific way: Sera still approaches alone, but she moves faster. A werewolf traveling with a group of non-werewolf companions, controlling the transformation, presenting as a member of the party rather than a threat — this is a profile she recognizes. It is the profile of someone who has been managing alone for a long time. She considers it worth moving faster for.

## Encounter Structure — When the Party Arrives

The Hidden Pack encounter does not have a fixed sequence. The following structure describes the range of experiences available, with sequencing determined by player behavior and AI DM judgment.

### Phase 1: Approach and Assessment (Sera)

Sera meets the party outside camp. She runs the assessment described above. If satisfied, she takes the party to the camp — or a holding position near it — and gives them an orientation: who is here, what the rules are, how long they can stay. She does not phrase this as a threat; she phrases it as information that the party needs to make good decisions.

The rules: no documentation of the camp's location; no transmission of the pack's existence to outside parties without Sera's explicit agreement; no weapons drawn within the camp without cause and warning. These are stated plainly. They are not negotiable.

### Phase 2: Individual Contacts (organic, order varies)

Once the party is received, individual contacts develop according to companion behavior and player choices:

- **Ragash** moves toward Lyss within the first hour. The hounds mediate the approach — Lyss is already watching them. The friendship develops from the specific ground of what it means to build a relationship with an animal that most people are afraid of.
- **Eleanor** finds Davan. The conversation takes the form of an information exchange that becomes increasingly mutual. She may not realize how much time has passed.
- **Talos** does not seek anyone out. He positions himself where he can watch the camp and remains there. Tae finds him, because Tae finds everyone. The interaction is brief and specific, and it lands.
- **Ronan** sits with Sera first. Then Horath finds him, at Sera's arrangement. Then the private conversation with Davan. The sequencing is deliberate — Sera knows Horath's conversation will be difficult, and she wants Ronan to have the full encounter before he has to carry the weight of it.

### Phase 3: The Factions Surface

At some point during the stay — typically when the party has been there long enough that the pack has opinions about them — Horath and Lyss's debate becomes visible. Not as an argument presented for the party's benefit; as the ongoing conversation it actually is, surfacing in the natural course of the pack's life.

The party may be asked directly, by either Lyss or Horath, for their perspective. Or they may simply be observers. Either way, they leave understanding what the debate is about.

### Phase 4: Departure

The party does not stay long — Sera sets the length of the visit, and she keeps it short enough that it does not compromise the pack's movement schedule. The farewell is not ceremonial. Sera tells Ronan specifically that he is welcome to return; this is not said to the party as a group. It is said to him. This distinction is intentional — she is telling him that she considers him pack-adjacent in a way she does not consider the others.

Lyss gives Ragash something — a small, practical object relevant to the hound work, appropriate to what they discussed. The gesture is casual and direct, which is exactly how Ragash receives it best.

Horath does not say farewell to Ronan specifically. He says, as the party is leaving: *"You know how it ends."* This is not a threat. It is the last thing he has to say.

## The Warden Tradition & *The Lost Howl* (additive expansion — 2026-06)

> **Additive refinement.** Everything above (the isolated survival community, the
> isolation-vs-coexistence debate, Sera and the named members) remains canon and
> is unchanged. This section adds the Pack's purpose toward **their own kind**,
> introduced in Ronan's companion quest
> [`The Lost Howl`](../quests/the_lost_howl.md).

The Hidden Pack is **not merely a tribe hiding from humanity.** Within their hidden
life they keep a **Warden tradition**: a quiet effort to **locate and protect
newly awakened werewolves** before fear, hunters, or isolation destroy them.
**Their mission toward their own is rescue, not conquest** — and it is fully
compatible with staying hidden from the wider world (they extract and shelter
quietly).

- **Highest law:** *"No wolf is left alone."*
- **Philosophy — "Know the beast."** The Pack does not teach members to *suppress*
  the wolf, nor to *surrender* to it. The wolf is **neither curse nor blessing — it
  is part of who they are.** The path to control is **understanding, not fear.**

### Pack Culture & Traditions (Canon v1.2)
Small, memorable customs that make the Warden tradition feel lived-in:

- **First Law — *"No wolf is left alone."*** Their defining principle; every other
  custom flows from it.
- **Philosophy — *"Know the beast."*** They neither suppress nor glorify the wolf;
  understanding brings balance (see above).
- **Greeting.** Members do not ask *"Who are you?"* They ask **"Who walks beside
  you?"** — because to the Pack, **companionship defines identity.**
- **Celebration.** The Hidden Pack **does not howl at the moon.** They howl
  **whenever someone finds their way home** — a returning wolf or a newly rescued
  one. It is their most joyful tradition.
- **Symbol.** Their emblem is **a wolf paw growing into tree roots**, meaning
  **"strength through belonging."**

### Alden Graymane — The Alpha *(major recurring NPC — introduced in The Lost Howl)*
The leader of the Warden/rescue tradition: **calm, compassionate, wise, quietly
exhausted; respected rather than feared.** For decades **Alden Graymane** has found
newly awakened werewolves before the rest of the world does.

**Character (Canon v1.2):** patient, compassionate, calm, quietly exhausted, wise,
and soft-spoken — a protector, never a monster. His bond with Ronan is the heart of
his role: **he never treats Ronan as an outsider. He simply treats him as someone
who has finally come home.**

Preserved lines:
- *"Every wolf deserves a pack."*
- *"Because someone once did the same for me."* (why he keeps risking his life).
