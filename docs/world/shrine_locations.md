# Named Shrine Locations

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — 2026-07-31.  
> **Authority:** This document defines named, fixed shrine locations that the AI DM must place consistently across all playthroughs. Generic unnamed shrines may still appear as procedural content; these named locations are specific and canonical. Any encounter that references a named shrine by location must use these entries.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/encounters/religious_encounters.md`](../encounters/religious_encounters.md) · [`docs/world/religions.md`](religions.md) · [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](WORLD_BIBLE.md)

---

## How to Use This Document

Each named shrine entry includes:
- **Shrine type** — references the encounter definition in `religious_encounters.md` (SC-S1, OW-S2, etc.)
- **Location** — where in the region it sits; specific enough to place consistently
- **Current state** — whether it is maintained, neglected, corrupted, or otherwise
- **Fixed lore or hook** — one piece of information or story content permanently attached to this location

Named shrines should appear in the same location every playthrough. A player who visited the Millford Spring in one session and then describes it to an NPC in another should get a response that treats it as a real, known location.

## Canonical Shrine Design Standard

> **Adopted 2026-07-31. Required for all future shrine creation.** Existing entries in this document satisfy these requirements through their extended narrative format. New entries must include all fields below explicitly.

### Required Template Fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Name** | The shrine's canonical name as used by the local community |
| **Region** | Canonical region name per the World Bible |
| **Dedication** | Divine Chorus tradition (Covenant, Old Ways, Ancestors' Path, Veiled Order) or "Unaffiliated" with era note |
| **Approximate Age** | Use the canonical Four Ages (Age of Awakening, Age of Harmony, Age of Sundering, Age of Restoration) or relative terms ("pre-Empire," "early First Empire"). Do not invent age names. |
| **Current Condition** | One of the five canonical conditions (see below) |
| **Caretaker** | Named individual or group, or "None" |
| **Historical Significance** | What this site meant in the past and why it matters now |
| **Local Traditions** | Active practices, regardless of formal institutional sanction |
| **Quest Hook** | A current unresolved situation the party can engage |
| **World State interaction** | How the shrine responds as the Corruption arc or major story events progress |
| **Journal/Chronicle entry** | What the player records when they first discover and investigate this site |

### Canonical Shrine Conditions

| Condition | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Maintained** | Actively cared for; rituals observed; physically intact |
| **Neglected** | Present and structurally sound; traditions faded; minimal or absent caretaker |
| **Abandoned** | No caretaker; physical deterioration visible; community connection severed |
| **Corrupted** | Corruption influence present — active or dormant; site altered by the Corruption arc |
| **Revitalized** | Previously Abandoned or Corrupted, recovered through player action or story events; condition is a permanent record of player impact on the World State |

> **Persistence rule:** Shrine conditions evolve with the campaign. A shrine the party Revitalizes remains Revitalized in all subsequent sessions. A shrine that reaches Corrupted reflects that in all future visits. Conditions are world-state facts, not resets.

> **Registry rule:** Before creating a new named shrine in any content document, check this file. New named shrines published elsewhere must be added here. This document is the single source of truth for all canonical shrine locations.

## The Frontier

### Covenant Shrines — Common

---

#### **The Waymark of Reaching Sun**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche)  
**Location:** At the junction of the Frontier's two primary trade roads, approximately one day's travel east of Cinderhold. The plinth stands at a crossroads that has existed for three hundred years; the shrine was placed when the Covenant first extended missionary activity this far into the Frontier.  
**State:** Maintained — but barely. The Canon responsible for this circuit stretches the work across a territory too large for one person. The shrine is clean and intact; offerings are cleared every few weeks.  
**Fixed lore:** The base of the plinth bears an older inscription in First Empire script, partially worn down. A scholar with First Empire literacy can read: *"This road was opened in the fourteenth year of the First Council. Let those who travel it carry more than they take."* The Covenant placed their shrine directly over this inscription without removing it. Whether this was intentional or indifferent is a theological question the local Canon finds uncomfortable.

---

#### **The Niche at Ashfall Ridge**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche), Corruption-touched  
**Location:** Carved into a limestone outcrop along the eastern Frontier road between the town of Millhaven and the Stonefang tribal territory. Travelers on this road pass it without stopping — it has a bad reputation that nobody can fully explain.  
**State:** Defaced — Ashen Tribunal marked. The solar disc has been ash-rubbed, and a secondary mark is scratched beneath it: the Tribunal's symbol, recognizable to anyone who has seen it elsewhere. The shrine's stone is faintly warm even in cold weather.  
**Fixed lore:** A Frontier merchant named Aldren Voss passed this shrine six weeks ago and reported to the Millhaven innkeeper that he had seen a cloaked figure at the niche at dawn, kneeling and speaking in a low voice. Aldren did not stop to investigate. He hasn't returned from his next eastward trip. The innkeeper will mention this to players who ask about the shrine. Whether the two facts are connected is worth determining.

---

#### **The Lantern House at Greyveil**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche), incorporated into a waystation  
**Location:** Built into the outer wall of a Frontier waystation called Greyveil — a permanent stone building at the edge of Blackfen that serves as a rest point for travelers crossing the marshland. The niche faces the road; a small sun-disc and oil lamp are visible from fifty feet.  
**State:** Maintained. A lay custodian named **Baret Sinne** (late forties, former soldier, Covenant-faithful without being devout) keeps the station and the shrine. He considers the shrine a practical service, not a theological statement, and will say so plainly.  
**Fixed lore:** The shrine's record book — a tradition Baret maintains without being asked — lists every traveler who stopped and left an offering over the past twelve years. Players who spend time with the book find three entries from a single traveler who signed as "T.V." — six months ago, four months ago, and six weeks ago. Each entry notes the same phrase after the offering: *"Still waiting."* Tessaly Vorn (from the Threshold Circle questline) uses this shrine as a waypoint. The most recent entry is not in the same handwriting.

### Old Ways Shrines — Common

---

#### **Millford Spring**
**Type:** OW-S1 (Tended Spring)  
**Location:** At the base of the Millford escarpment, approximately two hours' walk north of the settlement of Millhaven. The spring feeds a stream that the town uses for its mill. The sacred site predates the mill by centuries.  
**State:** Maintained. A part-time Grove Keeper named **Sanna Tull** tends the spring on the first day of each week. She is not a full practitioner — she learned the maintenance practices from her mother, who learned from hers — but she does the work correctly and the spring is healthy.  
**Fixed lore:** Three generations ago, a Covenant Canon tried to have the spring formally reconsecrated as a Covenant site. The town refused, citing a practical claim: the spring had been maintaining itself since before anyone could remember, and whatever the Covenant thought about it theologically, they weren't the ones keeping it clean. The Canon sent a formal letter to the regional hierarchy. It was never answered. The Millford town council has kept a copy of the letter for thirty years; they are quietly proud of it.

---

#### **The Three-Stone Cairn of Keldmoor Road**
**Type:** OW-S3 (Frontier Cairn)  
**Location:** At a field boundary along the Keldmoor road, roughly midway between two Frontier farming settlements. Three large stones arranged vertically; a fourth laid flat at the base as an offering surface.  
**State:** Maintained — by the Hast family, who farm the adjacent land. Three generations have left weekly offerings without knowing precisely why. Old Ways practitioners recognize the specific proportions as correct.  
**Fixed lore:** The youngest Hast child, a twelve-year-old named **Wren**, has recently begun spending extended time at the cairn. Wren says the stones are talking — not in words but in a kind of knowing: twice this season, Wren has come inside from the cairn and reported something about the weather that proved exactly correct two days later. The family is frightened but Wren is not. A Grove Keeper who hears this will recognize the phenomenon as an early-stage practitioner awakening; such children are rare and require careful attention. The family needs someone to explain what is happening before they do something inadvisable.

---

#### **The Healer's Grove above Ashwatch**
**Type:** OW-S3 (Frontier Cairn), marking an adjacent grove  
**Location:** A traditional Old Ways cairn at the threshold of a stand of very old trees on the hillside above Ashwatch Keep, reachable by a half-hour climb from the keep's eastern gate. The cairn marks entry to the grove; the grove predates the keep by at least two centuries and is the sacred site the cairn was placed to mark.  
**State:** Dormant — the quality that Old Ways practitioners associate with a living sacred grove has diminished in the past two years. The cairn is intact and offerings continue to appear, but the grove beyond it has lost whatever quality made it worth marking.  
**Fixed lore:** During the keep's construction three hundred years ago, the builder — a human commander with Old Ways practice — specifically sited the keep's eastern wall to avoid disturbing the grove. His decision was recorded in the keep's founding documents as an architectural note, with no theological explanation. A Covenant historian who reads the note will find it baffling. A Grove Keeper who reads it will find it deeply moving. The grove and the keep have coexisted without incident for three centuries. Whatever is causing the dormancy began two years ago, not three hundred — its cause is external, not the keep.

---

#### **Dawn's Crossing Shrine**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche)  
**Region:** The Frontier  
**Dedication:** Covenant  
**Approximate Age:** Age of Restoration (placed when Covenant missionary activity first extended this far into the Frontier)  
**Condition:** Maintained  
**Caretaker:** No permanent keeper; maintained collectively by the merchant community — the last traveler to pass before dark resets the oil lamp and clears the offering tray  
**Location:** At the crossing of two Frontier trade roads, one day east of Millhaven. The crossing has been a navigation point for over four hundred years; the Covenant placed the shrine over an earlier carved stone whose inscription is worn beyond reading.  
**Historical Significance:** The Canon who placed this shrine noted in her circuit records that "every merchant in the Frontier passes twice" through this crossing. The embedded earlier marker confirms the site was sacred before the Covenant arrived.  
**Local Traditions:** Merchants departing toward the Capital leave a coin face-down ("I owe something to this road; I will pay it on the way back"). Coins placed face-up mean the traveler is inbound and considers the debt settled. Neither tradition is formally Covenant; both predate the current shrine.  
**Quest Hook:** Three weeks ago, every coin in the tray was removed and replaced with a single carved wooden disc bearing a symbol no one in the area recognizes — not Covenant, Old Ways, or Ancestors' Path. The disc is still there.  
**World State interaction:** During active Corruption pressure in the Frontier, the oil lamp fails to stay lit regardless of wind or fuel level. Covenant clergy attribute this to spiritual interference; the merchant community uses their own lanterns and says nothing.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the crossing's history, the collective maintenance tradition, and the unidentified disc — with a note that the symbol has no match in any tradition they have encountered.

---

#### **The Ember Lantern Shrine**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche)  
**Region:** The Frontier  
**Dedication:** Covenant  
**Approximate Age:** Approximately one hundred and thirty years (continuous burning documented since early Age of Restoration)  
**Condition:** Maintained  
**Caretaker:** A relay of families from the hamlet of Ashwick (population forty), maintaining the lantern on an informal rotation never committed to writing  
**Location:** In a stone recess cut into a hillside along the southern Frontier road, six hours north of Cinderhold.  
**Historical Significance:** Local memory holds the lantern went out once, seventy years ago, for three days — concurrent with a severe sickness outbreak in Ashwick. The Covenant has twice attempted to formally incorporate this site; both times the Ashwick families declined, politely and completely.  
**Local Traditions:** Travelers who stop add oil if they have it, though the local families maintain the supply. Contributing has become inseparable from the act of stopping.  
**Quest Hook:** The lantern went out three days ago. No one touched it. The Ashwick families are frightened and have sent word to anyone who will listen. The local Canon has not responded.  
**World State interaction:** Cross-referencing the lantern's documented outage history against regional Corruption incident records reveals a consistent pattern the local families have not noticed because the records exist in different places.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the continuous-burning tradition, the seventy-year-old outage and its consequences, and the current unexplained extinction — noting the lantern's potential as a passive Corruption indicator.

#### **The Bridgekeeper's Shrine**
**Type:** OW-S3 (Frontier Cairn), bridge-integrated  
**Region:** The Frontier  
**Dedication:** Old Ways (originally); now unaffiliated — incorporated into bridge structure by a keeper who did not know what he was preserving  
**Approximate Age:** Offering stone is pre-Covenant; current bridge integration is three generations old  
**Condition:** Maintained (in function); practitioner continuity severed  
**Caretaker:** **Harren Cole** (fifties; bridgekeeper of the Longspán — the longest permanent bridge in the Frontier, crossing the Keth River south of Millhaven; has held the post twenty-two years)  
**Location:** Set into the bridgekeeper's post at the Longspán. The offering stone is a flat stone from the original Old Ways sacred site at this crossing, reused during the bridge's reconstruction because it was a good flat stone.  
**Historical Significance:** The original Old Ways shrine at this crossing was destroyed in a flood three generations ago. The rebuilding keeper incorporated the offering stone without understanding what he preserved. Harren has kept every accumulated offering in a box for twenty-two years without throwing anything away. He does not know why.  
**Local Traditions:** Anyone crossing the Longspán in dangerous weather leaves something at the shrine. Harren moves offerings to his box rather than clearing them.  
**Quest Hook:** An Old Ways practitioner passing through has identified the offering stone as from a pre-Covenant sacred site and wants access to the accumulated offering box. Harren is uncertain whether this is his decision to make.  
**World State interaction:** Corruption-adjacent events affecting the Keth River express at this site first — water quality, unusual animal behavior, eventually structural problems on the bridge inconsistent with its age and construction.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the preserved offering stone, the accidental continuity of the Old Ways site through Harren's unexplained box-keeping, and the practitioner's current interest in what the box contains.

### Ancient Ruins — Unaffiliated (Pre-Empire)

The following three sites in the eastern Frontier predate the First Empire and do not map to standard shrine encounter types. They are not maintained by any current religious tradition.

---

#### **The Hall of Echoes**
**Type:** Ancient Ruin — Unaffiliated (Pre-Empire)  
**Region:** The Frontier  
**Dedication:** Unknown; cosmological content does not match any documented tradition  
**Approximate Age:** Late First Empire in construction style; mural content suggests documentation of an earlier arrangement  
**Condition:** Abandoned; partially excavated by an unknown party within the last two years  
**Caretaker:** None  
**Location:** In a shallow valley in the eastern Frontier, partially concealed by tree growth. Appears as a rocky hillside until the carved lintels are within range.  
**Historical Significance:** The interior contains murals depicting a cosmological arrangement inconsistent with the current Divine Chorus. The recent excavation is careful and professional — tools match Mages Guild field equipment. One mural section has been cleanly removed from the wall.  
**Local Traditions:** None active. Frontier travelers who find the site tend to leave quickly; it has an unsettling reputation without specific cause.  
**Quest Hook:** The excavation is systematic. Someone knows this site and has been methodically clearing it. The removed mural section is the thread.  
**World State interaction:** Connects to the Forgotten Eighth arc. The murals include a group scene from which one figure appears deliberately excluded — compositionally visible. A divine history scholar will recognize this. One of several sites where the pattern appears before the revelation is named.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the murals, the non-canonical cosmological arrangement, the evidence of professional excavation, and the missing section — with a note that the excluded-figure composition matches nothing in known religious tradition.

---

#### **The Veiled Sanctuary**
**Type:** Ancient Ruin — Unaffiliated (Pre-Empire)  
**Region:** The Frontier  
**Dedication:** Unknown; references a figure outside the canonical Divine Chorus in terminology not found in any current theological document  
**Approximate Age:** Pre-First Empire, or very early First Empire; construction style predates dated First Empire examples  
**Condition:** Intact — preserved in a way that should not be physically possible given its age  
**Caretaker:** None contemporary; evidence of ancient maintenance that stopped abruptly  
**Location:** Accessible through a concealed entrance in a natural rock formation in the deep eastern Frontier. Can be found by a practitioner sensitive to sacred sites, or by following directions in a First Empire document held in the Capital's University Archive — borrowed eleven months ago by Mages Guild researcher Ceyne Aldrath and never returned.  
**Historical Significance:** The only First Empire scholars who know this site exists — three — have been quietly discouraged from publishing. The sanctuary references a figure beyond the canonical Divine Chorus in terminology absent from contemporary theology. This is where Ceyne Aldrath's note at the Scholar's Niche ("I found it. The pattern is real.") was pointing.  
**Local Traditions:** None. The site's existence is unknown to the local population.  
**Quest Hook:** The University Archive document was taken by Ceyne Aldrath, who is now missing. The sanctuary is the destination she reached. Whether she reached it, what she found, and where she went are all open.  
**World State interaction:** Central to the Forgotten Eighth arc. Parties who arrive before the arc's revelation encounter it as a mystery; parties who arrive after find confirmation.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the impossible preservation, the non-canonical theological references, the unfamiliar terminology for an unnamed figure, and the connection to the missing document and Ceyne Aldrath.

---

#### **The Silent Observatory**
**Type:** Ancient Ruin — Unaffiliated (Pre-Empire)  
**Region:** The Frontier  
**Dedication:** Unknown; astronomical in function, not religiously affiliated by any traceable tradition  
**Approximate Age:** Significantly older than the First Empire — astronomical alignments correspond to star positions from several thousand years ago, placing construction before any documented civilization in this region  
**Condition:** Structurally intact; functionally unknown  
**Caretaker:** None  
**Location:** On a high ridge in the northeastern Frontier, accessible only in good weather. Visible from a distance as a series of carved stone rings on the ridgeline.  
**Historical Significance:** The rings align with star positions from several thousand years ago — this cannot have been built by the First Empire. Frontier rangers consistently route around this site without formal prohibition or adequate explanation.  
**Local Traditions:** None active. The avoidance behavior among Frontier rangers is consistent and unexplained.  
**Quest Hook:** A gnomish astronomer from the Capital has been unable to find a guide willing to take the final section of the route for six months. She is camped at the last Frontier waystation.  
**World State interaction:** Connects to the Great Forgetting arc. The site is evidence that the gap in the historical record changes the timeline of civilization itself.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the impossible alignment dates, the implication for the historical timeline, the gnomish astronomer's situation, and the rangers' consistent unexplained avoidance.

## The Great Forest

### Old Ways Shrines — Very Common

---

#### **The Speaking Oak of Old Aranor**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove)  
**Location:** At the heart of the Old Aranor ruins — the remains of the oldest elven settlement in the Great Forest, now partially reclaimed by the forest itself. The oak at the center of the grove is approximately eight hundred years old; practitioners believe it predates the settlement.  
**State:** Active, but troubled. A senior Grove Keeper named **Erith of the Long Branch** maintains the site and spends most of each month here. She has been here for forty years. She is not alarmed easily.  
**Fixed lore:** Over the past six months, the oak has been producing new growth on branches that had been dead for decades — but the new growth has an unusual color, slightly off from the tree's established pattern. Erith has documented this carefully without reaching a conclusion. She will share her notes with anyone she trusts, and she will say plainly: she doesn't know if this is recovery or something else. The local Severance cell has been visiting the grove at night; Erith knows, has not confronted them, and is watching what they do.

---

#### **The Hollow Pool at Mistfen**
**Type:** OW-S1 (Tended Spring), partially corrupted  
**Location:** In the Mistfen region of the Great Forest — a natural pool fed by an underground source, surrounded by ancient willows. The site is named in Old Ways oral tradition as one of the forest's original water-memory locations.  
**State:** Corrupted — the water is clear to the eye but wrong in a way that animals recognize. Birds do not land at the pool's edge. No tracks of drinking animals have been found within fifteen feet of the water in at least one year.  
**Fixed lore:** The last Grove Keeper who regularly maintained this site — a practitioner named Sorell — stopped coming eight months ago without explanation. Local practitioners assumed Sorell had moved on; a Severance member who is also a former student of Sorell's knows that Sorell reported the pool's wrongness in a letter to the grove network seven months ago and was told to document it and continue maintenance. Sorell's response was silence. Sorell's whereabouts are unknown. The pool's condition is not publicly known to the wider grove network because the letter was intercepted by a practitioner now aligned with the Severance.

---

#### **The Thornwall Shrine**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove)  
**Location:** A natural clearing in the deep Great Forest, reached by a path that is not maintained but never entirely disappears. Practitioners find it by following specific landmark trees whose configuration is taught through oral tradition.  
**State:** Active. No permanent keeper; maintained collectively by multiple practitioners who visit seasonally.  
**Fixed lore:** The Thornwall is where the Great Forest's Grove Keepers hold their unscheduled emergency gatherings — the ones that don't appear on any seasonal calendar. Three such gatherings have occurred in the past year. Non-practitioners who find the clearing while the gathering is in progress will be asked to leave; refusing is possible but has significant reputation consequences. Players who are known to and trusted by the Grove Keepers may be invited to attend — or may arrive to find the aftermath: a clearing that has been used recently, a fire that has recently burned, and footprints going in eight different directions.

---

#### **The Rootwall of Hollow Deep**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove), Twisted  
**Location:** Near the Hollow Deep landmark — a section of the Great Forest where the canopy is so dense that daylight barely penetrates. The site is a ring of twelve ancient trees whose root systems have grown above ground and intertwined to form a continuous wall approximately waist-height.  
**State:** Twisted. The trees are physically intact but their growth pattern has changed — the roots, which historically grew outward from the ring, are now growing inward. Practitioners who visit find the enclosed space deeply uncomfortable in ways they cannot fully describe.  
**Fixed lore:** Old Ways oral tradition holds that the Rootwall was created by a practitioner during the Age of Sundering who needed a protected space to preserve something. What was preserved and where it went is not recorded. The inward root growth began approximately three years ago — Corruption-concurrent. An Old Ways practitioner who examines the roots carefully will find, woven into the oldest root structures, a specific pattern of stone and bone markers that is not traditional practice. Someone added these markers in the past few years. They are not Severance notation.

---

#### **The Whispering Grove**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Sacred Grove)  
**Region:** The Great Forest  
**Dedication:** Old Ways  
**Approximate Age:** At least four hundred years of continuous maintenance; the tree itself is estimated to be significantly older  
**Condition:** Maintained  
**Caretaker:** Three practitioners rotating seasonal care; they do not live on-site. The rotation lineage the Mossroot tribe considers ancestral.  
**Location:** In the interior of the Great Forest, approximately two days from the western edge. An ancient hardwood at the center of a natural clearing — the largest tree in the surrounding area. Whether the clearing's circular form is natural or cultivated is unclear.  
**Historical Significance:** Old Ways oral tradition holds this tree among the oldest living sacred sites in the Great Forest — significant not for what was built here but for what grew. The root system interacts with the forest's water table in ways local ecologists find notable.  
**Local Traditions:** Messages intended for the dead are written on cloth strips and tied to the lower branches. They are not removed — practitioners leave them to deteriorate naturally. The oldest intact strip currently on the tree dates by cloth style to the late First Empire.  
**Quest Hook:** A recent strip — tied within the past month, in fresh cloth — is written in a cipher. A scholar will recognize it as one used by a Capital intelligence network that officially dissolved forty years ago. The message is addressed to someone whose name, if decoded, will mean something to the party depending on how far they are in the Capital arc.  
**World State interaction:** The clarity of sacred-site impressions at this grove is a reliable Corruption early-warning indicator — the practitioners tending it have documented that clarity decreases before Corruption effects become visible in the surrounding forest. They have not connected this formally to anything external.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the cloth-strip tradition, the First Empire-era strip, the ciphered message, and the grove practitioners' documented reduction in site clarity — noting the potential as an early-warning system.

---

#### **Moonwater Spring**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Sacred Spring)  
**Region:** The Great Forest  
**Dedication:** Old Ways  
**Approximate Age:** Documented in Old Ways oral tradition beyond the range of recorded history; the spring's source is deeper than surrounding groundwater suggests  
**Condition:** Maintained  
**Caretaker:** **Yssel** (late thirties; has tended this site for twelve years; lives in a structure built into the hillside above the spring — the first caretaker to live on-site rather than visit seasonally, a decision made as Corruption began expressing in the surrounding forest)  
**Location:** In a basin in the western Great Forest, near a seasonal river that runs full only in spring. The spring itself runs year-round.  
**Historical Significance:** Documented as a healing site for as long as local records reach. No scientifically verifiable curative properties have been established — but practitioners and visitors consistently report reduced pain, clearer thinking, and emotional steadiness after time near it. The Mages Guild has visited twice; neither delegation produced a conclusive report.  
**Local Traditions:** Anyone who takes water from the spring leaves something that cost them something — not coin, but something meaningful. What people leave varies: unsent letters, objects from unrecovered losses, one sealed clay pot containing a contract. Yssel does not interfere with offerings. She keeps a record of what arrives.  
**Quest Hook:** Yssel has been treating a Mossroot hunter who arrived three weeks ago with a wound that should not still be open. It is not corrupted — she has checked repeatedly — but it is not healing by any means she can apply. The hunter says only that he was at the wrong place when "something moved through." Yssel wants a second opinion from someone with broader experience.  
**World State interaction:** Corruption events in the western Great Forest express at this spring through water quality degradation, unusual behavior in animals that come to drink, and eventually the spring running dry. A party returning to find Yssel after the spring has dried will find her in a specific state depending on the Corruption arc's progression.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the healing tradition, the offering practice, the non-healing wound and the hunter's account, and Yssel's twelve-year on-site commitment — noting the spring as a potential Corruption barometer.

---

#### **The Fallen Roots Shrine**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Reclaimed Sacred Grove)  
**Region:** The Great Forest  
**Dedication:** Old Ways  
**Approximate Age:** The original waypoint is pre-Covenant; the root-shelter shrine is three generations old  
**Condition:** Neglected — one elderly caretaker maintaining it against time  
**Caretaker:** **Maren** (eighties; sole caretaker for thirty-five years; a practitioner's apprentice who never formally completed her training because her teacher died; tends the site because she promised she would)  
**Location:** On the forest margin between the Great Forest and the southern Frontier, visible from the edge. A large tree fell here three generations ago; the root system, exposed when the tree went down, forms a natural shelter. The shrine is built inside that shelter.  
**Historical Significance:** The fallen tree was a waypoint on an Old Ways route through the southern forest that is no longer actively traveled. The previous caretaker believed the site's sacred quality had moved into the root system when the tree fell and built accordingly. What accumulated inside the shelter over three generations has become an accidental archive of forest-margin journeys — objects left by travelers who rested here and left something they no longer needed.  
**Local Traditions:** Travelers who rest in the root shelter leave things they have been carrying that they no longer need — not as formal offerings, but as unburdening. The practice is hyperlocal and informal; no one outside the immediate area knows it.  
**Quest Hook:** Maren has identified a young person in the nearest Frontier settlement who has the aptitude for this work and has been creating reasons for them to visit the site. She has not told them what she is doing. She is running out of time to be indirect. A party that passes through will encounter this situation at whatever stage it has reached.  
**World State interaction:** If Maren dies without passing the site on, it transitions to Abandoned. If the party facilitated a succession, it transitions to Maintained. The site's condition on subsequent visits is visible and affects the Mossroot tribe's assessment of whether the forest margin is being cared for.  
**Journal/Chronicle entry:** Player records the accidental archive, the unburdening tradition, and Maren's quiet succession effort — with a note that the site's future depends on one decision she has not yet made.

## The Iron Peaks

### Ancestors' Path Shrines — Very Common

---

#### **The Dur Waymarker at Iron Gate Pass**
**Type:** AP-S1 (Clan Waymarker)  
**Location:** At the western entrance to Iron Gate Pass — the primary route through the Iron Peaks from the Frontier. The waymarker is carved into the pass's left wall at eye height for a dwarf: the Dur clan symbol (a hammer over a horizontal chain link) with ancestor-token niches carved below it.  
**State:** Active, but contested. The Dur clan maintains this marker; the Ashval clan disputes that the pass was historically Dur territory, citing a counter-waymarker they erected on the right wall of the pass approximately thirty years ago.  
**Fixed lore:** The two waymarkers directly face each other across a twelve-foot gap. **Location:** At the summit of the primary mountain pass connecting the Frozen Highlands to the Iron Peaks — a stone column approximately two meters tall, carved with the symbols of four Highlands clans. This is the highest point most travelers reach before the descent into the Iron Peaks.

**Location:** A natural depression in the tundra near the settlement of Frostmere — a bowl-shaped hollow where, two centuries ago, a Highlands clan coalition stopped an Ice Peaks expansion by a group of Frozen Highlands raiders. The hollow still bears the physical marks of the confrontation.

## Cross-Region Notes

### Shrine Networks

**The Covenant Pilgrim Road** — The Capital Province shrines (Sunward Gate, Scholar's Niche, and the Radiant Spire itself) form the final leg of a pilgrimage route that begins at the Waymark of Reaching Sun in the Frontier and passes through a waystation near Cinderhold. Players who travel the full route will be recognized as pilgrims in the Capital Province even if they don't identify themselves as such.

**The Iron Peaks Ancestor Route** — The Dur Waymarker at Iron Gate Pass, the Ironhold Memorial, and the Stoneheart Hall Deep Shrine together form a coherent ancestral route used by clans traveling between the Frontier and the deep Peaks. A party that encounters all three will be recognized by Iron Peaks dwarves as having done something that carries informal cultural weight.

**The False Ancestor Pattern** — The Stoneheart Hall Deep Shrine (sealed, corrupted), the Windcatcher Shrine in the Highlands (receiving a consistent signal), and the CS-3 False Ancestor Site encounter type together indicate a coordinated pattern. Players who connect these three will have identified the Unbroken Line's infrastructure before anyone has told them the Unbroken Line exists.
