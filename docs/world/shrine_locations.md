# Named Shrine Locations

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — 2026-07-31.  
> **Authority:** This document defines named, fixed shrine locations that the AI DM must place consistently across all playthroughs. Generic unnamed shrines may still appear as procedural content; these named locations are specific and canonical. Any encounter that references a named shrine by location must use these entries.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/encounters/religious_encounters.md`](../encounters/religious_encounters.md) · [`docs/world/religions.md`](religions.md) · [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](WORLD_BIBLE.md)

---

## How to Use This Document

Each named shrine entry includes:
- **Shrine type** — references the encounter definition in `religious_encounters.md` (SC-S1, OW-S2, etc.)
- **Location** — where in the region it sits; specific enough to place consistently
- **Current state** — whether it is maintained, neglected, corrupted, or otherwise
- **Fixed lore or hook** — one piece of information or story content permanently attached to this location

Named shrines should appear in the same location every playthrough. A player who visited the Millford Spring in one session and then describes it to an NPC in another should get a response that treats it as a real, known location.

## The Frontier

### Covenant Shrines — Common

---

#### **The Waymark of Reaching Sun**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche)  
**Location:** At the junction of the Frontier's two primary trade roads, approximately one day's travel east of Cinderhold. The plinth stands at a crossroads that has existed for three hundred years; the shrine was placed when the Covenant first extended missionary activity this far into the Frontier.  
**State:** Maintained — but barely. The Canon responsible for this circuit stretches the work across a territory too large for one person. The shrine is clean and intact; offerings are cleared every few weeks.  
**Fixed lore:** The base of the plinth bears an older inscription in First Empire script, partially worn down. A scholar with First Empire literacy can read: *"This road was opened in the fourteenth year of the First Council. Let those who travel it carry more than they take."* The Covenant placed their shrine directly over this inscription without removing it. Whether this was intentional or indifferent is a theological question the local Canon finds uncomfortable.

---

#### **The Niche at Ashfall Ridge**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche), Corruption-touched  
**Location:** Carved into a limestone outcrop along the eastern Frontier road between the town of Millhaven and the Stonefang tribal territory. Travelers on this road pass it without stopping — it has a bad reputation that nobody can fully explain.  
**State:** Defaced — Ashen Tribunal marked. The solar disc has been ash-rubbed, and a secondary mark is scratched beneath it: the Tribunal's symbol, recognizable to anyone who has seen it elsewhere. The shrine's stone is faintly warm even in cold weather.  
**Fixed lore:** A Frontier merchant named Aldren Voss passed this shrine six weeks ago and reported to the Millhaven innkeeper that he had seen a cloaked figure at the niche at dawn, kneeling and speaking in a low voice. Aldren did not stop to investigate. He hasn't returned from his next eastward trip. The innkeeper will mention this to players who ask about the shrine. Whether the two facts are connected is worth determining.

---

#### **The Lantern House at Greyveil**
**Type:** SC-S2 (Waystation Shrine)  
**Location:** Attached to an active Frontier waystation called Greyveil — a permanent stone building at the edge of Blackfen that serves as a rest point for travelers crossing the marshland. The shrine occupies a room off the main hall.  
**State:** Maintained. A lay custodian named **Baret Sinne** (late forties, former soldier, Covenant-faithful without being devout) keeps the station and the shrine. He considers the shrine a practical service, not a theological statement, and will say so plainly.  
**Fixed lore:** The shrine's record book — a tradition Baret maintains without being asked — lists every traveler who stopped and left an offering over the past twelve years. Players who spend time with the book find three entries from a single traveler who signed as "T.V." — six months ago, four months ago, and six weeks ago. Each entry notes the same phrase after the offering: *"Still waiting."* Tessaly Vorn (from the Threshold Circle questline) uses this shrine as a waypoint. The most recent entry is not in the same handwriting.

### Old Ways Shrines — Common

---

#### **Millford Spring**
**Type:** OW-S1 (Tended Spring)  
**Location:** At the base of the Millford escarpment, approximately two hours' walk north of the settlement of Millhaven. The spring feeds a stream that the town uses for its mill. The sacred site predates the mill by centuries.  
**State:** Maintained. A part-time Grove Keeper named **Sanna Tull** tends the spring on the first day of each week. She is not a full practitioner — she learned the maintenance practices from her mother, who learned from hers — but she does the work correctly and the spring is healthy.  
**Fixed lore:** Three generations ago, a Covenant Canon tried to have the spring formally reconsecrated as a Covenant site. The town refused, citing a practical claim: the spring had been maintaining itself since before anyone could remember, and whatever the Covenant thought about it theologically, they weren't the ones keeping it clean. The Canon sent a formal letter to the regional hierarchy. It was never answered. The Millford town council has kept a copy of the letter for thirty years; they are quietly proud of it.

---

#### **The Three-Stone Cairn of Keldmoor Road**
**Type:** OW-S3 (Frontier Cairn)  
**Location:** At a field boundary along the Keldmoor road, roughly midway between two Frontier farming settlements. Three large stones arranged vertically; a fourth laid flat at the base as an offering surface.  
**State:** Maintained — by the Hast family, who farm the adjacent land. Three generations have left weekly offerings without knowing precisely why. Old Ways practitioners recognize the specific proportions as correct.  
**Fixed lore:** The youngest Hast child, a twelve-year-old named **Wren**, has recently begun spending extended time at the cairn. Wren says the stones are talking — not in words but in a kind of knowing: twice this season, Wren has come inside from the cairn and reported something about the weather that proved exactly correct two days later. The family is frightened but Wren is not. A Grove Keeper who hears this will recognize the phenomenon as an early-stage practitioner awakening; such children are rare and require careful attention. The family needs someone to explain what is happening before they do something inadvisable.

---

#### **The Healer's Grove above Ashwatch**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove)  
**Location:** A stand of very old trees on the hillside above Ashwatch Keep, reachable by a half-hour climb from the keep's eastern gate. The grove predates the keep by at least two centuries.  
**State:** Dormant — the quality that Old Ways practitioners associate with a living sacred grove has diminished in the past two years. The trees are physically healthy but the sense of presence is gone.  
**Fixed lore:** During the keep's construction three hundred years ago, the builder — a human commander with Old Ways practice — specifically sited the keep's eastern wall to avoid disturbing the grove. His decision was recorded in the keep's founding documents as an architectural note, with no theological explanation. A Covenant historian who reads the note will find it baffling. A Grove Keeper who reads it will find it deeply moving. The grove and the keep have coexisted without incident for three centuries. Whatever is causing the dormancy began two years ago, not three hundred — its cause is external, not the keep.

## The Great Forest

### Old Ways Shrines — Very Common

---

#### **The Speaking Oak of Old Aranor**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove)  
**Location:** At the heart of the Old Aranor ruins — the remains of the oldest elven settlement in the Great Forest, now partially reclaimed by the forest itself. The oak at the center of the grove is approximately eight hundred years old; practitioners believe it predates the settlement.  
**State:** Active, but troubled. A senior Grove Keeper named **Erith of the Long Branch** maintains the site and spends most of each month here. She has been here for forty years. She is not alarmed easily.  
**Fixed lore:** Over the past six months, the oak has been producing new growth on branches that had been dead for decades — but the new growth has an unusual color, slightly off from the tree's established pattern. Erith has documented this carefully without reaching a conclusion. She will share her notes with anyone she trusts, and she will say plainly: she doesn't know if this is recovery or something else. The local Severance cell has been visiting the grove at night; Erith knows, has not confronted them, and is watching what they do.

---

#### **The Hollow Pool at Mistfen**
**Type:** OW-S1 (Tended Spring), partially corrupted  
**Location:** In the Mistfen region of the Great Forest — a natural pool fed by an underground source, surrounded by ancient willows. The site is named in Old Ways oral tradition as one of the forest's original water-memory locations.  
**State:** Corrupted — the water is clear to the eye but wrong in a way that animals recognize. Birds do not land at the pool's edge. No tracks of drinking animals have been found within fifteen feet of the water in at least one year.  
**Fixed lore:** The last Grove Keeper who regularly maintained this site — a practitioner named Sorell — stopped coming eight months ago without explanation. Local practitioners assumed Sorell had moved on; a Severance member who is also a former student of Sorell's knows that Sorell reported the pool's wrongness in a letter to the grove network seven months ago and was told to document it and continue maintenance. Sorell's response was silence. Sorell's whereabouts are unknown. The pool's condition is not publicly known to the wider grove network because the letter was intercepted by a practitioner now aligned with the Severance.

---

#### **The Thornwall Shrine**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove)  
**Location:** A natural clearing in the deep Great Forest, reached by a path that is not maintained but never entirely disappears. Practitioners find it by following specific landmark trees whose configuration is taught through oral tradition.  
**State:** Active. No permanent keeper; maintained collectively by multiple practitioners who visit seasonally.  
**Fixed lore:** The Thornwall is where the Great Forest's Grove Keepers hold their unscheduled emergency gatherings — the ones that don't appear on any seasonal calendar. Three such gatherings have occurred in the past year. Non-practitioners who find the clearing while the gathering is in progress will be asked to leave; refusing is possible but has significant reputation consequences. Players who are known to and trusted by the Grove Keepers may be invited to attend — or may arrive to find the aftermath: a clearing that has been used recently, a fire that has recently burned, and footprints going in eight different directions.

---

#### **The Rootwall of Hollow Deep**
**Type:** OW-S2 (Ancient Grove), Twisted  
**Location:** Near the Hollow Deep landmark — a section of the Great Forest where the canopy is so dense that daylight barely penetrates. The site is a ring of twelve ancient trees whose root systems have grown above ground and intertwined to form a continuous wall approximately waist-height.  
**State:** Twisted. The trees are physically intact but their growth pattern has changed — the roots, which historically grew outward from the ring, are now growing inward. Practitioners who visit find the enclosed space deeply uncomfortable in ways they cannot fully describe.  
**Fixed lore:** Old Ways oral tradition holds that the Rootwall was created by a practitioner during the Age of Sundering who needed a protected space to preserve something. What was preserved and where it went is not recorded. The inward root growth began approximately three years ago — Corruption-concurrent. An Old Ways practitioner who examines the roots carefully will find, woven into the oldest root structures, a specific pattern of stone and bone markers that is not traditional practice. Someone added these markers in the past few years. They are not Severance notation.

## The Iron Peaks

### Ancestors' Path Shrines — Very Common

---

#### **The Dur Waymarker at Iron Gate Pass**
**Type:** AP-S1 (Clan Waymarker)  
**Location:** At the western entrance to Iron Gate Pass — the primary route through the Iron Peaks from the Frontier. The waymarker is carved into the pass's left wall at eye height for a dwarf: the Dur clan symbol (a hammer over a horizontal chain link) with ancestor-token niches carved below it.  
**State:** Active, but contested. The Dur clan maintains this marker; the Ashval clan disputes that the pass was historically Dur territory, citing a counter-waymarker they erected on the right wall of the pass approximately thirty years ago.  
**Fixed lore:** The two waymarkers directly face each other across a twelve-foot gap. Travelers through the pass are technically passing through contested clan territory, and some Ancestors' Path practitioners take this seriously enough to make offerings at both markers. The dispute predates the current clan leadership on both sides; the elders who know the full history are elderly and in disagreement about what the full history actually says. The Dur clan's current senior Ancestor Speaker, **Keldra Dur**, has access to a document from six generations ago that she believes settles the matter. She has not shared it because she is not certain it will be accepted, and the cost of presenting it and being rejected is higher than the cost of the current stalemate.

---

#### **The Ironhold Memorial**
**Type:** AP-S2 (Battle Memorial)  
**Location:** A wide ledge carved into the cliff face above the approach to Iron Delve — a position that looks down on the mine entrance and the settlement surrounding it. The memorial wall records the names of the dead from a mining collapse two centuries ago, the largest single-day death toll in Iron Peaks history.  
**State:** Maintained — by a rotating delegation from seven clans, since the collapse killed members of all seven. The maintenance agreement is one of the oldest continuous cross-clan cooperative arrangements in the Peaks.  
**Fixed lore:** The memorial wall has one name carved at the bottom, separate from the others: **Vorath No-Clan**, a dwarf who died pulling survivors out of the collapse, whose family affiliation could not be determined at the time. The seven clans have never agreed on whether to integrate this name into one of their clan rolls or leave it separate. Some clan elders consider the separate listing an honor — a name that belongs to all of them. Others consider it an unresolved ancestral obligation and a spiritual problem. Players who investigate will find that recent genealogical work by a Gnomish archivist in Iron Delve has produced a probable family identification for Vorath — information that has been sitting on a shelf for four years because no one is sure who to give it to.

---

#### **The Deep Shrine of the Stoneheart Hall**
**Type:** AP-S3 (Deep Shrine)  
**Location:** Accessible through Stoneheart Hall — a large underground chamber two hours' descent into the Iron Peaks below the settlement of Kethwald. The shrine itself is in a secondary chamber off the main hall, sealed behind a door that requires clan credentials to open.  
**State:** Sealed — recently, and with urgency. The attendant, **Keeper Maret Ashval**, sealed the shrine three weeks ago after an ancestral contact session produced responses she describes as "present, but wrong." She has sent messages to six other senior Ancestor Speakers asking for guidance. Two have responded with similar reports from their own sites. Four have not responded.  
**Fixed lore:** The Stoneheart Hall Deep Shrine is one of only three shrines in the Iron Peaks where ancestral contact has been reliable enough for clan governance decisions over multiple centuries. Its closure has produced a political crisis: the Ashval clan's elder council has a succession dispute that requires ancestral consultation to resolve legitimately. Without access to the shrine, both claimants are governing simultaneously and the clan is in a slow-motion split. Keeper Maret will open the shrine to players who can demonstrate both the relevant clan credentials and an understanding of why she sealed it. She will not open it to people who simply need it for political reasons — she has already been approached by both succession claimants and refused them both.

## The Frozen Highlands

### Ancestors' Path Shrines — Common

---

#### **The Waystone of the First Crossing**
**Type:** AP-S1 (Clan Waymarker)  
**Location:** At the summit of the primary mountain pass connecting the Frozen Highlands to the Iron Peaks — a stone column approximately two meters tall, carved with the symbols of four Highlands clans. This is the highest point most travelers reach before the descent into the Iron Peaks.  
**State:** Active. No permanent keeper; the four clans take seasonal responsibility. Current season's maintainer is the Greywall clan, whose mark is freshly re-cut.  
**Fixed lore:** The waystone's oldest carvings predate the four-clan system; beneath the clan symbols, if the accumulated offerings are cleared from the base, is a set of markings in a script that living Ancestors' Path practitioners cannot read. A Veiled Order archivist can identify it as a dialect of early First Age notation — not dwarven in origin, but human, which raises the question of which humans were in the Highlands during the First Age and why they placed a marker here. The mountain pass itself may be considerably older than anyone currently understands.

---

#### **The Battle-Hollow of Frostmere**
**Type:** AP-S2 (Battle Memorial)  
**Location:** A natural depression in the tundra near the settlement of Frostmere — a bowl-shaped hollow where, two centuries ago, a Highlands clan coalition stopped an Ice Peaks expansion by a group of Frozen Highlands raiders. The hollow still bears the physical marks of the confrontation.  
**State:** Neglected — the two primary clans who should maintain it, the Ironmaw and the Deepsnow, have been in a territorial dispute for eleven years and have let the joint maintenance lapse rather than acknowledge each other's ancestral claims to the site.  
**Fixed lore:** The Battle-Hollow is where the Ironmaw and Deepsnow clans' shared ancestors are buried. Both clans' elders know this. Neither will say it publicly because acknowledging shared burial ground in the Highlands tradition creates a permanent kinship obligation that neither clan currently wants. A Highlands scholar named **Seln Deepsnow** — a dissenting voice within his own clan — has been trying to broker a restoration agreement for three years. He will pay for help but his clan has made clear that if the agreement is formally attributed to him, he will face consequences for acting without elder authority.

---

#### **The Windcatcher Shrine**
**Type:** AP-S1 (Clan Waymarker), modified  
**Location:** On a ridge above the Crystal Icefields — a location known for consistent, unusual wind patterns that Highlands practitioners have interpreted as an ancestral communication channel for at least four generations. The "shrine" is a framework of carved bones and cord that functions as a sound instrument in the wind.  
**State:** Active, unusual. The wind instrument is maintained by a single practitioner named **Harra** (no clan affiliation given; she considers the site to belong to all Highlands clans) who has lived near the ridge for twenty years.  
**Fixed lore:** Harra has been documenting the sounds the windcatcher produces — not randomly, but in patterns she believes are structured. Over twenty years she has identified what she calls a grammar: specific sequences that correlate with specific subsequent weather events. She does not claim this as Ancestors' Path practice; she claims it as observation. An Ancestors' Path practitioner who reviews her documentation will find that the sequences also correlate with specific ancestral communications recorded in other locations — which means either the ancestors are coordinating across sites, or something else is producing a consistent signal that multiple traditions are independently receiving.

## Sol Kareth

### Covenant Shrines — Common

---

#### **The First Oasis Shrine**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche) over SC-S3 (Buried Solar Temple)  
**Location:** At the well outside Kareth's oldest permanent settlement — a location that has been sacred to desert sun-veneration for at least five centuries before the Covenant claimed theological kinship with it. The Covenant's shrine is a formal plinth placed next to the well; below the sand, the original pre-Covenant temple structure extends fifteen meters downward.  
**State:** Active (surface) / Undiscovered (below). The Covenant shrine above is maintained by a lay custodian employed by the local merchant council, who view the maintenance as a commercial investment in traveler comfort.  
**Fixed lore:** Locals know the well is old. Most do not know how old. A Sol Kareth antiquities dealer in the nearest market city has a fragment of carved stone that matches the iconography visible at the well's base — and the fragment came from a tomb three kilometers away. Whatever was built here was not a single structure; it was part of a connected system. The Covenant knows the First Oasis site is old and has quietly suppressed two scholarly investigations into exactly how old. A Covenant Scholar currently stationed at the surface shrine has instructions to report any visitors who ask too many questions about the well's construction. She finds this instruction deeply uncomfortable and will tell the right player exactly what she was told to do.

---

#### **The Merchant's Altar of the Golden Road**
**Type:** SC-S2 (Waystation Shrine), disputed purpose  
**Location:** A roadside waystation at the midpoint of the Golden Road — Sol Kareth's primary trade route. The altar is large, formal, and well-maintained; a dedicated Covenant attendant lives at the waystation.  
**State:** Active, commercially entangled. The waystation's operator — a merchant consortium — has developed a system of tiered offerings that correlate with tiered services: larger offerings receive better room allocations, preferential access to the well, advance market information from the attendant's contacts. The attendant knows this has become a commercial mechanism and is genuinely uncertain whether it violates Covenant theology or is simply a natural extension of how civilization works.  
**Fixed lore:** Three months ago, a Covenant Reformist cleric named **Idara Sel** stopped at this waystation during a pilgrimage and spent six hours documenting the offering-tier system. She wrote a full report and sent it to the Covenant hierarchy. The report was received, acknowledged, and filed. Idara is now on her way back to this location with a copy of the report and the intention to post it publicly outside the waystation. The attendant has been warned she is coming. The merchant consortium has also been warned. Neither party has a good plan for what to do when she arrives.

---

#### **The Sandkeeper's Niche at the Scorpion Dunes**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche)  
**Location:** Carved into the face of a large sandstone formation at the entrance to the Scorpion Dunes crossing — a dangerous route that experienced traders take and inexperienced ones avoid. The carving shows a sun disc in early Covenant style; a secondary carving below it, in pre-Covenant desert notation, shows a scorpion in a protective posture.  
**State:** Maintained — by the Dunes guides, a hereditary guild of desert navigators who are not Covenant faithful but maintain the shrine because travelers who see it in good condition are calmer and calmer travelers make the crossing safer. The guides leave their own offerings below the Covenant symbol.  
**Fixed lore:** The Dunes guides have oral tradition about this niche that predates the Covenant inscription by generations. Their version: the site marks where a desert navigator discovered a water source during a period of severe drought, shared it with five competing merchant caravans who would otherwise have died, and asked nothing in return. The Covenant's version: a missionary placed the sun niche here to protect travelers under the Radiant One's blessing. Both could be true. Whether either is true is less important than the fact that the two versions have coexisted peacefully at this niche for two hundred years and both communities are quietly proud of it.

## The Capital Province

### Covenant Shrines — Very Common

---

#### **The Hearthside Plinth of Coppergate**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche)  
**Location:** Set into the wall of a building at the corner of Coppergate Lane in the Capital's working craftsmen district — at the height of a workbench so a smith or cobbler can see it from their door.  
**State:** Maintained, intensely. The shrine has been adopted by the neighborhood as a collective project. Every morning, the occupant of the nearest house puts out a candle. On holy days, the lane closes to traffic for a block so the community can observe the Dawn Rites together.  
**Fixed lore:** The neighborhood's relationship with this shrine is not institutional Covenant faith — it is something older and more local. The plinth was placed here ninety years ago by a woman named **Mira Copp**, who is remembered in the neighborhood as a healer who asked no payment and is not remembered in any Covenant record. The current neighborhood elder has Mira Copp's personal journal, which includes a theological argument — written in untrained but clear language — that the Covenant's institutional hierarchy is spiritually unnecessary and possibly harmful. The elder has never shown this to any Covenant clergy and does not intend to.

---

#### **The Sunward Gate Archive Shrine**
**Type:** SC-S2 (Waystation Shrine), institutional  
**Location:** Built into the base of the Sunward Gate — the formal entrance arch to the Capital's noble quarter. The shrine chamber is accessible from a side door at street level; the gate above is ceremonial.  
**State:** Maintained by permanent Covenant staff. The shrine is where Solar Investiture is performed for officials too minor to merit the Radiant Spire but too significant to be handled by a traveling Blessing Merchant.  
**Fixed lore:** The shrine's record books go back two hundred and forty years and contain the Solar Investiture records of every official who has held appointment in the noble quarter. A researcher with access to these records and knowledge of Capital political history would find three gaps — periods where investiture records simply do not exist, corresponding to three periods of political crisis. Covenant archivists know about the gaps and attribute them to administrative failure. A political historian would recognize that all three gaps correspond exactly to periods when the Covenant hierarchy was internally divided on whether to support the sitting government. The gaps are not administrative failures. They are decisions.

---

#### **The Scholar's Niche at the University Quarter**
**Type:** SC-S1 (Roadside Sun-Niche), complicated  
**Location:** In an alcove in the outer wall of the Capital's primary university building. Formally Covenant; practically adopted by the university community with minimal theological engagement.  
**State:** Maintained, in a way. The university groundskeeper keeps it clean. Students leave notes in the offering tray alongside coins — questions about exams, anxieties about future careers, occasionally something genuinely searching. The Covenant cleric assigned to the university ignores the notes.  
**Fixed lore:** One of the current notes, folded very small and tucked behind the offering tray, is not from a student. It is from a Mages Guild researcher named **Ceyne Aldrath** who has been at the university for eight months studying Corruption-adjacent magical phenomena. The note reads: *"I found it. The pattern is real. I don't know who to tell."* Ceyne has not returned to retrieve the note. Her colleagues believe she is conducting field research. She has been missing for eleven days.

### Veiled Order Sites — Common

---

#### **The House on Coppergate Lane**
**Type:** VO-S1 (Order House)  
**Location:** On Coppergate Lane in the craftsmen's district — four buildings down from the Hearthside Plinth, on the same street. The building's small circle-and-line symbol above the door is the only external marker.  
**State:** Active. Two Order members are in permanent residence: **Archivist Sorel Demm** (sixties, has been here for thirty years) and a junior member named **Vel** (twenties, recently posted here, still adjusting to how slow the work is).  
**Fixed lore:** Sorel Demm has been conducting a Vigil for the past eleven years for a patient who has not died. The patient — an elderly woman named **Cath** — is in a terminal condition that should have resolved years ago. Cath is not in distress; she is present and aware and periodically says things that Sorel records but does not understand. The medical community has no explanation. Sorel has a hypothesis but considers it too significant to commit to until Cath either dies or says something that confirms it. Cath has told Sorel that she is waiting for something. She has not said what. Sorel believes the Order should know about this and has been unable to explain why she hasn't told them.

---

#### **The Thin Place at King's Meadow**
**Type:** VO-S2 (Thin Place)  
**Location:** At the edge of King's Meadow — the Capital's formal public park — specifically at the northeastern corner where an old oak tree stands alone inside a decorative iron fence. The fence was placed to protect the tree from public damage, but the location was chosen for architectural symmetry, not because anyone knew the tree was a thin-place marker.  
**State:** Monitored. The Order member who has been tracking this site — a practitioner named **Ellin Caus** — has been coming here early morning for six years. She has a permit that describes her as a "botanical researcher."  
**Fixed lore:** The thin-place quality at this site has been strengthening gradually over the six years Ellin has been monitoring it. The acceleration in the past eight months correlates with known Corruption developments in the Capital Province but not in a way that Ellin can explain mechanically. She has identified one specific observation that she considers significant but has not yet reported to her Order superiors: on three occasions, the impressions she perceives at this site have included something that feels like a deliberate message — structured, not ambient — and the message is the same each time. She cannot tell if it is coming from the past or the future, and she cannot determine who is sending it.

---

#### **The Unmarked Archive in the University Basement**
**Type:** VO-S3 (Unmarked Archive)  
**Location:** In an unmarked room in the sub-basement of the Capital's primary university library — accessible through a door labeled "Disused Storage" that requires an Order-level pass-key or a successful Investigation check to open.  
**State:** Intact, but one section has been disturbed. The disturbance is recent — within the past two months. Several specific records have been removed; the gaps are visible because the archive's cataloguing system (created by an Order archivist seventy years ago) uses a cross-reference system that marks when items have been consulted or removed.  
**Fixed lore:** The archive contains records from the period immediately before and after the First Empire's fall — the exact period that is most poorly understood by contemporary scholars. What was taken from the disturbed section: documents related to a specific event the First Empire's own records call "the Accounting." The Order has no duplicate copies of these specific documents. The Mages Guild researcher Ceyne Aldrath (who has been missing for eleven days) was working in the university's above-ground archives on a project that required background knowledge of the Accounting period. Whether these facts are connected, and who took the documents, is a thread that goes somewhere significant.

## Cross-Region Notes

### Shrine Networks

**The Covenant Pilgrim Road** — The Capital Province shrines (Sunward Gate, Scholar's Niche, and the Radiant Spire itself) form the final leg of a pilgrimage route that begins at the Waymark of Reaching Sun in the Frontier and passes through a waystation near Cinderhold. Players who travel the full route will be recognized as pilgrims in the Capital Province even if they don't identify themselves as such.

**The Iron Peaks Ancestor Route** — The Dur Waymarker at Iron Gate Pass, the Ironhold Memorial, and the Stoneheart Hall Deep Shrine together form a coherent ancestral route used by clans traveling between the Frontier and the deep Peaks. A party that encounters all three will be recognized by Iron Peaks dwarves as having done something that carries informal cultural weight.

**The False Ancestor Pattern** — The Stoneheart Hall Deep Shrine (sealed, corrupted), the Windcatcher Shrine in the Highlands (receiving a consistent signal), and the CS-3 False Ancestor Site encounter type together indicate a coordinated pattern. Players who connect these three will have identified the Unbroken Line's infrastructure before anyone has told them the Unbroken Line exists.
