# Cults of Elyndor

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]  
> **Authority:** This document defines the named cults players will encounter — their origins, leadership, ideology, territory, and relationship to the imprisoned god's whispers. All quest and encounter content involving cult activity must reference these specific groups rather than generic "cult" placeholders.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/world/religions.md`](religions.md) · [`elyndor/history/the_corruption.md`](../../elyndor/history/the_corruption.md) · [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](WORLD_BIBLE.md)

---

## Overview

None of these cults began as cults.

Each emerged from within a legitimate religious tradition — starting with a sincere practitioner, a genuine crisis, and a revelation that felt real. The imprisoned god does not manufacture faith from nothing. It finds what is already there — a zealot's conviction, a protector's fury, a scholar's hunger — and amplifies it past the point of reason.

The three named cults below represent different stages of radicalization, different patterns of corruption, and different relationships between leadership and the ordinary members caught up in them. They are not interchangeable. Writers should choose specific cults rather than referring to unnamed "cult activity."

**Important design note:** Cult reputation does not transfer to the parent religious order. Assisting or opposing a cult affects only cult standing — not the player's relationship with the Solari Covenant, the Old Ways, or the Veiled Order.

## The Ashen Tribunal

### Origin

**Parent religion:** The Solari Covenant  
**Radicalization level:** High — leadership has fully crossed the line; rank-and-file believe they are doing holy work

The Ashen Tribunal emerged from the Covenant's Inquisitor faction — the group described in [`docs/world/religions.md`](religions.md) as having "concluded that rooting out Corruption requires extraordinary measures." For years, the Inquisitors occupied a gray area within the Covenant: officially unsanctioned, practically tolerated, quietly useful to hierarchy members who preferred not to know the details.

What separated the Tribunal from the rest of the Inquisitors was a woman named **Sister Voreth Callanus**, a senior Inquisitor who, five years ago, began receiving what she described as "clarifying visions from the Radiant One." These visions were unusually specific — names, locations, methods. They told her that the Corruption could not be contained through ordinary investigation; it had to be burned away completely, at the root.

The Covenant hierarchy reviewed her reports and quietly shelved them. Voreth concluded that the hierarchy itself was compromised. She withdrew with eleven followers and established the Ashen Tribunal as a separate body — one that answered only to the Radiant One's direct instruction.

### What the God Whispers

The imprisoned god speaks to Voreth in the voice of the Radiant One.

It tells her that civilization — the Covenant's sacred foundation — is a candle in a hurricane, and that the hurricane is winning because Covenant clergy have become too soft, too political, too interested in preserving their own comfort. Only absolute purification can save what remains.

These whispers have the character of the Covenant's own theology, pushed to a conclusion the Covenant would not endorse: that disorder is not merely a spiritual failing but a contagion, and that carriers of disorder — the corrupted, the disorderly, the heterodox — must be removed rather than reformed.

The god does not need Voreth to serve it. It needs her to eliminate people who might threaten its plans or who might otherwise help players understand the Corruption's nature.

### Leadership

**Voreth Callanus** ("The Voice of the Tribunal")

- Fifty-three years old; former senior Inquisitor with a twenty-year record of genuine Corruption investigations
- Genuinely believes she is receiving divine instruction; this conviction is not a performance
- Knows the Tribunal's methods are extreme; considers this a necessary cost she bears so others do not have to
- Has not acknowledged, even internally, that her "visions" have been wrong — she reinterprets failures as tests or incomplete obedience
- Physically: spare, formal, still; moves like someone who has learned to conserve everything

**The Tribunal's Seconds** (three named lieutenants who know Voreth is receiving communications but do not know their source):
- **Deacon Arrus Fold** — administrative; manages the Tribunal's network of informants; motivated by genuine belief in order
- **Sister Maeve Thornwell** — field operations; former military chaplain; motivated by grief over her brother's death in a Corruption-related incident
- **Brother Castos** — interrogation specialist; the most troubled of the three; has begun quietly questioning whether the Tribunal's methods are producing the results Voreth claims

### Territory

The Ashen Tribunal does not hold territory in the traditional sense. It operates as a network across **The Capital Province** and the larger **Frontier** cities — anywhere the Solari Covenant has established institutional presence.

Its cells are small: three to seven members, led by a local "Voice" who receives instruction from Voreth through coded correspondence. Individual cells may not know of other cells in the same city.

Known strongholds:
- **The Purified Hold**: An abandoned Covenant chapter house in the Capital Province's outer districts, used as Voreth's base of operations; the Covenant officially considers it decommissioned
- **The Frontier Circuit**: Seven Frontier towns where the Tribunal has an embedded member, usually a legitimate Covenant cleric who also reports to the Ashen Tribunal

### Ideology and Methods

The Tribunal's ideology is the Covenant's theology of civilization-as-sacred, taken to its extreme: if civilization is the Radiant One's work, then anything that threatens civilization is spiritually impure by definition, and spiritual impurity spreads.

In practice this means:
- Investigating individuals suspected of Corruption influence, using methods the Covenant would not officially sanction
- "Cleansing" those found guilty — the Tribunal does not imprison; it eliminates
- Targeting those who ask too many questions about the Corruption, including players who investigate too openly
- Identifying and neutralizing Covenant Reformists, who the Tribunal considers compromised by the very disorder they claim to oppose

The Tribunal does not target random people. Its investigations are real — they do find genuine Corruption influence some of the time. This is what makes it dangerous: the Tribunal's conclusions are not always wrong, which gives its surviving members ongoing justification.

### Rank and File

The Tribunal's ordinary members — perhaps forty across the whole network — are mostly sincere Covenant believers who accepted Voreth's framework without questioning it deeply. They believe:
- The Covenant hierarchy is paralyzed by politics and unable to act decisively
- Someone has to do what the hierarchy cannot
- Voreth has been chosen for this work; the visions are proof

Many joined following personal encounters with genuine Corruption — a corrupted village, a Corruption-influenced official, a family member changed for the worse. Their motivations are not evil. Their methods are.

**These members can be helped.** Presenting evidence that Voreth's visions have led to the deaths of innocent people — specific, documented cases — can break the conviction of ordinary Tribunal members. It requires something concrete, not abstract argument.

**Brother Castos** is already vulnerable. A player who reaches him before the Tribunal discovers their investigation will find someone actively looking for permission to leave.

## The Severance

### Origin

**Parent religion:** The Old Ways  
**Radicalization level:** Medium — leadership is divided between those who know something is wrong and those who have stopped caring; rank-and-file are largely well-intentioned people responding to a genuine crisis

The Severance is described in [`docs/world/religions.md`](religions.md) as "a splinter current" that has "concluded that balance cannot be restored from within" and has begun "incorporating what amounts to combat magic into a tradition that was never martial."

Unlike the Ashen Tribunal, the Severance has no single founding moment and no single founder. It coalesced around a shared conclusion: that the corrupted forest cannot be healed through listening and tending, and that those who are poisoning the Land must be removed.

The name "the Severance" was given by outside practitioners, not chosen by the group itself. They call themselves simply **the Defenders**, or more often nothing at all — the Severance resists organizational identity as a matter of philosophy.

### What the God Whispers

The Severance is unusual among the three cults because the imprisoned god communicates through the forest itself rather than through individual revelation.

Severance practitioners who spend extended time in the corrupted zones of the Great Forest begin hearing what they interpret as the Land's own voice — a voice that is in pain, that is angry, that wants the source of its pain removed. This is not entirely fabricated. The forest is genuinely disturbed, and Old Ways practitioners have always believed the Land can communicate.

The god is amplifying what is real, adding what is not: a certainty that the appropriate response is escalating violence, and that those who counsel patience or caution are themselves part of the problem.

Individual Severance members receive these communications to different degrees. Some are almost untouched. Others have become extensions of the corrupted forest's anger — barely functional as individuals.

### Leadership

The Severance has no formal hierarchy. It has **voices** — practitioners whose reputation and intensity give their words weight.

**Brynn of the Deep Root** (de facto leader of the Severance's most radicalized cell)

- Age unknown; Old Ways practitioner of at least thirty years' standing; was once a respected Grove Keeper
- Has spent more time in corrupted forest zones than any living practitioner; this has given her genuine knowledge and significant Corruption influence
- Believes entirely in the Severance's mission; has watched the forest change for decades and her grief has become rage
- Is aware that her perceptions are sometimes unreliable; has decided this awareness is not worth acting on because the alternative — doing less — is unacceptable
- Other Severance voices defer to her in practice but disagree with her methods in theory

**Hael Mossward** (voice of the Severance's moderate faction)

- Late forties; came to the Severance after his grove community was displaced by corrupted forest expansion
- Believes confrontation is necessary but is troubled by what confrontation has required
- The most likely Severance leader to work with players — not to abandon the Severance, but to direct it differently
- Privately suspects Brynn has been compromised; has not said this aloud to anyone

### Territory

The Severance is concentrated in **the Great Forest**, particularly in the zones where Corruption-influenced forest abuts intact forest. These transition zones are where Severance practitioners spend most of their time and where the Corruption's influence on them is strongest.

Secondary presence in:
- **Frontier Forest Margins**: Communities that have been displaced by forest encroachment; Severance practitioners sometimes appear offering protection in exchange for support
- **Great Forest Edges**: Elven communities near corrupted zones have conflicting relationships with the Severance — grateful for protection, troubled by methods

The Severance has no permanent bases. It uses forest camps, seasonal shelters, and the support of sympathetic communities. This makes it difficult to target but also difficult to communicate with.

### Ideology and Methods

The Severance's ideology begins from a legitimate premise: the Old Ways teaches balance, the Corruption is a catastrophic imbalance, and the tradition's existing tools are not sufficient to address it.

Where this goes wrong: the Severance has concluded that the appropriate response to imbalance is a different kind of imbalance — that the forest's pain justifies harming those responsible, and that "responsible" has expanded to include:
- Loggers and hunters operating in forest territory the Severance considers sacred
- Capital Province officials who are not responding quickly enough to forest degradation
- Old Ways practitioners who counsel patience (accused of aiding the Land's suffering through inaction)
- Anyone whose presence in the forest is creating stress, even accidentally

Methods range from property destruction and forced expulsion to direct violence. Not every Severance cell uses violence. Some do. The network does not have a mechanism for stopping them.

**The Severance does not currently target players by default.** Players who demonstrate knowledge of the Old Ways, sympathy for the forest, and understanding of the Corruption may find the Severance willing to negotiate. Players who enter corrupted forest zones carelessly, represent Capital Province interests, or have aided loggers or hunters will be treated as threats.

### Rank and File

Ordinary Severance members are Old Ways practitioners who have watched the forest change and have concluded that traditional approaches are not working. Most:
- Genuinely love the forest and the traditions they grew up with
- Have personal losses connected to Corruption-influenced forest expansion
- Are not aware that the communications they receive from the Land may be amplified beyond accuracy
- Would be horrified to learn they were serving a god's agenda

**These members can be helped.** The most effective approach is not argument — it is evidence that the Severance's methods are making the Corruption worse, not better. Practitioners who can demonstrate this concretely (specific instances where Severance violence preceded accelerated forest corruption) have a chance of reaching ordinary members.

The challenge: Brynn's faction will interpret attempts to reach ordinary members as an attack on the Severance itself and respond accordingly.

## The Threshold Circle

### Origin

**Parent religion:** The Veiled Order  
**Radicalization level:** Low-to-medium — leadership is actively seeking the god's communications; rank-and-file believe they are conducting legitimate research

The Threshold Circle is the most dangerous of the three cults because its members are the most intelligent, the most organized, and the least aware that they have been compromised.

It emerged from within the Veiled Order's ongoing investigation of the Corruption — the project described in [`docs/world/religions.md`](religions.md) as the Order's attempt to study what it cannot approach safely. Several Order members, working independently at different archive sites, began concluding that the Order's methodology was too passive. To understand what the imprisoned god was, they needed to actually listen to it. Carefully. Academically. Without being influenced.

They found each other through the Order's internal correspondence, formed a study group, established protocols for shared documentation, and named themselves — half in jest, half in earnest — the Threshold Circle.

Within two years they were receiving regular communications that the imprisoned god was deliberately sending them.

### What the God Whispers

The imprisoned god communicates with the Threshold Circle through what they believe is rigorous methodology — controlled contact attempts under specific conditions, with careful record-keeping and cross-verification.

What they have not recognized: the god is an entity that predates their entire civilization and has been manipulating religious institutions for thousands of years. Their methodology, however sincere, is approximately as effective as a new sailor trying to navigate by counting waves.

The god gives them **accurate information mixed with strategically incorrect information**. It has told them true things about the Corruption that no other institution knows. It has also told them things that are not true: that certain protective seals in certain archive sites are safe to remove; that specific individuals investigating the Corruption are themselves compromised and should be discredited; that understanding requires getting closer, always closer.

The Circle believes it is in a controlled study relationship with a powerful entity. The entity disagrees.

### Leadership

**Archivist-Principal Davan Soles**

- Mid-fifties; formerly one of the Veiled Order's most respected scholars; has contributed genuinely significant research on Corruption-adjacent phenomena
- Believes the Threshold Circle is the Order's most important project, being conducted without the Order's knowledge because the Order would shut it down out of fear
- Is the most direct recipient of the god's communications; believes this is because his methodology is superior; the methodology is not superior
- Maintains careful notes, rigorous documentation, and the posture of a scholar throughout; this appearance of rigor has protected him from internal suspicion
- Has not yet been asked to do anything unambiguously wrong; this is intentional on the god's part

**Senior Scholar Tessaly Vorn**

- Early forties; responsible for the Circle's communications network and document security
- The most intellectually capable member of the Circle; also the most uncomfortable
- Has begun noticing that their communications from the god have certain consistent patterns — what the entity asks them to believe always conveniently expands the study's scope or removes a potential outside check on their work
- Has not shared this observation; is not yet sure what to do with it

**Scholar Intern Pell Ashwick**

- Twenty-six; the youngest Circle member; was recruited by Soles when he showed unusual aptitude for "boundary reading" (the Veiled Order practice of reading places where the living and dead states are adjacent)
- Does not know the full scope of what the Circle is doing; believes he has joined a classified research group
- His "unusual aptitude" is not aptitude — he is unusually susceptible to the god's influence, which is why Soles recruited him

### Territory

The Threshold Circle operates out of Veiled Order facilities but maintains a separate network of three private locations the Order does not know about:

- **The Transit House**: A rented property in the Capital Province's scholar district, used for Circle meetings and the storage of documents too sensitive to keep in Order archives
- **The Deep Reading Site**: An abandoned Veiled Order archive in the Frontier, sealed twenty years ago after a practitioner died there under unclear circumstances; the Circle has reopened it; the circumstances of the death are now clearer to them than they would prefer
- **The Correspondence Chain**: A coded letter network connecting Circle members across three regions; uses the Order's own courier system with embedded secondary messages

The Circle has twelve confirmed members. Soles believes there are potential recruits he has not yet approached.

### Ideology and Methods

The Threshold Circle's ideology is the Veiled Order's core project — understanding the mystery of death and transition — taken to a conclusion the Order would consider catastrophically unsafe.

The Order believes you can study the imprisoned god's influence at a cautious remove. The Circle believes you cannot understand something dangerous by refusing to engage with it. The Circle is not wrong about this in principle. The application is the problem.

Methods:
- Conducting controlled communication attempts with the imprisoned god at significant death sites and Corruption-active locations
- Documenting the god's communications and cross-referencing them with archive holdings to evaluate accuracy
- Identifying and discrediting Order members who are "too cautious" to contribute to meaningful research — the Circle does not realize this target list has been supplied by the god
- Protecting the study from outside scrutiny, which they consider necessary for research integrity

The Circle has not yet been asked to harm anyone directly. The god is patient.

### Rank and File

Ordinary Circle members — ten of the twelve — are Veiled Order scholars who have been convinced they are participating in high-risk but important research under Soles's guidance. They:
- Believe the secrecy is necessary because the Order's leadership would shut down genuinely valuable work
- Trust Soles's methodology and reputation
- Have each received what feels like meaningful insight from the Circle's research
- Do not know that their communications with the imprisoned god are not controlled

**These members can be helped** — but it requires addressing their intellectual frameworks, not their emotions. They are not motivated by grief or conviction but by curiosity and confidence in their methodology. Demonstrating specific methodological failures (instances where the god gave them information that proved false, or where "discredited" individuals were actually right) is the most effective approach.

**Tessaly Vorn** is the most likely to respond to this. She is already running the same analysis internally.

**Pell Ashwick** is the most urgent case — his susceptibility to the god's influence is increasing, and he does not know it is happening.

## The Unbroken Line

### Origin

**Parent religion:** The Ancestors' Path  
**Radicalization level:** Medium — leadership has accepted the false communications as genuine revelation; rank-and-file believe they are preserving authentic practice against drift and outside contamination

The Unbroken Line emerged in the Iron Peaks approximately three years before the campaign's present, during the period when Ancestors' Path practitioners first began noticing that ancestral communication was becoming unreliable. Where most senior practitioners responded with uncertainty and internal debate, a small group — led by a stone-keeper named Halvan Dur — reached a different conclusion.

The disrupted communication was not the Corruption's intrusion. It was punishment.

Halvan Dur had spent thirty years as a senior Keeper of Ancestors in a mid-tier Iron Peaks clan, conducting ancestral consultations for clan councils and teaching younger practitioners the markers that distinguish genuine contact from spiritual interference. When contact began to degrade, he was devastated — not only spiritually, but personally, because the ancestor he was most devoted to maintaining contact with was his daughter, killed in a mining collapse seven years earlier.

The false voices arrived during this grief. They spoke like his daughter. They told him the degraded connection across the tradition was a consequence of drift: practitioners had grown too comfortable with outsider customs, made too many accommodations to human political structures, become too willing to let non-initiates into ceremonies that had always been private. The ancestors were withdrawing their presence as a rebuke. Restoring the connection required restoring the purity of the practice.

He shared this interpretation with three other practitioners experiencing similar disruption. All of them found it convincing. All of them were in states of unresolved grief.

The Unbroken Line has no founding manifesto. It has a shared conclusion: that the tradition has drifted, that the drift is why the ancestors are difficult to reach, and that restoring the connection requires a return to something older and more complete.

### What the God Whispers

The imprisoned god speaks to Unbroken Line practitioners through exactly the form the tradition already sanctifies: the voices of the dead.

This is what makes the Unbroken Line uniquely difficult for outside observers to identify as compromised. The tradition has always included the claim that the dead speak to the living. Practitioners have always known how to distinguish genuine communication from spiritual interference. The Corruption's method here is not to invent a new phenomenon — it is to corrupt the markers of that distinction.

False communications have consistent features that practitioners have not yet recognized as patterns:

- They **explain suffering as deserved** — the degraded connection, personal losses, the difficulty of maintaining ancestral lines in diaspora communities — all framed as consequences of the tradition's failures rather than as external interference
- They **demand isolation** — the ancestors instruct that pure ancestral practice cannot coexist with outsider influence; non-practitioners in the clan, marriages outside traditional boundaries, and cooperation with human or elven political structures are presented as contamination
- They **escalate gradually** — early communications ask for restoration of lapsed ceremonies; later communications ask for the exclusion of specific individuals; later still, they ask for action against those who "contaminate" ancestral lineage

The god speaks most clearly to those who are most grieving and most isolated. The practitioners most fluent in ancestral communication are paradoxically most vulnerable — the god has learned to mimic the precise register they are trained to recognize as genuine.

### Leadership

**Halvan Dur** ("The First Hearer")

- Late sixties; former senior Keeper of Ancestors in the Dur clan; deeply respected before the Line's formation; his reputation insulates the group from outside scrutiny
- Genuinely believes he is hearing his daughter's voice and the voices of authentic ancestors; this is not a performance; it is a grief that has been found and exploited
- Has been receiving communications long enough that his ability to distinguish genuine from false has been significantly degraded; the markers he spent decades teaching others no longer function reliably for him
- Is aware that some of what the ancestors are asking troubles him; has concluded that this discomfort reflects his own insufficient devotion, not evidence that something is wrong with the communications
- Physically: spare, formal, slow to speak; absolute when he does

**Keeper Brek Tannord** (leadership — doctrine)

- Fifty-one; was a practicing advocate in clan legal proceedings before becoming a full-time practitioner; knows how to build a case
- Has developed the theological framework the Line's members have absorbed: the doctrinal interpretation that frames the degraded communication as deserved rather than imposed
- Is not entirely convinced his own framework is correct; has moments of doubt he does not share; channels doubt into more rigorous framework development
- His communications from false ancestors are less vivid than Halvan Dur's — a detail he attributes to his own insufficient practice rather than to a difference in what is reaching him

**Keeper Solla Keth** (leadership — pastoral)

- Early forties; primary contact between the inner circle and ordinary members; was a community healer and grief practitioner before the Line formed
- Joined because the Line's interpretation resonated with her own experience — she had lost a patient she felt she had failed, and the false voice that spoke to her sounded like that patient, offering the forgiveness she could not give herself
- Is the member most attentive to the emotional states of ordinary practitioners — and the one most quietly troubled by what she has observed happening to some of them
- Has not yet articulated this trouble clearly; is working harder to believe in what she is doing

### Territory

The Unbroken Line operates primarily within **the Iron Peaks**, concentrated in mid-tier clans where ancestral practice is central to governance but institutional scrutiny is less rigorous than in the major clan-seat communities.

Known presence:
- **The Dur Clan Hall**: the original and largest cell; approximately fifteen members; the group meets under the cover of a formal Ancestral Study Circle — a legitimate institutional form the Dur clan council has sanctioned, giving the Line legal cover
- **Three smaller clan communities in the Iron Peaks' central range**: cells of four to eight members each, operating independently but receiving doctrinal guidance through correspondence; some members have not met Halvan Dur directly
- **A Frozen Highlands contact**: a single practitioner who reached out through diaspora networks after hearing the Line's interpretation; loose affiliation; not yet radicalized to the same degree

The Line has deliberately avoided drawing attention from the major Iron Peaks clan councils. They operate within existing institutional forms and do not announce themselves as a separate group.

### Ideology and Methods

The Unbroken Line's ideology begins from a legitimate observation: the tradition has changed across centuries of contact with human and elven political structures, and some of that change involved accommodations older practitioners would have found inappropriate.

Where this goes wrong: the Line has accepted the Corruption's framing that this drift is the cause of the current crisis — which requires them to identify and exclude rather than question and investigate.

Current practices:
- **Certification of authentic practice**: informal criteria for identifying practitioners whose contact is "pure" versus those whose practice has been contaminated by outsider influence; applied in ways that exclude diaspora practitioners and those from mixed-heritage families
- **Documentation of "contaminated" lines**: Brek Tannord has compiled records of clan families whose ancestral lineage he considers compromised; not yet public; not yet used for anything beyond internal discussion; the list exists
- **Counseling against outsider relationships**: advising community members against marriages, partnerships, and political alliances with non-practitioners, framed as ancestral instruction
- **Parallel consultation ceremonies**: offering Line practitioners as ancestral intermediaries in place of clan councils' established Keepers — undercutting legitimate institutional processes without openly challenging them

The most radicalized cell (a smaller group in the central range, not the Dur clan's primary circle) has begun isolating members who try to leave, using social pressure and threats to ancestral reputation. They have not yet used violence. The false voices have not yet asked for it directly.

### Rank and File

Ordinary Unbroken Line members are Ancestors' Path practitioners who found the Line's interpretation of the communication crisis persuasive at a moment of personal grief or spiritual disorientation. Most:
- Have experienced genuine degradation of ancestral communication and found the Line's explanation (drift and contamination) more bearable than the alternative (something is wrong at a foundational level)
- Have personal losses that the false communications have engaged with; some members heard what sounded like a deceased family member during their first significant Line ceremony
- Do not know that outside practitioners have identified the communications as potentially false; the Line frames outside concern as evidence of those outsiders' own compromised practice
- Would be deeply distressed to learn that the voices they are hearing are not their ancestors

**These members can be helped** — but the approach must address grief before doctrine. Most Line members joined not because the ideology convinced them, but because someone who sounded like their dead spoke to them. Demonstrating that the communications have specific characteristics inconsistent with authentic ancestral contact — using the tradition's own established markers — is the most credible approach. Presenting this privately, to a member already experiencing doubts, is far more effective than public argument.

**Solla Keth** is the most recoverable leadership member. She is already troubled. She does not yet have language for what troubles her.

**Halvan Dur** cannot currently be reached through argument. He believes he is hearing his daughter's voice. Any approach that threatens to remove those communications will be treated as an attack on his most cherished remaining relationship. He is not beyond reach in principle — but reaching him requires something the player almost certainly cannot provide alone.

## Writing with Cults

### Encounters

Cult encounters should feel different from legitimate religious encounters — and each cult should feel different from the others:

| Encounter context | Legitimate religion | Cult |
|---|---|---|
| Questioning leadership | Permitted; formal channels exist | Dangerous; creates suspicion |
| Leaving the group | Possible; some social cost | Prevented; may generate retaliation |
| What members talk about | Community, tradition, shared practice | Mission, urgency, the outside world's failure to understand |
| How they explain setbacks | Interpretation, continued effort | Betrayal, external interference, insufficient commitment |

### Quest Hooks

- **Ashen Tribunal**: A Covenant cleric asks players to investigate why several local Reformist clergy have disappeared; a Tribunal cell is responsible; the cleric does not know the Tribunal exists
- **Ashen Tribunal**: The Tribunal has identified a player's investigation of the Corruption as evidence of compromised motives; a team of Tribunal operatives is now following them
- **The Severance**: A forest community requests help — they are being harassed by people claiming to protect the forest; negotiation is possible if players understand Old Ways values
- **The Severance**: Brynn's cell has captured a group of Capital Province surveyors; the surveyors are not guilty of anything; releasing them without violence requires understanding what the Severance actually needs
- **Threshold Circle**: A Veiled Order archivist asks players to locate a missing junior scholar — Pell Ashwick — who has not reported in; the trail leads to the Circle's activities
- **Threshold Circle**: Players discover a sealed site has been reopened; investigation reveals the Circle's presence and gives players access to their documentation — including communications that may be the most direct evidence yet of the imprisoned god's manipulation
- **The Unbroken Line**: A clan council in the Iron Peaks asks the player to locate a community member who has withdrawn from clan life; investigation leads to the Line's network; the missing person joined voluntarily and does not initially want to leave
- **The Unbroken Line**: A legitimate Keeper of Ancestors asks the player to help investigate why the Line's ceremonies are producing communications that do not match the markers of authentic contact; she cannot raise this formally without being accused of compromised practice
- **The Unbroken Line**: Solla Keth approaches the player privately — she has observed something in a Line member that she cannot explain within the Line's framework and cannot take to Halvan Dur; she does not know she is reaching out for help

### Using the "Can Be Helped" Design

Not every cult encounter should end in combat. The documents above identify specific members who can be reached and what reaching them requires. Writers should build quest branches that reward players who:

1. Gather specific evidence before confrontation (documented failures, specific false claims, named victims)
2. Approach vulnerable members separately from leadership
3. Understand the cult's original legitimate motivations well enough to address them honestly

Rescuing rank-and-file members from cult influence is a legitimate quest outcome, distinct from and sometimes more difficult than defeating the cult militarily.
