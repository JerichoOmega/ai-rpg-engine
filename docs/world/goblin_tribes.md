# Goblin Tribes

> **Document Status:** Canonical as of July 2026.  
> **Authority:** This document defines the canonical leadership, territory, traditions, and relationships for the three named goblin tribes. All future goblin content — quests, encounters, diplomacy, companion events — must align with these definitions.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](WORLD_BIBLE.md) · [`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../systems/reputation.md) · [`docs/heroes/RAGASH.md`](../heroes/RAGASH.md)

## Overview

There are three primary goblin tribes operating on the continent. They are not a unified people — they have distinct cultures, territories, and political interests that sometimes conflict with one another as much as they conflict with neighboring civilizations.

Goblins are intelligent, adaptive, and tribal in the deepest sense: loyalty runs to the tribe first, to individual leaders second, and to the broader concept of "goblinkind" last or not at all. Players who understand this will approach each tribe differently. Players who treat them as interchangeable enemies will miss significant diplomatic, informational, and quest opportunities.

The three tribes are:

| Tribe | Primary Territory | Leadership Style | Threat Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stonefang** | The Frontier (eastern contested zones) | Strength-based hierarchy; strongest leads | High — actively expanding |
| **Mossroot** | The Great Forest margins and Frontier woodland | Elder council; consensus-driven | Low under normal conditions |
| **Ashfire** | The Frontier (southern and central) | Unified command under a single warchief | Growing — politically consolidating |

## Stonefang Tribe

### Identity

The Stonefang Tribe is the oldest of the three in its current territorial form — they have held the eastern Frontier for generations, and they measure worth in a simple, consistent language: strength. The tribe's culture is built around physical dominance, direct confrontation, and the accumulation of power through victory. They are not mindless — they are calculating — but their calculations are always made through the lens of who is stronger and what can be taken.

The Stonefang are not genocidal by philosophy. They raid because raiding works. They expand because expansion means more resources and more status. A party that demonstrates overwhelming capability may find that the Stonefang respond not with suicidal resistance, but with a reassessment of terms.

### Leadership

**Warchief Grakkor Stonefang**

Grakkor is a second-generation leader who inherited command after defeating his father in a sanctioned leadership challenge at age nineteen — young even by goblin standards, which is the point. He is now in his mid-thirties, which in goblin terms means he has survived long enough that people have stopped underestimating him, and he has had time to develop into something more dangerous than raw aggression: a strategist.

Grakkor is large for a goblin — unusual enough that it became part of his reputation early on. He carries a weapon taken from a human knight he killed in single combat: a longsword he had rebalanced for his grip. He uses it ostentatiously, as a statement.

What makes Grakkor interesting as a political entity: he is not loyal to the idea of raiding. He is loyal to the tribe's strength and survival. If the party can demonstrate that cooperation would make the Stonefang stronger than raiding would, Grakkor will listen. He will not be easy to convince, and he will not apologize for what the tribe has done. But he is not irrational.

**Deputy:** Varkk, Grakkor's cousin and raid-commander — handles military operations while Grakkor manages politics. Varkk is more aggressive and less flexible than Grakkor; when Grakkor is absent or dead, Varkk's approach determines the tribe's direction.

### Territory

**Primary:** Eastern Frontier — the contested zone between the last organized human settlements and the beginning of genuinely lawless land. The Stonefang hold a network of camps, raiding positions, and one permanent fortified settlement (called the Fang-Hold) built into a rocky promontory overlooking a major trade road.

**Secondary:** Stonefang raiding parties operate as far north as the Frontier's northern edge and occasionally push into the margins of The Iron Peaks when dwarven supply lines are weak. They do not hold this territory — they exploit it seasonally.

**Controlled resources:** The trade road overlooked by Fang-Hold. Any caravan moving between The Capital Province and the eastern Frontier passes within Stonefang raiding range. This is their primary economic leverage: they tax, raid, or extract tribute from commerce they cannot stop but can make very expensive.

### Traditions

**The Proving**  
Leadership challenges are formalized, not chaotic. Any Stonefang goblin may challenge a superior to single combat at a recognized Proving. The challenger announces the challenge three days in advance; combat is to submission or incapacitation, not death (though deaths occur). This structure means Stonefang leadership changes are violent but predictable — there is always someone aware a challenge is coming. It also means that the current leader has survived every challenge thrown at them, which is a meaningful credential.

**Trophy Culture**  
Stonefang warriors display trophies from significant kills — armor pieces, weapons, occasionally a distinctive item that signifies who the defeated opponent was. Grakkor's longsword is the most prominent example. Reading the trophies on a Stonefang warrior tells a party what that warrior has faced and survived. This is not mere vanity; it is a social record of capability.

**The Debt of Steel**  
If a Stonefang goblin spares an enemy in a position where killing would have been easy, that enemy owes a Debt of Steel — a culturally binding obligation recognized by the tribe. The Stonefang take this seriously because the debt is also leverage. A party that a Stonefang commander has deliberately spared may later be called upon to honor that debt. The call cannot be ignored without making an enemy of the whole tribe.

### Relationships with Neighboring Factions

**Human settlements on the eastern Frontier:** Hostile and transactional. The Stonefang raid human settlements as a matter of economics; the humans defend and retaliate as a matter of survival. Neither side expects this to end. Some frontier settlements pay informal tribute in exchange for protection from other threats — the Stonefang do not see this as hypocrisy; they see it as a reasonable commercial arrangement.

**Adventurers Guild:** Complicated. The Guild has taken multiple contracts to suppress Stonefang raiding activity. Grakkor does not hold grudges against individual Guild members for this — contracts are contracts — but he tracks which Guild members have operated against the tribe and which have not. Guild adventurers with a history of anti-goblin contracts will be received differently than those without one.

**Mossroot Tribe:** Tense and territorial. The Stonefang and Mossroot share a border zone in the western Frontier woodland where their ranges overlap. The Stonefang periodically press into Mossroot territory when food is scarce; the Mossroot resist and occasionally retaliate. No formal war exists, but the relationship is not peaceful. Grakkor views the Mossroot as weak; Mossroot elders view the Stonefang as destructive guests who were never invited.

**Ashfire Tribe:** Watchful and suspicious. The Ashfire's rapid unification concerns Grakkor. A strong Ashfire means a possible rival for Frontier dominance. Grakkor has not moved against the Ashfire yet because he is not certain his tribe would win, and he does not make moves he cannot win. He is gathering information. The Ashfire are doing the same.

## Mossroot Tribe

### Identity

The Mossroot are hunters — not raiders. They take what the land provides, move with the seasons, and have developed a culture organized around the idea that disruption is the enemy of survival. Where the Stonefang accumulate and expand, the Mossroot sustain and adapt. Where the Ashfire consolidate power, the Mossroot distribute it across a council of elders who reach decisions slowly and honor them completely.

This makes the Mossroot the least immediately threatening of the three tribes and the most potentially useful to a diplomatic party. It also makes them the most likely to be overlooked or underestimated — a mistake. Mossroot hunters are among the best trackers on the continent, and the tribe's knowledge of the forest margins and travel routes through the Frontier is extensive. Their information is worth more than most parties realize.

They are not pacifists. They hunt. They defend their territory with lethal efficiency when cornered. The difference between the Mossroot and the Stonefang is not capability — it is intent.

### Leadership

**Elder Speaker Vess**

Vess is the current head of the Mossroot council of elders — a position that does not grant command but grants the right to speak last and be heard first. She is older than most goblins who survive to elderhood in Frontier conditions, which means she has either been very skilled or very lucky, and people who know her do not believe in luck.

She is small, quiet, and precise. She does not use a weapon — she carries a tracking staff that is also, practically speaking, a weapon, though she does not introduce it that way. Her value to the tribe is not combat effectiveness but information: Vess has spent decades learning the Frontier's patterns — where threats originate, where resources concentrate, what changes in the land mean before those changes become visible to others.

Vess distrusts humans and other outside parties on principle, not out of hatred. Her default is to observe and wait rather than engage. A party that demonstrates genuine respect for Mossroot territory and ways of doing things — not performance of respect, but actual behavior — will find her willing to talk. A party that approaches with obvious political maneuvering will find her unreachable.

**Council composition:** Five elders in addition to Vess. Each represents a territorial cluster — a set of camps and hunting grounds that the elder knows best. Major decisions require four of six votes. This makes the council conservative by design; changing its mind on a significant question takes time and genuine argument.

**War Leader:** Thorn, a younger goblin of significant combat ability who commands the tribe's hunters when defense or coordinated action is needed. Thorn is not on the council and defers to Vess in political matters, but the relationship is respectful rather than hierarchical in a strict sense. If the council votes for war, Thorn leads it.

### Territory

**Primary:** The forest margins along the southern and western edges of the Great Forest, where the woodland transitions into the open Frontier. The Mossroot have a complex network of seasonal camps rather than permanent settlements — they move with the game patterns and rotate their presence to avoid depleting any one area.

**Secondary:** The Mossroot range extends along certain Frontier travel routes that pass through or near forest cover. They know these routes intimately and use this knowledge both for hunting and for avoiding threats they do not wish to engage.

**Significant site:** The Deep Warren — a large underground network beneath a section of forest that the Mossroot use as a winter refuge and secure gathering place. The location is known to few outside the tribe. The Mossroot will not reveal it to outsiders under any ordinary circumstances; it is the one secret they protect above all others.

**Overlap zones:** The western Frontier woodland is contested with Stonefang raiding parties. The forest margins near elven settlements in the Great Forest are navigated carefully — the Mossroot have informal arrangements with some elven communities, but the relationships are fragile and require maintenance.

### Traditions

**The Quiet Passage**  
Before any major hunt, a Mossroot hunter observes a period of silence — a few hours to a full day depending on the significance of the hunt. During this time, they track the target without making contact, learning its patterns. The Quiet Passage is practical as well as ritual: it produces better hunting outcomes. It also reflects the tribe's core philosophy that understanding precedes action.

**Name-giving for prey**  
The Mossroot name significant animals they track regularly — not as pets or companions, but as acknowledgment that the animal is a real creature with a life worth recognizing. When they hunt a named animal, there is a brief ritual acknowledgment before the kill. This tradition horrifies Stonefang goblins and confuses most outsiders, but it is deeply embedded in how the Mossroot understand their relationship with the land.

**Council of Smoke**  
When a major decision requires the full council, the elders meet around a fire with a specific mix of herbs that produces a heavy smoke. The meeting does not end until a decision is reached or explicitly tabled. The smoke is not psychoactive — it is a practical way of making the meeting uncomfortable enough that no one is motivated to delay unnecessarily. Decisions made in a Council of Smoke are binding; reversing one requires calling another.

### Relationships with Neighboring Factions

**Elven communities in the Great Forest:** Cautiously positive. The Mossroot and certain elven groups have maintained informal territorial arrangements for generations — each respects the other's range, and there is occasional trade of information and resources. This is not a formal alliance and could dissolve under pressure, but it is real. Elven communities that know the Mossroot will speak of them with something closer to respect than most non-goblins manage.

**Adventurers Guild:** Neutral with positive potential. The Mossroot have not been targeted by the Guild in the way the Stonefang have, because they rarely threaten trade routes or settlements. Individual Guild members who have passed through Mossroot territory and behaved appropriately have sometimes been guided out of danger they didn't know they were in. The tribe does not advertise this.

**Human frontier settlements:** Wary coexistence. The Mossroot do not raid settlements, which means they are not active enemies of the humans who live near their territory. They are also not trusted, because they are goblins. This is a stable but joyless situation — the Mossroot avoid human settlements when possible, and the humans maintain defensive readiness without actively pursuing the tribe.

**Stonefang Tribe:** Tense (see Stonefang section). The Mossroot do not seek conflict with the Stonefang, but they have fought defensive actions against Stonefang incursions more than once. Vess maintains a careful read on Grakkor's movements.

**Ashfire Tribe:** Concerned distance. The Ashfire's consolidation is watched with alarm by the Mossroot council. A unified goblin political entity with territorial ambitions is, in the Mossroot analysis, a threat to every established arrangement the tribe has maintained. Vess has not yet determined what to do about this.

## Ashfire Tribe

### Identity

The Ashfire did not exist in their current form two years ago. What existed were eight separate goblin clans scattered across the central and southern Frontier, each small enough to be ignored, each occasionally raiding and occasionally feuding with the others. What exists now is something new: a unified political entity under a single warchief, operating with coordinated purpose.

This transformation was not inevitable. It was the work of one person.

The Ashfire are dangerous not because they are the strongest — the Stonefang may still have them in a direct test of arms — but because they are organized. They have moved from the chaos of fragmented clans to something approaching a state, and states can do things clans cannot: plan across seasons, negotiate as a unit, make commitments that survive the death of any single individual.

Whether this new structure survives its founder is the question everyone watching the Ashfire is asking.

### Leadership

**Warchief Skarra Ashfire**

Skarra is the reason the Ashfire exist. She was born into one of the eight original clans — the smallest, arguably, and the one with the least territorial claim — and she spent fifteen years building the argument that unified goblins could be more than any of them were separately. She did not build this argument with speeches. She built it with demonstrations: coordinated raids that no single clan could have pulled off, territorial negotiations with outside factions that required a unified front to survive, protection of smaller clans from threats that would have destroyed them if they faced those threats alone.

By the time she called the formal unification, six of the eight clan leaders supported it. The remaining two she defeated in short, efficient military actions that she had prepared for months in advance. She offered the defeated leaders positions within the new structure. One accepted. One refused and left the Frontier entirely.

Skarra is not yet thirty years old.

She is ideologically serious in a way that distinguishes her from both Grakkor's pragmatism and Vess's conservatism. Skarra believes that goblin tribes will always be exploited, raided, contracted against, and ultimately destroyed as long as they remain small and fragmented. Her project is not expansion for its own sake — it is the creation of something durable. She is, in the terms of the continent's other political entities, a nation-builder.

This makes her simultaneously easier and harder to negotiate with than the other tribe leaders. Easier, because she has genuine political goals that can be engaged with rationally. Harder, because her goals are large, and she will not trade them for short-term convenience.

**Inner Council:** Skarra rules through an inner council of three — one from each of the three clans that joined the unification first. This structure is partly political (it rewards early loyalty) and partly practical (it distributes intelligence and administration across the territory). The council advises but does not constrain; Skarra's authority as warchief is decisive in a conflict, and everyone knows it.

**Fire-Speaker Durn:** The tribe's ceremonial leader and Skarra's oldest advisor — a figure who predates the unification and whose endorsement was critical to bringing the more tradition-minded clans into the fold. Durn is old, not a fighter, and deeply respected. He and Skarra have occasional ideological tensions — he is more concerned with goblin spiritual continuity, she is more focused on political survival — but they are not enemies.

### Territory

**Primary:** The central and southern Frontier — a larger band of territory than either the Stonefang or Mossroot control, though the Ashfire's hold on some of it is recent and not fully consolidated. Their central base is a settlement called Cinderhold, a former ruin that has been rebuilt and significantly expanded over the past eighteen months. Cinderhold is the most permanent and most developed goblin settlement on the continent.

**Expanding into:** The Ashfire are pushing their territorial claims south, toward the edges of Sol Kareth's trade route network, and east, where they risk friction with Stonefang-controlled zones. The southern expansion is the more significant political development: if successful, it would give the Ashfire leverage over trade routes that currently operate entirely outside goblin control.

**Contested zones:** The boundary between Ashfire and Stonefang territory is increasingly volatile. Neither side has made a direct military move yet, but both are positioning.

### Traditions

**The Unification Fire**  
Each year on the anniversary of the unification, a massive bonfire is lit at Cinderhold and maintained for three days. All eight clan identities are acknowledged in the ceremony — including the two that resisted — because Skarra's position is that the Ashfire encompasses what came before rather than erasing it. The Fire-Speaker leads the ritual; Skarra participates as a member of the community, not its center. This is deliberate: she wants the tribe to be bigger than any individual, including herself.

**Clan-marks**  
Individual Ashfire goblins retain the tattoo or marking of the clan they came from, alongside an Ashfire mark added at unification. The dual marking is intentional — identity is additive, not replaced. This is a surprisingly sophisticated political design: it allows former clan loyalists to feel their heritage is honored while building a new shared identity on top of it. Older goblins who were clan-born wear both marks with visible pride. Younger goblins born after unification sometimes choose to receive a clan-mark from an elder as an act of connection to a past they did not live through.

**Ash Oaths**  
Significant commitments — alliances, agreements, leadership decisions — are formalized through an Ash Oath, spoken over the coals of a fire that has been burning for at least a full day. The oath is witnessed by as many tribal members as can reasonably gather. Breaking an Ash Oath is the most serious dishonor in Ashfire culture — not merely a betrayal of another party, but a rupture in the community fabric. Skarra takes this seriously enough that she has refused to make Ash Oaths she was not certain she could keep, even when doing so would have been politically advantageous short-term.

### Relationships with Neighboring Factions

**Sol Kareth merchants:** Contested engagement. Ashfire territorial expansion toward the south has brought them into contact with Sol Kareth's northern trade route network. The initial encounters were hostile — the Ashfire taxed caravans, the merchants hired guards, there were confrontations. More recently, Skarra has sent emissaries to Sol Kareth trade factors with an offer: formal acknowledgment of Ashfire territorial rights in exchange for guaranteed safe passage on clearly defined routes. Sol Kareth has not formally responded. The negotiation is ongoing.

**Human frontier settlements:** Complicated and evolving. Some settlements have found that dealing with a unified Ashfire is actually more predictable than dealing with eight separate clans — if you reach an agreement with Skarra, it holds, because she enforces it internally. Other settlements are more threatened by a unified goblin entity than they were by fragmented ones, because the potential scale of violence has increased. Opinions vary, sometimes within a single settlement.

**Adventurers Guild:** Monitored concern. The Guild has taken notice of the Ashfire's consolidation. No major contracts have been issued yet, but the Guild's Frontier headquarters is tracking the situation. Skarra is aware of this and has been careful to avoid directly attacking Guild members — she does not want to trigger a Guild response before she is ready for it.

**Stonefang Tribe:** Mutual wariness bordering on pre-conflict (see Stonefang section). Both tribes recognize the other as a potential obstacle to their respective territorial ambitions. The question is who will make the first significant move.

**Mossroot Tribe:** Skarra has sent cautious outreach to the Mossroot council, framing the Ashfire's expansion as something the Mossroot could benefit from — a unified goblin presence large enough to negotiate with outside factions could protect Mossroot interests as well. Vess has not responded. The offer is genuine; it is also clearly in Skarra's interest. The Mossroot council is discussing it slowly.

**Ragash (Orc Houndmaster):** The Ashfire have had limited but notable encounters with Ragash's kind. Orcs and goblins share certain social pressures — both frequently treated as threats rather than people by the dominant human civilization — and Skarra is aware of this. Whether this creates a basis for anything more than mutual recognition depends on how the party navigates interactions with both groups. Skarra would not trust an orc companion of human adventurers by default, but she would pay attention.

## Goblin Tribe Inter-Relationships

The three tribes are not allies. They are separate political entities with overlapping interests and recurring frictions.

| Relationship | Status | Key tension |
|---|---|---|
| **Stonefang ↔ Mossroot** | Tense; periodic conflict | Stonefang expansion into Mossroot border territory |
| **Stonefang ↔ Ashfire** | Cold war; pre-conflict positioning | Competition for Frontier dominance |
| **Mossroot ↔ Ashfire** | Cautious distance; open negotiation | Ashfire's offer of protection vs. Mossroot's independence |

A party that builds reputation with one tribe does not gain automatic standing with the others. Helping the Stonefang against a shared enemy may be read positively by the Ashfire as pragmatic — or negatively if the Ashfire believe it strengthens a rival. Helping the Mossroot defend their territory against Stonefang incursion will damage Stonefang standing significantly.

The AI Director should track tribal relationships as a dynamic system: what the party does with one tribe creates information that the others may learn, react to, or attempt to exploit.

## Ragash and the Goblin Tribes

Ragash is an orc. Her hounds are trained trackers. Both facts are relevant to goblin encounters.

**Her hounds and goblin trails:** Goblin camps are among the concealed presences her hounds are most effective at detecting. A companion event where Ragash's hounds lock onto a goblin trail can occur in any Frontier region, providing the party with advance information about tribal activity in the area — which tribe, rough numbers, direction of movement.

**Her status as orc:** The goblin tribes do not automatically trust Ragash because she is an orc, but they read her differently than they read human party members. The Mossroot may perceive her more neutrally — she is not from the civilization that has historically contracted against them. The Ashfire's Skarra may recognize in Ragash's situation (an outsider operating outside her people's expectations) something worth noting. The Stonefang will assess her based on capability, the same as anyone else.

**Diplomatic implications:** If Ragash is the party member who initiates contact with a goblin tribe, the tribe's initial assessment will be colored by her species in complex ways. This is not a simple positive or negative — it is nuance that writers and the AI Director should treat as an opportunity, not a shortcut.

## Design Notes for Writers

### What These Tribes Enable

With named leaders, territories, and traditions defined, goblin encounters can now do more than resolve into combat:

- A party that helps Grakkor survive a leadership challenge from Varkk has changed the Stonefang's future trajectory
- A party that locates the Mossroot's Deep Warren and does not reveal it has earned a significant trust deposit with Vess
- A party that witnesses an Ash Oath ceremony and shows appropriate respect has communicated something to Skarra without saying a word
- A party that brokers a territorial agreement between the Stonefang and Mossroot over the contested border zone has done something that affects the Frontier's politics long after the session ends

### What These Tribes Cannot Provide

The tribes are political entities, not saviors. They will not sacrifice their interests for the party's convenience. High reputation creates access and goodwill — it does not create unconditional allies who will march into suicidal situations on request.

### The Corruption and the Tribes

The Corruption's effect on goblin tribes is not yet fully defined. As a design direction: goblins are smaller communities than human kingdoms, which means The Corruption's influence may manifest faster and be harder to contain — but also that the tribes may be more nimble in recognizing and responding to something going wrong in their own ranks. The Mossroot, with their close relationship to the natural world, may notice environmental Corruption indicators before larger civilizations do.
