## The Veiled Order

- **Purpose:** Quietly investigate forgotten history · watch for evidence connected to the Forgotten Eighth · protect dangerous knowledge · operate mostly in secrecy.
- **Status:** One of the four religious traditions ([`docs/world/religions.md`](../religions.md) — authoritative), listed here because its *operational* face is a secret organization. Its entanglement with the Great Library and the First Temple, and the missing-scholar thread (Pell Ashwick), are established canon.
- **Visibility:** Some Archivists suspect their existence; most citizens do not. **Officially denied by the Great Library; unofficially acknowledged by a select few** — the canonical formulation of the relationship.
- **Player interaction:** The revelation arc's custodians — the faction that decides whether the player can be trusted with what the world forgot. Reputation is information-sensitive (the Veiled Archivists track in [`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../../systems/reputation.md)).

## The Black Market Network

- **Purpose:** Illegal trade — rare artifacts, smuggling, information.
- **Structure:** A *network*, not an empire: regional brokers who honor each other's introductions and debts, because reputation is the only enforcement mechanism criminals reliably respect. No hidden mastermind; the network's resilience is precisely that there is no head to cut off.
- **Trade:** Artifacts that never reach the Library (the Expedition Corps' constant leak), Consortium-controlled alchemy, tariff-free goods, and rumors priced by the buyer's need.
- **Player interaction:** Fencing and acquisition, smuggling runs, the moral seam between "illegal" and "wrong" — a relic saved from a burning region arrives through this network as often as a poison does.
- **World State:** Grows where institutions weaken; black-market density is a canonical decline signal.

## The Assassins' Circle

- **Purpose:** Small. Professional. Political. Rarely encountered.
- **Character:** A closed circle of contractors serving political clients — succession disputes, silenced witnesses, the occasional mercy commissioned as murder. **Never portrayed as cartoonishly evil:** the Circle's members have codes, refusals, and reasons, and its most interesting stories are about the contracts it declines.
- **Scale rule:** The Circle stays small and rare. It is a scalpel in the political toolkit, not a guild of killers on every rooftop.
- **Player interaction:** High-level political intrigue only — investigating a Circle killing, intercepting a contract, or facing the uncomfortable discovery of who paid.

## Regional Criminal Organizations

**Canon rule: keep criminal groups regional and believable. No world-spanning criminal empires.**

- **Bandits** — Frontier and border-country crews, mostly desperate rather than demonic; named Frontier bandit factions are pending authorship (see project task queue).
- **Smugglers** — coast, river, and pass specialists moving what tariffs and licenses forbid; overlap with the Black Market Network at the edges.
- **Pirates** — coastal raiders where geography permits (⚠️ pending World Bible coastal geography for specifics).
- **Local gangs** — city-district scale: protection, theft, gray-market labor; the street-level texture of urban quests.

Each is a *local* answer to local failure — where roads, courts, or harvests break down, these grow. They are World State symptoms with faces.

## Design Rules

1. Secret factions earn their secrecy: each has a concrete reason exposure would destroy it.
2. No secret organization may resolve protected mysteries (the Forgotten Eighth's full truth, vampiric origins, the Architects). They orbit the mysteries; they do not answer them.
3. Cults are a separate canonical category ([`docs/world/cults.md`](../cults.md)); do not blur cults into criminal networks — their motives, structures, and story roles differ.
