## First Principles

- **No Vampire King. No council that rules.** The Crimson Conclave ([`THE_CRIMSON_CONCLAVE.md`](THE_CRIMSON_CONCLAVE.md)) represents the ten houses but governs only by negotiation and consensus. Attempts to convert it into a ruling body have failed at least three times in recorded house history; each failure is remembered differently by every participant ([`VAMPIRE_HISTORY.md`](VAMPIRE_HISTORY.md)).
- **The Veil above all.** The one law all houses enforce collectively: the Houses' existence must never become common knowledge ([`THE_VEIL.md`](THE_VEIL.md)). Everything else — including the restraint on open inter-house war — descends from it, and the restraint weakens as civilization weakens.
- **Ideology over territory.** The deepest conflicts are about the Long Decline — preserve civilization, exploit it, or outlast it — not about land or blood.

## The Great Debates

Vampire houses regularly debate, in correspondence and at rare formal occasions:

1. **Should humanity know the truth?** (Vetharis: never. Valenor: someday, carefully. Drakmor: irrelevant — power decides. Kaelor: reveal to whom? We owe the world our absence. Formally unaskable at the Conclave; privately asked constantly.)
2. **Should vampires intervene — and can civilization survive?** (The Preservationist/Pragmatist/Fatalist divide — see [`VAMPIRE_HOUSES.md`](VAMPIRE_HOUSES.md).)
3. **Whether immortality is a blessing or burden — and what responsibility immortals have toward mortals.** (The private debate. Every house's culture is an answer to it — Draven's oaths, Seraphe's questions, Kaelor's guest-right, Noctis's silence.)
4. **Whether the Long Decline can truly be slowed.** (The debate under all others. A house's answer here predicts everything else about it.)

These debates should shape quests and relationships. A player can shift a house's internal balance on any of them.

## The Relationship Web

### Among the Great Houses

Canonical detail in [`docs/world/vampire_houses.md`](../../vampire_houses.md) — Inter-House Dynamics. In brief: Vetharis/Drakmor mutual contempt in functional check; Vetharis/Soleth guarded mutual respect; Drakmor/Soleth wary distance. The Corruption pressures all three toward a coordination none of them wants.

### Founding Houses toward the Great Houses

All seven Founding Houses share one background grievance: the Great Three are latecomers whose Conclave recognition several Founding Houses privately consider the council's greatest mistake ([`THE_CRIMSON_CONCLAVE.md`](THE_CRIMSON_CONCLAVE.md)). Individual relationships sit on top of that:

| Pair | Character |
|---|---|
| **Draven / Drakmor** | The bitterest feud in vampire society; predates the Third Age. A disagreement between soldiers about what soldiering is for: Draven holds that strength serves order; Drakmor holds that strength *is* order. Morreth: "a museum with a sword." Draven: "an army in search of a war worth fighting." |
| **Morcant / Vetharis** | Rivals in the same trade by opposite methods — Vetharis embeds inside institutions; Morcant brokers from outside them. Each is the other's most attentive customer through cutouts both pretend not to recognize. |
| **Valenor / Soleth** | Archive rivalry wrapped in courtesy. Valenor's claim that its archives rival the Great Library's irritates Soleth precisely because it might be true. They correspond constantly and cite each other never. |
| **Seraphe / Soleth** | The closest active relationship between houses. The Archivist exchanges materials with Seraphe more readily than with anyone; their shared interest in the Great Forgetting and the Forgotten Eighth makes them partners in the setting's deepest questions. |
| **Seraphe / Vetharis** | Nervous monitoring. A house that studies the Forgotten Eighth could bring the wrong kind of attention on every house; Cassiel keeps a standing watch on Seraphe's exposure. |
| **Kaelor / Drakmor** | Occasional probing of Kaelor's valleys, repelled with precise, minimal force. Drakmor respects this exactly as much as it resents it. |
| **Kaelor / Valenor** | Kaelor answers Valenor's letters out of courtesy and no one else's at all. If Kaelor ever re-enters continental affairs, it will be through Valenor. |
| **Noctis / everyone** | Indifference from a great height. The Archivist has sought an audience for over a century and received two replies — both corrections to his archive. |
| **Vesper / the Great Three** | Business with all, allegiance to none. Vetharis and Morcant regard Vesper's networks as encroachment; Drakmor taxes routes it can reach, which Vesper prices in rather than contests; Soleth is Vesper's best customer for provenance work. |

### Among the Founding Houses

- **Valenor–Draven:** warm, old alliance; Valenor negotiates, Draven guarantees. The nearest thing to a standing bloc among the ten.
- **Morcant–Seraphe:** Morcant sells Seraphe fragments touching their research and almost certainly keeps copies. Seraphe knows. The price is factored in.
- **Draven–Kaelor:** mutual respect between the two honor cultures, expressed almost entirely through not bothering each other.
- **Vesper–Valenor:** the provenance partnership — Valenor authenticates what Vesper acquires; each suspects the other of keeping the best pieces.
- **Vesper–Morcant:** goods and information move on the same routes, by wary arrangement; each considers the other's trade the vulgar version of its own.

## World State Swings

Per [`VAMPIRE_HOUSES.md`](VAMPIRE_HOUSES.md) — World State Integration:

- **Civilization weakening →** isolationists (Kaelor, Noctis) gain standing; predatory currents (Drakmor Expansionists, Vetharis Impatient Faction, Morcant opportunists) grow bolder; old feuds (Draven/Drakmor above all) intensify; the Crimson Conclave becomes increasingly divided.
- **Civilization resilient →** cooperative houses (Valenor, Vesper, preservationist Soleth and Draven) grow active; secret alliances with mortal institutions increase; ancient knowledge moves.

The player's actions subtly influence these shifts. The AI Director should treat founding-house posture as a *visible symptom* of World State: when Kaelor's gates open or a Morcant broker gets careless, players are reading the world's health.

## Political Quest Patterns

Build vampire political content from these canonical patterns — never "kill the vampire lord":

1. **The mediation** — two houses need an intermediary neither will name; the player carries the correspondence and learns too much.
2. **The disputed history** — a family-history entry, contested for two centuries, surfaces in evidence; whoever authenticates it shifts an alliance.
3. **The forgotten bloodline** — a mortal family's ancestor was a house member; three houses have opinions about the heir.
4. **The exposure crisis** — someone (mortal or vampire) is about to make a house visible; every house wants it stopped, none for the same reason.
5. **The coordination problem** — the Corruption forces houses toward cooperation; the player becomes the channel through which they negotiate what each will pay.

Reputation consequences are per-house and cross-cutting ([`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../../../systems/reputation.md)): serving one house's interest routinely costs standing with its rival, and the player should feel the web pull every time they touch it.
