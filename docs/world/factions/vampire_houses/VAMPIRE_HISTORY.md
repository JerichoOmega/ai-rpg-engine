## The Boundaries of Vampire Memory

The Vampire Houses are one of the world's oldest surviving political societies. Two boundary rules are canon:

1. **Older than the modern nations, but not older than the First Civilization itself.** The Houses remember kingdoms that have long since fallen. They do not remember the beginning.
2. **Even they possess only fragmented knowledge of the deepest mysteries** — the Architects and the earliest ages. Vampire memory is long, not complete. A vampire elder is a primary source for centuries of political history and an unreliable, fragmentary source for the world's foundations. Writers must never use a vampire as a shortcut to resolve protected mysteries (the Architects, the Forgotten Eighth's full truth, the origin of vampirism itself — [`BLOODLINES.md`](BLOODLINES.md)).

## A History in Five Movements

*Deliberately unfixed to precise dates — the Houses' own chronicles disagree, and that disagreement is canon.*

### 1. The Unremembered Beginning
Vampirism appears in the historical record already old. The earliest confirmed bloodline, House Noctis, predates most modern kingdoms; what preceded it is theory ([`BLOODLINES.md`](BLOODLINES.md) — the four theories, none provable). The oldest Houses' origins reach back toward the Age of Harmony and the fall of the First Empire — the horizon where their fragmentary earliest records give out.

### 2. The Age of Wars Between
Before the Veil, the fragmentary chronicles agree on one thing: episodes of open conflict between vampires and mortal civilization, each ending in mutual ruin — hunted bloodlines, burned archives, kingdoms destabilized. No House tells these stories the same way; every House draws the same conclusion. These episodes are the Veil's justification, and their details are deliberately lost.

### 3. The Founding and the Veil
Seven Houses — Noctis, Valenor, Draven, Seraphe, Morcant, Kaelor, Vesper — established the **Crimson Conclave** and codified the already-ancient survival practice of secrecy into the first law: **the Veil** ([`THE_VEIL.md`](THE_VEIL.md)). The Founding is vampire society's constitutional moment; every seated House's standing still traces to it.

### 4. The Rise of the Great Houses
During the wars of the Age of Restoration, while mortal kingdoms fought and the Founding Houses kept to their traditions, three younger Houses — **Vetharis, Drakmor, Soleth** — expanded aggressively into the vacuum: Vetharis into political structures, Drakmor into territory, Soleth into the preservation of what the wars were destroying. The Conclave formally recognized all three; by the wars' end they were the dominant powers of vampire society ([`docs/world/vampire_houses.md`](../../vampire_houses.md) — Historical Context). Several Founding Houses privately consider the recognitions the Conclave's greatest mistake — an opinion the Great Houses find useful to leave uncorrected.

### 5. The Present — the Long Decline and the Corruption
The Houses now face the same question as everyone else: whether civilization's decline can be slowed, and what an immortal owes a dying world. The Corruption attacks the specific thing each Great House depends on ([`docs/world/vampire_houses.md`](../../vampire_houses.md)), the Conclave grows divided as the World State weakens, and the revelation question — whether the Veil should ever be lifted — is asked more often than any elder will admit.

## Failed Attempts at Rule

At least three times, factions have attempted to convert the Conclave into a ruling body — a crown by another name. Each attempt failed. Each failure is remembered differently by every participant, and the chronicles of all three are among the most contested documents in any House archive. Canonical rule: **no attempt may be depicted as having nearly succeeded.** The Houses' independence is structural, not lucky.

## History as Story Surface

- **Vampires are living witnesses.** Some remember the First Empire; some knew individuals who lived during the Age of Harmony. Their testimony about *political* history is gold; about *deep* mysteries, fragments.
- **Contested chronicles are quests:** a document that settles (or unsettles) a two-century dispute; an elder's memory against another elder's memory; evidence from the Age of Wars Between that someone wants found — or burned.
- **The Great Library's restricted archive** ([`THE_VEIL.md`](THE_VEIL.md)) contains the mortal side of this history: centuries of incomplete evidence that some Archivists believe and others dismiss. Keep it incomplete.
