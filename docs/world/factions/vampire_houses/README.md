# Vampire Houses — Canonical Faction Folder

> **Canon Status:** Confirmed — established July 2026. Core world canon.
> **Authority:** This folder is the canonical reference for vampire society as a whole — its design philosophy, the Veil, the Crimson Conclave, the ten-house roster, bloodlines, history, culture, and politics. It **supplements** and does not replace [`docs/world/vampire_houses.md`](../../vampire_houses.md), which remains the authoritative operational document for the three Great Houses (leadership, territories, reputation tiers, first-contact scenes, and quests). Where the two disagree, `docs/world/vampire_houses.md` and the Lore Bible (`docs/lore/`) take precedence.

---

## Purpose

Vampires are an ancient hidden civilization that has quietly existed alongside mortal civilization for centuries — an aristocratic secret society, not a playable race, kingdom, or public nation, and not monsters hiding in castles. Their origins stretch back toward the Age of Harmony and the fall of the First Empire. The design draws on the political intrigue of classic secret-society fiction while remaining fully original and integrated into the world's established lore: they are living witnesses to history, deciding whether to preserve the world from behind the Veil or keep watching history repeat itself.

## Non-Negotiable Design Pillars

1. **The Veil is the first law.** The Houses' existence must never become common knowledge; breaking the Veil is among the greatest crimes in vampire society ([`THE_VEIL.md`](THE_VEIL.md)).
2. **No Vampire Empire, Kingdom, Emperor, or Pope.** Houses are independent; the Crimson Conclave governs only by negotiation and consensus and rules no one ([`THE_CRIMSON_CONCLAVE.md`](THE_CRIMSON_CONCLAVE.md)).
3. **No purely good or purely evil Houses.** No House exists solely as an antagonist or ally; each represents a distinct philosophy on immortality, secrecy, and civilization's future.
4. **The origin of vampirism remains a long-term mystery.** Competing theories exist ([`BLOODLINES.md`](BLOODLINES.md)); none is ever confirmed. Even vampires disagree.
5. **Vampires are uncommon and important.** A player may finish a playthrough meeting only a handful. They are political figures, historians, scholars, nobles, patrons, rivals, and allies — never generic enemies.
6. **No vampire-hunting quest lines.** Quests center on political intrigue, diplomacy, ancient history, bloodline disputes, forgotten archives, philosophical conflicts, secrets, and hard moral choices.
7. **Vampire memory is long, not complete.** Older than modern nations, not older than the First Civilization; only fragments about the Architects and the earliest ages ([`VAMPIRE_HISTORY.md`](VAMPIRE_HISTORY.md)).
8. **Per-house reputation.** Standing is tracked per house, never as a universal "vampire" meter ([`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../../../systems/reputation.md)).
