# Bloodlines — Origins, the Turning, and the Condition

> **Canon Status:** Confirmed — established July 2026.
> **Authority:** Canonical treatment of vampirism as a condition. The origin of vampirism is a protected long-term mystery — see the rule below.

## The Origin Mystery — Protected Canon

The exact origin of vampirism is unknown. Four competing theories circulate among scholars, vampires, and the rare mortal institutions aware of the question:

| Theory | Held chiefly by | The evidence for | The problem with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **An ancient magical affliction** — a disease of magic itself, contracted and transmitted | Mortal scholars who know of vampires at all | Transmission through the turning behaves like contagion | No affliction known to the Mages Guild persists unchanged across ages |
| **A forgotten experiment from the Age of Harmony** — something made, then lost in the Great Forgetting | Currents within House Seraphe; quietly entertained by the Archivist | The oldest bloodlines date to roughly that horizon; records that should exist do not | If it was made, someone made it — and no house's memory holds a maker |
| **A curse dating to the Long Decline** — a punishment or side-wound of the world's slow failure | Fatalist vampires; some Covenant theologians | Vampires and the Decline are entangled in every folk account | Houses older than the Decline exist; Noctis predates it |
| **A condition tied to primordial magic** — older than the ages, surfacing through bloodlines like deep water | House Noctis, to the extent they say anything | Noctis itself: a bloodline whose origin precedes all record | Unfalsifiable — which is why it endures |

**RULE (non-negotiable):** No quest, NPC, document, or revelation may conclusively prove any theory. Evidence may be found *for* and *against* each. The mystery is a load-bearing element of the setting: vampires who do not know what they are must decide what they mean, and that decision is what separates the houses. Writers who need a big revelation should reveal *history* (what a vampire witnessed) — never *origin*.

## The Turning

Vampirism is transmitted deliberately. The turning is rare, deliberate, and consequential — every house treats it as the most serious decision a vampire can make, though for different reasons:

- **Vetharis** turns rarely and selects for political utility. A turning is an investment.
- **Drakmor** turns in larger numbers; new vampires are raw material for military capacity.
- **Soleth** turns selectively, for rare skills or knowledge worth preserving for centuries.
- **Valenor and Draven** turn within long-cultivated mortal families who have served the house for generations — often with the candidate's informed consent, a practice other houses consider either noble or naive.
- **Seraphe** turns only those who have already devoted a mortal lifetime to the house's questions.
- **Kaelor** has not been known to turn anyone in living mortal memory.
- **Vesper** turns as it contracts: rarely, in writing, and with terms — a practice the other houses find bloodless and Vesper finds honest.
- **Morcant** is rumored to have turned creditors' heirs as a form of debt collection. Morcant denies this in a way that suggests they enjoy the rumor.
- **Noctis** — unknown. No confirmed Noctis turning exists in any house's records.

An unwilling or unplanned turning is a scandal in every house and a common seed for quests: the newly turned individual did not choose the condition, and the houses will contend over what becomes of them.

## Bloodline Character

Long descent within a house shapes its vampires — whether by blood, by training, or by the centuries spent inside one philosophy, no one can say (the ambiguity is deliberate; see the origin rule).

- **Great House bloodlines** trend toward their house's instrument: Vetharis toward reading people, Drakmor toward command presence, Soleth toward memory that does not fade even by vampire standards.
- **The oldest bloodlines** (Noctis above all) exhibit traits the other houses do not fully understand — including, in Noctis, an apparent indifference to some of the hungers that discipline every other vampire's life.
- **Thin or fading bloodlines** exist: houses that have nearly disappeared leave descendants and half-forgotten cadet lines. "Forgotten bloodline" quests — a mortal family that does not know what its ancestor was, an heir hunted for a legacy they cannot see — are a canonical quest pattern.

## The Newly Turned

Newly turned vampires may not have chosen their condition. They are the most human-facing vampires in the setting and the best entry point for players:

- They still have mortal families, debts, loves, and enemies.
- Their house (if any claims them) is deciding what they are worth.
- The hunger is new to them, and the discipline that veterans wear lightly is, for them, a nightly war.

## Feral Vampires

A vampire lost to instinct is called **feral**. Ferality is the condition every house fears and manages differently:

- **Vetharis** regards ferals as catastrophic failures and eliminates them quickly.
- **Drakmor** tolerates them at the margins and has occasionally weaponized them.
- **Soleth** regards them as tragic; the Archivist documents their condition.
- **Kaelor** holds that ferality is the consequence of a broken code — and that their code exists precisely to prevent it.
- **Seraphe** studies whether ferality is decay of the mind or of something else — carefully, and without conclusions.

Ferals are the one context where a vampire may appear as a straightforward combat encounter — and even then, the story should be about the house that lost them.

## The Hunger

Every vampire lives with the hunger. Canon treats it as a permanent moral pressure, not a solved problem:

- Some struggle against it their whole existence.
- Some embrace it.
- Every house has rules about feeding; every house has members who break them.
- Kaelor's guest-right, Valenor's consent traditions, Vetharis's discretion protocols, and Drakmor's requisition practices are all, at bottom, answers to the same question.

Writers should use the hunger as the private cost behind the public aristocracy — visible in small disciplines, not in gothic spectacle. See [`VAMPIRE_CULTURE.md`](VAMPIRE_CULTURE.md).
