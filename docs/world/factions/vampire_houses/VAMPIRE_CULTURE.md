# Vampire Culture — Aristocracy of Centuries

## Register: Ancient Aristocracy, Not Gothic Horror

Vampire culture should resemble **ancient aristocracy rather than gothic horror**. The touchstones are old courts, old estates, old law — not crypts, capes, and screaming villages. A vampire's home should feel like a family seat that has been inhabited, maintained, and quietly argued over for four hundred years, because it has.

Practical rules for writers:

- Understatement over spectacle. The oldest vampires signal age through restraint, not display.
- Their horror, where it exists, is the horror of *time*: outliving everyone, keeping oaths to the dead, watching the same mistakes recur.
- The hunger is a private discipline, visible only in small rituals (see [`BLOODLINES.md`](BLOODLINES.md) — The Hunger).

## What Every House Possesses

Each House maintains, and takes seriously:

1. **Heraldry — crest and colors** — a device carried on seals, signet rings, and estate gates; rarely displayed in mortal company. (Canonical devices in [`HOUSE_PROFILES.md`](HOUSE_PROFILES.md) and [`FOUNDING_HOUSES.md`](FOUNDING_HOUSES.md).)
2. **A motto** — recited at formal occasions; often the compressed core of the house's philosophy.
3. **Formal traditions and ceremonies** — greetings, precedence, the order in which elders speak, the forms of address between houses, and each house's own rites (Kaelor's formal hunt, Draven's oath-keepings, Seraphe's observances).
4. **Etiquette and codes of conduct** — internal law. Enforcement varies from Kaelor's absolute rigor to Morcant's cheerful selective reading.
5. **Internal hierarchy** — each house orders itself differently (a lord, a marshal, a circle of elders, a factor-general); no two hierarchies are alike, and mistaking one for another is itself an etiquette failure.
6. **Family histories, notable families, and famous historical members** — kept meticulously; in vampire society, *the history is the family*. Disputes over a two-century-old entry can end alliances. *(Named notable families and famous members are largely still to be authored — flag as TODO rather than improvising silently.)*
7. **Estate architecture** — each house builds in a recognizable idiom (below).
8. **Distinct clothing styles** — fashions frozen at, or evolved slowly from, the era of the house's greatest confidence.

## Formal Traditions Across Houses

- **Guest-right** is near-universal but strongest in Kaelor, where it is absolute: a guest under the roof will be fed, sheltered, and never fed upon.
- **The long address:** formal introductions recite lineage. An elder's full introduction can name individuals dead for three centuries; interrupting it is a serious insult.
- **Debts and oaths** persist across generations and centuries. Draven keeps oaths past the death of everyone who witnessed them; Morcant keeps ledgers of favors the way Soleth keeps archives.
- **Political marriage** exists between houses — a union of houses' interests, negotiated over decades, as much archive-merger and alliance-treaty as household.
- **Correspondence** is an art form. Houses that will not meet still write. Valenor's couriers are, by custom, inviolate.

## Estate Architecture and Dress by House

| House | Estates | Dress |
|---|---|---|
| **Vetharis** | No fortress; townhouses, salons, and offices indistinguishable from wealthy mortal holdings — deliberately | Impeccable current mortal fashion; the point is to be unremarkable in any room of power |
| **Drakmor** | Fortified holds at the Frontier's edges; functional, garrisoned, unornamented | Uniform-influenced; campaign dress even at court |
| **Soleth** | Underground archive complexes, sealed vaults, buried structures — architecture as preservation | Archivists' robes in styles centuries out of date, maintained rather than replaced |
| **Noctis** | Unknown; the few confirmed sites are ruins that predate current record | Described differently by every witness |
| **Valenor** | Open manor houses with famous libraries and guest wings that see actual use | Diplomatic finery, half a step behind mortal fashion — approachable, never careless |
| **Draven** | Old towers and keeps kept in perfect repair long after their wars ended | Formal military dress of armies that no longer exist |
| **Seraphe** | Estates with observatories, meditation halls, and libraries of forbidden theology | Layered robes in midnight colors; the veiled star worn as a small pin |
| **Morcant** | Nothing under their own name; a lattice of rented rooms, borrowed houses, and third-party holdings | Whatever the meeting requires; famously good at dressing as someone else's servant |
| **Kaelor** | Remote wilderness estates — high mountain valleys behind closed gates; austere, weatherworn, immaculate; hunting lodges older than the maps that omit them | Heavy, plain, and old; hunting dress as formal dress; quality visible only up close |
| **Vesper** | Counting-houses and warehouses under mortal-facing names; a single true seat whose location is a house secret | Prosperous mortal merchant dress, deliberately one notch below their actual wealth |

## Culture as Story Surface

Use culture to create quests and scenes, not set-dressing:

- A violated guest-right is a diplomatic incident that can mobilize Kaelor — the one thing that reliably does.
- A disputed entry in a family history can be worth more than a relic; Soleth or Valenor will pay for the document that settles it.
- An heirloom in a mortal market means a house has fallen far enough to be *sold* — someone will want to know which house, and someone will want it back.
- A player invited to a formal vampire occasion is being *tested*: precedence, address, and restraint are the encounter mechanics.
