# Non-Human Factions

> **Canon Status:** Confirmed — established July 2026.
> **Authority:** Continental frame for the recognized non-human polities. Detailed canon takes precedence where it exists: [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../goblin_tribes.md) (goblin tribes), [`docs/world/RACES.md`](../RACES.md) (races and design philosophy), [`docs/lore/civilization/FIRST_COUNCIL.md`](../../lore/civilization/FIRST_COUNCIL.md) (historical representation), [`docs/world/religions.md`](../religions.md) (elven and dwarven religious ties).
> **Design philosophy:** Avoid fantasy stereotypes. Each polity possesses government, culture, traditions, architecture, military, relationships with humanity, history, and modern goals — and answers the Long Decline in its own way.
> **Cross-references:** [`GOVERNMENT.md`](GOVERNMENT.md) · [`FACTION_BIBLE.md`](FACTION_BIBLE.md)

Under the federal structure ([`GOVERNMENT.md`](GOVERNMENT.md)), recognized non-human polities hold Imperial Council seats **in their own right** — the Council's proudest inheritance from the First Council, where elves, dwarves, orcs, halflings, and gnomes sat as founding peoples.

## Elven Houses

- **Government:** Independent Houses — each an old lineage governing its own community; no elven monarch. Houses coordinate through slow, consensus-based conclaves.
- **Culture & traditions:** Continuity is the elven art: groves, archives of living memory, crafts practiced past mastery into meaning. Deep ties to the Old Ways tradition ([`docs/world/religions.md`](../religions.md)) — many Grove Keepers are elven, though the tradition is not exclusively theirs.
- **Architecture:** Built *with* the land — settlements grown and shaped over centuries, designed to be maintainable forever rather than impressive immediately.
- **Military:** Small, superb, defensive; wardens of their own territories, not expeditionary forces.
- **Relations with humanity:** Courteous, wary, and long-memoried — humans keep promises for a lifetime; elves remember for five.
- **History & modern goals:** Founding people of the First Empire; the Great Forgetting cost them proportionally more than anyone, and some Houses have never stopped grieving for the memories. Modern goal: preserve what remains, and decide — House by House — how much to spend helping a civilization that forgets.

## Dwarven Holds

- **Government:** Independent Holds under clan governance. **Established canon:** Duskmantle Hold, with Clan-Keeper Orryn Duskmantle and the Ironmoot Chamber as the inter-clan deliberative body; the Ancestors' Path tradition is central ([`docs/world/religions.md`](../religions.md)).
- **Culture & traditions:** Memory as duty — ancestor speakers, stone records, craft lineages; a Hold's history is its constitution.
- **Architecture:** The Iron Peaks' deep halls — engineering as cultural expression, built to outlast the ages that keep proving the point.
- **Military:** Hold-guards and tunnel-fighters; formidable in their own ground, rarely deployed beyond it.
- **Relations with humanity:** The continent's most reliable treaty partners — slow to sign, slower to break; the Ironvale settlement trade is the standing example.
- **History & modern goals:** Founding people of the First Empire; the Holds held their archives through the Sundering better than the surface did. Modern goal: keep the Holds sound and the memory unbroken — and quietly determine how much of what the surface forgot the stone still remembers.

## Orc Clans

- **Government:** Clan councils; coalitions form for shared purposes and dissolve when the purpose ends. The First Council seated Clan Coalitions selected for civic service — a heritage modern clans cite with pride.
- **Culture & traditions:** Honor as accountability — oaths witnessed, service remembered, leadership earned and revocable. The stereotype of the raider is a slur with a history; some clans have borne it, most have carried roads, walls, and caravans instead.
- **Architecture:** Practical and mobile where clans travel; where settled, unsentimental stone-and-timber towns built for weather and work.
- **Military:** The continent's finest heavy auxiliaries — clans contract as units, under their own banners and officers.
- **Relations with humanity:** Working relationships — contracts, garrisons, shared frontier towns; friction where human realms treat clan service as hired muscle rather than allied arms.
- **History & modern goals:** Founding people of the First Empire. Modern goal: recognition equal to their service — including the Council seats their coalitions are owed and do not always get filled fairly.

## Goblin Tribes

- **Authoritative document:** [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../goblin_tribes.md) — the three canonical tribes are fully established, with reputation tracks in [`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../../systems/reputation.md):
  - **Stonefang** (Warchief Grakkor Stonefang) — strength-based, direct dealing, the Debt of Steel.
  - **Mossroot** (Elder Speaker Vess) — trust-based, territorial, keepers of the Deep Warren.
  - **Ashfire** (Warchief Skarra Ashfire) — politically sensitive, oath-serious, unification's stewards.
- **Status in the federal structure:** The tribes are **not yet recognized Council polities** — whether they should be is a live political question, entangled with frontier land disputes and human prejudice, and a deliberate story surface.
- Nothing here modifies goblin canon; this file only situates the tribes in the continental frame.

## Design Rules

1. No non-human faction is a monolith; internal disagreement is required (clan vs. clan, House vs. House, Hold vs. Hold).
2. Non-human polities are political actors, not encounter categories. Their Council representation — present, absent, or contested — is a standing quest surface.
3. Every polity's answer to the Long Decline should differ from the human answers in *kind*, not just in flavor: longer memory, deeper ground, harder-earned trust.
