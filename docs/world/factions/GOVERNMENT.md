## The Federal Structure (Canon)

The continent is governed on two levels:

1. **The Imperial Council** — the continental governing body, composed of representatives from **every recognized kingdom, province, race, and major territory**. The Council handles continental law, diplomacy, trade, infrastructure, defense, and matters affecting all nations.
2. **The sovereign realms** — each kingdom, province, and recognized territory retains full authority over its own internal governance.

There is **no emperor and no continental king**. The Council's authority is representative, not monarchical.

**Queen Merveth Aldenmoor** remains the sovereign of the Capital Province and one of the continent's most influential rulers. She is a **voting member of the Imperial Council** — influential, frequently decisive, but without absolute authority over it. Her rule of the Capital Province itself is defined in [`docs/lore/IMPERIAL_CAPITAL.md`](../../lore/IMPERIAL_CAPITAL.md) and is unchanged by this document.

## Lineage: The First Council

The Imperial Council is the Age of Restoration's conscious revival of the First Empire's governing principle ([`docs/lore/civilization/FIRST_COUNCIL.md`](../../lore/civilization/FIRST_COUNCIL.md)):

> *A representative is the voice of their people. They are not a ruler over them.*

The revival is imperfect and its imperfection is canon: where the First Council seated elected delegates of peoples, the Imperial Council seats a mixture — elected representatives from some regions, crowned sovereigns or their appointees from others (the Queen among them). Purists call it a compromise; realists call it the only structure every realm would join. Both are right, and the tension between the First Council's ideal and the Imperial Council's reality is a standing source of political story.

## Composition

- **One seat per recognized realm** — kingdom, province, or major territory — filled according to that realm's own custom (election, appointment, or the sovereign in person).
- **Racial representation:** each major race elects representatives; the recognized non-human polities ([`NON_HUMAN_FACTIONS.md`](NON_HUMAN_FACTIONS.md)) hold seats in their own right, not through human intermediaries — the Council's proudest inheritance from the First Council.
- Recognition of a new realm or polity is itself a Council decision, and among its most politically charged.

## Powers and Limits

| The Council does | The Council does not |
|---|---|
| Continental law (trade standards, road law, inter-realm disputes) | Govern any realm's internal affairs |
| Diplomacy between realms and with non-human polities | Appoint or depose sovereigns |
| Continental infrastructure (roads, bridges, waystations) | Tax directly — it is funded by realm assessments, perpetually in arrears |
| Coordinate continental defense through the Military High Command | Command realm militias in peacetime |
| Emergency measures during continental crises | Enforce anything a determined realm refuses — its real power is legitimacy and coordination |

## Strengths and Weaknesses (Canon)

**Strengths:** stable government, professional administration, infrastructure, diplomacy.
**Weaknesses:** political deadlock, regional disagreements, bureaucracy, corruption — and beneath all four, the structural truth that the Council can coordinate the willing but cannot compel the unwilling.

## The Council and the Long Decline

The Council regards the Long Decline as the greatest threat facing civilization — and many members underestimate its true cause, treating it as an aggregation of ordinary problems (bad harvests, banditry, failing trade) rather than something deeper. The Corruption is quietly widening the gap between members who sense the pattern and members who vote as if the pattern were weather.

## World State Behavior

- **Decline worsening:** the Council redirects aid, strengthens defenses, debates emergency measures — slowly, publicly, and behind schedule. Deadlock deepens; realms begin acting unilaterally.
- **Resilience:** coordination visibly works — roads repaired, disputes mediated, joint patrols funded — and Council legitimacy grows.

## Player Interaction

Political quests, regional disputes, Council decisions, diplomatic missions. The player will more often feel the Council through its effects — a mediator arriving at a border dispute, a road crew funded by continental assessment, a deadlocked relief bill — than through its chamber. When the chamber does appear, it should feel like what it is: the one room where the whole continent argues.
