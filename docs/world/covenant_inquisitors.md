# The Covenant Inquisitors

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]  
> **Authority:** This document defines the Covenant Inquisitors as a named internal faction of the Solari Covenant. All quest and encounter content involving Inquisitor activity must use these definitions. The Inquisitors are distinct from the Ashen Tribunal, which split from this faction five years ago and is defined in [`docs/world/cults.md`](cults.md).

## Overview

The Covenant Inquisitors are a faction within the Solari Covenant's hierarchy — not officially sanctioned, not formally dissolved, occupying a deliberate gray area that the Covenant's leadership has maintained for nearly two decades. They began as an internal review body tasked with identifying Corruption influence among the clergy. They have become something the Covenant cannot publicly endorse and cannot bring itself to end.

Their stated purpose: to find, document, and remove Corruption from within the Covenant's own institutions before it spreads to the communities those institutions serve.

Their practice: extrajudicial detention of suspected individuals, interrogation without council oversight, destruction of sites and objects deemed "disorder foci," and the exercise of authority that the Covenant's official structures have never voted to grant them.

They are not a cult. They have not crossed the line that the Ashen Tribunal crossed five years ago when Voreth Callanus took eleven followers and declared herself the Voice of a direct divine mandate. The Inquisitors still operate within a recognizable institutional framework — they file reports, they have a chain of command, they are conscious of Covenant opinion even as they strain against it. But the Reformists who watch them closely believe the line is closer than the Inquisitors themselves understand.

## History

The Inquisitors were formed eighteen years ago by **Grand Cleric Osovar Thane**, a Restorationist who argued that the Covenant's existing disciplinary structures were too slow, too public, and too entangled with the political interests of noble patrons to effectively address internal corruption — theological or otherwise.

Thane's original mandate was narrow: the Inquisitors would investigate suspected doctrinal deviation among Covenant clergy, report to a sealed council session, and recommend action. They would have no power to detain, punish, or act without council approval.

Within four years, the scope had expanded. The Corruption's spread made the urgency argument easy: official processes took months; a Corruption-touched cleric could infect a community in weeks. The sealed council sessions became infrequent. The "recommendation to council" requirement became "notification of council after the fact." The "after the fact" notification became a summary report that senior hierarchy members could acknowledge or ignore.

No one voted to make these changes. They accumulated through precedent, deference, and the fact that the Inquisitors were producing results — Corruption incidents identified and contained — that the official structures had not.

Five years ago, Senior Inquisitor Voreth Callanus broke away to form the Ashen Tribunal, taking eleven members. The Covenant's hierarchy responded to this crisis by doing something revealing: they did not disband the remaining Inquisitors. They quietly restaffed the departures and continued the program under new leadership, with a formal internal review that produced a document declaring the Inquisitors "reformed and operating within appropriate guidelines." That document has not been made public.

The Inquisitors have operated under their current structure for five years, since the Tribunal's split. They are smaller than they were. They are also more careful — the Tribunal's radicalization scared them, and the current leadership has made explicit effort to distinguish Inquisitor practice from Tribunal practice. Whether this distinction is real or a matter of degree is one of the things the Reformists are actively trying to determine.

## Leadership

### Inquisitor-General Aldren Coss

**The head of the Inquisitors; the named figure above Brother Verath**

- Sixty-one years old; former military chaplain turned Covenant administrator; appointed Inquisitor-General four years ago, one year after the Tribunal split
- A Restorationist by political affiliation, though he has become harder to categorize; he genuinely believes the Covenant's survival requires the Inquisitors' function, and has made this argument to three successive Grand Clerics
- Coss did not create the Inquisitors and has never fully controlled them; he inherited an institution with existing methods and precedents and has shaped it at the margins
- His central conviction: the Ashen Tribunal's failure was Voreth Callanus's certainty that she answered to the Radiant One directly. Coss believes the Inquisitors must always answer to *someone* within the Covenant — even if that chain of accountability has become thin
- What he avoids knowing: the specific methods his Senior Examiners use in the field. He reads their reports. He does not ask what was not written down.
- Physically: large, deliberate, wears his age visibly; gives the impression of a man carrying something heavy for a long time and having learned to carry it without showing the strain

**In encounters:** Coss does not appear in Frontier towns or low-level investigations. He operates from the Capital Province. Players who make significant waves with the Inquisitors — particularly those who cost the faction resources or exposure — may eventually be summoned to meet him. He is not a villain. He is a man who has made himself the institutional container for something he believes would be worse if he weren't containing it. He can be reasoned with, appealed to on institutional grounds, and genuinely moved by evidence that his Senior Examiners have crossed a line. He is not easily surprised.

---

### Senior Examiner Lysa Dorne

**The administrative and operational coordinator; the Inquisitors' organizational intelligence**

- Forty-four years old; trained as a Covenant legal scholar before joining the Inquisitors twelve years ago; the only person who consistently knows where all active Inquisitors are and what they are doing
- Not a field operative — Dorne does not conduct interrogations or burn shrines. She schedules, routes, briefs, and deconflicts. She is the reason the Inquisitors function as an organization rather than as independent operators
- Reformists have identified her as a key target: if Dorne's filing system were ever exposed or if she turned against the Inquisitors, the entire operation would become visible
- She is not ideologically committed to the Inquisitors' methods; she is committed to competence and order. She joined because the Inquisitors had a problem worth solving and an institution capable of solving it. She has stayed because leaving would mean someone less careful taking her role
- What she watches: the line between "investigation that produces actionable intelligence" and "investigation that produces confessions regardless of truth." She has quietly killed three field assignments that she believed had crossed it. She has not reported this to Coss; she handled it through scheduling.
- Physically: precise, efficient, unremarkable in appearance; affects the vestments of a mid-level Covenant administrator and is rarely recognizable as anything more

**In encounters:** Dorne is not present in field operations but may appear when players have become a problem that requires organizational response — she might arrange for Inquisitor interest to be redirected, or contact the party indirectly if she has determined they are useful rather than threatening.

---

### Brother Verath *(field-level Inquisitor; defined in the quest)*

The ground-level operational face of the Inquisitors in Frontier towns. Verath is not exceptional — he is representative. The Inquisitors' field personnel are typically former Covenant clergy who have internalized the faction's conviction that ordinary measures are insufficient. They have the authority of Covenant vestments without the accountability of a Covenant council.

See [`docs/quests/religious_order_quests.md`](../quests/religious_order_quests.md) — *The Inquisitor's Commission* — for Verath's specific portrayal, methods, and the Corruption-amplification dynamic that drives his behavior.

## Operational Methods

What distinguishes Inquisitor activity from standard Covenant enforcement is the *absence of institutional process*. Standard Covenant enforcement involves charges, witnesses, council review, and formal judgment. Inquisitors operate before and around that process — identifying targets, gathering evidence, and sometimes acting before any formal structure is involved.

### Standard Methods

| Method | Description | Threshold for use |
|---|---|---|
| **Surveillance commission** | Tasking local Covenant contacts — clergy, allied merchants, shrine attendants — to report on specific individuals | Low; standard for any initial investigation |
| **Spiritual interview** | A formal meeting with a suspected individual under the Covenant's authority; technically voluntary, practically coercive given the power differential | Medium; used when surveillance has produced concern but not evidence |
| **Extended examination** | Detention without formal Covenant charges; the Inquisitors' language is "protective custody pending spiritual assessment"; duration is indefinite | High; the most contested method; Reformists argue it has no legal basis |
| **Disorder designation** | Formal declaration that a location, object, or practice represents a "focus of spiritual disorder"; gives Inquisitors authority to destroy, restrict access, or commandeer the designated item or site | Medium-high; used more frequently than extended examination |
| **Canonical referral** | Escalating a case to the official Covenant disciplinary structure; used when the Inquisitors want to create a permanent record or when the evidence is strong enough that formal process will produce the outcome they want | Selective; Inquisitors choose which cases deserve this treatment |

### What the Inquisitors Will Not Do (Officially)

The Inquisitors distinguish themselves from the Ashen Tribunal on these grounds:

- They do not claim direct divine mandate; they claim institutional Covenant authority (however attenuated)
- They do not execute; they detain and refer
- They do not target individuals based solely on theological difference; they require some connection to Corruption activity or suspicion thereof
- They do not operate outside Covenant territory without explicit authorization from a local Covenant hierarchy

**What Reformists observe about this list:** The first two distinctions are real. The third has eroded — "suspicion" is defined by the Inquisitor conducting the investigation, and "Corruption activity" has expanded to include Old Ways practice in some field reports. The fourth is honored in Capital Province operations and ignored in Frontier towns where there is no local Covenant hierarchy to ask.

## Current Reach

### Capital Province
The Inquisitors' administrative base. Coss and Dorne operate from an unmarked Covenant annex in the capital city. Field personnel rotate through on quarterly reporting cycles. The Capital Province is where the Inquisitors are most restrained — too many eyes, too many rival Covenant factions watching for ammunition. Documented abuses in the Capital Province are rare because operatives there know they are visible.

### The Frontier
Where Inquisitor activity is most visible to players and most likely to escalate. Frontier towns have minimal Covenant oversight, communities that mix religious practices, and populations with limited institutional recourse when Covenant authority is exercised against them. Brother Verath's operation in Millhaven (*The Inquisitor's Commission*) is representative: a field Inquisitor operating with real authority and minimal supervision, in a place where official checks do not function.

Active Inquisitor presence in the Frontier is currently four to six field operatives rotating through a circuit of towns. They do not coordinate with each other in real time — Dorne manages their movements from the Capital — which means two Inquisitors can be pursuing contradictory agendas in adjacent towns without knowing it.

### Sol Kareth
The Inquisitors have recently established a presence in Sol Kareth's major oasis cities, following the Covenant's existing merchant-elite network. Operatives here focus almost entirely on identifying Corruption influence within Covenant-aligned trading families — a different profile from Frontier operations, more about financial leverage and information than about burning shrines. Coss authorized this expansion six months ago; Dorne is still building the operational infrastructure.

### Outside These Regions
Minimal to none. The Inquisitors do not have meaningful presence in the Iron Peaks, the Great Forest, or the Frozen Highlands. The Great Forest's elven community has made clear that Covenant authority does not extend to their territory. The Iron Peaks' dwarven clans have their own internal accountability structures that have declined to cooperate with Covenant investigative bodies. This is a source of genuine frustration within the Inquisitor leadership.

## Relationship with Covenant Factions

### The Restorationists
The Inquisitors' political patrons and institutional cover. Restorationists argue that civilization's defense requires hard measures; the Inquisitors represent those measures. Senior Restorationist clerics do not typically want to know the details of what the Inquisitors do — but they reliably prevent the Reformists from shutting the program down, provide the institutional authorization that keeps the Inquisitors nominally within Covenant structures, and occasionally supply the Inquisitors with names and locations of individuals they find politically inconvenient as well as spiritually suspect.

The Restorationist relationship is transactional: the Inquisitors produce containment; the Restorationists produce protection. What strains this relationship is cost. When an Inquisitor operation creates a scandal that requires political management, it is Restorationist credibility that absorbs the damage. Several senior Restorationists have grown quietly tired of managing these crises and have begun asking Inquisitor-General Coss for operations that produce fewer visible problems.

### The Reformists
The Inquisitors' active opponents within the Covenant. Reformists believe the Inquisitors represent exactly the pattern they most fear: the Corruption amplifying the Covenant's existing tendencies toward control into something harmful, with the institution unable to recognize or stop it because the people doing harm are doing it in the Covenant's name.

Reformist response to the Inquisitors operates through official channels — documentation, petition, testimony from communities who have experienced Inquisitor operations. Senior Cleric Aldavar Sune, the quest-giver in *The Inquisitor's Commission*, is a representative Reformist: he cannot oppose the Inquisitors openly without splitting the local hierarchy, so he works obliquely, using outside agents to gather the evidence that official processes will not produce on their own.

Reformists who have been building dossiers on Inquisitor operations for years believe they are close to having enough evidence to force a formal Covenant inquiry — but "close" has been the situation for three years, and the Restorationists continue to find ways to prevent the inquiry from being called.

### The Covenant Clergy (General)
Most ordinary Covenant clergy are not part of any faction. They are aware the Inquisitors exist; they have varying degrees of knowledge about what the Inquisitors do; they have opinions that range from uneasy support to genuine alarm. The majority find it easier to hold no opinion — the Inquisitors are officially unconfirmed, the Restorationists discourage scrutiny, and the Reformists' opposition looks uncomfortably like disloyalty to people who prefer not to examine the distinction.

## Relationship to the Ashen Tribunal

The Inquisitors and the Ashen Tribunal do not cooperate. They are not allied. From the Inquisitors' perspective, the Tribunal is an embarrassment and a danger — a faction that took Inquisitor methods to their logical extreme, went mad with certainty, and now makes every Inquisitor operation look like a step toward the same destination.

Inquisitor-General Coss has stated internally that the Tribunal is "a warning we carry." Dorne has quietly ensured that the Inquisitors' operational records contain clear distinctions from Tribunal methods wherever there is overlap.

From the Tribunal's perspective, the Inquisitors are insufficiently committed — people doing necessary work who cannot face the full implications of that work. Voreth Callanus has made contact with former Inquisitors twice since the split; both times the approached individuals reported the contact to Dorne rather than respond.

In play: Inquisitors and Tribunal members will not work together. If players attempt to use one faction to manipulate the other, both will respond with suspicion. A player who exposes Tribunal activity to an Inquisitor contact will gain limited credibility — the Inquisitors are relieved when the Tribunal is disrupted — but this does not translate into friendship.

## Encounter Hooks

### Hook A: The Wrong Name on the List

The party arrives in a Frontier town to find a local healer under "spiritual interview" by an Inquisitor. The healer is Old Ways — uses plant-lore and seasonal observances — but has spent the last year successfully treating a Corruption-related illness in the community. The Inquisitor's surveillance report identified her as a "disorder focus" based on her practices.

Investigation reveals the surveillance report was built from testimony by a local merchant who has a property dispute with the healer's family.

**How it can go:**
- The party gathers evidence of the fraudulent surveillance source and presents it to the Inquisitor — testing whether the Inquisitor cares that the process was corrupted
- The party contacts the Covenant Reformist network (if they have that relationship) to document the case and use it as one more entry in the dossier
- The party negotiates the healer's release by working within the Inquisitor's framework — acknowledging Covenant authority, requesting a formal canonical referral rather than indefinite examination
- The party simply extracts the healer, accepting the consequences with the Covenant
- The party does nothing and moves on — later learning the healer was referred to the Capital Province for "extended assessment"

### Hook B: The Inquisitor Who Wants Out

A mid-level Inquisitor makes quiet contact with the party — through a trusted intermediary, through a note left where only they would find it. She has been operating in the Frontier for two years and has begun keeping a secondary record of what her reports to Dorne omit.

She is not ready to defect openly. She wants to know if there is somewhere to go. She has information about three active Inquisitor operations she considers illegal even by the faction's own standards. She also has information about a genuine Corruption-touched individual the Inquisitors have been unable to locate — someone who has been actively harmful and who the party's intervention against would be unambiguously correct.

The genuine Corruption intelligence is her leverage: she wants the party to act on it and demonstrate they can be trusted, before she shares what she knows about the abuses.

**How it can go:**
- The party takes the Corruption intelligence, succeeds, and the relationship deepens — she becomes an ongoing contact inside the Inquisitors
- The party takes the intelligence and reports her existence to the Reformists without telling her — she eventually learns this; how she responds depends on what the Reformists did with the information
- The party alerts Inquisitor-General Coss that one of his operatives is maintaining unauthorized records — she disappears from active operations and resurfaces a year later as a Reformist ally
- The party declines the contact entirely — the Inquisitor finds another route

### Hook C: Competing Claims on a Captured Individual

The party has taken a prisoner who they believe is Corruption-touched — perhaps someone whose behavior triggered investigation, perhaps the target of a quest. Before they decide what to do with him, an Inquisitor arrives with Covenant authority to take the prisoner into "spiritual examination."

The prisoner is aware enough to beg the party not to let the Inquisitors take him. He may or may not have done what he is suspected of doing — the party may not yet know.

**How it can go:**
- The party hands the prisoner to the Inquisitor — he disappears into "extended examination," and the party later learns (or doesn't) what happened
- The party refuses, which creates a direct conflict with Covenant authority; the Inquisitor does not have the resources to compel the party but will file a report that has consequences
- The party negotiates: they accompany the prisoner to the examination, insisting on witnessing it — the Inquisitor has to decide whether their presence is worth more than it costs
- The party helps the prisoner escape and deals with the fallout from both the Covenant and whatever the prisoner actually is

## Writers' Notes

**The Inquisitors are not the Ashen Tribunal.** Writers should resist flattening them into generic religious zealots. The specific texture of the Inquisitors is *institutional capture* — an organization that started with a legitimate purpose and accumulated methods its original charter never authorized, staffed by people who believe in the mission and have not examined the methods closely enough.

**The Corruption's role is amplification, not replacement.** Inquisitors like Brother Verath are not possessed or controlled — they are themselves, pushed past the point where they can see what they are doing clearly. A good Inquisitor encounter makes the player understand how the Inquisitor arrived at this position before asking them to oppose it.

**The faction has internal dissent.** Lysa Dorne has quietly killed operations she considered too extreme. The junior clerk in Millhaven is deeply uncomfortable. There are Inquisitors who have resigned — quietly, carefully, without making their reasons public. Players who look for the dissent will find it; players who treat every Inquisitor as identical will miss the texture.

**Encounter outcomes should have consequences upstream.** An Inquisitor confronted, evidence gathered, and a report sent to the Reformists is one entry in a dossier that is building toward something. Players who oppose the Inquisitors repeatedly are not just managing individual incidents — they are part of a political contest whose outcome matters.
