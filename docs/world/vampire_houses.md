# Vampire Houses of Elyndor

> **Canon Status:** Confirmed — established July 2026.

## Overview

Vampires are one of Elyndor's primary supernatural powers. They are ancient, organized, and politically aware. The three major houses have endured for centuries — long enough to have watched kingdoms rise and collapse, to have outlasted dynasties, and to have learned that the most dangerous thing a vampire can be is visible.

The houses do not agree on what survival requires. That disagreement is the engine of vampire politics.

Each house operates with full awareness that the other two exist. They are not at open war — open war would expose them all — but they compete, undermine, and occasionally cooperate when a common threat demands it. The Corruption is beginning to feel like such a threat. None of the houses agree on what to do about it.

## House Vetharis

*"Power held openly is power with a target on it."*

### Philosophy

**Political manipulation.** House Vetharis pursues power through influence, placement, and information — never through open force. Their vampires do not rule kingdoms; they stand beside the rulers, advising, steering, and occasionally replacing those who become inconvenient.

A Vetharis vampire does not own an army. It does not need one. It owns the general.

The house's founding belief is that immortality is wasted on creatures who announce themselves. Vetharis vampires maintain human identities — merchants, advisors, magistrates, scholars — and sustain them across generations by carefully managing who knows what, and when. An individual Vetharis vampire may have lived under six different names in the same city, watching each persona age and die while they remain.

### Lord of the House

**Lord Cassiel Vetharis** — the house's founder, though he has not used that name in decades. His current identity is Aldric Hourne, a senior advisor to a provincial council in the Capital Province. He has held political positions in this city continuously for over two centuries, rotating identities every thirty to fifty years.

Cassiel is not the oldest vampire alive, but he may be the most patient. He thinks in political cycles, not in seasons. He has watched three different ruling families consolidate and collapse the same city's power structures. He finds this neither tragic nor amusing — simply instructive.

He is not cruel by preference. Cruelty is inefficient, and it creates enemies with nothing left to lose. He is, however, utterly willing to destroy a person's life through entirely legitimate means if they become a problem.

**Appearance and manner:** Cassiel presents as a man in his mid-fifties — distinguished, unhurried, the kind of person rooms quiet for. He listens more than he speaks. When he does speak, it tends to be the last word on a subject.

**His known weakness:** Cassiel's greatest blind spot is his certainty that information is the only real power. He underestimates how often people act against their own interests, and how often force resolves what subtlety cannot.

### Territory and Sphere of Influence

House Vetharis operates primarily in **the Capital Province** and major trade cities. They have no fortress, no army, no claimed land — their territory is the halls of power itself. Vetharis agents hold positions in merchant guilds, noble councils, judicial bodies, and diplomatic offices across every major region.

They are deliberately difficult to map. If you could draw a Vetharis territory, it would look like a network of carefully maintained relationships rather than any geographic space.

Their presence is thinnest in the Frozen Highlands and the Frontier — places where political complexity does not exist in forms they can exploit. They have agents in Sol Kareth and the Great Forest, but those are watching posts, not influence centers.

### Internal Politics

House Vetharis is held together by information asymmetry: Cassiel knows more about every member than any member knows about Cassiel. This is deliberate. It prevents coalitions from forming around him.

**The Loyalist Core** — a cadre of vampires who have served the house for over a century and genuinely believe in Cassiel's philosophy. They do not simply obey; they agree. These are dangerous people who have chosen subtlety because they find it elegant.

**The Impatient Faction** — younger vampires (in the sense of those turned in the last century) who watch The Corruption reshaping the continent's politics and believe the house is being too cautious. Their argument: the old order is destabilizing. New power structures are forming. Vetharis should be shaping those structures from the inside, more aggressively, before someone else does.

**The Mask-Wearers** — a small, dangerous minority who have been maintaining human identities for so long they have begun to question whether the masks are worth wearing. Not defectors; they have nowhere to go. But they are psychologically fraying, and fraying vampires are unpredictable.

**The fault line:** Cassiel's children (those he turned personally) versus those turned by other house members. His direct progeny have access to information the others do not, and they know it. This creates resentment that Cassiel monitors carefully but does not fully resolve — he uses the tension to keep factions from unifying against him.

## House Drakmor

*"The world respects strength. Everything else is storytelling."*

### Philosophy

**Military conquest.** House Drakmor pursues power through force, territory, and the credible threat of violence. Where Vetharis hides, Drakmor demonstrates. Where Vetharis advises, Drakmor commands.

The house's founding belief is that all political arrangements ultimately rest on the question of who can hurt whom. Merchant wealth, noble titles, diplomatic relationships — all of these dissolve the moment someone with sufficient force decides not to honor them. House Drakmor intends to always be the one with sufficient force.

This does not make them mindless. Drakmor vampires are capable of strategy, patience, and tactical restraint. They simply regard these as instruments of violence rather than alternatives to it.

### Lord of the House

**Lady Morreth Drakmor** — the house's war-lord. She is one of the continent's oldest vampires and has spent six centuries building and losing and rebuilding military power with the systematic attention of someone who regards war as a craft to be perfected.

She does not maintain a false human identity. She is known — not widely, but known. The warlords and mercenary captains in the Frontier who have encountered her forces know the name Drakmor. Some of them have worked for her. All of them are careful not to antagonize her needlessly.

Morreth is direct to the point of brutality in conversation. She has no patience for indirection, which puts her at permanent odds with Cassiel Vetharis's philosophy. She respects competence, regardless of source, and is contemptuous of weakness regardless of rank.

**Appearance and manner:** Morreth appears as a woman in her late forties — broad-shouldered, battle-scarred, with the kind of physical presence that makes rooms recalculate their own geometry. She does not pretend to be something she is not. She finds pretense inefficient.

**Her known weakness:** Morreth's direct nature is also her limitation. She understands force and territory well; she understands institutional power and long-term political positioning poorly. She has conquered things that other houses have quietly absorbed and redirected. She wins battles and sometimes loses campaigns.

### Territory and Sphere of Influence

House Drakmor claims **the Frontier and its border regions** as primary territory. They maintain fortified positions in the wilder, less governed edges of the continent — places where their forces can operate openly without triggering the kind of response that would destroy a more exposed operation.

They have raiding capacity into most adjacent regions and conduct intermittent territorial pressure tests against the Iron Peaks' surface settlements and the edge of the Great Forest. Neither has resulted in sustained occupation, but both serve a purpose: keeping potential opponents aware that Drakmor's reach exists.

In cities and governed regions, they are known primarily by their absence — and by the occasional message that arrives to make clear they are watching.

### Internal Politics

Drakmor's hierarchy is simple: strength earns rank. This produces clarity of command and constant ambient competition, because rank can always be challenged.

**The Old Guard** — veterans of Morreth's early campaigns, some of them centuries old. They have absolute loyalty to her but are increasingly skeptical of younger vampires' willingness to exercise the kind of ruthlessness the house was built on. They regard restraint as a symptom of weakness.

**The Expansionists** — a faction that believes The Corruption is creating a generational opportunity. The old political structures are weakening. Established armies are distracted by domestic crises. This is the moment to push territorial expansion, not wait for stability to return. Morreth has not fully endorsed this view; she is suspicious of strategies that depend on chaos continuing to cooperate.

**The Pragmatists** — a smaller faction that has been watching Vetharis succeed through subtlety for long enough to believe the house should adopt at least some of those methods. They are not popular. Morreth tolerates their existence because they occasionally produce useful intelligence. The Old Guard regards them as philosophical contamination.

**The fault line:** Morreth's succession question. She has not named an heir. The Old Guard, the Expansionists, and the Pragmatists each have a candidate, and all three know that Morreth's death or incapacitation would immediately produce a conflict that could fracture the house. Some of them suspect she is aware of this and has made no designation deliberately — using the uncertainty to maintain control.

## House Soleth

*"Everything that was written down still exists, if you know where to look."*

### Philosophy

**Ancient knowledge and the survival of vampire society.** House Soleth's defining priority is the accumulation, preservation, and strategic use of accumulated history. They have been alive long enough to have witnessed things no living institution remembers. They regard this knowledge as their primary power and their primary responsibility.

But survival of vampire society is the second pillar — and it is not merely rhetorical. Soleth genuinely believes that vampires as a people (if "people" is the right word) face extinction threats that neither Vetharis's politics nor Drakmor's armies are equipped to address. Someone has to take the long view. Soleth has appointed themselves to that role.

Their relationship to secrecy is different from Vetharis's. Vetharis hides to accumulate power. Soleth withdraws to preserve what power they have. The distinction matters: Soleth is not ambitious about influence, but they are fiercely protective of what they already possess.

### Lord of the House

**The Archivist** — House Soleth's lord has not used a personal name in living memory. The house refers to him only as the Archivist, and the name has become both title and identity. The few outsiders who know of his existence typically assume "the Archivist" refers to a position that has changed hands. It has not.

He is, by all surviving evidence, the oldest vampire on the continent. How old is uncertain even within the house. The Archivist himself gives contradictory answers to this question, which may be deliberate or may reflect genuine uncertainty about timelines that predate reliable record-keeping.

He speaks in the careful, qualified language of someone who has been burned by overconfidence enough times to build permanent epistemic caution into his voice. He never claims to know something he does not know. He never dismisses something as impossible unless he has watched it fail to happen. This makes him, paradoxically, one of the most reliable sources of information in the world — and one of the most unsettling to speak with, because his caution implies depths of experience most people cannot imagine.

**His known weakness:** The Archivist's drive to preserve means he is deeply reluctant to act in ways that risk what he has already secured. He is extraordinarily cautious. In crisis situations where speed is essential, this caution can look indistinguishable from paralysis.

### Territory and Sphere of Influence

House Soleth occupies **hidden places** — underground archive complexes, sealed vaults beneath ancient ruins, abandoned towers in the Iron Peaks' deep passes, and buried structures in Sol Kareth that predate the current civilization. They do not claim surface territory in any conventional sense.

Their physical resources are primarily located in places that have not been occupied by living civilization in centuries. They have spent those centuries making those places difficult to find and harder to reach.

They have agents in the Mages Guild — not controlling it, but embedded within it. Scholars who have made contact with Soleth operatives and received access to historical sources in exchange for information about current events. Neither side would describe this as an alliance. Both sides would acknowledge it is useful.

Sol Kareth is their richest territory for active archaeological interest: the desert's buried ruins contain records from before the Age of Legends, and Soleth has been excavating and documenting those ruins, quietly, for as long as Sol Kareth has been a civilization.

### Internal Politics

Soleth's internal conflict is fundamentally philosophical rather than personal. The house does not quarrel over rank or territory in the way the other houses do — they quarrel over what the archive's purpose is.

**The Preservationists** — the dominant faction, aligned with the Archivist's core philosophy. Knowledge must be protected first. It can be used only when the risk of its loss has been adequately assessed and controlled. They are extraordinarily conservative about sharing what they know, even within the house.

**The Weaponizers** — a growing faction that believes the house is sitting on power it refuses to use. Specifically: they believe Soleth's archives contain information about The Corruption's true nature — about the imprisoned god, its seal, the mechanics of how the presence bleeds into the world — and that this information should be weaponized, either as a bargaining tool with other factions or as a means of solving the problem before it destroys the conditions vampires need to survive. They argue that hoarding knowledge while the world deteriorates around them is a form of suicide in slow motion.

**The Sharers** — a small, ideologically distinct faction that believes Soleth's knowledge should be offered to qualified scholars and mages who might make use of it to stop The Corruption. Not sold, not leveraged — shared, because vampire survival ultimately depends on the survival of a stable world. They are a minority, regarded by the Preservationists as dangerously naive and by the Weaponizers as insufficiently strategic.

**The fault line:** The Archivist has not resolved the debate between Preservationists and Weaponizers, and his deliberate non-resolution is beginning to be read by both factions as a form of endorsement of the other. His patience may be his greatest asset and his greatest liability simultaneously. The question of what Soleth knows about The Corruption — and whether they will share it — is potentially the most consequential unanswered question in vampire politics.

## Inter-House Dynamics

The three houses are aware of each other. They have dealt with each other across centuries. None of them trusts either of the others.

| Relationship | Character |
|---|---|
| **Vetharis / Drakmor** | Mutual contempt held in functional check. Cassiel regards Morreth as a blunt instrument that occasionally points itself at the wrong target. Morreth regards Cassiel as a coward who dresses his fear in philosophy. They have not been at open conflict in decades because open conflict would expose both houses. This restraint is transactional, not principled. |
| **Vetharis / Soleth** | Guarded mutual respect. Cassiel values what Soleth knows. The Archivist values Cassiel's ability to move in political spaces Soleth cannot reach. They exchange information occasionally, carefully, through intermediaries. Neither would call it trust. |
| **Drakmor / Soleth** | Wary distance. Morreth finds the Archivist's caution maddening. The Archivist finds Morreth's directness dangerous to be near. They rarely interact directly. Drakmor occasionally destroys something Soleth was interested in preserving, which the Archivist regards as an argument for why Drakmor should not be allowed near things that matter. |

### The Corruption Complication

The Corruption is beginning to create pressure for inter-house coordination that none of the three houses wants. It is destabilizing the political structures Vetharis depends on, creating chaos that makes Drakmor's territorial claims harder to hold, and threatening the physical sites where Soleth maintains its archives.

Whether the three houses can coordinate a response — and what each would demand in exchange for cooperation — is a live political question with no resolution in sight.

## Design Notes

### Vampire Types by House

The vampire type classifications from the World Bible manifest differently across houses:

| Type | Vetharis | Drakmor | Soleth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ancient lords** | Cassiel himself; the Loyalist Core | Morreth; the Old Guard | The Archivist; senior Preservationists |
| **Vampire nobles** | Primary strategy; extensive false identities in human society | Present but less common; mostly in frontier town leadership | Rare; embedded in scholarly institutions |
| **Newly turned** | Carefully selected; turned for specific political utility | Turned in larger numbers; raw material for military capacity | Selectively turned; valued for rare skills or knowledge |
| **Feral vampires** | Regarded as catastrophic failures; eliminated quickly | Tolerated at the margins; occasionally weaponized | Regarded as tragic; the Archivist documents their condition |
| **Secret covens** | Vetharis's natural environment; they may not even call it a coven | Less common; Drakmor prefers organized units | Soleth's deep archive sites function as a form of this |

### Reputation and Diplomacy

Each house tracks reputation separately. Favorability with one house does not imply favorability with the others — and may actively harm standing with a rival house.

A party that assists Vetharis in undermining a regional governor will not be thanked by Drakmor, who was using that governor as a reliable source of mercenary contracts. A party that shares Mages Guild research with Soleth may find Vetharis's agents quietly closing doors they were not aware were open.

Diplomatic access to any house requires demonstrating something the house values: information, capability, or discretion. Trust is not given; it is purchased incrementally.
