## World Structure

The game world uses a **two-layer exploration system**.

| Layer | What it is | Design goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic Continent Map** | Travel between major locations on a continent-scale map (Solasta-inspired) | Scale without traversal tedium |
| **Regional Exploration** | Handcrafted explorable area upon arrival at each destination | Depth without empty space |

> Travel should never feel like simply moving from Point A to Point B.

## Travel Encounters

Travel is gameplay. Moving between locations on the strategic map can trigger encounters from any of the following categories.

### Combat Encounters

| Encounter | Notes |
|---|---|
| Goblin camps | Territorial; may be negotiated in some circumstances |
| Orc warbands | Organized; may have leadership worth targeting |
| Bandit ambushes | Human antagonists; motivation-dependent |
| Corrupted wildlife | Tied to The Corruption's spread in the region |
| Vampire covens | Major supernatural faction; see Vampires section |
| Werewolf packs | May include turned individuals rather than born werewolves |
| Necromancer rituals | Risk of undead escalation if not interrupted |
| Trolls | Environmental hazard; territory-based |
| Ancient monsters | Awakened by The Corruption; rare; significant threat |

### Social Encounters

| Encounter | Notes |
|---|---|
| Traveling merchants | Trade opportunities; may carry rare inventory |
| Refugees | World state indicator; may carry information or quests |
| Pilgrims | Faction-affiliated; may reveal religious plot threads |
| Wandering musicians | Lore carriers; lighten tone |
| Mercenary companies | May be hired, fought, or negotiated with |

### Discovery Encounters

| Encounter | Notes |
|---|---|
| Ancient ruins | Environmental storytelling; may conceal dungeon entrances |
| Hidden shrines | Faction or deity-affiliated; potential buffs or quest hooks |
| Forgotten watchtowers | Strategic information; may reveal map details |
| Treasure maps | Leads to hidden caches or dungeons |
| Secret caves | Hidden areas; may hold rare content |
| World bosses | Major encounters; campaign-significant |

### Companion Events

Travel triggers companion-specific moments tied to each hero's identity and history.

| Companion | Example event |
|---|---|
| **Talos** | Recognizes an old battlefield where he once fought |
| **Eleanor** | Senses a magical disturbance in the area |
| **Ragash** | Her hounds detect a hidden trail or concealed presence |
| **Ronan** | Hears distant howls that he cannot fully explain |
| **Steven** | Discovers a hidden mine entrance others have walked past |

These events are not random flavor — they are character moments that expand lore and deepen companion relationships.

## Encounter Philosophy

Not every encounter should immediately resolve into combat.

Many encounters should present **choices**:

### Example — Goblin Camp Discovered

| Option | Consequence type |
|---|---|
| Attack | Direct resolution; potential loot; eliminates threat now |
| Scout first | Information; may reveal weaknesses or alternatives |
| Sneak through | Avoids conflict; may forfeit loot or information |
| Wait until night | Different tactical context; may change enemy positioning |
| Avoid entirely | Threat persists; may grow or appear again later |

**Player decisions should create future consequences.**

Ignoring encounters is a valid choice — and sometimes the correct one. But threats that are ignored do not disappear. They grow, move, or complicate later situations.

> Randomness should enhance storytelling rather than replace it.

## Canon Regions

The following six regions are confirmed canonical locations on the continent. Each has a distinct identity, characteristic content, and a relationship to The Corruption.

---

### The Frontier

The untamed edge of civilization.

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Dangerous, lawless, contested; the place where order runs out |
| **Settlements** | Frontier towns, military outposts, refugee camps |
| **Threats** | Goblin tribes, orc warbands, ruined watchtowers, ancient battlefields, dangerous trade routes |
| **Corruption** | The Frontier often experiences The Corruption before the rest of the continent — the weakest institutions fall first |

The Frontier is where the world's decay is most visible and least managed. It is also where most campaigns begin — the place where a party of adventurers is most needed and least supported.

---

### The Great Forest

An ancient wood of significant magical and historical depth.

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Atmospheric, old, layered; beautiful and potentially dangerous |
| **Settlements** | Elven settlements; hidden communities |
| **Points of interest** | Hidden shrines, ancient ruins, rare herbs, magical wildlife |
| **Corruption** | As The Corruption spreads, portions of the forest become twisted — the natural world turning against itself |

The Great Forest is the world's most visible indicator of The Corruption's effect on nature. Players who visit early in a campaign may find it peaceful; those who return later may not recognize it.

---

### The Iron Peaks

A massive mountain range defined by the things buried within it.

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Harsh, vertical, claustrophobic underground; vast and exposed above |
| **Settlements** | Dwarven strongholds, mining operations, ancient underground cities |
| **Points of interest** | Frozen passes, endless cave systems, buried civilizations |
| **Threats** | Dragons (confirmed presence), underground hazards |
| **Companion connection** | Steven's Master Miner abilities excel here — he finds what others miss |

The Iron Peaks hold things that have not been disturbed in centuries. As The Corruption wakes ancient things, the mountains become more dangerous than anyone expected.

---

### The Frozen Highlands

Remote northern wilderness defined by endurance and isolation.

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Brutal, isolating, ancient; survival is not assumed |
| **Settlements** | Northern settlements; scattered and self-reliant |
| **Points of interest** | Ice caves, ancient fortresses |
| **Threats** | Giant beasts, harsh weather as a system, The Corruption manifesting in cold forms |

The Frozen Highlands are far enough from the continent's center that The Corruption's effects take longer to arrive — and hit harder when they do, because there is less to cushion the impact.

---

### The Desert Kingdom

A civilization built around scarcity and the things buried beneath sand.

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Ancient, mercantile, layered; a culture built on trade and secrets |
| **Settlements** | Oasis cities, merchant caravans |
| **Points of interest** | Ancient tombs, buried civilizations, forgotten temples |
| **Environmental hazards** | Sandstorms, extreme travel conditions |
| **Corruption** | The Desert Kingdom hides things — and The Corruption is waking what was buried |

---

### The Capital Province

The political center of the continent.

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Intricate, political, high-stakes; power concentrated and contested |
| **Settlements** | Great cities, noble estates |
| **Content** | Noble house politics, government, military command, trade, political intrigue |
| **Corruption** | Political Corruption is most visible here — powerful people becoming worse versions of themselves |
| **Campaign sensitivity** | The condition of The Capital Province changes depending on campaign events; it is the world's most responsive region to player choices |

The Capital Province is where decisions made everywhere else eventually land. Campaigns that end here often culminate in confrontations not just with monsters, but with the human darkness The Corruption has amplified.

## Intelligent Factions

Not every intelligent creature or group in this world is automatically evil.

The following are confirmed examples of intelligent factions that possess internal complexity:

| Faction | Design note |
|---|---|
| **Goblins** | Territorial and organized; motivation-dependent; not a monolith |
| **Orcs** | Society and politics exist; warbands are not the whole story |
| **Vampires** | Major supernatural faction; see Vampires section |
| **Werewolves** | Includes both born and turned individuals; society exists |
| **Witches** | Practice and tradition; not inherently antagonistic |
| **Necromancers** | Goal-driven; some are scholarly or philosophical |
| **Cults** | Receiving whispers from The Corruption; varied in ideology and structure |
| **Mercenary companies** | Professional; motivation is contract-driven |

### Design Principle

Every intelligent faction must possess:

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Goals** | What the faction is trying to achieve |
| **Territory** | Where they operate and why |
| **Leadership** | Who leads and how that leadership is maintained |
| **Politics** | Internal power dynamics; not every member agrees |
| **Internal conflicts** | Factions are not monoliths; dissent exists |

**Diplomacy should sometimes be possible.**

The player's first instinct should not always be violence. Some encounters that appear combat-mandatory are not, if approached correctly.

## Vampires

Vampires are one of the world's major supernatural factions.

They should **never feel like generic enemies**.

### Confirmed Vampire Types

| Type | Character |
|---|---|
| **Ancient vampire lords** | Old, deliberate, politically significant; the kind that plan in decades |
| **Vampire nobles** | Society-embedded; may be known to the world under other identities |
| **Newly turned vampires** | May not have chosen their condition; still oriented toward their former life |
| **Feral vampires** | Lost to instinct; the collapse of a person into a predator |
| **Secret covens** | Operating quietly within cities and settlements; the most dangerous kind |

### Campaign Presence

Vampire presence varies significantly between campaigns.

- Some campaigns will barely feature vampires — they exist in the world, but are not the current story
- Other campaigns may center on stopping an expanding vampire threat — an organized coven, an ancient lord's plan, a city quietly converting

This variation is part of the campaign-to-campaign replayability. Players who become expert at reading vampire politics in one campaign will bring that knowledge to the next — and find a completely different situation.
