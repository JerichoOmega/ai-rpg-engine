## ⚠️ Naming Disambiguation

Two pairs of similar names refer to distinct locations. Writers must use the full names to avoid confusion.

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Deep Warrens** | A natural cavern network beneath the Iron Peaks, predating dwarven settlement. See: Caves & Dungeons section below. |
| **Deep Warren** | The Mossroot goblin tribe's underground settlement in the Great Forest. See: [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](goblin_tribes.md). |
| **Temple of the First Dawn** | An ancient pre-Radiant-Spire temple to Solaryn, now a ruin at the Frontier/Capital border. See: Ruins section below. |
| **First Temple** | The oldest temple to the Divine Chorus, with Architect structures beneath it, located in the Capital Province. See: [`docs/lore/FIRST_TEMPLE.md`](../lore/FIRST_TEMPLE.md). |

## Lakes

### Serenity Lake

*Region: Great Forest / Frontier border*

The surface of Serenity Lake reflects the sky with uncanny clarity, undisturbed by wind even when the surrounding trees are bending. Local legends speak of figures seen standing just beneath the water's surface — watching, never rising. Scholars who have studied the lake's edge have found stonework beneath the water older than any known civilization, and have declined to publish a full account of what else they found.

---

### Frostmere

*Region: Frozen Highlands*

A wide, partially frozen lake whose center never fully freezes, even at the height of winter. The peoples of the Frozen Highlands have built their northern settlements with their backs to Frostmere, using it as a natural barrier — though what exactly they are keeping out is a matter of tradition rather than recorded history. Old songs describe a structure beneath the deepest water, but no diver has confirmed it.

---

### Stonewater Lake

*Region: Iron Peaks*

Nestled in a high mountain basin carved by ancient glaciers, Stonewater Lake is fed by underground springs running through deep mineral deposits, giving the water an extraordinary clarity and a faint mineral taste prized by local dwarves. Its depth has never been satisfactorily measured — each expedition's ropes have come up dry before reaching the bottom. The shoreline shows evidence of deliberate stonework, suggesting the lake once held significance beyond a simple water source.

---

## The Iron Peaks — Named Features

### Mount Ironheart

*Region: Iron Peaks*

The highest visible peak in the Iron Peaks range and the one most prominently seen from the western approaches, Mount Ironheart has served as a navigational landmark for travelers and merchants for generations. The dwarves regard it as sacred — not because of any surviving formal tradition, but because the oldest of their oral histories describes it as the first place their ancestors descended from after the Age of Legends. Whether this is history or myth, no one has excavated the summit to settle the question.

---

### Skyspire

*Region: Iron Peaks*

A narrow, imposingly tall peak at the northeastern edge of the Iron Peaks, distinguished by its almost perfectly vertical column of stone — a regularity that appears too precise to be entirely natural. Climbers who have attempted the summit report strange sensations of being observed and return with no memory of their final hours near the top. The dwarves leave it alone.

---

### Black Anvil Range

*Region: Iron Peaks — western sub-range*

The western sub-range of the Iron Peaks, named for its dark metallic-flecked stone and the low, rhythmic tone the wind produces as it passes through the high mountain channels. The deepest dwarven forges were once located here, and centuries of their smoke stained the stone permanently black. Those forges have been silent for generations, but the stains remain.

---

### Thundercrest Range

*Region: Iron Peaks — northern sub-range*

The northern sub-range of the Iron Peaks, where storms arriving from the frozen north collide with the mountain mass and produce spectacular, nearly continuous lightning during certain seasons. The dwarves who work these mountains have developed construction techniques specifically designed around lightning — channeling, grounding, and harvesting the electrical discharge. Travelers are advised to time their passage between storms; those who do not are seldom given a second opportunity to be advised.

---

### Iron Gate Pass

*Region: Iron Peaks*

The primary passage through the Iron Peaks for anyone moving between the continent's eastern and western reaches — a strategic and commercial chokepoint for as long as the continent has had trade. A succession of fortifications has been built, abandoned, and rebuilt at its narrowest point, each generation leaving their mark over the previous one's stonework. The current gate structures are dwarven in construction but carry older carvings beneath them that no translator has fully deciphered.

## Swamps

### Blackfen

*Region: Frontier (southwestern)*

A dark, sprawling wetland at the southwestern edge of the Frontier, defined by near-permanent mist and the dark brackish water sitting just below the waterlogged soil. Travel through Blackfen is possible but deeply unpleasant — the ground shifts without warning, old paths vanish and reform, and the local wildlife has adapted to conditions that favor ambush. During dry seasons, ruins are occasionally visible at the fen's deeper points, but approaching them is considered inadvisable.

---

### Mistfen

*Region: Great Forest / Frontier border*

A quieter and more ancient wetland than Blackfen, Mistfen sits at the edge of the Great Forest where tree roots have partially claimed the boggy ground. Its mists are thick and its interior difficult to navigate, but those who know its paths speak of rare plants found nowhere else on the continent. Old Ways practitioners are said to visit Mistfen for materials unavailable elsewhere, though they do not discuss what they find there.

---

## Plains

### Golden Plains

*Region: Capital Province / Frontier border*

Wide, rolling grassland forming the boundary between the Capital Province's cultivated farmland and the wilder Frontier beyond. The plains turn a rich amber during the harvest season, visible for miles in every direction. Caravans and military patrols are a common sight; so are the remnants of smaller forts and watchtowers that once marked territorial claims. Whatever disputes shaped those claims have largely been forgotten — the plains continue being farmed.

---

### King's Meadow

*Region: Capital Province*

A large, formally maintained grassland on the outskirts of the Imperial Capital, historically serving as a mustering ground for armies, a site for royal proclamations, and a location for public ceremonies requiring more space than the city's districts can provide. Its turf is maintained by the Crown and access is regulated. What happens in King's Meadow is always, in some sense, a matter of state.

---

### Wind Plains

*Region: Frontier (northern)*

A broad, largely featureless expanse of open grassland in the northern Frontier, named for the near-constant wind sweeping across it — strong enough to be disorienting, carrying sound from distances that make it difficult to judge where threats originate. Settlement here has always been temporary. The few permanent structures that exist are old enough that no one is certain who built them; they now serve as waypoints rather than habitations.

---

### Sunfields

*Region: Capital Province*

Rich agricultural land in the inner Capital Province, among the continent's most productive farming territory and a significant portion of the Capital's food supply. Their prosperity is maintained by the Capital's military presence and the trade networks running through them. As The Corruption spreads, the Sunfields are among the first places where agricultural disruption becomes visible — too important to ignore, and not well-defended enough to be fully protected.

---

## Sol Kareth — Named Landmarks

### Sea of Glass

*Region: Sol Kareth*

A vast expanse of desert where, in the deep past, something transformed the sand into a flat, reflective surface of fused silica stretching for miles in every direction. The event that created it is not recorded in any surviving text, and Sol Kareth's people do not discuss its origins with outsiders. During certain hours of the day, the reflections it produces create visual distortions severe enough to cause travelers to walk in circles for days. What lies beneath it, if anything, remains unknown.

---

### Scorpion Dunes

*Region: Sol Kareth*

The highest and most forbidding dune field in Sol Kareth, where sand is deep enough to swallow a person whole and the local scorpion population has adapted to a size and temperament well beyond what the species displays elsewhere. Caravans route around rather than through the Scorpion Dunes, and the routes around them are long. The dunes shift with the seasons, occasionally exposing architectural remnants that are quickly reburied — expeditions to investigate have consistently found the sand moves faster than they can excavate.

---

### Whispering Sands

*Region: Sol Kareth*

A wide basin where the desert wind produces a low, modulated tone as it passes through the surrounding rock formations — not quite voices, but close enough that travelers repeatedly report hearing their own names spoken. Old Sol Kareth merchants treat the Whispering Sands as neutral ground for negotiations, a tradition whose origin no one recalls but that everyone respects. The basin contains markers from multiple different eras, suggesting the tradition is older than Sol Kareth itself.

---

### Oasis of Kings

*Region: Sol Kareth*

The largest and most famous oasis in Sol Kareth, serving as a meeting place for trade agreements, royal negotiations, and military summits since the earliest records of desert civilization. It is maintained by a neutral council whose legitimacy derives from the oasis's water supply rather than any military authority. The council archives in the surrounding settlement hold records of every major agreement made here — not all of which have been publicly acknowledged.

---

### The Broken Obelisk

*Region: Sol Kareth (eastern desert)*

A massive stone monument at the desert's eastern edge, broken at roughly a third of its original height, the top section lying where it fell at some unknown point in the past. The carvings on its intact lower section use a script that Sol Kareth's scholars can partially read — partial because no complete guide to the script has ever been found. What has been translated suggests the obelisk marked a boundary of some kind, though between what territories or powers remains unclear.

---

## Frozen Highlands — Named Locations

### Icewind Valley

*Region: Frozen Highlands*

A deep valley that channels wind from the north into a concentrated, near-constant gale, navigable only at its edges during the coldest months and only by those who know exactly where to walk. The valley floor is not fully frozen — something below keeps it moving — and the ice walls on either side preserve whatever became encased in them during past ages. Settlements on the valley's rim exist primarily to watch what the ice occasionally gives back.

---

### Crystal Icefields

*Region: Frozen Highlands*

A wide expanse of ice whose unusual mineral composition causes it to grow in geometric formations rather than the flat planes of ordinary ice — columns, spires, and terraced shelves that rise and fall with seasonal temperature changes. The patterns have been observed by scholars to repeat in ways that appear deliberate, though no mechanism for this regularity has been identified. Those who spend extended time among the formations report difficulty distinguishing them from architecture.

---

### White Fang Ridge

*Region: Frozen Highlands*

A jagged ridge of exposed rock and permanent ice forming the highest continuously accessible line in the Frozen Highlands, serving as a navigational reference across much of the surrounding territory. The ridge marks the traditional boundary between the northern and southern settlement territories — a distinction that still carries social weight even among communities that have long since ignored it practically. The ridge is frequently dangerous and occasionally impassable, which helps the boundary remain meaningful.

---

### Frozen Crown

*Region: Frozen Highlands (far north)*

A circular arrangement of ice and stone formations at the Highlands' far northern extent, the Frozen Crown is rarely visited intentionally. The formations' arrangement is not natural — the symmetry is too precise — but who placed them and why is not recorded anywhere. Local tradition holds that the Frozen Crown was old before the people of the Frozen Highlands arrived, and that it was already abandoned when it was found.

## Coastlines

### Sapphire Coast

*Region: Southern coast (near Capital Province and Great Forest)*

A stretch of southern coastline known for the unusual blue-green color of its shallow waters, serving as a navigational reference for maritime travelers. The Sapphire Coast hosts several fishing communities and small trading ports that operate semi-independently from the Capital's political structures — near enough to benefit from protection, far enough to maintain their own arrangements. Beneath the cliff edges along the coast's southern reach lie several partially collapsed sea caves whose interiors have not been fully mapped.

---

### Storm Coast

*Region: Northern coast (near Frozen Highlands and Frontier)*

The northern coastline where weather systems arriving from the polar sea collide with the continent's landmass, producing particularly severe and prolonged storms. The Storm Coast's communities are built for conditions that would destroy more sheltered settlements; it is not a place that welcomes the unprepared. Wrecks from centuries of maritime attempts are visible at low tide along much of its length, and salvagers have been working the same coastline for generations without exhausting what it holds.

---

### The Shattered Coast

*Region: Western coast (remote)*

A coastline defined by the dramatic geological fragmentation of its cliffs and rock formations — collapsed sections, isolated sea stacks, and partially submerged ruins suggesting a far more extensive coastline existed before whatever broke it. Whether this was geological, catastrophic, or connected to the Sundering that reshaped the continent's interior is discussed among scholars without resolution. Navigation near the Shattered Coast is considered expert-level work, and most maritime routes detour significantly to avoid it.

---

### Emerald Bay

*Region: Southern coast (near Great Forest)*

A wide, sheltered bay whose waters run deep green from the concentration of sea vegetation in its shallower areas. Emerald Bay has historically been the primary maritime entry point for trade traveling to and from the Great Forest region, and its port communities are shaped by that relationship — culturally distinct from the Capital's coastal towns in ways that occasionally produce friction. The deepest parts of the bay are unusually cold, and what lives there is not well characterized.

---

## Caves & Dungeons

### Hollow Deep

*Region: Great Forest*

A cave network that descends beneath the Great Forest's root systems, accessible through entrances concealed by centuries of undergrowth. The Hollow Deep is vast enough that its full extent has never been mapped — early passages are well-documented, deeper sections less so, and the deepest areas appear on no map at all. What has been recovered from the accessible portions includes tools, remains, and architectural fragments from multiple different eras layered atop each other without obvious connection.

---

### Forgotten Halls

*Region: Iron Peaks*

An abandoned dwarven complex whose location was lost following a seismic event that blocked the primary entrance and caused the settlement records to be reclassified. Rediscovery was accidental; what was found inside raises questions that the dwarven communities have been reluctant to answer publicly. The halls are structurally intact in most sections, which is itself unusual given the time elapsed.

---

### Iron Delve

*Region: Iron Peaks*

A large-scale mining operation expanded progressively deeper over several centuries before operations ceased under circumstances that the dwarven histories describe differently depending on which community is consulted. The upper levels are occasionally used by salvagers; the lower levels are actively discouraged. What the deeper sections contain — aside from rich and unmined ore deposits — has not been formally established.

---

### The Deep Warrens

*Region: Iron Peaks (deep caverns)*

> **Naming note:** "The Deep Warrens" refers to this Iron Peaks cave network. "Deep Warren" is the Mossroot goblin settlement in the Great Forest. Use the full name "The Deep Warrens" when referring to this location.

A natural cavern network running beneath the lowest dwarven settlements in the Iron Peaks, far below the inhabited tunnels. The Deep Warrens predate dwarven settlement in the region — their geometry suggests something other than water erosion — and their full extent is unknown. Dwarven frontier patrols monitor the upper connections to the Warrens; what they have actually encountered in the deeper sections is not publicly shared.

---

### Shadow Cavern

*Region: Frontier (eastern)*

A cave system in the eastern Frontier whose interior is unusually dark even by cavern standards — torches and lanterns produce light, but it seems to carry less distance inside than it should. Local guides who know Shadow Cavern are available but selective about who they will take in. What is inside has economic value, but the working conditions are considered a significant drawback.

---

### Ember Hollow

*Region: Sol Kareth*

A cave complex beneath the desert where geothermal activity produces heat without visible flame — the walls are warm, the air is dry and sharp, and the deeper chambers maintain a constant temperature that once made them useful for specific types of storage. The people who used it for this purpose are no longer present. Some of what they stored remains.

---

### Whispering Caves

*Region: Sol Kareth (near Whispering Sands)*

A cave network at the edge of the Whispering Sands basin whose chambers produce acoustic effects that carry sound from distant sections to unexpected locations. Whether this was engineered or natural is genuinely unclear — the cave walls show evidence of both deliberate shaping and natural settling. The effect can be useful or deeply disorienting depending on whether one understands how the sound moves.

---

### Titan's Tomb

*Region: Sol Kareth*

A burial complex of unusual scale, constructed for someone or something much larger than any recorded historical figure. The scale alone has made it a subject of significant archaeological interest; the traps and seals remaining on the inner chambers have made that interest difficult to act on. What is interred here, and whether it remains so, is one of the desert's more persistent open questions.

---

## Ruins

### The Fallen City

*Region: Frontier*

The most extensive ruin in the Frontier — the remains of a city whose name has not survived, discovered beneath layers of encroaching vegetation and sediment. The scale of the construction suggests it was once among the larger settlements on the continent. When it fell and what caused its fall is not established in any surviving record. Its streets are intact enough to navigate; many of its structures are not.

---

### Old Aranor

*Region: Great Forest*

The remains of an elvish settlement older than any surviving elvish community on the continent, partially absorbed by the forest that has grown through and over it for centuries. The Great Forest's Old Ways practitioners consider Old Aranor significant but speak of it with reluctance — acknowledging it as part of their heritage while not welcoming visitors. What is preserved inside the forest-claimed structures has not been fully catalogued.

---

### Sunken Temple

*Region: Sapphire Coast (partially submerged)*

> **Note:** The Sunken Temple is a coastal ruin distinct from the First Temple (the oldest temple to the Divine Chorus, with Architect structures beneath it). They are different locations with different histories.

A temple complex now sitting partially below the waterline along the Sapphire Coast, visible from the cliffs during low tide and requiring diving to access at high tide. Whether it was built at the coast and then submerged by geological change, or built inland and the land changed around it, has not been resolved. The iconography inside does not match any of the four established religious traditions — some scholars suggest it predates them.

---

### Kingsfall

*Region: Frontier (northern)*

A battlefield and collapsed fortification in the northern Frontier where, by most accounts, a kingdom's final military stand took place during the wars of the Age of Restoration. The specific kingdom and conflict are disputed — several different traditions claim this as the site of their own history's defining defeat. The battlefield is large enough that portions remain unexamined, and the fortification's lower levels remain sealed.

---

### Ashwatch Keep

*Region: Frontier (near Cinderhold and Ashfire territory)*

A watch fortress at the edge of the Ashfire goblin tribe's territory, built during a period when this particular approach to the central Frontier was considered strategically significant. The Keep changed hands several times and is now abandoned — though "abandoned" in the Frontier means something different than it does elsewhere. Those who last held it left in some haste; what they left behind has been partially sorted through by subsequent visitors.

---

### Grey Fortress

*Region: Frozen Highlands (southern approach)*

A military fortification at the southern approach to the Frozen Highlands, built from the region's characteristic grey stone and designed to be operated by a small garrison under conditions that make reinforcement difficult. It held for a long time. What ended it is not in any text that has reached the Capital's archives — the Highlands communities who remember are not forthcoming, and the Fortress itself has been stripped of anything that might have explained it.

---

### Temple of the First Dawn

*Region: Frontier / Capital Province border*

> **Note:** The Temple of the First Dawn is distinct from the First Temple (the continent's oldest temple to the Divine Chorus, with Architect structures beneath it). They are different buildings from different eras.

An ancient temple to Solaryn, pre-dating the Radiant Spire in the Capital by a significant margin, whose existence was documented in the Great Library's restricted records long before its physical location was confirmed. The temple was built facing east, precisely aligned to a particular seasonal sunrise, and its interior architecture amplifies and focuses that first light in ways that surviving scholars can observe but not fully explain. The Solari Covenant acknowledges its history but does not discuss its original practices.

---

### The Forgotten Citadel

*Region: Frozen Highlands (far north)*

A fortification at the far northern edge of the Frozen Highlands whose construction suggests military purpose but whose scale exceeds any defensive need relative to the surrounding territory. It is larger than Grey Fortress and better preserved, which is unusual given its far greater remoteness. The Frozen Crown formation is visible from its highest point on clear days. Local tradition holds that the Citadel was here before the people who built Grey Fortress arrived, which — if true — makes its builders unknown.
