# Halfling Trading Houses

## Overview

Halfling society is organized around family trading houses rather than territory (see `RACES.md`). "Major house" is not a legal category — it is a Reckoning category: a house is major when the other major houses settle debts with it directly rather than through an intermediary. By that measure there are four major houses at present, and everyone at the Reckoning can name them in the order they would prefer you didn't ask about.

The houses compete on overlapping routes while maintaining the outward courtesy the culture demands. Feuds are conducted in ledgers, contract terms, and seating arrangements — almost never in the open, and never at a laden table.

|---|---|---|
| **Brambleford** | The central river corridor — barge lines, ferries, floating markets | Odessa Brambleford |

## House Brambleford

**Route focus:** The continent's central river corridor — the heaviest concentration of halfling life on the continent (`RACES.md`). Brambleford barges, ferry rights, and floating market platforms move more tonnage than any other halfling operation. If it travels by water through the middle of the continent, a Brambleford has probably touched it, taxed it, or fed the person carrying it.

**Current head:** **Odessa Brambleford** — third head in living memory, in the seat for over twenty years. Odessa is the closest thing halfling society has to an elder stateswoman: she has hosted the Reckoning twice, her table is the standard other laden tables are measured against, and she has a reputation for settling other houses' disputes almost as a courtesy. She is warm, unhurried, and has never once forgotten a debt.

**Internal tension:** The Settled and the Road, in its sharpest form. Brambleford wealth has made much of the family sedentary — canal-side compounds, market-quarter townhouses, children who have never worked a barge season. Odessa's presumptive heirs split along exactly this line: one has run the river routes for fifteen years; the other has tripled the house's fixed holdings in the Capital and considers the barge tradition sentimental overhead. Odessa has not indicated a choice, and the house quietly understands that whichever way she decides, the other half of the family will feel the house has stopped being what it was.

## House Copperwade

**Route focus:** The overland corridor from the Iron Peaks to the Capital Province — the centuries-old commercial relationship that moves dwarven metalwork, stone goods, and gnomish instruments out of the mountains (`RACES.md`, Relations: Dwarves). Copperwade wagon trains are the reason a Capital smith can buy Iron Peaks steel without ever meeting a dwarf. The house's standing rests on its dwarven relationships, some of which are older than the current dwarven clan heads who honor them.

**Current head:** **Perrin Copperwade** — elderly, exacting, and by broad agreement the finest contract mind of his generation. Perrin negotiates the way dwarves forge: slowly, and for permanence. His Book of Memory annotations are studied by other houses' heirs when they can get access, which is never.

**Internal tension:** Perrin has two capable children and has refused, for eleven years, to designate which one inherits the Book of Memory. The elder holds the dwarven relationships; the younger holds the Capital contracts. Perrin's stated position is that the road always continues and the question will settle itself. The rest of the house has noticed that Perrin is now very old, the question has not settled itself, and a Copperwade succession dispute conducted at a Reckoning would be the commercial event of the decade — for every house except Copperwade.

## House Fenholt

**Route focus:** The Frontier — wayhouses on the edge of settled land, supply runs to outposts and refugee settlements, and the routes other houses refuse to price. Fenholt's business model is simple: where the risk is highest, the margin is highest, and someone has to feed the edge of the world. Fenholt wayhouses double as informal Adventurers Guild contact points more often than any other house's (`RACES.md`, Relations).

**Current head:** **Delvey Fenholt** — younger than any other major-house head, in the seat six years after her predecessor died on the road. Delvey is blunt by halfling standards, has personally driven every route the house runs, and is regarded by the older houses with the specific unease reserved for someone whose gambles keep paying off.

**Internal tension:** Some Fenholt routes stay open only because of quiet arrangements the house does not discuss — including tribute paid on the eastern trade road that passes under Stonefang raiding range (see [`goblin_tribes.md`](goblin_tribes.md), Stonefang controlled resources). The arrangements are pragmatic, ledger-sound, and profoundly deniable. If the human settlements Fenholt supplies ever confirmed that their supply line is partly financed by payments to the goblins raiding them, the house's Frontier standing would take damage no margin covers. Delvey inherited the arrangements; she has neither ended them nor entered them in the Book of Memory, and the omission itself is a breach of house tradition that only she and her route-masters know about.

## House Marchbanks

**Route focus:** The interchange where the river networks meet Sol Kareth's caravan trade — the docks, warehouses, and transfer contracts that turn river cargo into desert cargo and back. Marchbanks does not run the desert routes; it owns the point everything passes through on the way to them. The house's other stock in trade is quieter: information moves through an interchange as reliably as goods, and Marchbanks has never been careless with either.

**Current head:** **Posy Marchbanks** — polished, deliberate, and the house's fourth consecutive head to maintain the family's discreet understanding with the Mages Guild (`RACES.md`, Relations: the Guild has quietly cultivated relationships with several major trading houses — Marchbanks is the principal one). Posy regards the arrangement as ordinary route maintenance: the Guild pays well, asks little, and the house has never sold anything it promised to keep.

**Internal tension:** A growing faction within the house — led by Posy's own cousin, who runs the Sol Kareth dockside operation — argues that the Guild relationship has quietly inverted: the house no longer sells the Guild information, the Guild directs what the house collects. Discretion is a halfling value; being someone's instrument is not. Posy maintains that the ledger still favors the house. The cousin has begun keeping a private record of Guild requests, which is either due diligence or the first page of a schism, depending on who eventually reads it.

## The Brambleford–Copperwade Rivalry: The Disputed Page

The continent's defining halfling feud, four generations old, conducted with perfect courtesy.

**The origin:** A joint venture — Copperwade cargo on Brambleford barges during a season of flooded roads — ended with a sunken barge, a lost consignment of dwarven commission work, and a debt. Both houses recorded the settlement in their Books of Memory. The two Books do not agree. Brambleford's page records the debt discharged in full the following spring. Copperwade's page records a partial payment and a balance outstanding — a balance that, with four generations of customary interest, is now a figure neither house says aloud.

**Why it cannot be resolved:** A halfling house's Book of Memory is part of what the house *is* (`RACES.md`). For either house to accept the other's page is to concede that its own Book contains a false entry — a wound to the house itself, categorically worse than any sum. Third-party arbitration has been proposed at three separate Reckonings. Both houses have declined each time, politely, for exactly the same reason.

**How it is conducted:** Never openly. The houses attend the same Reckoning, share the same tables, and marry into the same families. The feud lives in undercutting on contested contracts, in whose barges carry whose goods at what rate, in which house is invited to host, and in a thousand small courtesies performed with exquisite precision. Every major house takes quiet advantage of it; Fenholt in particular has built a profitable sideline carrying cargo neither rival will consign to the other.

**The hook writers should know:** Persistent halfling rumor holds that a first-generation Brambleford barge-master's personal ledger — which would show what was actually paid — survived and is out there: in a flooded wayhouse cellar, in a gnomish archive's uncatalogued acquisitions, in an estate lot sold four owners ago. Both houses deny looking for it. Both houses are looking for it. Neither is certain it wants to be the one that finds it, because the page it confirms destroys one Book's integrity forever — and the houses have built four generations of identity on the dispute itself.

## Using the Houses in Play

- **A traveling merchant encounter** (`WORLD_BIBLE.md`, Social Encounters) can now be a named house wagon or barge: a Copperwade train with dwarven cargo and an heir aboard proving themselves; a Fenholt supply run that knows more about the road ahead than any patrol; a Marchbanks factor who pays for news in coin or in kind; a Brambleford ferry where the pilot remembers the party's last crossing — and what they said during it.
- **Wayhouses are stages.** A Fenholt wayhouse on the Frontier is a natural quest hub: Guild notices, refugee information, and a house-master who hears everything that moves on the road.
- **The Reckoning is an event.** A party invited to (or hired to work security for, or asked to quietly investigate something at) a Reckoning has walked into all four houses' tensions in one neutral hall.
- **Reputation:** the houses do not have system reputation tracks; standing with them is narrative, carried by the halfling Memory value — they remember names, debts, favors, and insults across generations, which the AI DM should honor literally.
