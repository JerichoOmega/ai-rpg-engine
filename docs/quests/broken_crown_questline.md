# The Broken Crown — Named Questline: *The Unsigned Order*

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — 2026-08-02.
> **Authority:** This document defines the canonical entry questline for the Broken Crown dynamic story arc (Political Corruption, the Capital). All AI DM and writer content involving unlisted detentions, the Continuity Directives evidence trail, or the NPCs named here should use these beats and resolution paths. Do not invent Broken Crown activity that contradicts what is established here.

> **Scope note:** This questline builds the evidence trail that leads toward the Talos–Voss meeting in the Capital. It deliberately does **not** author that meeting, the specific order Voss once gave, or Commander Vael's Frontier scene — those are defined (or reserved for definition) in `docs/heroes/TALOS.md` and their own tasks. ## Questline Overview

**Name:** *The Unsigned Order*
**Source arc:** The Broken Crown — Political Corruption (`docs/systems/dynamic_story_arcs.md`)
**Region:** The Imperial Capital and the garrison town of **Lowbridge** on the eastern road
**Entry hook:** A hunted veteran of Talos's old regiment brings proof that soldiers are vanishing into custody that officially does not exist
**Companions centered:** Talos (institution he served), Eleanor (reform vs. rot), with authored beats for Ronan, Ragash, and Torren

**Narrative rationale.** The Broken Crown arc is about Corruption amplifying institutional virtues — loyalty, discipline, the protective instinct — until the institution begins consuming the people it exists to protect (Corruption-as-Imbalance). *The Unsigned Order* makes that abstraction concrete and personal: the people being consumed are soldiers from Talos's own past, and every antagonist in the questline sincerely believes they are doing what is necessary. There is no cackling villain. That is the point.

**Tone.** Procedural dread, not dungeon crawl. The horror is paperwork with no signature on it. Combat is available but almost always represents a failure state or a last resort; the arc's enemy types (corrupted enforcers, political inquisitors, loyalist officers, informant networks) are disciplined people who are wrong, and the questline should keep reminding the player of that.

## The Corruption Pattern: "Continuity Measures"

Somewhere inside Military High Command, a doctrine has quietly hardened. Soldiers and junior officers who ask the wrong questions — about withdrawn garrisons, sealed reports, orders that arrive without provenance — are being reclassified as "continuity risks" and transferred into custody that appears on no roster. Their families are told they deserted. The paperwork exists, but it is *unsigned*: transfer ledgers with no issuing authority, sealed with an old High Command continuity mark that predates the current reign and answers to no living office.

This is not a conspiracy in the theatrical sense. It is the Corruption doing what it does to institutions ([`docs/systems/dynamic_story_arcs.md`](../systems/dynamic_story_arcs.md)): amplifying the protective instinct of loyal officers until "protect the realm" and "silence the realm's doubts" become the same act. Most participants handle only their fragment of the process and believe the rest of the chain is lawful.

**What this questline establishes (and no more):** the existence of the unsigned-transfer pattern, one detention site, and the evidence trail that points *upward* — toward High Command and Marshal Voss — without yet establishing what Voss knows, ordered, or is. That ceiling is deliberate. (Preserving Wonder: the player should end this questline certain something is wrong at the top and unable to prove exactly what.)

## Named NPCs

**Corporal Dess Maran** — the hook. A veteran of Talos's old regiment — one of the soldiers who served under his eastern command in the last years before he left the service. Weeks ago Maran started asking where a friend from her old company had been transferred; three days ago her own name appeared on an unsigned ledger. She has been running since. She is not brave by temperament — she is stubborn, which under enough pressure looks the same. She seeks out Talos because he is the only officer she ever served under who she is certain would rather break than sign.

**Captain Aldous Cantrell** — the loyalist officer. Commander of the Ostmark Granary detention site. He and Talos served in the same theater during the wars of the Age of Restoration; they were never friends, but they respected each other's competence, which between career soldiers is more durable. Cantrell's protective instinct has been Corruption-amplified into something airless: he genuinely believes the detainees are safest *in his custody*, because whatever office produced the unsigned ledgers might otherwise produce something worse. He is a jailer who thinks he is a shelter. He follows the questline's central pattern — a good virtue, curdled — and he is the questline's test of the Choice vs. Nature pillar: he can be reached, at cost, because nothing in him is *inherently* rotten.

**Under-Registrar Ferra Sarnholt** — the frightened collaborator. A civil clerk in the Undervault, the sealed records level beneath the Capital's civil registry. Sarnholt noticed the pattern in the filings months ago — transfers logged as received with no originating office — and has been carefully *mis*-filing copies ever since, an act of resistance so small she can pretend to herself it isn't one. She is the informant-network enemy type inverted: proof that the same fear that makes collaborators can make witnesses.

**The detainees** — eleven soldiers and two junior officers held at Ostmark, including **Sergeant Wick Halloway**, the friend Maran went asking about. None of them know why they are held. That is not a mystery to be solved; it is the fact the questline turns on. There was never a reason a court would recognize.

## Named Documents (in-world handouts)

1. **The Unsigned Ledger** — Maran's stolen page: a transfer manifest listing seven names (hers among the appended), destination coded, no issuing authority, sealed with the High Command continuity mark. Talos recognizes the mark on sight; he has seen it exactly once before, and where he saw it is his to say or not say (AI DM: do not force the disclosure — offer it as a Talos trust beat).
2. **The Undervault Reciprocals** — Sarnholt's mis-filed copies: eleven months of received-transfer records with the originating-office line blank. Together they turn one ledger into a *pattern*. This is the document that makes the reform path possible.
3. **Cantrell's Standing Instruction** — a single sheet posted in the Ostmark commandant's office: custody procedures, humane in every particular, thorough in every particular, and nowhere bearing a name, a seal, or a lawful basis. The most damning document in the questline is the one that proves the jailer wrote his own rules because no one above him would.

## Narrative Beats

### Beat 1 — The Hook: A Name on the Ledger (the Capital)

Maran makes contact — in a Capital tavern, a temple porch, or wherever the party's routine makes plausible — and she makes contact *badly*: exhausted, half-convinced Talos will turn her in, leading with the ledger because she no longer trusts her own account of events. If Talos is in the party, she addresses him by his old rank. If he is not present, she waits until he is; this hook is his.

**What the party learns:** the unsigned ledger; Halloway's disappearance; that families are being told "desertion" — which, for soldiers, is not just a lie but a *dishonoring* one, the kind of detail that makes Ragash and Ronan look up from the table.

**The immediate problem:** Maran's name is on the appended list. She is being looked for by men with good boots and no insignia. The party's first choice is small and characteristic: hide her, move her, or use her as bait for the followers — and the AI DM should let that choice color how much she trusts the party for the rest of the questline.

**Investigation threads opened (any order):**
- **The mark** — Talos's recognition of the continuity seal (trust beat, optional disclosure).
- **The registry** — all transfers, even false ones, generate reciprocal records; someone in the civil registry has been receiving these. Leads to the Undervault.
- **The road** — the destination code on the ledger matches an eastern-road provisioning route. Leads to Lowbridge.

### Beat 2 — The Pattern: The Undervault and Lowbridge

**The Undervault.** The sealed records level is a legitimate government archive — hushed, lawful, and guarded by clerks rather than soldiers, which makes it the questline's proof that not every locked door in the Capital hides malice (Living World: the registry has its own routines, feuds, and tea schedule; play them). Finding Sarnholt is a social discovery, not a heist: she has been waiting eleven months for someone to ask the right question and is terrified of being the one to answer it. Winning the Reciprocals means winning *her* — persuasion, a demonstrated ability to protect her, or Eleanor's transparent sincerity are all valid keys. Stealing the records without her costs the reform path its living witness (see Outcomes).

**Lowbridge.** A garrison town gone quiet in the wrong way: gate checks doubled, market half-empty, garrison soldiers who won't meet a veteran's eye. The Ostmark Granary — a fortified provisioning depot on the town's edge — has been "closed for stores audit" for most of a year. Locals have learned not to look at it. The town is the compromised-garrison-town location type from the arc definition, and it should feel *administered* rather than occupied: nothing here is illegal-looking, which is the problem.

**The Cantrell parley (optional, strongly signposted).** Cantrell knows within a day that veterans are asking questions, and he does the in-character thing: he invites Talos to dinner. The scene is the questline's centerpiece parley — two competent soldiers, one curdled premise. Cantrell lays out his sincere case (the detainees are safer here than wherever the unsigned chain would send them next; he has kept every one of them alive and fed; what exactly does Talos propose to release them *into?*) and in doing so hands the party Standing Instruction–level knowledge of the site. Whether the party leaves the dinner as guests or fugitives depends on how hard they push. Cantrell does not arrest guests at his own table — his virtues are real, which is what the Corruption had to work with.

**The camp scene (authored companion beat — mandatory before Beat 3).** The arc definition requires it and this questline places it here: the night after Lowbridge, Eleanor and Talos argue.
- **Eleanor's position:** the Undervault *worked*. The registry kept honest records even of dishonest acts; Sarnholt mis-filed copies at real risk; the institution is full of people holding the line — the rot is a wound in the body, not the body. Take the evidence to the civil magistracy and let the realm heal itself.
- **Talos's position:** every document they hold is proof the institution already routed around its own laws once. Magistrates need weeks; the detainees have whatever time the unsigned chain gives them. You do not reform a fire out of a house with people inside it. Get them out; argue about the realm after.
- **The rule (from the arc definition):** neither is written as right. The AI DM must not resolve the argument for the player, and companions do not swing to consensus. The scene ends with the "harder kind of respect" beat: each concedes the other's position is the honest version of something. The player's Beat 3 choice is then made *inside* that unresolved argument, not above it.
- Ronan, if present, says the quiet thing: he understands Cantrell better than he'd like to — knowing what it is to fear what your own hands might do, and to prefer a cage you control. Ragash keeps returning to Lowbridge's civilians: whatever the party does at Ostmark, the town gets the aftermath. Torren notes the material fact everyone else stepped over: the granary is *provisioning infrastructure* — the eastern road eats from it, and a siege or a fire there hurts people who have never heard of any of this.

### Beat 3 — The Resolution: Ostmark Granary

The party moves on the detention site. Three authored paths, freely mixable at the AI DM's discretion; the tiers in the Outcomes section describe results, not required scripts.

- **The Reform Path (Eleanor's argument):** deliver the Unsigned Ledger, the Undervault Reciprocals, and (ideally) Sarnholt's testimony to uncorrupted civil magistrates in the Capital, obtaining a lawful writ for the release of the Ostmark detainees. Slowest, safest for the town, and the only path that attacks the *pattern* rather than the site. Its risk is time (see Escalation) and exposure: filing the evidence tells the unsigned chain exactly what the party holds.
- **The Extraction Path (Talos's argument):** get the detainees out quietly — over the wall, through the provisioning gate, or by turning Cantrell's own humane procedures against the site (a medical transfer, a stores run). Fastest and it saves *these* people; it leaves the pattern intact, proves nothing, and makes fugitives of thirteen soldiers whose families were already told they deserted.
- **The Confrontation Path:** force, open or threatened. Ostmark's garrison are loyalist soldiers, not cultists; every one of them killed is the Choice vs. Nature pillar presenting the bill. Cantrell will fight for his detainees' safety as he understands it — unless the parley happened and went well, in which case there is a narrow authored line where he stands his men down and surrenders custody *to Talos personally*, on the logic that has governed him throughout: they are safest with the most trustworthy jailer available. What breaking that logic open does to Cantrell afterward is an Aftermath thread.

**Cantrell's resolution:** reachable, not redeemable-by-default. The best outcomes turn him into the reform path's most devastating witness — the officer who ran the site testifying that no lawful order ever arrived. The worst outcomes leave him dead defending a shelter that was always a cage, or loose and doubling down. The AI DM should protect one truth in every branch: Cantrell never once acts from malice.

**The ceiling (mandatory):** every path ends with the same discovery — the trail above Ostmark goes into High Command and stops at the continuity mark. No document names Voss. No confession reaches him. The questline's final beat is Talos looking at where the trail ends, and knowing the name of the office it ends outside of. (Hook: the Talos–Voss Capital meeting; see Future Hooks.)

## Outcomes — Quest Integration Standard

Per [`docs/systems/quests.md`](../systems/quests.md): five tiers plus Escalation (inaction). Regional changes use visible channels per [`docs/design/VISIBLE_CONSEQUENCES.md`](../design/VISIBLE_CONSEQUENCES.md); World State categories per [`docs/design/world_state/WORLD_STATE_SYSTEM.md`](../design/world_state/WORLD_STATE_SYSTEM.md).

### Excellent — *The Writ and the Wall*
Detainees freed lawfully **and** the pattern exposed: magistrates' writ served at Ostmark with the party present, Sarnholt and Cantrell both standing as witnesses, Maran's name cleared on the record.

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Immediate consequences** | The writ is read at the granary gate; Cantrell stands his garrison down and surrenders the Standing Instruction as evidence; Halloway walks out to Maran waiting; the "deserter" notices are publicly rescinded. |
| **Long-term consequences** | Weeks later: a civil inquiry into "continuity" filings opens in the Capital; unsigned-transfer activity goes dormant in the eastern provinces — dormant, not dead. Sarnholt is promoted into the inquiry as records custodian. Cantrell awaits judgment as a cooperating witness. |
| **World State impact** | Hope and Political Stability rise in the Capital region (the system visibly corrected itself); Security ticks down short-term (High Command resents civil oversight); Knowledge Preservation rises (the Reciprocals enter the legal record). |
| **Faction reactions** | Civil magistracy: strong trust in the party. Military High Command: cold, formal, and newly *aware* of the party by name. Solari Covenant Inquisitors: uncomfortable — the case is a mirror. Veterans' circles: quiet, durable gratitude. |
| **Companion reactions** | See Companion Reactions — Excellent/Good column. |
| **Regional changes (visible channels)** | Lowbridge: gate checks return to normal and the Ostmark "stores audit" hoardings come down (checkpoint channel); Capital: rescinded desertion notices posted on garrison boards (proclamation channel). |
| **Journal updates** | Entry at writ service; ripple entries when the inquiry opens and when Lowbridge normalizes. |

### Good — *Out the Gate*
Detainees freed (lawfully or by clean extraction) and the evidence preserved, but the pattern not yet formally exposed — the writ came too late, or the party extracted first and filed after, weakening the case.

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Immediate consequences** | All thirteen detainees out alive; Ostmark stands but empties; Cantrell relieved of a custody he can no longer justify to himself — his surrender or standing-down plays per the path taken. |
| **Long-term consequences** | The freed soldiers scatter to families and safehouses; the inquiry stalls at "irregularities noted" without a live site to inspect; the unsigned chain adapts — future transfers route around civil registry reciprocals, making the *next* pattern harder to prove. |
| **World State impact** | Hope rises locally (people came back); Political Stability unchanged (nothing was corrected on the record); Security unchanged; Knowledge Preservation rises slightly (evidence archived, unused). |
| **Faction reactions** | Magistracy: sympathetic but constrained. High Command: officially silent, unofficially probing who moved against Ostmark. Veterans' circles: the story travels by word of mouth, growing less accurate and more loyal to Talos with each telling. |
| **Companion reactions** | See Companion Reactions — Excellent/Good column, with Eleanor's addendum: she does not say "we should have waited." She thinks it where Talos can see. |
| **Regional changes (visible channels)** | Lowbridge: the granary reopens as an actual granary within a season (livelihood channel); Capital: no visible change — which Eleanor will name aloud as exactly the problem. |
| **Journal updates** | Entry at extraction/release; ripple entry when the inquiry stalls. |

### Mixed — *The Ledger or the Lives*
One goal won at the other's cost: detainees freed but the evidence lost/discredited (Sarnholt burned as a source, documents seized), **or** the pattern exposed but detainees transferred beyond reach before release could be won.

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Immediate consequences** | Per branch: an empty granary and a dead case, or a live inquiry and a cold trail. Maran gets one of the two things she came for, and the scene where she learns which one is authored to be quiet, not grateful. |
| **Long-term consequences** | The surviving half-victory calcifies: freed men live as unofficial fugitives, or the inquiry proceeds over an absence — findings without survivors to apply them to. Cantrell's fate follows the branch: fugitive-adjacent guilt, or star witness at a trial about people he can no longer produce. |
| **World State impact** | Hope and Political Stability move in opposite directions (one up, one down, per branch); Security down slightly in the eastern corridor (the chain reacted); Knowledge Preservation per evidence branch. |
| **Faction reactions** | Magistracy or veterans' circles (whichever was served) warm; the other cools measurably. High Command treats the party as a named irritant. Sarnholt's treatment becomes a reputation fact: sources notice what happened to the last source. |
| **Companion reactions** | See Companion Reactions — Mixed column; this is the tier where the camp-scene argument reopens, and the AI DM should let it. |
| **Regional changes (visible channels)** | Lowbridge: partial normalization only — gate checks ease but the granary stays sealed, or vice versa (checkpoint + livelihood channels split). |
| **Journal updates** | Entry at the resolution; ripple entry when the cost of the lost half lands (a fugitive arrested, or findings shelved). |

### Poor — *Ash in the Registry*
Detainees mostly lost (transferred, scattered, or dead in a botched assault) and the evidence compromised; the party survives with fragments.

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Immediate consequences** | Ostmark either empty (moved overnight — the unsigned chain works fast when warned) or a battlefield; casualties among detainees and/or garrison per the path; Sarnholt vanishes from her desk within the week. Maran's name stays on the ledger. |
| **Long-term consequences** | The pattern hardens and *learns*: future continuity detentions leave no reciprocals at all. Talos carries a second failed defense of his own people. The Lowbridge garrison is quietly rotated and replaced wholesale. |
| **World State impact** | Hope falls (people vanished and nothing answered for it); Political Stability falls (the lawful path was seen to fail); Security nominally rises in Capital reporting — the arc's signature perversity: the World State's official channels record this as order restored. |
| **Faction reactions** | Magistracy: burned, distant. High Command: no longer probing — *acting*; the party should expect the arc's enemy types proactively hereafter. Veterans' circles: grief, and a colder strain of loyalty to Talos that he visibly does not want. |
| **Companion reactions** | See Companion Reactions — Poor/Disastrous column. |
| **Regional changes (visible channels)** | Lowbridge: garrison replacement is visible on the walls within weeks (uniform/patrol channel); Capital: a new "continuity office" doorway appears in the High Command annex — unmarked (construction channel). |
| **Journal updates** | Entry at the loss; ripple entries at Sarnholt's disappearance and the garrison rotation. |

### Disastrous — *The Fire at the Granary*
Open assault gone wrong or catastrophe deliberately caused: Ostmark burns with detainees inside, or the fight spills into Lowbridge itself.

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Immediate consequences** | Detainee and civilian deaths; the granary — eastern-road provisioning infrastructure, as Torren warned — destroyed; the party publicly blamed, plausibly, because the visible facts support it. |
| **Long-term consequences** | The unsigned chain achieves what it could never have engineered: a lawful pretext. Continuity detentions expand under emergency provisions with genuine public support. Cantrell, if he survives, becomes the pattern's sincerest advocate — he was *right* that release was the danger, and the Corruption feasts on his vindication. |
| **World State impact** | Hope, Prosperity (eastern corridor provisioning), and Political Stability all fall; Security "rises" in official channels while lived security collapses — maximum divergence between the recorded world and the real one. |
| **Faction reactions** | Magistracy closes to the party. High Command designates them continuity risks — the questline's machinery turned on its investigators. Lowbridge remembers the fire, not the reasons. Veterans' circles fracture over Talos's name. |
| **Companion reactions** | See Companion Reactions — Poor/Disastrous column, plus authored breaks: Eleanor's crisis of confidence in the party (not in reform); Ragash's explicit civilian-cost reckoning; a genuine Talos–player trust rupture if the fire traces to player choice. |
| **Regional changes (visible channels)** | Lowbridge: burn scar and food-price spikes down the eastern road (ruin + market channels); Capital: emergency-provision proclamations posted (proclamation channel). |
| **Journal updates** | Entry at the catastrophe; ripple entries as emergency provisions expand and prices move. |

### Escalation — Inaction
If the party shelves the questline after Beat 1:

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Immediate consequences** | Maran is taken within days — quietly, professionally, from wherever the party left her. Her name moves from the ledger's appended list to its body. |
| **Long-term consequences** | Halloway and the Ostmark detainees are dispersed beyond any single-site rescue; Sarnholt, never found, keeps mis-filing alone until she isn't there anymore; the pattern spreads to a second province. The questline can be re-entered later at Mixed ceiling at best. |
| **World State impact** | Political Stability and Hope decay slowly, registered as scattered disappearance rumors rather than events — the Corruption's preferred pace. |
| **Faction reactions** | None visible. That silence is the content: the pattern's entire design is that inaction reads as normalcy. |
| **Companion reactions** | Talos raises Maran's name unprompted at two later camps — the second time, he stops mid-sentence and doesn't finish. No mechanical penalty; the authored cost is that scene. |
| **Regional changes (visible channels)** | Lowbridge gate checks spread to a second eastern town (checkpoint channel, delayed). |
| **Journal updates** | A single dated entry when word of Maran's disappearance filters back. |

## AI DM Integration

**Availability:** From the party's first extended Capital stay after the Broken Crown arc activates. Beat 1 requires Talos in the roster; the hook waits for him.

**Timers (soft, fiction-first):**
- Maran's safety runs on a clock from Beat 1 (see Escalation).
- From the moment the party's investigation becomes visible to the chain (the parley, an Undervault filing, or a loud mistake in Lowbridge), Ostmark begins preparing to transfer its detainees. Reform-path delay past that point degrades the achievable tier from Excellent toward Mixed. The AI DM should telegraph this through fiction (wagons at the granary, Cantrell's dinner invitation arriving *sooner*), never through exposed mechanics.

**Persistence:** Named NPC fates, the granary's condition, Lowbridge's checkpoint state, the evidence's legal status, and Maran's ledger status persist in world state and must be honored by all subsequent Capital and eastern-road content.

**Enemy mechanics note:** All encounters use existing engine mechanics (standard and elite enemy stat adjustments per established combat rules). The arc's enemy *types* (corrupted enforcers, political inquisitors, informant networks) are narrative framings over existing mechanics, not new systems. Any future distinct mechanical identity for these types is an AI-DM overlay pending engine support.

**Hard rules for the AI DM:**
1. Never resolve the Eleanor–Talos argument. Not in this questline, not in its aftermath.
2. Never let any document or confession name Voss. The trail ends at the continuity mark.
3. Cantrell never acts from malice, in any branch.
4. Inaction is an outcome, not a pause. Run the Escalation table.

## Lore Impact Report

*Per the project's content-production standard: every quest ends with this report.*

- **Does this quest establish new canon?** Quest-level only: the Continuity Measures detention pattern, four named NPCs (Maran, Cantrell, Sarnholt, Halloway), two named sites (the Undervault beneath the Capital civil registry; Ostmark Granary), and the garrison town of Lowbridge on the eastern road. No pillar, faction-structure, cosmology, or timeline canon is created or altered. The questline deliberately establishes a *ceiling*: nothing here defines what Voss knows or did.
- **Does it reference existing canon?** The Broken Crown arc definition (enemy types, locations, companion dynamics, the mandatory camp scene); Talos's backstory (his regiment, the wars of the Age of Restoration, his reform-vs-abandonment stance) without touching his reserved scenes (Vellmar's Ford details, the Vael Frontier scene, the Voss meeting); Eleanor's reform stance and wound; the Imperial Capital's civil magistracy and Military High Command; the Quest Integration Standard and World State systems.
- **Which civilizations are involved?** The human Capital polity (civil registry, magistracy, military) and the eastern-road garrison-town corridor. No nonhuman civilizations are load-bearing, by design — this is the *human institutions* arc.
- **Which companions react?** All five, with authored beats: Talos and Eleanor centrally (the arc's paired argument), Ronan (fear-of-self mirror in Cantrell), Ragash (civilian cost), Torren (infrastructure cost).
- **Which Divine Voices are reflected?** Thematically: Tyras (order and law — both its worth and its curdling) and Solenne (truth and record-keeping, embodied in the Undervault and Sarnholt). No Voice appears, speaks, or is invoked mechanically; per Preserving Wonder, the resonance stays thematic.
- **Which philosophical pillars are reinforced?** Corruption-as-Imbalance (every antagonist is an amplified virtue); Choice vs. Nature (Cantrell and the garrison are judged by deeds, reachable in every branch); Living World (Lowbridge and the registry exist and suffer independently of the party; official World State can diverge from lived reality); Preserving Wonder (the trail ends short of certainty about Voss; the continuity mark's origin stays unexplained).
- **Does it create any future quest hooks?** (1) The Talos–Voss Capital meeting — this questline builds its evidence trail and motive without authoring it. (2) The continuity mark's pre-reign origin — deliberately unexplained. (3) Sarnholt's inquiry role or disappearance, per tier. (4) Cantrell's post-resolution arc (witness, fugitive, or vindicated advocate). (5) The pattern's adaptation in a second province (Escalation/Poor tiers). All hooks are open doors, not commitments; none expands canon by existing.
- **Are there any continuity concerns?** Two watch-items: (a) the reserved Talos scenes — future authors must not collapse this questline's ceiling by having Ostmark evidence name Voss; (b) Lowbridge is newly established geography on the eastern road and should be added to any future geography-canon pass (it is deliberately minor and sited generically to avoid conflicting with existing maps).
