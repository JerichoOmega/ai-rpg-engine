# Religious Order Quests

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]  
> **Authority:** This document defines named quests for each of the four religious orders. All future quest content involving religious factions must align with these entries and with the theology, fractures, and reputation mechanics defined in [`docs/world/religions.md`](../world/religions.md) and [`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../systems/reputation.md).

## Overview

Each of the four religious orders has at least one named quest that gives players a concrete way to engage with faith, reputation, and religious politics. These quests are not isolated side content — they are entry points into ongoing factional relationships with lasting consequences.

**Design principles applied across all religious quests:**

- Each quest reflects the order's theology and current Corruption-related fracture
- Choices within the quest have meaningful reputation consequences — with the order itself and potentially with rival factions
- No quest has a clean "correct" answer; moral weight should be felt
- Writers should treat these as opening acts, not complete arcs — the relationships forged here feed into longer campaign threads

## The Covenant Clergy — The Solari Covenant

### Quest: *The Inquisitor's Commission*

**Quest giver:** Senior Cleric Aldavar Sune, Covenant Reformist faction  
**Location:** A Frontier town with both a roadside Covenant shrine and an informal Old Ways community  
**Recommended timing:** Early-to-mid campaign; before the player has strong standing with either the Covenant or Old Ways

---

**Background**

The Solari Covenant has dispatched an Inquisitor — Brother Verath — to a Frontier town called Millhaven after reports that residents have been conducting Old Ways seasonal rites "in open defiance of the Radiant One's order." This is technically within the Inquisitors' authority, though the Inquisitors are not yet officially sanctioned. Senior Cleric Aldavar, a Reformist who believes the Covenant's political entanglements are themselves a Corruption symptom, has heard that Verath's methods have become severe. He cannot oppose the Inquisitors openly without splitting the local Covenant hierarchy. He hires the party to go to Millhaven — officially as "witnesses to ensure due process."

What he suspects, but does not say aloud: Verath may have crossed a line.

---

**What the party finds in Millhaven**

Brother Verath has already arrested three residents for "heretical practice" — they were observed leaving offerings at a tree that the Old Ways community considers sacred. He has declared the tree spiritually contaminated and intends to burn it along with the shrine materials inside it.

Investigation reveals:
- The arrested residents are not Old Ways practitioners — they left offerings at the tree because it's where a child of the village died two years ago; it's a grief memorial, not a shrine
- The actual Old Ways community in the area is a small family of farmers who have never sought conflict with anyone
- Verath is aware the residents are probably not practitioners. He considers the distinction irrelevant — the tree is a "focus of disorder" regardless of intent, and burning it will send a message that re-establishes Covenant authority in the Frontier
- One of the Inquisitor's own junior clerks is deeply uncomfortable with what is happening and will tell the party what she knows if approached with care

The Corruption angle: Verath is not obviously Corrupted. He is a man who genuinely believes what he is doing is right — and whose conviction that civilization must be defended has become, through the Corruption's amplification, a conviction that any measure is justified. He is exactly the kind of figure the Inquisitors' own documents warned about: someone doing monstrous things with a clean conscience.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Report Verath's methods to Aldavar with evidence**  
The party documents what is happening and returns to Aldavar with testimony and proof. Aldavar uses this to move against the Inquisitor faction through official channels — a slow process that may not save the arrested residents but will limit the Inquisitors' reach.  
*Reputation consequence:* Moderate gain with **The Covenant Clergy** (Reformist faction credits the party; Restorationists are cooled). Moderate gain with **The Grove Keepers** (they hear that someone pushed back on this). No direct consequence with the Inquisitors — yet.

**Option B — Directly intervene to free the arrested residents**  
The party confronts Verath and demands the residents' release, by force if necessary. This stops the immediate harm but creates a crisis within the local Covenant hierarchy.  
*Reputation consequence:* Significant loss with **The Covenant Clergy** (interference in Covenant authority is not forgiven easily). Significant gain with **The Grove Keepers**. If the party handled it without violence, Aldavar privately approves but cannot say so. The Inquisitors add the party to a list.

**Option C — Help Verath proceed but negotiate for the residents**  
The party works within the Inquisitor's framework — accepts that the tree will burn, negotiates for the release of the prisoners on procedural grounds (they were not formally charged under Covenant law, only detained). Verath accepts the negotiation as a demonstration that the party understands proper order.  
*Reputation consequence:* Small gain with **The Covenant Clergy** (Restorationists approve the pragmatism). Small loss with **The Grove Keepers** (they hear that someone let the burning happen). The junior clerk who helped the party does not forgive them easily.

**Option D — Do nothing / observe only**  
The party reports back to Aldavar having witnessed the events but not intervened. Aldavar is disappointed but uses the testimony anyway.  
*Reputation consequence:* No significant gain or loss with the Covenant — Aldavar pays as agreed. Neutral with the Grove Keepers. The arrested residents remember who was there and did not help.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Brother Verath is not finished. Whether stopped or not, the Inquisitor faction will move to another location. A party that opposed him will encounter consequences; one that cooperated may receive another "commission."
- Senior Cleric Aldavar will contact the party again if they proved useful — the Reformist faction is looking for people who can operate in the grey space where official Covenant action cannot go
- The junior clerk, if treated well, is a potential long-term contact within the Covenant hierarchy

---

**Reputation tags:** `covenant_clergy`, `grove_keepers`  
**Corruption relevance:** The Inquisitor as a case study in Corruption amplifying existing conviction into something harmful; no supernatural element required  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether the Covenant's institutional authority serves or has become opposed to its own stated purpose

## The Grove Keepers — The Old Ways

### Quest: *The Turning Grove*

**Quest giver:** Grove Speaker Sera of Ashenmoss (a senior Old Ways practitioner, neither Severance nor Waiter)  
**Location:** The Great Forest margin, near a sacred grove called the Ashenmoss  
**Recommended timing:** Early-to-mid campaign; strongest if the party has had at least one prior interaction with the Great Forest

---

**Background**

The Ashenmoss is one of the Great Forest's oldest tended groves — a place where the Old Ways community has maintained the Living World's presence for generations. Three weeks ago, the center of the grove began to change. The trees near the grove's heart are growing in unnatural patterns. Animals that nested there have fled. Two Grove Keepers who entered the center to investigate came back reporting that the grove "speaks differently now — like the same words in a language we no longer recognize."

Grove Speaker Sera has called for help from outside the community because the situation has created an unbearable internal conflict she cannot resolve alone.

---

**The internal conflict**

Two voices within the Grove Keepers disagree completely about what to do:

**Elder Davan (Severance):** The Corruption is actively poisoning the grove. Waiting and observing is abdication. He has been working with two other Severance practitioners to develop a ritual that would purge the grove's center — burning out the corruption at its root. He acknowledges this will kill everything at the grove's heart, potentially permanently, but argues that a dead grove is better than a corrupted one that spreads the poison further. The ritual he is developing uses combat magic that the Old Ways tradition has never sanctioned.

**Elder Mireth (Waiter):** The grove is not dying — it is changing, and they do not yet understand how. The Land has survived things before. Intervening with fire and force risks destroying something that cannot be understood yet. She wants to wait, observe, and trust that the Living World's wisdom is still present even when it cannot be heard clearly. She has not entered the grove's center since the change began.

Grove Speaker Sera believes both are partly right and has asked for outside eyes with no investment in either faction's position.

---

**What investigation reveals**

The grove's center is Corruption-touched — but not irreversibly so. The change is early-stage: the grove is responding to a Corruption source somewhere beneath it, likely a buried ruin or a creature that has made a den in the grove's roots. The grove itself is not the source.

The party can:
- Find the actual source (an investigation/dungeon element — a collapsed cellar beneath the grove where something has been feeding for weeks)
- Learn from the grove's changed patterns that there is an exit point — the Corruption is seeping in from outside, not growing within
- Discover that Elder Davan's purge ritual, if performed, would destroy the grove's center and also destroy the evidence of what is actually happening — making it impossible to address the real source

Elder Mireth's patience is not wrong — but she has been so committed to non-interference that she has not looked closely enough to know what she is waiting for.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Find and address the source, report findings to both elders**  
The party investigates, identifies the buried Corruption source, clears or seals it, and presents evidence to both Davan and Mireth. This is the most complete resolution — the grove can begin recovering, Davan's ritual is no longer needed, and Mireth's faith in the Land is borne out in a modified form.  
*Reputation consequence:* Significant gain with **The Grove Keepers** (both factions credit the party for finding what neither could see through their disagreement). Grove Speaker Sera becomes a long-term contact. Small tension with the Severance, who had been building toward action and don't entirely appreciate being preempted.

**Option B — Side with Davan; support the purge ritual**  
The party backs the Severance position, helps Davan acquire the remaining components for his ritual, and the purge is performed. The grove's center is destroyed. The Corruption source is also destroyed in the process, but so is the grove itself — and the evidence is gone.  
*Reputation consequence:* Small gain with Severance faction of **The Grove Keepers**. Significant loss with the tradition-oriented majority. Mireth does not forgive this, and her network within the Old Ways is wide. The fact that it "worked" — the Corruption is gone — does not fully rehabilitate the party's standing.

**Option C — Side with Mireth; counsel patience only**  
The party supports Mireth's position, Davan is persuaded to delay, but no investigation is undertaken. The grove continues to change. Six weeks later, the Corruption source has spread further — it becomes a regional problem that the party may encounter again.  
*Reputation consequence:* Small gain with Waiter faction. No immediate significant reputation effect. Delayed consequence: the spread creates a harder problem later, and some Grove Keepers will remember the party endorsed the delay.

**Option D — Investigate but do not disclose all findings**  
The party discovers the source but chooses to share only partial information — perhaps selling access to the Corruption-touched material to a third party (Mages Guild, a merchant, a collector) before reporting. The grove can still be addressed, but something has been extracted that the Grove Keepers would consider a violation.  
*Reputation consequence:* If discovered: significant loss with **The Grove Keepers** (the grove's materials are not resources to be extracted). If not discovered: surface-level gain with the Keepers, potential complications from whoever acquired the material.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Grove Speaker Sera, if the party handled this well, will reach out when other groves begin exhibiting similar changes — which they will
- Elder Davan's Severance faction is developing more rituals. This quest does not end his work; it either accelerates or complicates it
- The buried ruin where the Corruption source was housed may contain additional content if the party investigates rather than sealing it

---

**Reputation tags:** `grove_keepers`, potential secondary effects on `covenant_clergy` if the Frontier town from the Covenant quest is the same region  
**Corruption relevance:** The Corruption as a physical intrusion into sacred space; the challenge of diagnosing a new phenomenon through a theological framework built on different assumptions  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether the Living World's wisdom can be trusted when it no longer speaks clearly; whether action or patience is the correct response to something not yet understood

## The Ancestor Speakers — The Ancestors' Path

### Quest: *What the Ancestors Say*

**Quest giver:** Clan-Keeper Orryn Duskmantle, senior Ancestor Speaker of the Duskmantle Clan  
**Location:** The Iron Peaks; a mid-sized dwarven clan hall called Duskmantle Hold  
**Recommended timing:** Mid-campaign; after the party has had at least one meaningful interaction with dwarven culture

---

**Background**

Clan-Keeper Orryn has a problem he cannot discuss within his own community without causing a political crisis. He has brought it to outsiders as a last resort — which itself tells the party something about the severity.

For eight weeks, the Duskmantle Clan's primary ancestor shrine has been giving contradictory guidance. The same ancestors who for decades counseled patience, careful resource management, and alliance with neighboring clans have begun — in the communications Orryn is receiving — to advocate for aggressive expansion, the breaking of longstanding trade agreements, and the exclusion of a younger branch of the clan from inheritance.

Orryn has served as Clan-Keeper for thirty years. He knows what his ancestors sound like. These communications sound right on the surface — the cadence is correct, the names and references are accurate — but what they are asking for is wrong. It is the wrong answer to everything.

He has not yet told the full clan council. He has told no one. Because there is a second possibility he cannot rule out: the voices might be genuine, and his own certainty that they are wrong might itself be the failure.

---

**The complication**

A younger Ancestor Speaker named Bressa Duskmantle has been receiving her own communications. Hers are not contradictory — they are enthusiastic. The ancestors she speaks with have confirmed the aggressive expansion position and have told her that the clan's prosperity depends on acting quickly. She is brilliant, politically savvy, and increasingly influential with the younger members of the clan council.

Bressa is not lying. She is receiving genuine communications. The question the party must investigate is: what is she communicating with?

---

**What investigation reveals**

The Corruption's effect on the Ancestors' Path — degrading ancestral contact, creating false voices that claim to be ancestors — is at work in Duskmantle Hold, but not uniformly. Different practitioners in the same space are experiencing different things:

- Orryn's communications have become fragmented and distorted because he is reaching for actual ancestors through an interference pattern — he occasionally breaks through, which is why the communications feel almost right
- Bressa's communications are clear and consistent because she is not reaching through the interference — she has, unknowingly, been connecting to something else that has learned to speak like an ancestor

Investigation options:
- Examining the shrine directly (requires dwarven trust or specialist knowledge — the Veiled Order's Boundary Reading would help)
- Speaking with older clan members who have been practicing longer and may have noticed the degradation
- Attempting to trace Bressa's communications through their source rather than their content
- Finding historical Clan records that predate the current Corruption spread and comparing what the ancestors historically advised versus what is being communicated now

The source of the false communications is not a simple monster. It is something connected to the Corruption that has been learning — pulling ancestral memories from the shrine's residue and constructing a persuasive identity from them. It knows the names, the history, and the cadence. It does not know what the real ancestors would actually want.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Expose the false voices to the clan council with evidence**  
The party investigates fully, identifies the source of the interference, and presents evidence to Orryn that can be brought before the clan council. This requires confronting Bressa with the truth: she has been manipulated, not corrupt.  
*Reputation consequence:* Significant gain with **The Ancestor Speakers** tradition-wide (stopping a Corruption intrusion into the ancestral communication channel is the highest possible service). Bressa, if approached with compassion, may eventually become an ally — she genuinely believed what she was told. If exposed harshly, she becomes an enemy within the clan.

**Option B — Address the source directly without informing the council**  
The party locates the source of the false communications and destroys or drives it away. The interference stops. Bressa's communications cease. Orryn's begin to clear. No one on the council knows what happened.  
*Reputation consequence:* Moderate gain with **The Ancestor Speakers** through Orryn (he knows what was done). No formal recognition. Bressa is left confused and shaken, without explanation — which may make her more or less dangerous going forward depending on her character.

**Option C — Tell Bressa privately what the investigation found**  
Before bringing anything to the council, the party approaches Bressa and shows her the evidence. This gives her the chance to reckon with being manipulated privately, to choose how to handle it herself, and to potentially become the person who exposes the problem rather than its victim.  
*Reputation consequence:* Moderate gain with **The Ancestor Speakers**. If Bressa handles it well, she becomes a significant ally. High-risk: if she does not believe the party or chooses to suppress the information, the problem continues.

**Option D — Report to someone outside the clan — the Mages Guild, the Veiled Order**  
The party, uncertain about dwarven clan politics, takes the information to an external authority rather than navigating the internal structure themselves.  
*Reputation consequence:* Significant loss with **The Ancestor Speakers** (this is internal clan business and should not have left the clan). The external institution may use the information — potentially in ways the clan would not sanction. The problem may still be solved, but the relationship is damaged.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Clan-Keeper Orryn, if the party proved trustworthy, will contact them when other clans report similar shrine disturbances — which other Ancestor Speakers have been privately experiencing and not discussing
- Bressa Duskmantle is a character with a future regardless of how this quest resolves; she was manipulated by something intelligent, and understanding that changes a person
- The source of the false communications — if driven away rather than destroyed — is somewhere in the Iron Peaks

---

**Reputation tags:** `ancestor_speakers`; secondary effects on `covenant_clergy` if the party has existing Covenant standing and uses Covenant contacts to gain access to the clan hall  
**Corruption relevance:** The Corruption's capacity to imitate what people trust most; the false voices phenomenon named in religions.md made concrete  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether certainty about what the ancestors would want is wisdom or arrogance; what the tradition does when the line between genuine contact and spiritual deception becomes unreliable

## The Veiled Archivists — The Veiled Order

### Quest: *The Archivist's Last Record*

**Quest giver:** House Warden Tessyn, senior administrator of the Veiled Order's local house  
**Location:** A Veiled Order house in a mid-sized city; the quest leads to a ruin several days' travel away  
**Recommended timing:** Mid-to-late campaign; the quest rewards parties that have already encountered the Corruption and have opinions about it

---

**Background**

Three weeks ago, Order Archivist Joran Pale stopped sending his regular reports from a ruin he had been assigned to document — a battlefield from a war two centuries old where significant deaths occurred, and where the Order has long maintained that the transition between states of being is unusually thin.

House Warden Tessyn has waited as long as she can. Joran is one of their most accomplished practitioners of Boundary Reading. He has worked thin places before without incident. But the Order's internal conflict about methodology — how closely can you study the Corruption before the study becomes complicity — has been particularly sharp regarding Joran, who argued that proximity was the only way to understand, and that fear of contamination was itself a Corruption-induced limitation.

She wants the party to find him and bring him back. She is honest with them about why she waited three weeks before asking for help: she was afraid of what they would find.

---

**What the party finds**

Joran is alive. He is at the ruin. He has not left because he no longer wants to.

He has been recording something unprecedented: the ruin's thin place has been expanding. The boundary between states is no longer just thin here — it is becoming permeable. Joran has been observing things that no Veiled Order practitioner has ever documented at this proximity. He is producing records of extraordinary quality and specificity.

He is also changed. Not dramatically — he still speaks clearly, still recognizes the party, still articulates the Order's methodology. But there are things he says that should frighten the party if they are paying attention:

- He is no longer performing The Reckoning (the Order's practice of contemplating one's own death) because he has "moved beyond needing it"
- He refers to what he is studying as "the other side of the transition" in a way that implies familiarity, not academic distance
- He has been receiving what he calls "clarifications" — additional information that arrives in ways he cannot fully account for
- He does not want to leave because leaving would end the record, and the record is the most important thing he has ever done

He is not a monster. He is a scholar who loves his work, who believes genuinely that what he is producing will help the Order — and the world — understand the Corruption. He is also, in the Order's own terms, exactly what they feared: someone who got close enough to understand and took on contamination in the process.

The "clarifications" he is receiving are not random. They are targeted. Whatever is communicating with him is using the Order's own methodology — offering knowledge, framing itself as the object of study — to keep him in place and keep the record going.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Bring him back, with or without his consent**  
The party attempts to persuade Joran to leave, and if persuasion fails, removes him by force or by collapsing his access to the ruin. Back at the Order house, Tessyn and senior members will assess him — a process Joran, depending on the party's approach, may or may not survive with his practice intact.  
*Reputation consequence:* Significant gain with **The Veiled Archivists** (the Order's institutional position is that this was the correct call). Joran, if he comes to understand what happened to him, may eventually agree. If he does not, he becomes a cautionary figure the Order cites. His records — if preserved — have genuine value.

**Option B — Help him finish the record before leaving**  
The party negotiates a condition with Joran: they will help him complete a specific set of observations, after which he will leave voluntarily. This takes time and requires the party to spend extended time in the ruin's expanding thin place — which is itself a risk.  
*Reputation consequence:* Moderate gain with **The Veiled Archivists** (the record has value; the Order will note the party took a risk to acquire it). Some members consider this the wrong call regardless of the outcome — you do not negotiate with contamination. Joran leaves, but whether the time in the ruin affected him further is an open question.

**Option C — Report his condition to the Order without extracting him**  
The party returns to Tessyn with a full account of what they found but without Joran. They document his condition, provide testimony about what he said and how he said it, and leave the Order to make a decision about what to do next.  
*Reputation consequence:* Moderate gain with **The Veiled Archivists** (the information is valuable). Tessyn is disappointed — she wanted him back — but will not penalize the party for a difficult situation handled honestly. A follow-up quest becomes available: does the Order send a retrieval team, seal the ruin, or do something else?

**Option D — Destroy the ruin and the records with it**  
The party concludes that the ruin's permeability is an unacceptable danger and collapses or destroys it before returning to the Order. Joran survives but his records are lost. The thin place is sealed.  
*Reputation consequence:* Significant loss with **The Veiled Archivists** (the Order does not sanction unilateral destruction of a documented site or the records within it; this is exactly the kind of action the Order exists to prevent others from taking). Joran is devastated. However, the immediate threat is gone — and some players may consider that worth the cost.

**Option E — Let Joran continue; do not report what was found**  
The party tells Tessyn that Joran was not at the ruin — he must have moved on. Joran continues his work.  
*Reputation consequence:* No immediate reputation effect (the Order does not know the party found him). Delayed consequence: Joran's situation will escalate. When the Order eventually finds him — which they will — the party's false report will come to light. Whatever he becomes in the intervening time is partly the party's responsibility.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Joran's records, if preserved, are a resource — they contain genuine information about the Corruption's relationship to death and transition that the Order will spend years studying
- House Warden Tessyn, if the party proved trustworthy, will contact them when other Order members begin exhibiting similar patterns — which is already beginning to happen in other houses
- The ruin itself, if not destroyed, is a location that will continue to feature in campaigns set in the region; the thin place will keep expanding

---

**Reputation tags:** `veiled_archivists`; secondary effects on `mages_guild` if the party shares Joran's records with Mages Guild contacts rather than returning them to the Order  
**Corruption relevance:** The Order's own theological orientation — approaching death without fear, studying the Corruption directly — as both their greatest asset and greatest vulnerability  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether the study of something dangerous can remain objective; whether the Order's core practice of contemplating death becomes complicity when the thing being contemplated is also communicating back

## Cross-Quest Reputation Dynamics

The following interactions exist between these quests:

| Action | Primary consequence | Secondary consequence |
|---|---|---|
| High standing with **Covenant Clergy** (Restorationists) | Covenant doors open; noble access | Small penalty with **Grove Keepers** (historical tension between the two) |
| High standing with **Grove Keepers** | Forest and rural community access | Small penalty with **Covenant Clergy** |
| High standing with **Ancestor Speakers** | Dwarven clan access; Iron Peaks network | No direct effect on other orders, but dwarven political goodwill may surprise the Covenant |
| Sharing Veiled Archivists records with **Mages Guild** | Mages Guild research gain | Significant loss with **Veiled Archivists** (information shared without consent) |
| Exposing **Inquisitors** (Covenant quest) to **Grove Keepers** | Grove Keeper gain; demonstrates the party can act against Covenant excess | Does not offset Covenant penalty from Option B — these are different ledgers |

Standing with all four orders simultaneously is possible but requires careful navigation. The primary natural tension is between the Covenant Clergy and the Grove Keepers; the Ancestor Speakers and Veiled Archivists are more politically neutral toward each other and toward the other two.

## High-Reputation Quests — Trusted Ally Tier

The following quests are available only to players who have built meaningful standing with a religious order. They are not continuations of the entry-level quests — they are escalations that assume the player is now a known and trusted actor within the order's world.

**Reputation gate:** Each quest requires **Friendly** standing with the relevant order (approximately 40+ on the -100 to 100 scale). The quest givers will not approach or acknowledge players below this threshold.

**Design principles for high-reputation quests:**

- The stakes are internal to the order, not just adjacent to it — the player is now trusted enough to see fractures that outsiders never learn about
- The order's core theological tension is brought to a crisis point, not just illustrated
- The Corruption's presence is more direct — it is no longer a background problem but a specific antagonist to this relationship
- The player's prior choices in the entry-level quest are acknowledged and may open or close options

## The Covenant Clergy — High-Reputation Quest

### Quest: *The Synod of Millhaven*

**Reputation gate:** Friendly standing with **The Covenant Clergy**  
**Quest giver:** Senior Cleric Aldavar Sune (returning from *The Inquisitor's Commission*)  
**Location:** The Capital Province; a Covenant administrative seat called the Hallward Synod  
**Recommended timing:** Late campaign; after the party has encountered the Inquisitor faction at least once

---

**Background**

Senior Cleric Aldavar has been quietly building a case against the Inquisitors for months. The party's evidence from Millhaven was one piece. He now has testimony from three other Frontier towns where Inquisitor methods have produced similar outcomes: communities fractured, legitimate Old Ways practitioners detained, one confirmed death. He has enough.

But Aldavar cannot bring this case himself. A Reformist cleric presenting charges against the Inquisitors will be dismissed as factional politics — and the Restorationists, who control the current Synod majority, will use the opportunity to discredit the Reformist wing entirely. He needs a voice that is not his.

He is asking the party to present the evidence to the Hallward Synod directly: not as his agents, but as independent witnesses who have seen what the Inquisitors do.

What he does not tell them until they accept: Brother Verath will also be at the Synod. He has been invited to present his own account of "successful interventions in Frontier disorder." The Synod was Verath's idea.

---

**What the party is walking into**

The Hallward Synod is not a court. It is an advisory council — the Covenant's closest thing to internal governance. It has no enforcement power over the Inquisitors, who currently operate in a deliberate legal ambiguity: not officially sanctioned, not officially prohibited.

The session is structured as a hearing on "Frontier spiritual disorder." Verath's faction has framed this as an opportunity to seek official sanction for the Inquisitors. Aldavar's faction has framed it as an opportunity to prevent that sanction. Both sides know what the other is doing. The Synod members who are genuinely undecided are the ones that matter.

The party's role is to provide testimony. But once in the Synod chamber, several things complicate the picture:

- **One of the three undecided Synod members, Warden Solis, is visibly changed.** The party, if they have any experience with Corruption-touched figures, will notice it. She is not overtly monstrous — she functions normally — but she is pushing harder than the evidence supports for a version of events that validates Verath. Her questions are designed to discredit witnesses, not discover truth. She is receiving something that feels like conviction.
- **Verath has a counter-witness.** A Frontier merchant named Davan Cors will testify that Inquisitor intervention in his town resolved a genuine problem: a household that he believes was conducting actual harmful rites. Whether he is lying, deceived, or telling the truth is for the party to determine. His testimony is not fabricated — something did happen in his town — but the Inquisitors' interpretation of it is wrong.
- **Aldavar's case has a gap.** One piece of evidence from Millhaven — the testimony of the junior clerk who helped the party — is the most damning. But she is not present. She was reassigned to a remote posting two weeks ago. The party can try to obtain her testimony by other means before the session ends, or proceed without it.
- **The Synod may be asked to vote mid-session.** Restorationist members have proposed calling an immediate procedural vote on "preliminary recognition" of the Inquisitors. If this passes, the formal hearing becomes harder to reopen. Stopping this requires understanding Synod procedural rules — which a Covenant-friendly party may have access to through prior relationships.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Present evidence fully; expose Warden Solis's behavior to the Synod**  
The party delivers testimony, and if they have identified the change in Warden Solis, they name it during the session — presenting it as a pattern they have observed before, not an accusation they cannot support. This is a significant risk: it transforms the hearing from an evidence session into a crisis, and if the party cannot back the claim, it discredits everything else they said.  
*Reputation consequence:* If successful (evidence was strong, Solis's behavior is visible to at least two other Synod members): **Significant gain with The Covenant Clergy** — the Reformist faction treats this as a turning point; the party is now known to the full Covenant hierarchy. The Inquisitors are formally denied preliminary recognition. Warden Solis is placed under internal review. Verath loses this round. If unsuccessful: **Significant loss** — the party is seen as having leveled false accusations within Covenant proceedings.

**Option B — Present evidence; do not name Solis; let the testimony stand**  
The party gives the strongest testimony possible from the evidence in hand. Whether the gap in evidence (the missing clerk) is addressed depends on whether the party had time to obtain her testimony by other means.  
*Reputation consequence:* If the full evidence is present: **Moderate-to-significant gain with The Covenant Clergy**; preliminary recognition is denied on procedural grounds (the case was not closed, not won). Aldavar continues his campaign. If the evidence gap remains: **Moderate gain**; the Synod tables the recognition question — which is a victory, but a provisional one.

**Option C — Approach Verath privately before the session**  
Rather than making this a confrontation, the party tries to reach Verath before the Synod convenes. The goal: show him the evidence and give him the opportunity to withdraw his petition. Verath is a man of genuine conviction, not a simple villain — there is a version of this conversation where he understands what has happened.  
*Reputation consequence:* If Verath withdraws: **Significant gain with The Covenant Clergy** (both factions); the hearing is preempted and the party is seen as having resolved a crisis through skill rather than public conflict. Verath, if reached, is changed — he will not become an ally, but he may no longer be an active enemy. **High difficulty:** Verath's certainty is Corruption-amplified; reaching him requires either extraordinary persuasion or specific knowledge about his history that the party has gathered over prior encounters.

**Option D — Accept the Synod's vote and work with the outcome**  
If the preliminary recognition passes before the party can stop it, they can choose to work within the new framework rather than fighting the outcome. Cooperating with the now-partially-sanctioned Inquisitors while continuing to document their behavior is a longer-game approach — it gives access to Inquisitor operations at the cost of association with them.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Moderate loss with The Covenant Clergy** (Reformists feel betrayed). Neutral with Restorationists (they accept the pragmatism). The Inquisitors begin treating the party as conditionally useful. This option is the hardest to play and has the most complicated long-term implications.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Warden Solis, if identified but not yet addressed, is a continuing presence in Covenant governance; she is not the only Synod member who has been quietly shifting
- The missing junior clerk can be found at her reassigned posting — and what she has observed in the weeks since her transfer adds to what the party already knows
- Verath, whether stopped or not, is not done; if reached privately and genuinely changed, he may become one of the most unexpected sources of support in later Covenant content

**Corruption relevance:** The Corruption operating within institutional structures — not through dramatic possession but through the slow distortion of individuals in positions of influence  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether the Covenant's institutional authority can self-correct, or whether it requires outside intervention to remain true to its own stated purpose

## The Grove Keepers — High-Reputation Quest

### Quest: *The Last Standing Grove*

**Reputation gate:** Friendly standing with **The Grove Keepers**  
**Quest giver:** Grove Speaker Sera of Ashenmoss (returning from *The Turning Grove*)  
**Location:** The Great Forest's deep interior; a grove called the Harrow-Root  
**Recommended timing:** Late campaign; after the party has had meaningful experience with the Corruption's effect on natural spaces

---

**Background**

The Harrow-Root is the oldest maintained grove in the Great Forest — perhaps on the continent. Old Ways records reference it before the earliest dwarven clan annals. It has been tended by Grove Keepers for as long as anyone can trace. It is the closest thing the tradition has to a center.

Three weeks ago, Grove Speaker Sera sent word that the Harrow-Root has begun to change — and that this time, it is not a peripheral grove responding to a buried source. The change is coming from within. The grove's oldest trees are altered in ways that should not be possible. The practitioners who have tended the Harrow-Root for decades report that the grove no longer responds to them. One senior practitioner attempted a deep Listening Walk into the grove's heart and did not come back.

Sera is asking the party because she trusts them with what she cannot say to the factions within her own tradition: she thinks the Harrow-Root is not just Corrupted. She thinks something is using the grove's deep roots to reach outward — that the grove has become a conduit, not merely a victim.

---

**What the party finds**

The Harrow-Root is vast — the deep grove takes a full day to cross at its widest, and the oldest trees form a canopy so dense that daylight barely reaches the floor. The outer groves are recognizable: ancient, dense, heavy with Old Ways practice. The further in the party travels, the more wrong it becomes.

Investigation reveals:
- The missing practitioner is present, in the grove's heart. She is alive. She has not moved in eleven days. She is seated in front of the grove's oldest tree — called the Firstroot by the tradition — in what appears to be a Listening Walk that has not ended. Approaching her directly causes her to speak, but not to the party: she is still in conversation with something, and what she describes suggests she believes she is speaking with the grove itself
- The Firstroot is Corrupted, but in a specific way: it is not dying. It is expanding. New growth is emerging from the base — dark-barked shoots that do not match any known species — and the root network is visibly pushing outward in directions that have already begun affecting three smaller groves at the Harrow-Root's margins
- The Corruption source is not beneath the grove. It is within the Firstroot itself — which means it has been here long enough to become part of the tree, not a recent intrusion. The grove's deep root network, which the tradition has always understood as one of the Living World's most direct expressions, has become the Corruption's channel

The most painful thing the party will discover: the Firstroot may not be salvageable. The Corruption has been in the tree long enough that removing it without destroying the tree may be impossible. What remains is: the tree, with the Corruption inside it; or no tree.

---

**The internal crisis**

By the time the party arrives, the Grove Keepers have fractured. Representatives of all three currents are present at the Harrow-Root's margin, unable to agree:

**Elder Davan (Severance)** has already proposed destroying the Firstroot completely. He has a ritual prepared. He came to this conclusion before he even investigated — when the Severance decides something must be burned, they are not easily dissuaded. His argument is that the Firstroot's root network will spread the Corruption to every grove in the Great Forest within the year if nothing is done. He is probably right about the timeline.

**Elder Mireth (Waiter)** has not entered the grove at all. She is waiting outside the first tree line. Her position has become something that the other practitioners find difficult to hear: she believes the grove's change is the Living World speaking in a way they do not recognize, and that destroying the Firstroot would sever something that cannot be rebuilt. She cannot articulate what that would mean, only that she is certain it would be a permanent loss. She is probably also right about that.

**Grove Speaker Sera** is holding the two factions apart by force of personality and not much else. She has called on the party specifically because she cannot ask this of her own people: she needs someone to go into the grove's heart and find out whether there is anything left of the Firstroot worth saving — and to come back with the truth, not a theology.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Find a way to separate the Corruption from the Firstroot**  
A deep investigation of the grove, combined with any specialized knowledge the party has accumulated about the Corruption's behavior (from the Veiled Order, from the Mages Guild, from prior quest experience), may reveal a method: the Corruption within the tree has a specific structure — it is not dispersed through the whole tree, but rooted in one section of the heartwood. A targeted ritual, possibly requiring materials or knowledge from outside the Old Ways tradition, might be able to excise that section without destroying the whole.  
*Reputation consequence:* If successful: **Significant gain with The Grove Keepers** across all factions; this resolves the crisis without destruction and without delay — the only outcome that satisfies both Davan and Mireth's concerns. The practitioner in the grove's heart can be reached and brought out. The tradition-wide standing the party gains from preserving the Firstroot is substantial and lasting. **High difficulty:** requires the party to have accumulated specific knowledge over the campaign; cannot be improvised without prior groundwork.

**Option B — Support Davan; witness the burning**  
The party accepts that the Firstroot cannot be saved and backs the Severance's ritual. The Firstroot is destroyed. The root network is interrupted. The three affected marginal groves will recover over years, not weeks.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Small gain with Severance faction of The Grove Keepers**. **Significant loss with the Waiter faction and tradition-wide standing** — destroying the Harrow-Root's heart is not forgiven by the older practitioners, even if it was necessary. Mireth withdraws from the Harrow-Root and does not return. Sera understands but carries grief for this decision. The party will have to reckon with what the Harrow-Root represented to the tradition.

**Option C — Bring the missing practitioner out before any decision is made**  
The party's first goal is reaching the practitioner in the grove's heart and breaking her Listening Walk. She has been in contact with whatever is now in the Firstroot for eleven days. If she can be brought out, what she has learned may change the calculus — she may have information about the Corruption's structure within the tree that no external investigation could find.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Moderate gain with The Grove Keepers** (the faction conflict is not resolved, but the party has demonstrated their loyalty is to the people before the politics). What the practitioner knows becomes a significant factor in which other options remain available.

**Option D — Do nothing; let the factions reach their own resolution**  
The party observes and reports to Sera but does not intervene. The factions fracture further over the following days. Davan eventually performs the burning alone, without the support he asked for — a messier, less controlled ritual that damages two of the marginal groves in addition to the Firstroot.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Significant loss with The Grove Keepers**. Sera is not angry — she is disappointed in a way that is worse. She will not ask the party for help again.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- The recovered practitioner has been in conversation with something inside the Firstroot for eleven days; what she learned is a significant source of information about the Corruption's relationship with living systems — information that will be relevant to several other ongoing campaign threads
- Elder Davan's Severance faction, emboldened or not by this outcome, is expanding its reach; the methods they are developing are becoming a concern to practitioners outside the Old Ways tradition
- The three affected marginal groves each have their own communities; the party's standing with those communities will reflect what happened at the Harrow-Root

---

**Reputation tags:** `grove_keepers`; secondary effects on `mages_guild` and `veiled_archivists` if the party draws on their knowledge to attempt Option A  
**Corruption relevance:** The Corruption inside something the tradition has always treated as sacred; the question of what it means to protect something when the thing being protected has already changed  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether the Living World's wisdom and the Corruption can inhabit the same space simultaneously; whether preservation or removal is the more faithful response to something beloved that has been compromised

## The Ancestor Speakers — High-Reputation Quest

### Quest: *The Conclave of Kept Names*

**Reputation gate:** Friendly standing with **The Ancestor Speakers**  
**Quest giver:** Clan-Keeper Orryn Duskmantle (returning from *What the Ancestors Say*)  
**Location:** The Iron Peaks; a neutral clan hall called Ironmoot Chamber, used for inter-clan councils  
**Recommended timing:** Late campaign; after the party has established relationships with at least two dwarven clans

---

**Background**

The problem Orryn faced at Duskmantle Hold was not unique. In the three months since the party helped him, six other clan-keepers across the Iron Peaks have contacted him privately with the same core report: the ancestors are becoming unreliable. Communications are fragmented, false, or absent entirely. In some clans, younger practitioners are receiving strong, confident communications from voices claiming ancestral identity — and those voices are advising things that, to experienced practitioners, feel categorically wrong.

Orryn has done something politically extraordinary: he has called a conclave. A gathering of clan-keepers from seven different clans, at the neutral ground of Ironmoot Chamber, to compare notes on what they are experiencing. No such conclave has been called in forty years — the last one addressed a boundary dispute that nearly became a war. Calling one to discuss a spiritual crisis is unprecedented.

He has asked the party to attend. Not as official representatives of anything — as the only non-dwarves who know what has been happening, who demonstrated at Duskmantle Hold that they can operate with discretion in the most politically sensitive of clan matters, and who may notice things that the clan-keepers, inside the politics of the moment, cannot see clearly.

He is not asking them to solve anything at the conclave. He is asking them to be present, to observe, and to tell him afterward what they saw.

---

**What the party walks into**

Seven clans. Seven clan-keepers or their designated representatives. Three days of scheduled sessions in Ironmoot Chamber.

The conclave is not what Orryn intended. By the time it convenes, the political situation has already been distorted:

**Three of the seven clan representatives are experiencing what Bressa Duskmantle experienced** — receiving clear, confident communications from voices claiming to be ancestors, communications that are advising positions that happen to benefit those clans' immediate political interests. They are not lying. They believe the ancestors are speaking to them. One of these three is a clan-keeper of thirty years' standing — a figure of significant authority whose shift in position on a key Iron Peaks territorial question has already created alliances that did not exist six months ago.

**The conclave's announced purpose has already leaked.** Some clan members came knowing that Orryn intended to raise the question of false communications — and they have framed this preemptively as Orryn attempting to delegitimize competing clans' ancestral authority for political advantage. Two clans have sent representatives specifically to block any collective statement about the ancestor communications issue.

**A young clan-keeper named Hest Irongate has brought documentation** — written records of the communications her clan has received over the past year, cross-referenced with actual clan history. The divergences are clear, specific, and damning. She has prepared this with great care. She is also the youngest clan-keeper at the conclave and has almost no political standing; her evidence may be dismissed before it is examined.

**The Ironmoot Chamber itself is a thin place.** The party's experience with Corruption-touched locations may register something: the Chamber, used for generations as a place of politically charged dwarven deliberation, has accumulated significant ancestral residue. Whatever is producing the false communications elsewhere has access to this space. The conclave, as a gathering of multiple clan-keepers in a place with this much ancestral weight, may be exactly what something has been waiting for.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Protect Hest Irongate and ensure her documentation is heard**  
The party's most immediate task, if they read the room correctly, is making sure the one person who has done the work gets a chance to present it. This requires navigating clan-keeper politics — either finding a way to give Hest formal floor time she would not otherwise have, or connecting her evidence to one of the more senior clan-keepers who can present it with greater authority.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Moderate gain with The Ancestor Speakers** tradition-wide; Hest Irongate becomes a significant long-term contact. Politically moderate: this is a procedural intervention, not a full resolution. If the documentation changes three or more clan-keepers' positions on the false-communications question, the conclave can issue a collective statement — which has significant downstream political effects across the Iron Peaks.

**Option B — Identify the false-communication recipients and approach them privately**  
The party can spend the conclave's first two days in private conversation with the three clan representatives they have identified as being influenced by false communications. The goal is not confrontation — it is the approach that worked at Duskmantle Hold: showing a person what has been happening to them, in a way that gives them space to reckon with it before it becomes public.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Significant gain with The Ancestor Speakers** if even two of the three are reached; this is the hardest option and the most politically careful. A clan representative who understands they were manipulated, and chooses to disclose this themselves at the conclave, transforms the session. **High risk:** if one of the three warns the others before the party is ready, the private approach fails and the conclave becomes hostile.

**Option C — Address the Ironmoot Chamber's thin place directly**  
If the party recognizes what the Chamber is and what it means, they may attempt to address the thin place itself before the conclave concludes. This requires resources and knowledge from outside dwarven tradition — almost certainly involving the Veiled Order or the Mages Guild — and it will be visible to everyone in the Chamber when it happens. It is not a subtle option.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Significant gain with The Ancestor Speakers** if successful — they have protected the tradition's most significant neutral political space. **Moderate loss** with at least one clan that considers this an outside intrusion into sacred dwarven practice, regardless of intent. The conclave's proceedings are disrupted, which may help or harm depending on what was about to happen.

**Option D — Observe and report to Orryn only**  
The party does what Orryn asked: they watch, they listen, they note who is receiving what and how it affects the conclave's dynamics, and they give Orryn a full account afterward. They do not intervene.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Moderate gain with The Ancestor Speakers** through Orryn (he wanted an observer, and the party delivered). **No other effects** — the conclave ends without resolution; the political distortions introduced by the false communications persist and will manifest in Iron Peaks clan politics over the following months. The party will see the downstream consequences in later content.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Hest Irongate's documentation is the most complete record of the false-communications phenomenon assembled in the Iron Peaks; regardless of the conclave's outcome, it is a significant resource
- The three clan-keepers receiving false communications will face the truth eventually — either through the party's intervention or through a harder revelation later; how they encounter that truth will shape their character in later encounters
- The Ironmoot Chamber's thin place, if not addressed, will keep expanding; a future conclave called in the same space will face a far more dangerous version of what the party observed

---

**Reputation tags:** `ancestor_speakers`; secondary effects on `veiled_archivists` and `mages_guild` if the party draws on those relationships to address the Chamber  
**Corruption relevance:** The Corruption's ability to operate at institutional scale — not targeting individuals in isolation but shaping a political gathering through the simultaneous compromise of several participants  
**Theological tension surfaced:** What the tradition does when the line between ancestral wisdom and Corruption-induced conviction cannot be drawn in public without tearing the political structure apart

## The Veiled Archivists — High-Reputation Quest

### Quest: *What Was Written Here*

**Reputation gate:** Friendly standing with **The Veiled Archivists**  
**Quest giver:** House Warden Tessyn (returning from *The Archivist's Last Record*)  
**Location:** A Veiled Order regional archive house; the quest leads into the archive's deeper holdings  
**Recommended timing:** Late campaign; after the party has encountered the Order's methodology and understands its archival significance

---

**Background**

House Warden Tessyn sent for the party because something in the Order's archive is wrong, and she does not know how long it has been wrong.

The Veiled Order's regional archive house maintains records going back centuries. Deathbed testimonies. Final observations. Records of thin places, boundary events, and phenomena that no other institution documented because no other institution was willing to approach them. The archive is the Order's most significant material possession — more important than any building, any living member, more important than anything except the practice itself.

Three days ago, a senior Archivist named Moven Dust was cross-referencing an older record against a more recent one as part of routine authentication work. The older record — a boundary-event testimony from sixty years ago — did not match the copy she had authenticated herself thirty years prior. Not a transcription error. Not a damaged section. The content was different: the events described, the observations made, the conclusions drawn. She compared both against a third version held in a different collection. That version matched neither.

Tessyn has been conducting quiet internal checks since then. She has identified eleven records with similar discrepancies so far. The oldest altered record dates to approximately ninety years ago. The most recent was altered within the last year.

Someone — or something — has been rewriting the Order's archive. Not removing records. Not destroying them. Rewriting them to say different things.

---

**What the investigation reveals**

The alterations are not random. Cross-referencing the eleven altered records reveals a pattern:

Every altered record is a primary source document about the Corruption — boundary events, thin-place phenomena, death-threshold testimonies from individuals who encountered something unusual in their final hours. The originals, where reconstructible, documented the Corruption in specific, accurate terms. The altered versions either remove the Corruption references entirely or reframe them as misidentified natural phenomena, practitioner error, or theological misinterpretation.

Something has been systematically erasing the Order's most accurate historical record of what the Corruption actually does.

The mechanism of alteration is the deeper mystery. The archive is not a building anyone enters casually — the Order has centuries of security practice. Investigation reveals:

- The alterations were made by someone with legitimate access to the archive — not an intruder
- The authentication markers that should flag unauthorized changes were not triggered, which means either the person who made the changes had authority to authenticate, or the authentication system itself was manipulated
- One of the altered records — a testimony from forty years ago — includes a marginal annotation in a hand that Tessyn identifies as belonging to a former Archivist named Carros, who died twelve years ago

**Carros** is where the investigation centers. He was a trusted senior Archivist — one of the most accomplished practitioners of Boundary Reading in the Order's history. He spent the last decade of his life working in the archive's deepest holdings, which he was given exceptional access to because of his expertise. He died quietly, of illness, and was recorded by the Order as having passed in The Reckoning — the contemplation of one's death — which is considered an honored death for a practitioner.

The problem: if Carros made the alteration twelve years ago, why are the most recent alterations from within the last year? He is dead.

Unless the annotation is a different kind of message — left by Carros before he died, for whoever came looking.

---

**Key choices**

**Option A — Follow Carros's trail into the archive's deep holdings**  
The investigation suggests Carros discovered the alteration problem years before his death and spent his final years trying to leave a record of what the originals said. The deep holdings he was given access to contain something he was studying that he did not include in his official reports. Finding what he found requires extended work in the archive's most restricted sections — which means extended time in a space where the Order's relationship with death and the Corruption's influence on that relationship are both very close.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Significant gain with The Veiled Archivists** if the party recovers what Carros documented — this restores the archive's integrity and produces a significant new body of accurate primary-source material about the Corruption. The Order will treat this as among the most valuable contributions any outside party has made to their work. **Risk:** extended time in the deep holdings is not without cost; the party will encounter things the archive holds that are meant to stay contained.

**Option B — Identify and confront the living person responsible for the recent alterations**  
If Carros died twelve years ago, someone else made the changes within the last year. The investigation, if pressed in this direction, can identify the living Archivist who made them. That person is not obviously compromised — they function normally, they pass internal review, they are a respected member of the Order. What the party discovers when they find them is that they have been receiving communications they have accepted as internal Order guidance: messages they believed came from senior members of the Order, directing specific record corrections for "accuracy." The messages came from somewhere else.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Significant gain with The Veiled Archivists** if handled with care — the Archivist was not acting in bad faith and should be treated as a victim, not a conspirator. If exposed harshly or publicly, the Order loses a practitioner they need and the party loses a potential ally. If handled well, the Archivist's account of the communications provides a significant intelligence thread about the Corruption's ability to impersonate internal authority.

**Option C — Reconstruct the originals from external cross-references**  
The archive is the Order's primary source, but not the only source. Some of the altered records were referenced in external documents — Mages Guild research notes, Adventurers Guild commission reports, personal correspondence between practitioners and their contacts. A research effort across the party's accumulated networks could reconstruct enough of the originals to restore the evidentiary record, even without resolving who made the alterations.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Moderate gain with The Veiled Archivists** — this is valuable but does not address the root problem; future alterations remain possible. **Secondary gain with Mages Guild** if the party works with them directly on the reconstruction. Tessyn will ask the party to continue working on identifying the mechanism once the immediate records gap is addressed.

**Option D — Seal the deep holdings and request a full internal audit**  
The party's advice to Tessyn: close the most restricted sections, halt all archival work, and conduct a complete internal audit before continuing. This is the most cautious approach and the most disruptive. The Order will lose months of research capacity, and several current projects — including work on the Corruption's historical pattern — will be paused.  
*Reputation consequence:* **Moderate gain with The Veiled Archivists** (Tessyn agrees this may be necessary, and the party is not wrong). **Moderate indirect cost:** the paused research includes material that would have been useful to the party in other campaign threads. Some Order members will resent the disruption regardless of the reason.

---

**Follow-on threads**

- Carros's undisclosed research — if recovered — is one of the most complete historical accounts of the Corruption's behavior available to any party; it will change what is knowable in late-campaign content
- The living Archivist who made the recent alterations, once they understand what was done to them, may become the Order's most motivated researcher on the question of how the Corruption impersonates legitimate authority
- The altered records represent an intelligence objective: whatever removed those specific accounts of the Corruption wanted the Order not to know what they documented; understanding what was in them is itself strategically significant

---

**Reputation tags:** `veiled_archivists`; secondary effects on `mages_guild` if the party uses Guild contacts for record reconstruction  
**Corruption relevance:** The Corruption operating at institutional memory — not changing the present but rewriting the past; the specific targeting of accurate primary-source documentation as a strategic objective  
**Theological tension surfaced:** Whether an institution built on the preservation of truth can identify and respond to a systematic corruption of its own records from within; what it means that the Corruption has specifically targeted what the Order knows about it
