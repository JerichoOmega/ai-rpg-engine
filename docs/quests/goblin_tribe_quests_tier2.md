# Goblin Tribe Quests — Tier 2 (High Reputation)

> **Document Status:** Canonical as of 2026-07-31.
> **Authority:** This document defines the second-tier named quest for each goblin tribe — the authored payoff for sustained engagement. Each quest is gated by high standing earned through the tier-1 quests in [`goblin_tribe_quests.md`](goblin_tribe_quests.md) and subsequent behavior. All future content referencing deep goblin political engagement should draw from or extend these quests.
> **Cross-references:** [`goblin_tribe_quests.md`](goblin_tribe_quests.md) · [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../world/goblin_tribes.md) · [`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../systems/reputation.md) · [`docs/systems/quests.md`](../systems/quests.md) (Major Quest Integration Standard)

## Overview

The tier-1 quests activate each tribe's reputation track. These quests are what that investment buys. Each is only offered when the tribe's leadership has concluded the party can be trusted with something that matters — an internal vulnerability, a protected secret, a political project at its most fragile moment. None of them is available to strangers, and none of them can be reached through violence.

Each quest deliberately touches the tribe's Corruption manifestation (see `goblin_tribes.md`, Corruption Effects by Tribe) without resolving it. The champion Krath, the hollow game, and the Burned Claw remain live threats with their own authored content; these quests position the party inside each tribe's response to its crisis.

**Prerequisite standings:**

| Tribe | Gate | In-fiction signal that the gate has opened |
|---|---|---|
| Stonefang | ~+40 Stonefang (Debt honored well; ideally direct political access to Grakkor) | A runner arrives with Grakkor's mark — but the summons is unusually quiet: no escort, no ceremony, a meeting place *away* from the Fang-Hold |
| Mossroot | Vess treats the party as trustworthy (+25 or higher via "What the Forest Carries," no betrayals of Mossroot secrets) | Thorn appears at the party's camp, alone, and says the Elder Speaker is asking for them — a sentence Thorn has never carried to outsiders before |
| Ashfire | Sol Kareth negotiation arc concluded positively in "Smoke and Terms" (Ash Oath witnessed) | An Ashfire emissary delivers a formal invitation to the anniversary Unification Fire — an honor extended to no other outsiders this year |

## Stonefang Tribe

### Quest: "The Proving Ground"

**Quest-giver:** Warchief Grakkor Stonefang — privately, off the record
**Location:** The Fang-Hold and the recognized Proving ground in the eastern Frontier
**Reputation track affected:** Stonefang Tribe

---

#### Background

Varkk has announced a Proving. In three days, under the tribe's own formalized challenge tradition, he will fight Grakkor for leadership of the Stonefang — to submission or incapacitation, before the assembled tribe.

Grakkor is not afraid of losing a fair fight; he has won every challenge thrown at him. What he has learned — and cannot say publicly — is that this Proving is not fair. Varkk has been spending time near Krath the Unyielding, studying what is happening to the champion, and Grakkor believes Varkk intends to arrive at the Proving carrying some of what Krath carries. Grakkor's culture gives him no language for the accusation: a warrior who wins is a warrior who is right, and "my cousin has touched something unnatural" sounds, to Stonefang ears, like the excuse of a leader who fears the challenge.

So Grakkor calls in the relationship he has built with the party. He needs proof of what Varkk has done — evidence concrete enough that the tribe's own traditions can reject the challenge as tainted — or he needs the source of Varkk's advantage cut off before the Proving. What he will not accept: outsiders visibly interfering in a Proving. If the tribe sees the party fighting Grakkor's battle, he loses even if he wins.

*What he tells them:* the challenge, the timing, his suspicion about Varkk and Krath, and that the party must work invisibly.
*What he does not tell them (discoverable):* he has privately prepared a successor instruction with a neutral raid-commander in case he loses — Grakkor plans for defeat the way he plans for everything; and two of Krath's proximity-affected warriors have already pledged to Varkk.

#### Quest Stages

**Stage 1 — Three Days.** The party has until the Proving. Investigation around Krath's tent perimeter, Varkk's movements, and the affected warriors establishes what Varkk has actually done: he has been deliberately exposing himself, in controlled visits, to whatever radiates from Krath — and it is working.

**Stage 2 — The Varkk Problem.**

| Approach | Method | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigation** | Document Varkk's visits to Krath — witnessed by Stonefang warriors, not just the party | The tribe's own members raise the taint before the Proving; the challenge collapses under tradition, not authority |
| **Diplomacy** | Confront Varkk privately: what he is borrowing does not stop when the fight is won — Krath is the proof | Varkk is ambitious, not suicidal; hard Persuasion, but he can be turned; the challenge is withdrawn "pending the champion's matter" |
| **Deception** | Feed Varkk's pledged warriors the (true) accounts of what proximity has done to Krath's hunting parties | Varkk's support erodes; he delays the challenge rather than fight without backers |
| **Stealth/Severance** | Interrupt Varkk's access — quarantine Krath's tent perimeter under cover of Grakkor's champion-care orders | Varkk arrives at the Proving without the edge he built the challenge on |
| **Do nothing / party absent** | Let the Proving proceed | Grakkor wins a brutal, close fight or loses; either way Varkk's Corruption exposure is now inside the leadership question permanently |
| **Side with Varkk** | Help conceal the exposure | The Proving proceeds with Varkk's edge intact; he is favored but the fight is real — see consequences |

**Stage 3 — The Proving.** Whatever the party did, the Proving date arrives and resolves in front of the tribe. The party attends as recognized guests — visible, silent, and known by Grakkor (and Varkk) to have shaped what happens.

#### Reputation Consequences

| Outcome | Stonefang change | Other effects |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge collapsed via tribal witness of the taint | +25 Stonefang; Grakkor's trust becomes personal, not transactional | Krath's condition is now openly named a problem — the tribe can begin responding to it |
| Varkk turned privately; challenge withdrawn | +20 Stonefang | Varkk remains deputy, chastened and watching; a live thread |
| Grakkor wins an untainted Proving after party groundwork | +15 Stonefang | Grakkor's authority reinforced |
| Grakkor loses; Varkk leads | −10 Stonefang (Varkk knows the party worked against him) unless the party sided with him | Varkk's aggressive posture reshapes the eastern Frontier (see tier-1 Cross-Quest table) |
| Party sided with Varkk | +15 with Varkk's Stonefang; Grakkor faction hostile | A Corruption-touched warchief; escalating raiding; the Ashfire and Frontier settlements react |

#### Integration (per the Major Quest Integration Standard)

*Constant across all outcomes:* **Companion reactions** — Ragash reads the Proving through her own culture's lens of formalized violence, and her hounds' refusal to approach Krath's tent is the party's earliest confirmation Varkk's advantage is real. **Journal** — records the Proving and the party's quiet role in every branch.

| Outcome tier | Immediate | Long-term | World State | Faction reactions | Regional changes | Journal-specific |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Challenge collapsed (taint witnessed)** | Tribe rejects the challenge under its own tradition; Varkk publicly diminished | Grakkor secure; tribe can openly organize a response to Krath | Eastern Frontier stability holds; Krath thread unlocked for tribal action | Settlements/Guild note continuity; Skarra notes Grakkor weathered an internal crisis | Raiding stays economically calculated; tribute arrangements hold; quarantine posture around Krath's tent becomes visible | Flags Krath's now-openly-named condition as an active thread |
| **Varkk turned privately** | Challenge withdrawn "pending the champion's matter"; no public rupture | Varkk remains deputy — chastened, watching, a live thread | Stability holds but leadership tension persists as a tracked pressure | Outsiders see nothing; Skarra's informants report only a cancelled Proving | No visible change — itself notable to observers expecting a Proving | Records the private turn and Varkk as an unresolved actor |

#### Design Notes

The party's constraint — act invisibly or ruin the man they are helping — is the quest. This is a political stealth problem wearing a combat tradition's clothes. Krath himself is deliberately not the encounter here; his stat block and confrontation are authored separately. Varkk's deliberate self-exposure is the canonical realization of the "Varkk wants what Krath has" thread in `goblin_tribes.md`.

## Mossroot Tribe

### Quest: "The Warren Below"

**Quest-giver:** Elder Speaker Vess — directly, for the first time
**Location:** The Deep Warren and the silent hunting grounds of the southern forest margins
**Reputation track affected:** Mossroot Tribe

---

#### Background

Vess commissions nothing from strangers. That she is asking at all is the measure of the situation.

The Deep Warren — the tribe's winter refuge and the one secret it protects above all others — is being probed. Animals that do not belong underground have been found in the entrance tunnels: hollow game, moving with the purposeless, doubling-back wrongness the tribe has learned to dread, pressing *inward* against every instinct their species should have. The Mossroot are spending hunters they cannot spare keeping something out that should not want in. Winter is coming, and the tribe cannot shelter in a refuge that something is trying to enter.

Worse: the council is locked. Four of six votes are needed to act on the scale required — relocating the winter refuge, or mounting an organized response to the hollow game — and two elders still hold that this is a bad season, not a wrongness. Vess needs two things, and she needs outsiders for both: an investigation of *where the intrusion is coming from* (following hollow-game backtrails through silent zones no Mossroot hunting party can currently be spared for), and evidence of a kind the skeptical elders cannot dismiss.

Asking means showing the party the Warren. The council authorized this by a single vote. Every Mossroot the party meets underground knows exactly what that vote cost.

#### Quest Stages

**Stage 1 — Below.** The party is brought into the Deep Warren's outer galleries — the first outsiders in living memory. They see the intrusion evidence directly: carcasses that fought forward instead of fleeing, tunnel mouths the tribe has collapsed and re-dug as animals found other ways.

**Stage 2 — The Backtrail.** Guided by Thorn (and Ragash's hounds, if present — one of the only things that will still track hollow game reliably), the party follows the intrusion trails out through the silent zones. The trails converge: the affected animals are moving *away from* a specific area of the deep margins — the avoided meadow the tribe stopped visiting without ever deciding to. The party can establish the pattern (the meadow as epicenter; the Warren intrusion as displacement, not attack — the animals are not hunting the Warren, they are fleeing what is behind them, into the deepest dark they can find).

*Resolution approaches:* investigation (map the spread pattern; recover a specimen or unambiguous physical evidence), confrontation (a hollow-game encounter along the backtrail — run per the corrupted-animal encounter canon, authored separately), knowledge (bring in external corroboration — Mages Guild pattern, shrine-anchor knowledge from elsewhere in the campaign), or withdrawal (report what was seen without pressing to the meadow's edge; honest but weaker evidence).

**Stage 3 — The Council of Smoke.** Vess calls the council with the party's evidence in hand. This is a social encounter, not a formality: the two unconvinced elders must be moved — one is persuadable by physical evidence, the other only by what the party's *conduct* has shown (whether they treated the Warren, the named animals, and the silent zones with the care the tribe respects). If four votes are reached, the council acts: the winter plan changes, hunting protocols change, and the Mossroot begin systematically mapping the spread — becoming, openly, the continent's early-warning system for Corruption in the natural world.

#### Reputation Consequences

| Outcome | Mossroot change | Other effects |
|---|---|---|
| Council unlocked with strong evidence; Warren secret kept | +30 Mossroot; the party is inside the tribe's trust in a way almost no outsider has been | Vess begins sharing spread-mapping intelligence — Corruption information no other faction has |
| Council unlocked on conduct alone (weaker evidence) | +20 Mossroot | Slower, more cautious tribal response |
| Evidence gathered but council still deadlocked (party conduct undermined it) | +10 Mossroot; Vess does not blame them, which is worse | The tribe enters winter exposed; a later crisis is now seeded |
| Party reveals or carelessly exposes the Deep Warren's location | −50 Mossroot; effectively unrecoverable | The one unforgivable act; the tribe relocates the refuge at terrible cost |
| Party sells the spread-mapping to another faction first | −25 Mossroot when it surfaces | Vess's watchfulness was correct; trust resets to zero |

#### Integration (per the Major Quest Integration Standard)

*Constant across all outcomes:* **Companion reactions** — Ragash's hounds are load-bearing in Stage 2, and the Warren (a hidden people protecting a hidden home) lands on her personally; other companions react to being trusted with a secret worth more than treasure. **Journal** — the Warren visit is recorded deliberately without mappable detail; the journal itself honors the secret.

| Outcome tier | Immediate | Long-term | World State | Faction reactions | Regional changes | Journal-specific |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Council unlocked, strong evidence** | Four votes reached in front of the party; guard posture at the Warren visibly reorganizes; Kett (if alive) greets them by name at the tunnels | Tribe survives winter intact; becomes the continent's open early-warning system for natural-world Corruption | Corruption-spread intelligence enters play; Great Forest margin stability improves | Ferath's Edge reassured by Mossroot stability; Skarra's outreach lands on a confident council; Stonefang find no soft border | Silent zones mapped and marked; hunting parties move in new visible patterns; the avoided meadow formally quarantined with boundary signs | Records the spread pattern and flags the meadow epicenter as unresolved |
| **Council unlocked on conduct alone** | Four votes reached, reluctantly; slower reorganization | Cautious tribal response; early-warning role emerges late and partial | Spread intelligence enters play degraded — pattern known, extent uncertain | As above, muted; elven communities notice hesitation | Meadow avoided but not formally marked; hunting-pattern changes gradual | Records the vote and the evidence gap |
| **Council deadlocked** | Vess thanks the party without blame — which is worse; no vote reached | Tribe enters winter exposed; a displacement crisis is seeded toward Stonefang range and elven margins | Spread intelligence stays locked inside a paralyzed council; margin stability degrades | Ferath's Edge detects Mossroot stress at the boundary; Stonefang probe the border; Skarra's protection offer gains traction from weakness | Silent zones keep expanding unmarked; Warren tunnel-guard drain becomes visible in thinner hunting parties | Flags the deadlock and the winter clock as an active threat |
| **Warren exposed by the party** | Immediate evacuation protocols; the party is escorted out and the relationship ends | Refuge relocated at terrible cost; the tribe's one absolute secret is spent | Mossroot capability and early-warning potential collapse | Every faction that learns the location gains leverage; elven arrangements strain as the tribe crowds west; Vess's distrust of outsiders becomes council doctrine | Old Warren region abandoned and dangerous; new refuge unknown to all outsiders permanently | Records the breach — and that the tribe knows |
| **Spread-mapping sold to another faction first** | Nothing visible — until it surfaces | Trust resets to zero when it does; Vess's watchfulness proven correct | The intelligence exists in play but serves the buyer's agenda, not the Frontier's | The buying faction acts on Mossroot knowledge; the tribe eventually traces the leak | Third parties appear in the silent zones, disturbing them | Records what was sold, to whom, and when the tribe learned |

#### Design Notes

The reward structure is inverted from a normal dungeon: the Deep Warren is the treasure, and the party proves worthy of it by *not* treating it as one. The meadow epicenter is deliberately left unresolved — what is in or under it connects to the Corruption shrine-anchor canon and is authored separately. The specific mechanics of hollow-game encounters are likewise defined in their own encounter document; this quest consumes that content, it does not define it.

## Ashfire Tribe

### Quest: "The Second Fire"

**Quest-giver:** Warchief Skarra Ashfire, at the Unification Fire
**Location:** Cinderhold, during the three-day anniversary ceremony
**Reputation track affected:** Ashfire Tribe

---

#### Background

The party is invited to the Unification Fire — the annual three-day ceremony at which all eight clan identities are acknowledged and the tribe renews the argument that holds it together. No other outsiders are invited this year. The invitation is an honor; it is also, characteristically for Skarra, a use.

Brenn has chosen the ceremony to make his move. Not a challenge — Ashfire authority does not work like a Stonefang Proving — but a public question: he intends to stand at the clan-acknowledgment and call for the tribe to debate its direction, with the Sol Kareth accommodation as his proof that Skarra trades goblin leverage for outsider approval. It is within his rights. It is also the most dangerous possible venue: the ceremony is the one place the whole tribe's identity is assembled in one fire.

What Skarra knows and Brenn does not: the Burned Claw intend to ride his moment. Brenn's grievance is political — he genuinely believes raiding serves the tribe better than negotiating, and he has never broken a commitment or an oath. The Burned Claw's grievance has become something else, and members of the faction have been observed near a Corruption anchor shrine. If Brenn's debate becomes their platform, Skarra faces the Fire-Speaker problem in miniature: legitimate dissent lending its face to something that is no longer merely dissent. And Durn, who will lead the ceremony, has not decided what he will say.

Skarra asks the party — the neutral witnesses of "Smoke and Terms," people the tribe saw her make an Ash Oath in front of — to do what she cannot do without proving Brenn's point: separate the argument from the infection. Find out what the Burned Claw are planning for the ceremony; establish, with evidence, the difference between Brenn's faction and theirs; and if possible, give Brenn a reason to see the difference himself before he stands up.

#### Quest Stages

**Stage 1 — Guests of the Fire (Day One).** The ceremony begins. The party has access no outsider has had: clan camps, the coal-tenders, Durn's circle. Social investigation establishes the landscape — which clans stand with Brenn's argument, which Burned Claw figures are attaching themselves to him, and the detail that matters: a Burned Claw inner group has prepared something for the third night's clan-acknowledgment, and has been to the shrine within the past month.

**Stage 2 — The Difference (Day Two).**

| Approach | Method | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigation** | Trace the Burned Claw preparation — what they carried back from the shrine's vicinity and intend to introduce to the ceremonial fire | Hard evidence; Skarra can act with legitimacy instead of suspicion |
| **Diplomacy (Brenn)** | Show Brenn who is standing behind him and what they have been near; appeal to the one thing he has never done — break faith with the tribe | Brenn separates himself; his debate happens *and* the Burned Claw lose their platform — the strongest outcome |
| **Diplomacy (Durn)** | Bring the evidence to the Fire-Speaker before the third night | Durn's ambiguity ends; his ceremony speech draws the line between the old purpose and its counterfeit |
| **Deception** | Isolate the Burned Claw inner group with a manufactured errand or dispute | The third night passes safely, but the underlying faction is only delayed |
| **Confrontation** | Move on the Burned Claw group directly during the ceremony | Whatever they carried is stopped; the tribe watches outsiders spill goblin blood at the Unification Fire — grave political cost even in success |
| **Stand aside** | Witness only | The third night arrives with every thread live |

**Stage 3 — The Third Night.** The clan-acknowledgment. Depending on the party's work: Brenn stands and makes an honest argument that the tribe debates as a tribe should — or the Burned Claw contaminate the ceremony (literally or politically), and Skarra's project takes its deepest wound yet. If the party achieved the strongest outcome, Skarra closes the ceremony with an unplanned Ash Oath: that dissent within the tribe will always be answered with argument, never with fire — an oath aimed at Brenn, at Durn, and at the Burned Claw simultaneously, and one she can only afford to make because the party made it true.

#### Reputation Consequences

| Outcome | Ashfire change | Internal effect |
|---|---|---|
| Brenn separated from the Burned Claw; ceremony intact | +30 Ashfire; the party is named in the tribe's record a second time | Brenn becomes loyal opposition — a stronger tribe, not a quieter one; Burned Claw isolated and identifiable |
| Burned Claw exposed via Durn's speech | +25 Ashfire | Durn fully re-anchored to Skarra's project; the Fire-Speaker vulnerability closes |
| Third night protected by deception/confrontation only | +15 Ashfire (less if blood was spilled at the Fire) | The faction survives underground; a harder fight later |
| Party stood aside; ceremony damaged | −5 Ashfire (they were invited to matter and chose not to) | Skarra's legitimacy crisis begins in earnest |
| Party amplified Brenn without separating the Burned Claw | −20 Ashfire from Skarra's faction | The counterfeit wears the honest argument's face from now on |

#### Integration (per the Major Quest Integration Standard)

*Constant across all outcomes:* **Companion reactions** — Skarra pointedly addresses Ragash as a fellow outsider-to-her-own-people's-expectations during the ceremony; companions with strong views on faith and institutions react to Durn's position between spiritual continuity and political capture. **Journal** — records the ceremony and the shrine connection as an open investigative thread in every branch.

| **Brenn separated; ceremony intact** | Skarra closes with the unplanned Ash Oath — dissent answered with argument, never fire | Brenn becomes loyal opposition; a stronger tribe, not a quieter one; Burned Claw isolated and identifiable | Sol Kareth accommodation holds; the goblin state-building project passes its first internal crisis | Sural Vayne's channel reports Cinderhold stability; settlements' confidence in Ashfire agreements rises; Grakkor reads a rival that survived what he may not; Guild monitoring relaxes | Burned Claw raids on agreement-holding settlements stop (exposed and disciplined); shrine-adjacent territory actively patrolled; Cinderhold opens slightly to vetted outsiders | Records the Brenn/Burned Claw distinction as the party established it |

#### Design Notes

The design load of this quest is the distinction between dissent and Corruption. Brenn must remain sympathetic — he is right that Skarra is trading leverage, and the tribe is stronger for having the argument. The Burned Claw are what the argument becomes when the Corruption amplifies it past the point of good faith. Players who conflate the two (or let the tribe conflate them) hand the Corruption exactly the outcome it works toward. The shrine connection is consumed here, not resolved — the anchor-shrine escalation pattern is authored separately.

## AI DM Guidance

**These quests are invitations, not postings.** Each begins with the tribe reaching out because standing has been earned. The AI DM must not offer them to parties below the gate — the gate is the content.

**The Corruption threads are consumed, not resolved.** Krath's confrontation, the hollow-game encounter mechanics, the meadow epicenter, and the shrine escalation pattern all have (or will have) their own authored content. These quests position the party relative to those threats and must hand off to that content rather than improvising resolutions.

**Failure is playable.** Every failure branch above is a world-state change, not a dead end. A Varkk-led Stonefang, an exposed Mossroot winter, a wounded Unification — each generates the next season of Frontier content.
