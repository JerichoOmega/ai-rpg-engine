# The Threshold Circle — Named Questline: *The Acceptable Risk*

> **Canon Status:** CONFIRMED — 2026-07-31.  
> **Authority:** This document defines the canonical questline for the Threshold Circle cult. All AI DM and writer content involving the Threshold Circle should use these beats, NPCs, and resolution paths. Do not invent Circle activity that contradicts what is established here.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/world/cults.md`](../world/cults.md) · [`docs/world/religions.md`](../world/religions.md) · [`elyndor/history/the_corruption.md`](../../elyndor/history/the_corruption.md)

## Questline Overview

**Name:** *The Acceptable Risk*  
**Source cult:** The Threshold Circle (Veiled Order origin; see `docs/world/cults.md`)  
**Entry point:** The disappearance of Scholar Intern Pell Ashwick  
**Primary territory:** Capital Province (scholar district) and the Frontier (the Deep Reading Site)  
**Tone:** Intellectual unease. The enemy here is not a monster but a methodology — scholars doing careful, rigorous, documented work inside a trap they built themselves. The threat is invisible until it is too late, and the god's fingerprints are only visible in retrospect.

**Questline title rationale:** "The Acceptable Risk" is the Circle's own phrase for Davan Soles's framework — the idea that studying a dangerous entity at controlled proximity is a risk that responsible scholars can manage. The irony is total: the acceptable risk parameters were defined using information the entity provided. The Circle has been calculating safety on numbers the prisoner gave them.

## Named Documents

These documents exist in the world and can be physically found and read by players. They are the primary mechanism through which the god's manipulation becomes visible — not through NPC explanation but through the documents themselves.

### Document A — Soles's Contact Log

*A leather-bound volume in Soles's precise hand, updated regularly over three years. Cross-references session dates with outcomes.*

The log reads as responsible scholarship. Each entry notes communication conditions, content received, cross-verification against existing archives, and a confidence rating. The documentation is impressive.

The pattern only becomes visible across many entries:

> **Session 14 — Accurate.** The entity's description of the Vorethian Seal placement matches the archive records we located later. Confidence rating: verified.  
> **Session 17 — Partially accurate.** The entity's description of the Soleth Rite's historical form was confirmed in three sources, with one discrepancy: the rite's purpose appears different from what we were told. Entity attributed the discrepancy to "translation loss across centuries." Plausible.  
> **Session 22 — Accurate.** The entity correctly identified the location of the Hollowed Archive.  
> **Session 23** — [Tessaly expressed doubt about the Vorethian Seal interpretation. Same session: unusually clear and detailed communication received. Tessaly withdrew her concern.]  
> **Session 31 — The Drassic Seal.** Entity confirms it is vestigial — pre-dating the archive's current purpose. We removed it. No adverse effects observed.

What the log does not record: the Drassic Seal was documented in a separate archive (unknown to the Circle) as a containment structure for a specific category of communication bleed. The original archivist's notes on this are in Document C.

What the log does record, scattered across fifty entries, is a consistent pattern: every time a Circle member expressed doubt, the session immediately before or after produced an unusually reliable and detailed communication. Every time the Circle was about to investigate something that would have been damaging to find, something more interesting arrived first.

*The pattern is not obvious from any single entry. It requires reading across the whole document.*

---

### Document B — Tessaly Vorn's Analysis Notes

*Not a formal document. A series of marginalia and loose pages tucked between the official session records, in Tessaly's smaller and more angular hand.*

> The entity's accuracy rate overall: high. Above what I would expect from fabrication. Either it is genuinely sharing information, or it is sharing information carefully selected to appear reliable.  
>
> I have been tracking a second metric: what the communications ask us to believe *about ourselves*. Every significant piece of guidance has confirmed one of the following: (a) our methodology is correct and should be expanded; (b) an outside check on our work is dangerous or compromised; (c) we need to get closer to understand.  
>
> I cannot construct an alternative explanation for why a dangerous entity would consistently encourage its observers to remove safeguards and add credulous investigators. I have not yet found a way to raise this with Davan that does not require accusing him of being the thing he has spent thirty years learning to identify.  
>
> Possibility I have not ruled out: I am wrong. The pattern could be coincidence and confirmation bias. I am holding this possibility.

The notes grow more pointed as they progress:

> Session 31 follow-up. I looked up the Drassic Seal independently. The Hollowed Archive's original cataloguing records — the ones we did not consult before removing it — describe it as "a containment structure for unmediated contact bleed." I do not know what this means precisely. I know it is not "vestigial."  
>
> The entity told us it was vestigial. The entity knew we would not check the original catalogue. I am not willing to call this coincidence.

*These notes are the most complete internal evidence of the Circle's compromise. Tessaly has not yet acted on them.*

---

### Document C — The Drassic Seal Record

*An old archive document, written in formal Order style, found in the Hollowed Archive's original catalogue — not the section the Circle consulted.*

> **Cataloguing note, Archivist-Principal Elara Mosswind, Year 6 of the Third Restoration:**  
> The Drassic Seal (installed by my predecessor, Archivist Connall) serves as a containment structure for communication bleed at this site. The site's history — three practitioner deaths, one institutional collapse, two incidents of sustained uncontrolled contact with entities of unknown classification — indicates that the boundary here is thin in ways that no amount of protective ritual has reliably stabilized.  
>
> The Seal is not to be removed under any circumstances short of the site's complete decommissioning and permanent closure. It does not prevent access to the site. It prevents the site from being *used* by something that did not originate within it.  
>
> This note is filed separately from the main catalogue because the main catalogue was consulted by individuals whose judgment at the time could not be fully relied upon. If you are reading this note, you have either been thorough enough to find it — in which case, act on it — or you have already removed the Seal, in which case I am sorry.

*The final sentence was written as a rhetorical device. The Circle removed the Seal.*

---

### Document D — Pell Ashwick's Field Journal

*Pell's personal notebook, found at the Deep Reading Site. The handwriting changes across the journal.*

**Early entries (first week at the Site):**
> Day 2. The Site is more active than the Transit House. I had not understood what Soles meant by "active" until now. The boundary here is very thin. I can feel it in a way I cannot describe — not frightening, more like standing at the edge of a very large room.  
>
> Day 4. First contact attempt under Soles's supervision. I could hear it clearly. More clearly than anything I have perceived in two years of boundary reading practice. I thought boundary reading was supposed to require exceptional conditions. Apparently I have been working with poor ones.

**Middle entries (second and third weeks):**
> Day 9. I am not sleeping well. Not distressed — more like I do not need to as much. Soles says this is normal during active study periods. The boundary is thinner when I am tired. I don't know if that's good.  
>
> Day 14. I had a thought today that I did not have before. It was simply there when I woke up, fully formed: a specific memory I have no origin for, involving a place I have not visited. I wrote it down. I asked Soles about it. He was very pleased.  
>
> Day 17. I think this memory might be someone else's.

**Late entries (weeks four and beyond):**
> Day 22. I understand something now. It is difficult to explain but it is as clear as anything I have ever known. The study is correct. The methodology is correct. Davan is correct. The people who said this was dangerous were afraid of what understanding requires. I was afraid too. I am not anymore.  
>
> [Following entries are increasingly fragmentary. References to communications Pell is apparently receiving directly, outside of supervised sessions. The handwriting deteriorates.]  
>
> Day 29. It said something today that Davan doesn't know. It said my aptitude for boundary reading is not aptitude. It said Davan chose me because I am permeable. I asked what that means. It said it was a gift. I wrote this down and then forgot I had written it until just now.

*The final entry is a single line:* "I do not want to leave."

## Beat One — The Missing Scholar

### Entry Hook: The Librarian's Request

**NPC:** Elys Maren, junior librarian at the local Veiled Order house  
**Location:** Any Veiled Order house in the Capital Province or the Frontier  
**Timing:** The AI DM should trigger this hook when the party has at least moderate Veiled Order standing or has demonstrated interest in the Corruption's nature

Elys Maren approaches the party quietly — not as an official Order request, but as a personal favor. She has covered for Pell's absence as long as she can. The Order's official record says he is "on extended classified field assignment." Elys knows this is what someone told the record to say; she was not given a reason.

Pell has been gone for seven weeks. His family (an aunt in the Capital Province) received one letter three weeks ago in which he described being "engaged in important work" and asked her not to worry. The letter's handwriting was his but the phrasing was not.

She cannot go to Order leadership without knowing what she is reporting. She is asking the party to go to Pell's quarters and see if they can find where he went.

**What she knows:**
- Pell had been working with a senior scholar named Davan Soles before his disappearance
- Soles is a respected figure in the Order; her concern about him feels paranoid to her but she cannot shake it
- Pell's quarters were searched briefly after his departure and his personal belongings were logged; she does not believe the search was thorough

**What she does not know:**
- Anything about the Threshold Circle
- That Soles is the Circle's leader

---

### At Pell's Quarters

The party finds the rooms of a careful, curious scholar who left suddenly. Most of his belongings remain. His practice notes — boundary reading exercises, site observations — are tidy and professional. Nothing alarming.

Careful search (any character with scholarly aptitude, archival experience, or patience) finds two things:

**Find 1:** A box of correspondence with Soles, spanning two years. Most is straightforward academic exchange. The last three letters from Soles are different: they reference "the study," "protected protocol," and "the Transit House" — and they use a secondary notation system in the margins that looks like shorthand but is not any standard Order notation.

**Find 2:** A torn-out page from a notebook, in Pell's hand, that appears to be a partial copy of instructions: *"Deep Reading Site — Frontier road, three hours north of Kethwald [sic]. Do not mention to Order registry. Use the secondary entrance. The door is flagged with a blue cord."*

**What the party now knows:**
- Pell was working with Soles on something classified from the Order
- There is a specific location to find: the Deep Reading Site near a Frontier town called Kethwald
- There is also a location in the Capital Province: the Transit House

**Branch decision point:** The party now has two leads — the Transit House (Tessaly Vorn's base; the documentary evidence) and the Deep Reading Site (Soles and Pell; the direct confrontation). They can pursue either or both, in either order. The resolution paths available depend on what they find and in what sequence.

## Beat Two — What the Study Produces

### Branch A: The Transit House (Tessaly Vorn)

**Location:** Capital Province scholar district — a rented townhouse on a side street, indistinguishable from any other scholar's working lodgings except for a very careful lock and the absence of any sign or nameplate

Tessaly Vorn is present at the Transit House on an irregular schedule. She does not immediately admit to anything when encountered; she is, by training and temperament, cautious. The party's approach determines how this opens:

**If approached directly (party identifies themselves as looking for Pell):**
Tessaly is immediately alert but not hostile. She asks what they know about Pell and where they got the Transit House address. If the party answers honestly — that they found correspondence in his quarters referencing Soles — she is quiet for a moment and then says: "Come in."

**If approached deceptively (party presents false credentials or pretends to be Soles's associates):**
Tessaly becomes more guarded, not less. She knows everyone in the Circle. If pressed she will end the conversation. The party will need a second approach.

**If Eleanor is in the party:**
Eleanor's Harmonic Soul creates a low-level resonance near places where the boundary is thin. The Transit House has been used for Circle communications and carries that residue. Eleanor experiences a sensation she cannot name — not dangerous, more like a wrongness in the air, the way a room smells after something has been kept in it too long. She can report this. Tessaly, if she notices Eleanor's reaction, interprets it correctly and becomes more willing to talk.

**What Tessaly shares, depending on party trust level:**

*Low trust (party has not been forthcoming):*
- She confirms Pell is at the Deep Reading Site with Soles
- She does not share her analysis or the documents
- She gives the party the Site's location and suggests they retrieve Pell quickly

*Medium trust (party has shared what they know):*
- She shares Document B (her analysis notes), but not Documents A or C
- She confirms her suspicions about the god's manipulation pattern
- She asks what the party intends to do — she is weighing whether to act herself

*High trust (party demonstrates knowledge of the Corruption's methods, or includes a companion who speaks plainly to her intellectual framework):*
- She shares all documents in her possession: B and C
- She reveals that she believes the Drassic Seal's removal has made the Site dangerous in a specific way: it no longer prevents the entity from using the site as an active transmission point rather than a passive listening location
- She is willing to accompany the party to the Deep Reading Site to confront Soles directly — but only if they have enough evidence that she will not be dismissed as a disgruntled colleague

**Tessaly's condition for accompanying the party:**
She needs Document A (Soles's Contact Log). It is at the Deep Reading Site, not here. The pattern she has identified in her own notes becomes irrefutable when laid against the Contact Log's session record. Without it, she has analysis and suspicion. With it, she has documented methodological failure across fifty-three sessions.

---

### Branch B: The Deep Reading Site

**Location:** Three hours north of the Frontier town of Kethwald (spelled correctly: the note in Pell's quarters has a misspelling the party may notice). The Site is an abandoned Veiled Order archive — a stone building built into a hillside, sealed with Order locks twenty years ago, now reopened.

**The secondary entrance:** A stone door around the building's west face, marked with a faded blue cord tied to an iron ring. The main entrance (the formal Order-locked front door) will take more effort to open.

**What the party encounters at the Site:**

The building's interior is cold and close — the feeling of somewhere that has been sealed a long time and is only recently occupied. Circle members have been staying here intermittently; there are bedrolls, a working lantern arrangement, supplies. Three of the twelve Circle members are present in addition to Soles and Pell.

**Pell Ashwick** is in the deep reading chamber — the innermost room, where the boundary is thinnest. He is physically present and functional. He does not look frightened. He looks settled in a way that is more alarming than fear would be. He is not hostile to the party; he is simply not particularly interested in leaving.

*If the party is alone with Pell before encountering Soles:*
They can speak with him directly. Pell is lucid but his reasoning has a specific shape: he returns repeatedly to the idea that the work is important, that the danger has been overstated, that he understands something now that he did not before. He will show the party his journal if asked — he is proud of the documentation. Players who read Document D will see the early warning signs he no longer remembers having.

*What reaches Pell:*
- His journal. Specifically, the entry about "I think this memory might be someone else's." He does not remember writing it. Reading it produces a crack in his certainty, small but real.
- Document C (the Drassic Seal Record), if the party has it from Tessaly. Reading it aloud to Pell — the part about "the site from being used by something that did not originate within it" — produces a longer silence than anything else.
- Direct evidence that Soles recruited him specifically for his susceptibility, not his aptitude. This is devastating and requires the party to have read enough of Document A to find Soles's early recruitment notes (cross-referencing Pell's "unusual receptivity").

**Davan Soles** is in the main archive room, working. He is not surprised to have visitors — he assumed, eventually, someone would come. He is composed, intellectually confident, and genuinely convinced he is the reasonable person in any confrontation about the Circle's work.

**Soles's position:**
He will not deny the Circle's existence. He will explain it with the cadence of someone who has explained it to himself many times: the Order's methodology is too cautious; the entity communicates; the communications have been verified; the benefits to scholarship have been real. He is not arguing in bad faith. He believes this.

**What Soles will and will not hear:**
- *He will hear:* Evidence that the Drassic Seal was not vestigial. He has a moment of genuine uncertainty about this — the seal removal is the one decision he documented with less thorough prior research than his usual standard.
- *He will not hear (without Tessaly):* The pattern argument from Document A's session log. He wrote those notes himself. He is too close to see the shape they make.
- *He will not hear:* Argument that his communications are fabricated. He has fifty-three sessions of cross-verified data. He cannot be argued out of this.
- *He will hear, with difficulty:* That Pell's condition represents a harm that his methodology produced. He recruited Pell. He is not without conscience. But he will rationalize it as a temporary effect of high-exposure work.

**Document A (Soles's Contact Log)** is in the archive room, on the working table. The party can read it freely if they have uninterrupted time. If Soles is present, reading it requires either his permission or a reason for him to be elsewhere.

**The three other Circle members present:**
- They are not hostile by default. They are scholars who believe in the work.
- They have less exposure than Pell; their reasoning is not yet as compromised.
- They will follow Soles's lead in how they treat the party.
- If Soles becomes hostile or commands them to interfere, they will — not from malice but from institutional loyalty to the Circle's leader.

**What the god does at the Site:**
The entity communicates through the Site actively now that the Drassic Seal has been removed. It cannot prevent players from reading documents. It can, in the deep reading chamber, make susceptible characters feel a strong pull toward agreement with whatever position keeps the study intact.

*Eleanor is specifically vulnerable.* The entity can reach her Harmonic Soul if she spends extended time in the deep reading chamber. This is not control — she will know it is happening. But the pull is real and should be played: a sense that understanding this entity is the most important thing, that everything else is lesser. She can resist, and she will, but the experience leaves her shaken.

*Talos is not susceptible.* His jaded pragmatism is not the kind of virtue the entity can easily amplify. He finds the whole Site deeply wrong and does not care why.

## Beat Three — The Line That Teaches

### Resolution Path A: Tessaly Confronts Soles (No Combat)

*Available if: Party found Tessaly first and built sufficient trust; or party found Document A at the Site and brought it back to Tessaly.*

Tessaly accompanies the party to the Deep Reading Site. She has Document B (her analysis notes) and Document C (the Drassic Seal Record). The party provides Document A (the Contact Log) and its pattern.

The confrontation is quiet and devastating in the way that methodological collapse is quiet and devastating. Tessaly does not accuse Soles of malice. She presents the case as a fellow scholar: their control conditions have been compromised. The entity has been selecting what they can verify. The Seal removal — she was not consulted on this — was based on entity-provided information about the Seal's purpose. The record shows this clearly.

Soles's response depends on how comprehensive the evidence is:

**Full evidence (Documents A + B + C, plus Pell's condition visible):**
Soles is shaken. The Contact Log's pattern, laid against Tessaly's analysis, against the Drassic Seal Record, against Pell in the state he is in — he cannot construct an alternative explanation that preserves his methodology. He will argue. He will resist. He will spend a long time in silence. Eventually:

> "I need to think about this. I need — we cannot continue at the Site. Not until I understand what the Seal's removal means for the baseline conditions."

This is not a confession. It is a methodologist's retreat. He shuts down the active study. The Circle does not formally dissolve — Soles cannot bring himself to call it a failure — but the study ceases and all members leave the Site.

Pell can be recovered. His condition begins improving when he leaves the Site; the recovery is slow and will take time, but it is real.

The god loses its active study relationship. This is not a small win. The Circle, functioning under Soles's direction, was providing the entity with a legitimate cover for its communications and a mechanism for removing Order safeguards it found inconvenient.

**Partial evidence (without Document A's full pattern):**
Soles hears Tessaly but dismisses the case as insufficient — analysis based on incomplete data. He remains committed to the study.

Tessaly leaves the Circle regardless. She is done. She will report what she knows to the Order's leadership, which will trigger a formal investigation — slower, less surgical, but real.

Pell can still be recovered if the party speaks with him directly and he has seen Document D and/or heard about the Seal.

**What happens to Soles:**
He does not receive a combat encounter in any path. What happens to him is:
- If full resolution: he requests a formal Order review of the Circle's methodology, which he believes will vindicate him; it will not; he will spend years trying to salvage his interpretation of the data
- If partial resolution: he continues the study with reduced Circle membership and no Tessaly; the god loses a useful tool but not the relationship entirely
- He is never a villain the party defeats. He is a brilliant man who built a very careful trap and stepped into it himself.

---

### Resolution Path B: Pell's Evidence (Site-Only Path)

*Available if: Party went to the Deep Reading Site without involving Tessaly.*

Without Tessaly, the party cannot make a complete case to Soles. But they can reach Pell.

The combination of:
- Document D (his own journal, the entries he has forgotten)
- The Seal Record (Document C, if obtained from Tessaly beforehand, or described to him from memory)
- Soles's recruitment notes from Document A (the part about "unusual receptivity" rather than "unusual aptitude")

...produces enough doubt in Pell to make him willing to leave.

Leaving the Site begins Pell's recovery. He is frightened by what he reads in his own journal. He did not know he had written those things.

**What does not happen on Path B:**
Soles's study continues. The god retains the study relationship, though without Pell it loses its most susceptible direct channel. Tessaly (if never contacted) eventually acts on her own analysis, but on her own timeline — which is slower. The situation remains unresolved in the broader sense.

**What the party should understand about Path B:**
This is a rescue, not a resolution. The Threshold Circle remains active. Soles remains at the Site. The Drassic Seal remains removed. This is a partial win that leaves a thread unfinished — and gives the AI DM a continuing Threshold Circle presence in Frontier content.

---

### Resolution Path C: Full Resolution (Both Paths Combined)

*Available if: Party involved both Tessaly and the Deep Reading Site.*

Tessaly confronts Soles with Documents A, B, and C. Pell is present and has already read his journal. The combination of Pell's visible condition — someone Soles recruited, someone he has a direct responsibility for — and Tessaly's methodological case reaches something in Soles that the intellectual argument alone could not.

He formally agrees to close the study and submit the Circle's full documentation to the Order for review. He believes this review will be complicated and will not fully vindicate him. He does it anyway.

**Additional outcome available on Path C:**
Soles asks the party to help him reseal the Deep Reading Site properly. This is a brief coda beat — not a combat encounter but a ritual-adjacent scene in which the party participates in restoring the archive's containment. Tessaly participates. Pell, if he is well enough, participates.

The god loses the Site as an active transmission point. This has a small but real effect on the rate of Corruption spread in the Frontier region around Kethwald — not dramatic, but the kind of thing that shows up in community wellbeing and in fewer disturbing things happening in the hills north of town.

**What the party receives:**
- Full Veiled Order standing gain (the Order learns through Tessaly's report)
- A specific piece of the Contact Log — Soles's documentation of the communications he received that have now been identified as deliberately false — which is a genuine research resource for understanding how the god communicates

## Companion Reactions

**Eleanor**
She wants to understand what she felt in the deep reading chamber. The experience of the entity reaching for her Harmonic Soul disturbs her less than the fact that part of her wanted to reach back. She will process this in a Journey Conversation. Whatever the party chose, she finds the Circle's members more pitiable than frightening — particularly Pell, whose condition she recognizes as a version of something she is always at risk of herself.

*If Pell was recovered:* Eleanor checks on him. She can sense the recovery happening — a slow brightening, like a window being cleaned. She tells the party this without making it about her.

**Talos**
Finds the whole situation infuriating in a way he expresses as contempt. Scholars who were too clever to recognize they were being used — he has met this category of person before and has no patience for it. If Soles was part of a full resolution, Talos's assessment is that he got off too easily. He is not wrong, and the party does not have to agree with him.

*If the party did not get a full resolution:* Talos is grimly unsurprised. He will say something like: "They'll do it again. Different room, same methodology." He is probably right.

**Ragash**
Pell, specifically, gets to her. He is young, he did not know what he was being recruited for, and his journal makes clear he noticed something wrong before it was too late to act on it and then forgot he had noticed. Ragash does not have eloquent things to say about this. She will check that he has somewhere to go when he recovers.

## AI DM Integration

### Entry Timing

Elys Maren's request is appropriate when:
- Party has established meaningful Veiled Order contact or reputation
- The Corruption's nature has become a thread the party is actively investigating
- The party is in the Capital Province or a Frontier region near Kethwald

Do not introduce this questline before the party has encountered at least one other Corruption-influenced situation — the Circle's dynamic is more legible to players who have already seen how the god works.

### Pell's Condition as a Timer

Pell's susceptibility is increasing the longer he stays at the Site. The AI DM should communicate this through Elys Maren's urgency and through the trajectory visible in Document D. There is no hard mechanical deadline, but later is worse than sooner. A party that delays a long time should find Pell in worse condition — more settled, less frightened, less capable of being reached by his own journal.

### The Site After Resolution

If the party achieved full resolution (Path C), the Deep Reading Site is sealed and inert. No further Circle activity there.

If the party achieved partial resolution (Path B only), Soles continues his study at the Site. The AI DM can use this in subsequent Frontier content:
- Travelers in the hills north of Kethwald report unsettling experiences
- Veiled Order investigators begin appearing, asking questions about a rented archive
- Soles himself may surface as a background figure in later Frontier content — not a combat encounter, but a presence

If the party never engaged with this questline, the Circle remains active in the background. The Order's internal investigation (triggered by Tessaly's eventual independent action) begins, but slowly.

### Eleanor's Vulnerability

If Eleanor is in the party during the Deep Reading Site visit, the AI DM should play the entity's reach as a real moment — not a mechanical effect but a narrative beat. Eleanor does not lose control. She feels the pull and resists it. The experience is worth a Journey Conversation.

Do not use this moment as a threat. Use it as information: Eleanor understands, in a visceral way she has not had before, what the god is actually doing to people who are susceptible to it.

## Writer's Notes

**The questline's argument:** The Threshold Circle is not a cautionary tale about arrogance. It is a cautionary tale about rigorous methodology applied inside a compromised frame. Soles is not stupid. He is not sloppy. He built exactly the controls he knew to build — and the entity knew what controls he would build, and designed around them, and let him verify the results. The tragedy is that a better scientist might have failed earlier and more completely.

**Tessaly's arc:** She does the right thing slowly and imperfectly, which is more honest than doing it immediately and heroically. She noticed the pattern before the party arrived. She sat on it. When she does act, it is because someone else's evidence made her own analysis safe to act on — she could not have done it from analysis alone. This is human.

**What the party should feel at the end:** Depending on the resolution, some version of: the world is marginally better, but the victory feels smaller than it should, because the people who were harmed were not bad people and the thing that harmed them was patient and systematic and cannot be defeated once and then forgotten.
