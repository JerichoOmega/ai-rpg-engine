# Goblin Tribe Quests

> **Document Status:** Canonical as of July 2026.  
> **Authority:** This document defines the named quests for each of the three goblin tribes. All future encounters and campaign content referencing goblin political engagement should draw from or extend these quests.  
> **Cross-references:** [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../world/goblin_tribes.md) · [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](../world/WORLD_BIBLE.md) · [`docs/systems/reputation.md`](../systems/reputation.md)

---

## Overview

These quests are the primary entry points for goblin political engagement. Each tribe has one named quest that activates the tribe's reputation track and opens the possibility of sustained diplomatic, military, or informational relationships. They are not exhaustive — they are the opening moves.

> **Tier 2:** Each tribe has a second named quest unlocked by high reputation — "The Proving Ground" (Stonefang), "The Warren Below" (Mossroot), and "The Second Fire" (Ashfire). See [`goblin_tribe_quests_tier2.md`](goblin_tribe_quests_tier2.md).

Each quest supports multiple resolution approaches per the Player Choice Philosophy. Writers and the AI DM should present all viable paths and let player choices determine outcomes and reputation consequences.

**Prerequisite standing:** None. These quests are accessible from the start of a Frontier campaign, though the party's prior encounter history with each tribe will affect how they are received at the outset.

## Stonefang Tribe

### Quest: "The Debt Comes Due"

**Quest-giver:** Warchief Grakkor Stonefang (or his deputy Varkk, if Grakkor is unavailable or the party lacks sufficient standing for a direct meeting)  
**Location:** The Fang-Hold, eastern Frontier  
**Reputation track affected:** Stonefang Tribe

---

#### Background

Some time before the campaign begins — weeks or months, depending on campaign seeding — a Stonefang patrol spared a group of travelers rather than killing them. The patrol's commander, acting under the Debt of Steel tradition, chose mercy as leverage rather than a clean kill. That debt has not been called in yet. It will be called in now.

The reason Grakkor is calling in the debt: a trade caravan of significant value is moving through Stonefang territory. Grakkor has intelligence that the caravan is carrying something specific — a weapons shipment contracted by human Frontier settlements to arm a militia capable of challenging Stonefang raiding patterns. He wants the weapons intercepted, the shipment disrupted, or the humans convinced to redirect the contract. He does not want a battle that costs him raiders he cannot easily replace.

If the party was among the travelers who were spared, the quest hook arrives as a direct summons: a Stonefang runner finds them and delivers a message bearing Grakkor's mark. If the party has had no prior Stonefang contact, they encounter the runner approaching a Frontier settlement with the summons directed at someone else — and must decide whether to intercept, follow, or ignore.

---

#### Quest Stages

**Stage 1 — Meeting Grakkor**

The party travels to the Fang-Hold or is escorted there under a temporary truce. Grakkor presents the situation directly: the debt exists, the favor is owed, here is what he wants.

*What he tells them:*
- The caravan's route and approximate timing
- What the weapons shipment contains (crossbows, bolts, light armor — enough to outfit fifty militia)
- That he does not want the caravan destroyed (burning goods is waste; he wants the weapons or wants them gone from the equation)
- That Varkk disagrees with the approach and would prefer a direct raid — the party is being used, in part, to cut around Varkk's preference

*What he does not tell them (discoverable):*
- The caravan also carries a private courier with correspondence between two Frontier settlement leaders that Grakkor would very much like to read
- Varkk has already sent a scout to track the caravan independently, with standing orders to act if the party fails

**Stage 2 — The Caravan Situation**

The party locates the caravan on the road. What they find depends on how quickly they move:

- If they arrive before Varkk's scout, they have time and options
- If Varkk's scout has arrived first, the situation is already complicated — the scout is watching, will report failure back to Varkk, and Varkk will raid within a day

*Resolution approaches:*

| Approach | Method | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Diplomacy** | Negotiate with the caravan master — offer information about a safer route, or reveal that the shipment is already known to the Stonefang, making delivery risky | Caravan diverts; weapons arrive late or not at all; no violence |
| **Deception** | Convince the caravan master the weapons have already been flagged for seizure by a legitimate authority, or that the militia contract has been cancelled | Caravan turns back; information may unravel later |
| **Investigation** | Find the courier correspondence Grakkor wants; use it as leverage with the caravan master to negotiate a diversion in exchange for silence | Achieves Grakkor's two goals simultaneously; very high Stonefang reward |
| **Bribery** | Buy the weapons from the caravan outright, then destroy or redirect them | Expensive; effective; caravan master is satisfied |
| **Stealth** | Steal the weapons shipment at night without confronting the caravan | Achieves the goal; risks harming the caravan master's livelihood |
| **Combat** | Raid the caravan directly | Weapons obtained; militia contract disrupted; caravan master likely dead or fled; creates enemies in Frontier settlements |
| **Refuse the debt** | Tell Grakkor the party will not honor the Debt of Steel | Significant Stonefang penalty; Varkk raids the caravan anyway; party has made a capable enemy |

**Stage 3 — Reporting Back**

The party returns to Grakkor (or sends word) with the outcome.

- If the weapons are gone from the equation, Grakkor is satisfied and the debt is honored
- If the party also recovered the courier correspondence, Grakkor is genuinely impressed — this exceeds what he asked for
- If Varkk's scout reports that the party failed or delayed, Varkk is positioned to argue that Grakkor's diplomatic approach was wrong; this has internal Stonefang political consequences
- If the party refused the debt, Grakkor considers the relationship hostile from this point; the Debt of Steel tradition is not a suggestion

---

#### Reputation Consequences

| Outcome | Stonefang change | Other effects |
|---|---|---|
| Debt honored, weapons gone | +20 Stonefang | Frontier settlement militia loses capability (world state note) |
| Debt honored + correspondence recovered | +30 Stonefang; unlocks direct political access to Grakkor | As above, plus Grakkor has intelligence leverage on Frontier settlements |
| Debt honored through diplomacy (no violence) | +15 Stonefang; Varkk is dismissive but cannot argue with results | Caravan master survives; Frontier settlements receive late or reduced shipment |
| Debt refused | −30 Stonefang; Varkk becomes active antagonist | Raid occurs regardless |
| Party warned the caravan about the Debt/situation | −40 Stonefang; treated as betrayal | May open a separate positive thread with Frontier settlements |

---

#### Design Notes

The Debt of Steel tradition is the mechanical hook here — it is specific, culturally grounded, and gives the quest a reason to exist that is not "go do a job for goblins." Grakkor's use of the party as an end-run around Varkk's preferences is an intentional layer: the party is being used in an internal Stonefang political maneuver as well as the surface-level task. Players who notice this can engage with it. Players who don't will still complete the quest.

Varkk's standing order to act independently ensures that even parties who delay or refuse face consequences — the world does not pause.

## Mossroot Tribe

### Quest: "What the Forest Carries"

**Quest-giver:** Elder Speaker Vess (indirectly — the quest begins as an observation by the party or Ragash, not a direct commission; Vess's involvement emerges as trust is established)  
**Location:** The southern Great Forest margins; the elven settlement of Ferath's Edge (see below)  
**Reputation track affected:** Mossroot Tribe; minor effect on elven community standing

---

#### Background

The Mossroot tribe has maintained an informal territorial arrangement with an elven community on the Great Forest margins for generations. The arrangement is simple: the goblins hunt the outer margins; the elves hold the deeper forest; neither crosses into the other's recognized space without signaling first. It has worked because both sides have honored it and neither has needed to test it.

It is being tested now.

A young Mossroot hunter named Kett has been missing for three days. Kett crossed into the elven margin while tracking a wounded animal — this is not unusual, the boundary is not a wall — but did not return. The Mossroot council suspects the elven community has detained Kett, possibly in response to something that happened at the boundary that the council does not have full information about. What actually happened is more complicated.

The elven settlement, Ferath's Edge, has also reported something: strange tracks near a sacred grove at the margin — not Mossroot tracks, and not human. Elder Speaker Vess is aware of this and is watching to see if the party already knows anything, or can find out.

---

#### Quest Entry Points

The quest can begin in several ways depending on campaign context:

- **Ragash trigger:** Her hounds detect a goblin trail that breaks off abruptly near the forest margin — unusual enough to investigate. Following it leads toward Ferath's Edge and the signs of Kett's passage.
- **Elven contact:** The party enters Ferath's Edge for another reason and learns from the elves that they are holding a Mossroot goblin pending an explanation of what he was doing near the grove. They ask the party to investigate whether this was innocent or not.
- **Mossroot contact:** A Mossroot runner (moving carefully, not willing to approach the elven boundary directly) encounters the party near the forest margin and asks if they have seen a young goblin traveling alone.
- **Discovery:** The party finds Kett before anyone asks them to — he is hiding near the margin, frightened and unwilling to return to either community without understanding what he saw.

---

#### What Actually Happened

Kett tracked the wounded animal to the edge of the sacred grove and found the strange tracks before the elves did. The tracks belong to a small scouting party from a Frontier human group — treasure hunters probing the forest margins looking for ruins. The scouts left before Kett arrived, but they disturbed the grove edge: some old boundary markers were moved, and a shallow pit was dug and abandoned.

Kett, panicked, covered up the disturbance as best he could and retreated toward Mossroot territory — but the elves spotted him moving away from the grove and detained him as a suspect. He has not told the elves what he saw because he does not trust that they will believe him over their assumption that a goblin disturbed their sacred space.

The treasure hunters are still in the area, planning to return.

---

#### Quest Stages

**Stage 1 — Finding Kett**

The party locates Kett — either at Ferath's Edge under the elves' watch, or hiding near the margin if he escaped. Getting him to talk requires establishing that the party is not going to hand him to the elves as a simple answer or take the elves' side by default.

*What Kett knows:*
- The tracks he found (human, booted, at least four individuals)
- What the disturbance looked like (markers moved, pit started and abandoned)
- That he covered it up, which he knows looks bad
- That the scouts seemed to be working from a rough map — he saw one of them consulting it

**Stage 2 — The Treasure Hunters**

The party must locate the scouting group before they return to the grove. Investigation reveals the scouts are camped two hours from the margin, preparing to go back with better tools.

*Resolution approaches:*

| Approach | Method | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Diplomacy** | Convince the scouts that the grove is actively dangerous (cursed, monitored, guarded) and the risk outweighs the reward | Scouts abandon the plan; no violence; requires convincing delivery |
| **Negotiation** | Offer the scouts an alternative lead — a different ruin location that is not sacred space | Scouts redirect; requires the party to have or fabricate a viable alternative |
| **Intimidation** | Make clear to the scouts that they have been seen, their camp is known, and the elves are already aware | Scouts leave the region entirely |
| **Investigation** | Find out who hired the scouts and what they are looking for; this information may be valuable to the elves or to the Adventurers Guild | Larger questline hook; scouts may or may not be stopped |
| **Stealth** | Steal the scouts' map and notes, removing their lead without confrontation | Scouts eventually give up without the information; map content is now in the party's hands |
| **Combat** | Drive off or eliminate the scouts | Grove protected; but violence creates its own complications depending on who the scouts report to |

**Stage 3 — Resolving Kett's Situation**

With the immediate threat addressed, the party returns to the matter of Kett and the elven community's concern.

- If the party can demonstrate that Kett was not responsible and that the actual threat has been handled, the elves release him and the territorial arrangement is preserved
- If the party presents the evidence to Elder Speaker Vess directly (rather than to the elves), Vess is the one who negotiates with Ferath's Edge — which she does, successfully, because the arrangement has generations of trust behind it; the party's role becomes one of information provider rather than arbitrator
- If the party handled the scouts without understanding the full picture, they may have stopped the immediate threat but left open questions about who hired them and why

---

#### Reputation Consequences

| Outcome | Mossroot change | Elven community effect |
|---|---|---|
| Kett cleared, scouts handled, Vess briefed directly | +25 Mossroot; Vess begins treating party as worth watching (positive) | Elven community satisfied; territorial arrangement intact |
| Scouts stopped, Kett cleared via party intermediation | +15 Mossroot | Ferath's Edge relationship stable |
| Party sided with elves against Kett without investigating | −20 Mossroot; Kett is punished for something he didn't do | Elven community short-term satisfied; territorial arrangement strained when truth emerges |
| Party recovered the scouts' map and kept it | +10 Mossroot (threat handled); but if Vess learns the party kept useful intelligence, slower trust-building going forward | No direct effect |
| Party discovered who hired the scouts and shares this with Vess | +30 Mossroot; Vess flags this as genuinely useful; opens next-stage relationship | Elves interested in this information too |

---

#### Design Notes

Vess's default is to observe and wait rather than commission. This quest does not begin with her handing the party a job — it begins with a situation the party discovers or stumbles into. Her involvement grows as the party demonstrates that they handle things with the care her tribe respects.

Kett is not a significant character beyond this quest, but he is a named NPC the party will remember — a young goblin who covered up evidence to protect the grove and got blamed for it anyway. Writers should give him a specific voice: nervous, young, not at all what the party may expect a goblin in custody to sound like.

The treasure hunter thread is intentionally left open. Who hired them, and what they were looking for in the grove, is a hook for a follow-up questline or a thread the AI DM can develop based on campaign context.

## Ashfire Tribe

### Quest: "Smoke and Terms"

**Quest-giver:** Warchief Skarra Ashfire, via an emissary who approaches the party in a Frontier settlement or on a trade road  
**Location:** Cinderhold (Ashfire central settlement); the Sol Kareth trade route northern spur  
**Reputation track affected:** Ashfire Tribe; minor effect on Sol Kareth merchant standing

---

#### Background

Skarra has been negotiating with Sol Kareth trade factors for formal acknowledgment of Ashfire territorial rights in exchange for guaranteed safe passage on clearly defined routes. The negotiation has stalled. The Sol Kareth factors want proof of two things: that Skarra can actually enforce safe passage (i.e., that the Ashfire are more than a new name on old chaos), and that any agreement would hold after the current warchief dies or is deposed.

A Sol Kareth merchant caravan is scheduled to pass through Ashfire-claimed territory in the coming days — one of the northern spur caravans that has been operating with hired guards rather than a formal agreement. Skarra wants to use this caravan as a demonstration: the caravan passes safely, the merchants see it happen, and she sends a message to the trade factors that this is what formal acknowledgment buys them. She wants the party to facilitate the caravan's safe passage through Ashfire territory as a visible, verifiable third party — witnesses who can confirm to the trade factors that the Ashfire controlled the situation.

Her secondary goal: an internal one. There is a former clan leader within the Ashfire ranks — Brenn, who was among the six who supported unification originally — who has been making arguments that the Sol Kareth negotiation is a mistake, that Skarra is trading leverage for recognition, and that the tribe should simply raid the caravans and take what they need. Brenn's position has gained some traction. A successful, visible demonstration of the alternative is, among other things, an answer to Brenn.

---

#### Quest Stages

**Stage 1 — Meeting Skarra**

The party is brought to Cinderhold. Skarra does not waste time: she explains what she wants, why she wants it, and what she is offering. She does not make an Ash Oath over this initial commission — she is careful about that — but she makes clear that if the party facilitates the passage and the demonstration works, she will make Ash Oaths over whatever more significant agreement comes next.

*What she tells them:*
- The caravan's route, timing, and the name of the trade factor's senior representative traveling with it (a woman named Sural Vayne, a Sol Kareth factor with authority to report back)
- That the Ashfire will not interfere with the caravan in any way during the party's escort — she will give them a token of safe passage that Ashfire patrols will recognize
- That there is internal dissent over the strategy, and the demonstration needs to go well

*What she does not tell them (discoverable):*
- Brenn has already sent word to one of his old clan contacts that the caravan is coming — not to raid it, but to be positioned visibly along the route, which would undermine the demonstration even without violence
- Sural Vayne is carrying not just evaluation authority but a counter-proposal from the Sol Kareth factors — one that is less favorable to the Ashfire than what Skarra is working toward; if the party can see this counter-proposal, they could help Skarra negotiate from a stronger position

**Stage 2 — The Caravan Escort**

The party picks up the caravan at the northern spur entry point and escorts it through Ashfire-claimed territory.

Early in the route, they discover (through Ragash's hounds, investigation, or direct encounter) that Brenn's people are positioned along the route — not raiding, but watching visibly enough that Sural Vayne will see them and draw conclusions.

*Resolution approaches for the Brenn problem:*

| Approach | Method | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Diplomacy** | Talk to Brenn's contact directly; explain what is at stake and that undermining the demonstration harms the tribe, not just Skarra | Contact withdraws; Brenn's position weakens without confrontation |
| **Negotiation** | Offer Brenn's people something — acknowledgment, a role in the outcome — that gives them reason to stand down | More complex; may create political complications for Skarra if Brenn gains standing |
| **Deception** | Convince Brenn's contact that Skarra already knows they are there and has prepared a response that will make Brenn look bad | Contact withdraws to avoid the fallout |
| **Investigation** | Find the communication between Brenn and his contact — this is evidence Skarra can use politically regardless of how the caravan situation resolves | Caravan demonstration is preserved; Skarra has a tool to address Brenn directly |
| **Intimidation** | Make clear to Brenn's contact that the party, as neutral escorts, can and will neutralize any disruption — and that disruption here, in front of a Sol Kareth representative, will trigger consequences Brenn has not thought through | Contact withdraws |
| **Combat** | Drive Brenn's people off the route by force | Demonstration preserved; Brenn becomes an active internal problem for Skarra; violence was witnessed by Sural Vayne, which has its own implications |
| **Ignore it** | Continue the escort without addressing the visible positioning | Sural Vayne notes the unsecured presence; her report to the trade factors is less favorable |

*Optional — Sural Vayne's counter-proposal:*

If the party investigates the caravan's cargo or has opportunity to interact with Sural Vayne's private correspondence (requires stealth, deception, or a relationship established through conversation), they may discover the counter-proposal. This can be:
- Shared with Skarra directly, giving her advance knowledge
- Used by the party themselves to negotiate with Sural Vayne on Skarra's behalf during Stage 3
- Ignored

**Stage 3 — The Report**

The caravan completes its passage. Sural Vayne is about to compose her report to the Sol Kareth trade factors. The party has an opportunity to speak with her directly — Skarra has arranged this as part of the demonstration.

*What Sural Vayne needs to see to give a positive report:*
- The passage was genuinely safe (not merely uneventful — she has been around long enough to tell the difference)
- The Ashfire's control of their own territory was visible (patrol presence with the token, no freelance disruption)
- That the party can confirm these things as neutral witnesses

If the party handled Brenn's positioning and has built any rapport with Sural Vayne during the escort, they can advocate for the Ashfire's position directly. If they discovered the counter-proposal, they can address it. This is a diplomacy/negotiation encounter rather than a combat one.

The outcome of the conversation with Sural Vayne determines what the trade factors receive:

- Positive report: Sol Kareth formally acknowledges the negotiation; Skarra gains political momentum
- Neutral report: Negotiation continues without progress; Skarra's position within the tribe is not strengthened
- Negative report: Negotiation fails; Brenn's position strengthens significantly; Skarra faces an internal challenge

---

#### Ash Oath Resolution

If the outcome is positive — Sural Vayne sends a favorable report and the demonstration succeeded — Skarra asks the party to witness an Ash Oath. She makes a commitment, over fire, to the terms she has been negotiating: that the Ashfire will guarantee safe passage to Sol Kareth caravans on defined routes, that this applies as long as Sol Kareth honors the terms, and that the party's facilitation of this moment will be acknowledged in the tribe's record.

This is not a casual moment. The AI DM should treat it as a significant scene — the kind of thing players will remember.

---

#### Reputation Consequences

| Outcome | Ashfire change | Sol Kareth effect | Internal Ashfire effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Successful demonstration; positive report; Ash Oath witnessed | +30 Ashfire; party recognized as political allies | Trade factor negotiation advances; Ashfire gain economic standing | Brenn's position weakens; Skarra's authority is consolidated |
| Successful demonstration; neutral report | +15 Ashfire | Negotiation stalled but not failed | Internal debate continues |
| Brenn's positioning exposed and reported to Skarra | Additional +10 Ashfire | No direct effect | Skarra can act on Brenn politically; significant internal consequence |
| Party used counter-proposal to strengthen Ashfire position | Additional +10 Ashfire | Sol Kareth factors receive a less favorable deal; relationship is functional but the party is noted as working for the Ashfire | No direct internal effect |
| Demonstration failed; negative report | −10 Ashfire | Negotiation fails | Brenn's position strengthens; Skarra may face internal challenge |
| Party sided with Brenn's position visibly | −25 Ashfire; treated as having worked against Skarra's project | Varies | Brenn gains standing; Skarra's relationship with the party is damaged |

---

#### Design Notes

This quest is explicitly political — it is about a nation-building project in motion, and the party is being used as instruments of that project. Skarra is transparent about this in a way that Grakkor (with the Debt) is not. She tells the party what she is doing and why. What she does not tell them is the internal pressure from Brenn, because that would be showing weakness.

The Ash Oath scene, if reached, should feel earned. The AI DM should not rush it. Skarra's careful relationship with oaths — she does not make them she cannot keep — means this one lands with weight.

The Sol Kareth trade route negotiation is a world-state thread. If the party resolves this successfully, the Ashfire's territorial standing changes in ways that affect future Frontier encounters.

## Cross-Quest Interactions

These quests are written to be played independently, but the AI DM should track consequences across them.

| Interaction | Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Party completes Stonefang quest before Ashfire quest | Grakkor learns the party operates for goblin factions | Skarra's initial assessment of the party is more favorable; she has heard of them |
| Party completes Mossroot quest and shares intelligence with Vess | Vess learns the party respects the border between the tribes | Possible Mossroot warning about Ashfire's Cinderhold expansion into Mossroot-adjacent zones |
| Party brokers any inter-tribal arrangement | Any two tribes | Third tribe gains +5 reputation (party observed as neutral political actor); see reputation.md cross-tribal dynamics |
| Party assists Stonefang against Mossroot interests | Stonefang quest sided with Grakkor on territorial matter | Mossroot standing penalty; Vess is aware; makes "What the Forest Carries" harder to resolve |
| Varkk becomes Stonefang leader (Grakkor absent or killed) | Post-Stonefang quest complication | Varkk's more aggressive posture affects Frontier trade route safety; Ashfire's negotiation with Sol Kareth becomes harder |

## AI DM Guidance

**Entry points are flexible.** These quests do not require the party to seek out goblin leaders proactively. The AI DM should seed them through incidental encounters: a runner with a message, Ragash's hounds on a trail, an elven border guard asking for help with an unexpected situation. The quests arrive when the world's logic brings them to the party.

**Multiple resolution paths are genuine.** The AI DM should not guide players toward one approach. A party that raids the caravan in "The Debt Comes Due" gets a different Stonefang relationship than one that uses diplomacy — neither is wrong, both are real.

**Goblin leaders are not naive.** Grakkor, Vess, and Skarra will assess the party based on what they do, not what they say. Promises not backed by action register as weakness or deception. Results register as capability.

**Reputation changes are immediate but not final.** The tribe's standing shifts based on how the quest resolves, but sustained relationships require continued behavior. A party that earns +30 with the Ashfire in "Smoke and Terms" and then ignores Skarra's interests in subsequent Frontier decisions will find that standing erodes.
