## What the Document Is

A bound clan ledger in the standard Irongate record format: left column, the received communication as transcribed at the shrine on the day it arrived (dwarven practice requires shrine communications relevant to clan governance to be entered into the record verbatim, with witness); right column, the corresponding entry from the clan's historical annals, cited by volume and year; below both, a comparison note in Hest's own hand. She calls the method nothing grander than **"keeping the columns honest"** — it is ordinary clan bookkeeping applied to the dead, which is precisely why it is so hard to dismiss on procedure and so easy to dismiss on standing.

She has one year of entries: forty-one communications, of which she marks eleven as divergent. The excerpt below is the four she opens with — chosen, she will say if asked, "because these are the ones where the ancestor is named, the annal is unambiguous, and the divergence cannot be blamed on my transcription."

## The Handout — Excerpt from the Irongate Comparative Record

> *Entered in the record of Irongate Hold, under the hand of Clan-Keeper Hest Irongate, witnessed as marked. The left column is the communication as received. The right column is the annal. The note is mine. — H.I.*

---

**Entry the Ninth — received at the spring Remembering, witnessed by three.**

*Received, in the name of Forge-Mother Brenna Irongate (d. 214 years):* "The eastern galleries hold seams the clan has never cut. Drive the third gallery two hundred spans past the old face and the iron will pay for ten years of what is coming. Do not consult the Deepdelver kin on this — they will claim the vein crosses their boundary stone."

*Annal, Vol. XI, Year of the Split Anvil:* Forge-Mother Brenna's own recorded judgment on the eastern galleries, entered in her lifetime: "Past the old face the stone is faulted and the air goes bad. I closed the third gallery myself and set my mark on the closing. Let no Irongate open it for greed of iron; the mountain has said no, and Brenna heard it."

*Comparison note:* The voice cited Brenna's closing of the gallery as proof of her identity — it knew the year and the mark. It then advised the opposite of everything she recorded. And it knew where the iron runs, which Brenna, who never cut past the old face, could not know. I have asked the senior miners: no living Irongate has assayed past the closing. **Where did the knowledge come from, if not from the living and not from her life?**

---

**Entry the Seventeenth — received at private consultation, witnessed by two.**

*Received, in the name of Keeper Vorn Irongate (d. 61 years):* "The marriage offered by the Stonevault kin is a slow theft. Refuse it, and refuse the next. The line must stay unmixed until the mountain is quiet again."

*Annal, Vol. XXIII, Year of the Long Thaw:* Keeper Vorn's ruling on the Harnnek marriage dispute, his last recorded act as Keeper: "The line is not metal to be kept from alloy. Our dead were themselves once strangers' children. I rule for the marriage, and I rule that no Keeper after me shall use the shrine to block a match, for the dead owe the living guidance, not governance of their beds."

*Comparison note:* Vorn's ruling was famous enough that I learned it as an apprentice. The voice claiming his name gave, as ancestral wisdom, the exact instruction Vorn prohibited with his last authority. If this is Vorn, death has reversed him. **The annals record no case — not one, in twenty-three volumes — of an ancestor reversing their own recorded ruling.**

---

**Entry the Twenty-Sixth — received at the shrine, witnessed by four, in the presence of the council.**

*Received, in the name of First-Delver Marra Irongate (d. 340 years):* Asked by the council whether the winter stores would hold, the voice answered at once, at length, and in detail: the count of the grain, the weakness of the southern cistern seal, the name of the trader in Kelvren Pass who would sell salt fish at a fair rate before the snows, and — unasked — that the Deepdelver kin were already buying against a hard winter and would not share.

*Annal — procedural, Vol. I, the Founding Practice:* The Founding Practice on Consultation, unchanged in three hundred years: "The Keeper shall put the question and wait. The dead answer rarely, briefly, and in the shape of who they were. If the shrine gives counsel, it is weighed; if it gives silence, the silence is also an answer."

*Comparison note:* I have sat consultation since I was twelve, first behind my mother and then in her place. The dead I have known answer as the Practice describes: rarely, briefly, and only ever out of what the clan itself has spoken at the shrine. This voice answered like a factor reading from his books — fluent, immediate, obliging, and stocked with knowledge no Irongate ever laid before the shrine. The count of the grain was correct. I checked. **That it was correct is my whole complaint. Marra could not have counted it. No one had told the shrine. Who counted?**

---

**Entry the Thirty-Third — received at private consultation, witnessed by two.**

*Received, in the name of Forge-Mother Brenna Irongate again:* "You are doubted, little Keeper, and you will be doubted worse. Keep these consultations closer. Bring only Dolvek and Rin to witness. The others carry what they hear to ears that wish the clan ill."

*Annal, Vol. XI:* Brenna's recorded practice as Forge-Mother: she held all consultation before the full hearth, and is credited in the annal with the saying every Irongate child learns: "What the dead say to Irongate, they say to all Irongate. A whisper at the shrine is a hand on the ledger."

*Comparison note:* This entry I include with shame, because for two months I obeyed it. The consultations grew clearer when I narrowed the witnesses, and I let the clarity persuade me. Then I reread Vol. XI and heard my own grandmother's shrine telling me to hide from my clan in the voice of the woman who forbade exactly that. From that day I widened the witness again — and the communications grew scarce and cold. **The dead of Irongate never asked for the dark. Whatever asks for the dark is not the dead of Irongate.**

---

> *Eleven divergences in one year, of forty-one communications received. I have brought the full ledger. I make no claim about the other clans' shrines. I make one claim about ours: the columns do not agree, and I can show every keeper here exactly where. — H.I.*

## Hest Irongate — Characterization

**Who she is:** Clan-Keeper of Irongate Hold at twenty-nine, the youngest keeper at the conclave by three decades — keeper only because her mother died in a gallery collapse two winters ago and the Irongate line does not skip. Small clan, modest holdings, no political weight. Every senior keeper in Ironmoot Chamber remembers her as a child at their Rememberings.

**How she speaks:** Like the ledger. She is not eloquent and does not try to be; she cites — volume, year, witness — and she has the young record-keeper's habit of correcting small factual errors reflexively, including in the speech of keepers whose goodwill she badly needs. She is at her strongest when asked hostile questions about the *records*, because the records are where she lives, and at her weakest when the attack shifts to *her* — her age, her mother's death, her clan's standing — where she goes quiet rather than sharp. Writers should play her precision as courage's substitute: she is terrified through the entire conclave, and the columns are the only place her hands don't shake.

**Her relationship to the evidence:** It cost her. Entry the Thirty-Third is a public confession that she herself obeyed a false instruction for two months — she includes it *because* it is the strongest entry, knowing it hands her enemies the word "compromised." She has not been trained in the Keeper's Discernment beyond the fragment every practitioner knows ("the dead ask only what was always owed"); everything else she rediscovered structurally, by comparison — which trained Keepers who examine the ledger honestly will recognize, with some vertigo, as the Discernment reassembled from bookkeeping. Her documentation independently exhibits Tells 1, 2, 3, and 6 without her knowing their canonical names.

**Her political vulnerability, precisely:** Not that her evidence is weak — that her *standing* is. The three compromised representatives do not need to answer her columns; they need only note that the youngest keeper of the smallest clan, who admits she followed the false voice herself, is accusing the shrines of seven clans on the strength of her own transcriptions. Her verbatim shrine-record practice is her shield: every divergent entry was witnessed, and the witnesses are living Irongate dwarves who can be sent for.

## Running the Documentation Scene

**If the party ensures it is heard (Option A):** The decisive moment is not the reading — it is the *checking*. Hest's method invites any keeper present to test one entry against their own clan's annals, and Ironmoot Chamber keeps copies of major clan volumes. Run the scene so that one senior, skeptical, *uncompromised* keeper does exactly that, on an entry of their own choosing, and finds the columns honest. That keeper's verdict — not Hest's presentation — is what moves the room; the party's craft is engineering the conditions where checking happens before dismissal does. If the documentation lands, the quest's stated threshold applies: three or more keepers moved enables the collective statement. The three compromised representatives will attack standing, never substance; a party that notices this pattern — that no one disputes a single citation — can name it, and naming it is itself evidence of the Discernment's Tell structure at work in the room.

**If it is suppressed:** Suppression at the conclave is procedural, not dramatic — a motion that transcription disputes are internal clan matters outside a conclave's competence, raised by one of the compromised three and supported by the two blocking clans, likely carried unless the party has built Hest a senior sponsor. Played straight, the ledger is never opened in session. What suppression cannot do is destroy the document: Hest returns to Irongate Hold with it, marked now as the keeper who tried to indict the shrines. The AI DM should establish two consequences — the false voices know her name (her clan's communications afterward turn from scarce and cold to warm and specific, courting her again, which frightens her more than the silence did), and Orryn receives the full ledger privately regardless, making it available to later content whatever the conclave decided.

**Either way:** The ledger is canon's most complete comparative record of the false-communications phenomenon (per the quest's follow-on threads). Treat the four entries above as verbatim handout text; the remaining seven divergent entries are unauthored and available for later content, provided they stay inside the established tells and do not reveal the source's nature or purpose — Hest's records prove *divergence*, never *identity*. She does not know what is speaking, and the handout must not tell the players either.
