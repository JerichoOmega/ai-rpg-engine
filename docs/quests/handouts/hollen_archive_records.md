# Handout: The Hollen Records

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]
> **Purpose:** Discoverable handout content for the Veiled Archivists high-reputation quest *What Was Written Here* ([`../religious_order_quests.md`](../religious_order_quests.md)). These are the documents recovered on Option A (Hollen's trail into the deep holdings). Everything Hollen records here is **accurate** — that is the point of him, and the reason it was worth erasing.

## Who Wrote These

**Archivist Hollen** — died twelve years before the campaign's present, recorded by the Order as having passed in The Reckoning, an honored death. One of the most accomplished Boundary Readers in the Order's history; spent his final decade in the archive's deepest holdings on exceptional access. What the Order did not know: he spent most of that decade discovering, verifying, and finally hiding from the thing that was rewriting the Order's memory of the Corruption.

**Where the records are found:** Hollen hid his research inside the one part of the archive nothing touches — the uncatalogued vigil records of the Order's own dead. Vigil records are sacred; no one rereads another Archivist's final words without cause, and — as Hollen verified over years of checking — the alterations never touch them, because they are records of dying, not records of the Corruption. He interleaved his pages among the final testimonies of Archivists dead a century, in plain sight, unindexed, protected by reverence on one side and irrelevance on the other.

## The Trail Marker — Hollen's Marginal Annotation

*The annotation Moven Dust finds, in Hollen's hand, in the margin of an altered boundary-event testimony from forty years ago. Give this to the players verbatim; it is the key that makes Option A possible.*

> *This copy has been corrected. So has the copy it will be checked against. Do not authenticate — sit vigil.*
> *The dead of this house still say what they said.*
> *— H.*

**What it means (for the AI DM):** "Sit vigil" is Order language — the Vigil is sitting with the dying and recording their words. Hollen is telling the finder that the truth is preserved among the vigil records of the Order's dead, where "correction" does not reach. An Archivist who takes the annotation literally — who goes to the uncatalogued vigil shelves and starts reading the dead of the house — finds his pages within days. Anyone else can pass over it as an old man's piety.

## The Five Recovered Fragments

Written across roughly ten years, on archive authentication stock, in the dry format of a working Archivist. Present them in this order; the voice moves from professional caution to something else.

---

### Fragment One — The Mismatch
*Dated approximately twenty-two years before the campaign's present.*

> Authentication memorandum, drafted and withheld.
>
> In cross-referencing the Hallow Field boundary testimonies (three witnesses, recorded independently within a season of each other) against the master copies, I find the master copies agree with one another and disagree with my memory. I authenticated two of these documents myself, nineteen years ago. The hand is correct. The seals are correct. The aging of the ink is correct. The *content* is not what I certified.
>
> The alterations are not clumsy. Where the original witnesses described the affliction at Hallow Field arriving in the pattern I remember — first in the most devoted, the most dutiful, the most loving, each of them becoming a larger and worse version of what they already were — the corrected copies describe an ordinary contagion of malice. Evil arriving from outside. The one account that recorded the phenomenon precisely is now an account that records nothing anyone couldn't have guessed.
>
> I have not reported this. A report would name the documents. I no longer know who — or what — reads what we write.

---

### Fragment Two — The Pattern of the Erasures
*Dated approximately eighteen years before the campaign's present.*

> Seven years of quiet comparison. I record what I can demonstrate:
>
> First. Only records concerning the Corruption are altered. Political records the powerful would pay to change are untouched. Scandal, heresy, the private failures of great houses — untouched. Whatever corrects our archive is not for hire and has exactly one interest.
>
> Second. Within that interest, precision is the criterion. Vague accounts survive. Moralizing accounts survive. The accounts that are altered are the ones that documented the thing's actual behavior — the amplification, the patience, the way it works through what is best in people. The more accurate the record, the sooner it is corrected. Our archive is being graded, and the highest marks are being destroyed.
>
> Third. The alterations follow the reading. A record can sit unaltered for decades; let it be consulted, cited, copied for a correspondent — corrected within the year. It is not erasing what we have. It is erasing what we are *about to understand.*
>
> Conclusion I am not yet willing to write anywhere but here: the erasures are a map. List what has been corrected, and you hold a list of everything the Corruption most needs us not to know. I am compiling that list.

---

### Fragment Three — The Withdrawal
*Dated approximately fifteen years before the campaign's present. This is the fragment containing intelligence documented nowhere else — the piece that changes late-campaign play.*

> I have finished the reading at the Verren Marches sites, and I will write the finding once, here, plainly, because no other copy of it will survive:
>
> **The Corruption cannot take root in what accurately understands it.**
>
> The Marches outbreak, two generations ago, ended without battle, cleansing, or miracle — the histories are honestly baffled by it. The boundary testimony at the sites is not baffled. The affliction receded across the same two-year span in which the Marches wardens circulated their survey of its behavior — a crude document, but *correct*: where it took hold first, whom it took first, and above all *how* — that it does not arrive as a foreign evil but as the growth of what a person already is. Their virtues, loosed from their limits.
>
> Understand what that knowledge did. It did not repel anything. It gave every warden, every parish elder, every frightened family the one thing the affliction cannot survive: the ability to recognize the amplification *early, in themselves and in the people they loved* — to see devotion beginning to curdle into control, grief into despair, duty into obsession — and to interrupt it while the person was still themselves. The thing works through consent, through the small justifications each of us supplies. A justification that has been named out loud in advance is very hard to make. The outbreak did not lose a battle in the Marches. It lost its *hosts*, one refused compromise at a time, until there was nothing left there for it to grow in.
>
> And the enemy — the deliberate hand behind the worst of it, the one that corrects our records — moved its cultivation elsewhere, to regions where the affliction was still preached as ordinary evil arriving from outside. Where every wrong measure taken against it fed it.
>
> This is why our archive is corrected and the Covenant's is not: sermons about darkness cost the enemy nothing. A true description of the mechanism, in enough hands, costs it every host who has read one.
>
> If this is ever recovered, then understand what you are holding. Documentation does not win the fight. It is what lets every ordinary person fight — and win — the only part of it that is theirs.

---

### Fragment Four — The Hand
*Dated approximately thirteen years before the campaign's present.*

> I set a trap and I am ashamed of how long it took me to think of it. Three fabricated boundary testimonies, precise in the manner the thing corrects, planted in three collections, each known to exactly one person: myself.
>
> All three have now been corrected.
>
> No one entered the holdings who should not have. The correction of the third occurred during a season in which I held the only key and slept, most nights, in the reading room. I am forced to the conclusion I have been avoiding for a decade: the hand that corrects our records is one of ours, and does not know it is doing it. It receives its instructions the way we receive everything — as guidance from the Order, in the Order's forms, through the Order's channels — and it authenticates, and corrects, and remembers doing routine work. I cannot find them because there is nothing to find. They pass every review because they are genuinely innocent.
>
> This is also why I cannot report through the hierarchy. A report is guidance in the Order's forms, moving through the Order's channels. I would be handing it my list.
>
> So: no report. I will do what we do. I will sit vigil with the originals, and I will put them where the Order keeps the only words it never corrects and never doubts — with our dead. The dead of this house still say what they said. It has never once corrected a dying voice. I have checked for ten years. Either it cannot, or it does not think of them — and I no longer believe there is anything it cannot do inside these walls. I believe it does not look there. It corrects what we are about to understand, and no one studies the dying for intelligence.
>
> Except us. Except whoever you are.

---

### Fragment Five — The Last Entry
*Undated. Written in a deteriorating hand; Hollen died within the year, twelve years before the campaign's present.*

> I am dying of an illness I have no reason to find remarkable, and I have looked, believe me, with a Reader's care. Not everything is the enemy. That is one more thing it teaches you falsely, if you let it — to see it everywhere, until your fear does its work for it.
>
> The list is complete as I could make it: forty-one corrected records, reconstructed from memory, from external correspondence, from readings at the sites themselves. Fragment Three is the heart of it. If you can carry only one page out of this archive, carry that one, and *circulate it* — copy it, cite it, teach it. Locked away, it is worth nothing. Its whole power is in being widely and accurately known. That is the entire lesson.
>
> Two cautions, from a dead man who was careful his whole life and was still ten years slow:
>
> Do not trust what arrives as guidance, however perfect its form. Verify the person. Verify them in daylight, face to face. The forms are exactly what it wears.
>
> And do not hate the hand that corrected these records, when you find them. They are the most injured person in this house.
>
> I have asked to make my Reckoning early, and the Order has honored it. They believe I am at peace with my death. I am. But that is not why. I intend to be a vigil record. I intend to say, in the one register it has never corrected, where I put everything — and the Order will write my words down with perfect care, and file me with the dead, and there I will keep.
>
> If you are reading this, it worked.
>
> — Hollen, Archivist. Authenticated by no one. Correct anyway.

## AI DM Usage Notes

- **Everything Hollen asserts is true in canon.** The amplification pattern (Fragment One), the erasure criteria (Fragment Two), the withdrawal mechanic (Fragment Three), the innocent hand receiving false internal guidance (Fragment Four — this is also the exact mechanism behind the quest's Option B Archivist, which Hollen diagnosed a decade early), and the vigil-record blind spot (Fragments Four and Five).
- **The late-campaign lever is Fragment Three.** Accurate, circulated documentation of the Corruption's mechanism lets communities recognize and interrupt amplification early — the Corruption loses hosts where the true description is widely known, because it works through consent and self-justification (consistent with the Corruption canon: it does not make choices for people; it needs the compromises people supply). This is a defensive tool for regional play, not an automatic suppression field: it protects people who internalize it, it does nothing for those who don't, and the god's *directed* efforts (cults, false guidance, tailored manipulation) shift toward wherever the truth has not reached. Parties that circulate the survey gain a genuine strategic instrument — and inherit Hollen's problem: they are now the record the enemy needs corrected.
- **Fragment Five's final device:** Hollen's own vigil record — his recorded dying words, held with honor in the Order's files — contains, in Order vigil formulae, the location of the hidden pages. If players approach through his death rather than his research, the trail works in reverse. Whichever direction they come from, the discovery scene should land on the same beat: the Order's most sacred practice, sitting with the dying, turns out to be its one incorruptible archive.
- **What these handouts must not do:** name or identify the imprisoned god, her history, or the Great Forgetting. Hollen documented behavior, not identity. He says "the thing," "it," "the enemy." His accuracy is behavioral — the deeper revelations remain gated by the campaign's revelation layers.
- **The Verren Marches** are a two-generations-past outbreak region referenced only in these records; writers may place them at the Frontier's settled edge and should keep them unremarkable today — a place where nothing has happened for sixty years, which is the point.
