# Companion Relationships

## Companion-to-Companion Relationships

**Companions interact with each other independently of the player.**

The party is not six individuals orbiting the player. It is a group of people who have been thrust into proximity with each other and must figure out what that means. Some of them will become friends. Some will develop professional respect. Some will have friction that eventually resolves into something productive. Some will have friction that never fully resolves.

**Not every companion relationship involves the player.**

The player witnesses and influences companion relationships — but they do not control them. Two companions may develop a friendship that the player had nothing to do with, based on conversations they had while the player was occupied with something else, based on something they have in common that the player never discovered.

### Relationship Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Friendship** | Genuine warmth; these companions are glad the other is here |
| **Respect** | Professional or personal admiration; they may not be close but they value each other |
| **Rivalry** | Productive friction; they push each other; neither has given ground |
| **Mentorship** | One companion has knowledge or experience the other genuinely wants; the dynamic is not condescending |
| **Romance** | Emotional intimacy that develops between companions; rare; must be earned |
| **Professional Admiration** | They recognize each other's competence without necessarily liking each other personally |

These relationships evolve over the course of the game. A rivalry can become respect. Respect can become friendship. Mentorship can become something more equal as the student grows. Nothing is static.

### Companion Relationship Network

Every companion pair has a defined relationship — not just the player and each companion. This means the relationship network includes:

- Every player↔companion pair (6 relationships)
- Every companion↔companion pair (15 relationships)

All 21 relationships are real. Not all of them are dramatically significant — some are simply collegial — but none of them are absent. The companions know each other.

## Personal Quests

### What Personal Quests Are

A personal quest is the story of why a companion is who they are.

Every companion carries something they have not said yet — a burden, a failure, a dream, an unresolved conflict. The personal quest is what happens when the party has traveled far enough together that the companion is ready to face it.

Personal quests exist to deepen characters, not to provide combat encounters. Combat may be present — but it is never the point. The point is revelation.

Personal quests reveal:

- **Hidden burdens** — something the companion has been carrying that the player did not know about
- **Past failures** — something the companion did or failed to do that shaped who they are
- **Personal dreams** — something the companion wants for themselves that has nothing to do with the main quest
- **Unresolved conflicts** — a relationship, a situation, or a choice that was never finished

### How Personal Quests Unlock

Personal quests unlock naturally through accumulated experience — not through a stat threshold or a dialogue menu.

Unlock conditions include:

- **Trust** — the companion has reached a level of trust with the player where they are ready to let the player see this
- **Shared experiences** — the party has been through something that made the companion think about what they carry
- **Journey Conversations** — a prior conversation touched on something close enough to the personal quest that the door is now open
- **Story progression** — the main story has reached a point that made the personal quest urgent or unavoidable

The personal quest should feel like something that emerged from the journey, not like a quest that was waiting to be activated.

### Design Standard for Personal Quests

A personal quest is successful when:

1. The player finishes it and understands the companion better than they did before.
2. The companion is different in some specific way after the quest — not necessarily changed dramatically, but the player can point to what shifted.
3. The quest could not have happened with any other companion. The events, the relationships, the revelation are specific to this person.
4. The journey to this point has made the quest feel earned. A player who skipped all of the companion's Journey Conversations should not be able to unlock the personal quest easily.

### Personal Quest Outcomes

Personal quests do not always resolve cleanly. A quest that ends with a companion getting everything they wanted is one option — but so is a quest that ends with a companion accepting that they cannot have it, or discovering that what they wanted was not what they needed, or finding that the thing they feared was true.

The outcome matters less than the truth of it. Personal quests should be honest about who the companion is.

## The Party as a Family

The arc of companion relationships across the full game moves toward a specific destination: a group of strangers who have become a family.

This is not metaphor. By the late game, the companions treat each other with the particular texture of people who have spent real time together — specific shorthand, genuine concern, the particular kind of comfort that comes from knowing someone has been through the same things you have.

The party's emotional bonds are not a subplot. They are one of the primary stakes of the game. When the story asks what there is worth protecting, the companions are part of the answer.

The Corruption isolates. Companionship preserves. This is the thematic truth the relationship system exists to make real.
