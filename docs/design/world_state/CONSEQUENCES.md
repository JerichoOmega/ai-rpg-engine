## Multiple Outcomes (Canon)

**There should rarely be only success or failure.** Most questlines produce one of five outcome tiers:

| Tier | Character |
|---|---|
| **Excellent** | The best plausible result — earned by insight, effort, or sacrifice, not just completion |
| **Good** | The quest resolved well; most stakeholders better off |
| **Mixed** | Something won, something lost — the most common honest result |
| **Poor** | The problem addressed badly; consequences linger |
| **Disastrous** | Intervention made things worse, or collapse ran its course |

**Every outcome changes the world differently.** Outcome tiers move World State categories, faction reputations, regional conditions, and Journal history in distinct ways — a Mixed outcome is not a smaller Good outcome; it is a *different world*.

## Shrine Consequences

**Neglected shrines:** lose pilgrims · fall into disrepair · weaken community morale · reduce regional hope.

**Restored shrines:** attract pilgrims · inspire settlements · strengthen local culture · preserve history.

Shrine conditions (Maintained / Neglected / Abandoned / Corrupted / Revitalized — [`docs/world/shrine_locations.md`](../../world/shrine_locations.md)) are the most legible barometer of regional Faith and Hope; the Shrine Wardens and Covenant respond per [`WORLD_REACTIONS.md`](WORLD_REACTIONS.md).

## Settlement Consequences

**Helping communities results in:** economic growth · expanded markets · improved defenses · happier NPCs · new quests · better equipment · increased trade.

**Ignoring them results in:** abandoned buildings · refugees · fewer merchants · weaker defenses · increased crime · declining morale.

Settlements are the Living World's most tangible surface: a settlement's condition is World State the player can walk through. Settlement decline feeds regional escalation ([`REGIONAL_ESCALATION.md`](REGIONAL_ESCALATION.md)); settlement recovery is the visible proof that resilience is winning.

## The Quest Integration Checklist (Canonical, Mandatory)

Every major quest must answer:

- [ ] **If completed:** How does civilization improve?
- [ ] **If ignored:** How does civilization decline?
- [ ] **Who is affected?**
- [ ] **What regions change?**
- [ ] **Which factions respond?**
- [ ] **How does the World State evolve?**

**If a quest cannot answer these questions, it is not yet fully integrated into the Living World system** — return it to design.

## Design Rules

1. Consequences are never framed as punishment. The tone is civilization responding, not the game retaliating.
2. Consequences must be discoverable: the player who returns to a region they ignored should be able to reconstruct what happened and why.
3. Positive consequences deserve equal craft — thriving markets, new construction, and grateful NPCs must be as visible as ruin.
4. Outcome tiers apply to inaction too: never attempting a questline is itself an outcome, resolved by escalation ([`REGIONAL_ESCALATION.md`](REGIONAL_ESCALATION.md)).
