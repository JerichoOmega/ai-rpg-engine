# Game Tone

> **Source:** Project Dungeon Keeper design documentation (canonical)  
> This document defines the tonal direction for the Tactical RPG. All writing, dialogue, quest design, and narrative decisions should be made with this tone in mind.

---

## Tone Statement

> *"A mix between dark fantasy and adventure — adding a bit of lighthearted feeling."*

---

## What This Means in Practice

### Dark Fantasy
The world of this game takes its dangers seriously. War has real cost — Talos carries those costs on his body and in his manner. Curses are genuinely devastating — Ronan has lost his home, his social life, and his sense of safety because of his. Betrayal has real consequences — Eleanor's potential expulsion from the Guild and family conflict are not played for drama but treated as genuine losses.

The world is not safe. People die. Institutions fail the people they are supposed to serve. History is heavy.

### Adventure
Despite the weight, this is not a hopeless game. The characters choose to act. There is camaraderie, warmth, and moments of genuine levity. Talos visits orphanages and tells children stories. Eleanor's enthusiasm is infectious. Steven solves problems with disarming simplicity.

Adventure here means: the world is worth fighting for, and the people fighting for it are worth knowing.

### The Balance
The tone should never become so dark that it feels punishing, nor so light that it undercuts genuine stakes. Each character carries real pain; each character also has real warmth or purpose underneath it. The interaction between those two layers is where the game's emotional resonance lives.

---

## Tonal Guidelines for Writing

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Let difficult moments land with weight | Undercut serious scenes with forced humor |
| Find warmth in character relationships | Make every interaction grim or transactional |
| Show consequences that feel real | Use darkness as decoration without meaning |
| Let characters have humor, preferences, quirks | Make every character uniformly brooding |
| Ground the fantasy in human feeling | Let the worldbuilding overwhelm the characters |

---

## Character Tone Notes

Each character embodies the tone in a specific way:

| Character | Their version of the tone |
|---|---|
| Talos | Dark side of the balance — carries the most weight, but his warmth is real and shows through with children and trusted companions |
| Eleanor | Light side of the balance — her optimism is genuine, not naive, and the story tests it seriously |
| Ragash | Pragmatic middle — doesn't perform either; says what she thinks, protects what she loves |
| Ronan | Dark with hope underneath — depression as a lived reality, not a pose; the cure/control arc is about whether hope wins |
| Steven | Comic relief without being comedy — solves problems earnestly; his silence gives the party space |
