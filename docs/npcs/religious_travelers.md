# Named Religious Travelers — Recurring Pilgrim NPCs

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]
> **Authority:** This document defines named, recurring pilgrim NPCs for the religious encounter table. When the AI DM rolls a pilgrimage encounter from [`../encounters/religious_encounters.md`](../encounters/religious_encounters.md), it may cast the appropriate NPC below in place of the anonymous role — and should strongly prefer doing so on a second or later encounter of the same type, so the religious world accumulates familiar faces rather than generating strangers.
> **Cross-references:** [`../encounters/religious_encounters.md`](../encounters/religious_encounters.md) · [`../world/religions.md`](../world/religions.md) · [`../world/cults.md`](../world/cults.md) · [`../systems/reputation.md`](../systems/reputation.md)

## How to Use These NPCs

- **First meeting:** run the encounter entry as written; the NPC supplies the face, voice, and personal stakes.
- **Recurrence:** each NPC has a journey with stages. When the party crosses paths with them again — different region, weeks or months later — the NPC is further along their arc, and remembers exactly how the party treated them. Recurrence is the point: reputation with an order should feel like reputation with *people*.
- **Secrets:** each NPC carries one secret or complication. It should never surface in the first meeting unless players dig; it is the engine of the second and third meetings.
- These NPCs are individuals, not faction spokespeople. Their orders' politics move through them at a personal scale.

## The Solari Covenant — Penitent Iselle Marr

**Slots into:** SC-P2 (The Lone Penitent) — primary; SC-P1 (The Sunward Procession) as a figure walking apart from the column; SC-S2 (The Waystation Shrine) resting between stages.

**Who she is:** A woman in her forties, formerly a lay record-keeper for a Covenant chapter house in the Capital Province. Walking barefoot to the Radiant Spire on a self-imposed penance she will complete "when the walking is done," which she declines to define. Dry-humored in a way that surprises people who expect a penitent to be broken; precise with words, the habit of a career spent writing things down for other people.

**Why she travels:** Officially, penance for "carelessness in her duties." Actually: she copied names onto a list she was told was a tithing census. She has since understood what the list was for.

**The secret:** Iselle is the origin point of the lists in SC-P3 (The Blessing Merchants). She was ordered — through proper channels, by a superior she can name — to compile households that skip Covenant observances. She complied for over a year. Her penance is not assigned; the hierarchy considers her guilty of nothing. She assigned it herself, and she is walking to the Spire to decide whether to hand what she knows to someone above her superior — or to someone outside the Covenant entirely. She carries a copy of one list sewn into the lining of her prayer-disk bag.

**Personality notes for the AI DM:** Accepts kindness with genuine grace (per SC-P2 interaction table) but deflects admiration — "Don't waste respect on me until you know what it's for." If the party has encountered the Blessing Merchants' frightened assistant (SC-P3 hook), Iselle recognizes the description of the lists immediately, and the party watches her decide, in real time, that her walking is done.

**Recurrence arc:** (1) On the road, penance unexplained. (2) Nearer the Capital, feet worse, resolve clearer; asks the party a hypothetical about whether an honest report can be treason. (3) At or after the Spire — either she handed the list up and is being quietly managed, or she never arrived at the hierarchy at all and wants the party to carry the list instead. Her fate is a live Covenant-politics thread either way.

**Reputation:** Helping her (per SC-P2) earns Covenant Clergy gains as written. Taking her list is a fork: significant standing with Covenant **Reformists** if it surfaces through them, hostility from the parties compiling the lists.

## The Old Ways — Brona Ollery, the Route-Carrier

**Slots into:** OW-P1 (The Grove Walkers) — the senior practitioner; OW-P3 (The Seasonal Gathering) as a route-teacher hosting younger walkers; OW-S1 (The Tended Spring) doing maintenance alone.

**Who she is:** A woman in her sixties who has walked the same seasonal grove route for thirty years and carries four other routes in memory that no longer have carriers — their keepers dead or scattered. (She can be cast as the OW-P1 senior practitioner whose grove "felt wrong last season"; that hook is hers.) Weathered, patient, blunt about plants and evasive about people. Teaches by walking slightly too fast and waiting to be asked.

**Why she travels:** The routes only exist while someone walks them. She is trying to pass five routes to a generation that produces fewer walkers every year, while the groves at the ends of those routes change into things she no longer recognizes.

**The secret:** One of the four inherited routes ends at a grove Brona has quietly stopped taking students to — and has told no one why. Two seasons ago the grove answered her Listening Walk with something that was not the Land's voice: too direct, too *interested*, offering her the one thing she wants (walkers for her routes, a tradition that survives her). She refused it and walked out. She has not reported it to any Grove Speaker, because the Severance would burn the grove and the Waiters would do nothing, and she cannot decide which is worse. The route is dying with her silence, and she knows it.

**Personality notes for the AI DM:** Warm to anyone who treats the landscape as something to read rather than cross. Goes quiet — visibly, teachably quiet — if the party mentions groves that speak, offers, or bargains. Players who have seen corruption-shrine patterns elsewhere can earn the withheld route by demonstrating they understand what refusal costs.

**Recurrence arc:** (1) On the route with students. (2) At a seasonal gathering, one student short — the student asked to walk the fifth route, and Brona said no, badly. (3) She asks the party to walk the fifth route with her: she will not go alone, and she will not send anyone in her place.

**Reputation:** As OW-P1/OW-P3. Walking the fifth route with her, whatever is found there, is a **significant Grove Keepers gain** and hands the AI DM a corrupted-grove discovery seeded with a named witness.

## The Ancestors' Path — Dagna Emberholt, the Diaspora Pilgrim

**Slots into:** AP-P2 (The Diaspora Pilgrim) — primary; AP-P1 (The Remembering Party) as an out-of-region cousin traveling to join a Remembering; VO-P1 sites incidentally (her destinations overlap with archival sites).

**Who she is:** A dwarven woman in her ninth decade (middle-aged, for a dwarf), a Capital Province ironmonger's widow, third generation out of the Iron Peaks. Travels with a portable shrine box and a hand-copied map of every site in the lowlands where an Emberholt did something worth remembering — a battle-fall, a bridge-forging, a waystone. Cheerfully talkative, fiercely organized, grieving in a way she has converted entirely into itinerary.

**Why she travels:** Her husband kept the family's ancestor observances; she kept the shop. He died two winters ago. She is walking his map — the pilgrimage he always planned — to learn, late, how to speak to the dead she now has to carry alone.

**The secret:** At the third site on the map, Dagna did everything correctly — and something answered. That should not have happened: she is a novice, the site is minor, and the contact was *easy*, warm, fluent, exactly her husband's voice. It asked her, gently, to bring the shrine box to a specific place that is not on the map. She was overjoyed for a full day before the doubt set in: her husband, in forty years, never once called her "Dagna-love" — that was her mother's endearment, from her childhood household shrine. The voice knew the wrong intimacy. She is still traveling, but now she is testing every site against the markers of genuine contact she has been frantically learning from an old copy of the Speakers' teachings — and she is terrified of reaching the map's end and having to decide about the place that is not on it. (Her situation is a mobile, personal instance of the false-contact phenomenon defined in [`../world/cults.md`](../world/cults.md) — the Keeper's Discernment applies; the wrong-endearment tell is a surface-marker failure she caught by luck.)

**Personality notes for the AI DM:** Feeds people. Asks the party about *their* dead within an hour of meeting them, without morbidity — to her it is asking after family. If the party has any Unbroken Line or false-ancestor experience, she is the most receptive possible student, because she wants to be wrong about the voice and knows she isn't.

**Recurrence arc:** (1) Mid-map, bright, the secret one day old or not yet happened. (2) Further along, visibly frayed, testing sites; shares the story if trust was built. (3) At the map's end, deciding about the unlisted place — she goes, with or without the party, because it sounds like him. This can feed any false-ancestor or Unbroken Line content as a sympathetic civilian entry point.

**Reputation:** As AP-P2. Bringing her situation to a legitimate Ancestor Speaker (rather than letting her walk into the unlisted site alone) is a **significant Ancestor Speakers gain**; the Speakers treat a documented mobile false-contact case as important intelligence.

## The Veiled Order — Archivist Neth Corvane

**Slots into:** VO-P1 (The Archival Expedition) — primary; VO-P3 (The Boundary Reader) when traveling alone; VO-P2 (The Vigil Keeper) when a death intersects his route.

**Who he is:** A gaunt, courteous man in his fifties, gray-cloaked, traveling with one assistant and a black-bound record book whose earlier pages are not in his handwriting. Speaks slowly because he is choosing words, not because he lacks them. The rare Order member who is genuinely good with the living.

**Why he travels:** He inherited the record book — and the unfinished survey it contains — from his mentor, the Order member who disappeared three years ago (the VO-P1 hook about notes that stop mid-sentence is his). He is completing her documentation circuit site by site, in her exact order, because the order was not arbitrary: she was following something.

**The secret:** Neth has worked out what his mentor was following, and it frightens him enough that he has not filed it with his Order house. Her circuit tracks sites where the *record* of death is thin — places where deaths demonstrably occurred but left almost no boundary impression, as if the site had been wiped smooth. Her final, mid-sentence entry breaks off in the middle of the phrase "*not absence — remov*". His complication is procedural and real: Order doctrine says file the pattern; his own vigil-trained judgment says the last person who filed nothing and kept walking is the one who never came back — and the one who filed might not have fared better. He is quietly deciding, site by site, whether he is finishing her work or repeating it.

**Personality notes for the AI DM:** Shares road knowledge generously (per VO-P1). Answers questions about his mentor honestly but incompletely — the incompleteness is audible and deliberate; players who notice and *don't* push earn more than players who push. His pattern (wiped boundary sites) is distinct from, and complementary to, the directional-pull anomaly in VO-P3's hook — if the party carries information between the two threads, they will have done the Order a service no one inside it has managed.

**Recurrence arc:** (1) Early circuit, professional, the book closed. (2) Later circuit, another wiped site behind him; asks the party — carefully, hypothetically — whether they have ever stood somewhere that should have felt like a battlefield and felt like nothing. (3) The final site of his mentor's planned circuit, the one she never reached. He would rather not stand there alone, and by now he trusts the party more than his filing procedures.

**Reputation:** As VO-P1/VO-P3. Genuinely new information about wiped or thin-record sites is a **modest-to-significant Veiled Archivists gain** through him; standing with him personally travels further inside the Order than the numbers suggest.

## Cross-Encounter Notes for the AI DM

- **Pacing:** introduce at most one of these NPCs per travel chapter; their value is familiarity, not density.
- **Cross-pollination:** Iselle's lists (Covenant surveillance), Brona's speaking grove, Dagna's false voice, and Neth's wiped sites are four personal-scale windows onto the same campaign-level phenomenon. The NPCs do not know each other; a party that has met all four is the only entity in the world holding all four threads.
- **Mortality:** these NPCs may die, and it should matter. If a pilgrim the party knows by name is harmed by a third party, the associated order's response (per the general pilgrimage rules) arrives with a face on it.
- **Do not resolve their secrets for the players.** Each secret is authored as an engine for a second and third meeting; front-loading it collapses the recurrence arc.
