# Pronunciation Guide

> **Document Status:** Official canonical reference. This document governs pronunciation for all future development, writing, trailers, and voice acting.  
> **Authority:** Any ambiguity in pronunciation defaults to this document.  
> **Updates:** New proper nouns must be added here before or at the same time they are committed to any other document.

---

## How to Read This Guide

Pronunciations are written in plain English phonetics only — no IPA.

- Capital letters mark the **stressed syllable** (say it louder and slightly longer)
- Hyphens separate syllables
- Sound guides use familiar English words as anchors where helpful
- "Alternate" pronunciations are acceptable in casual use; the listed canonical pronunciation is preferred for official recordings

## World & Setting

---

### Elyndor
**Type:** World / Universe Name

**Pronunciation:**
EL-in-dor

**Notes:**
Three syllables. The first syllable is stressed. The final syllable rhymes with "more." Do not run it together as two syllables ("EL-ndor") — the middle syllable ("in") is light but present.

> Example: *"We are taking this fight across all of __EL-in-dor__."*

---

### Elyndoran
**Type:** Demonym / Adjective (describing people, things, or culture of Elyndor)

**Pronunciation:**
el-IN-dor-an

**Notes:**
Four syllables. Stress shifts to the second syllable when used as a demonym — this distinguishes it clearly from the world name itself. The final syllable is unstressed and short ("an" as in "an apple").

> Example: *"She speaks with a true __el-IN-dor-an__ accent."*

---

### Age of Legends
**Type:** Historical Era Name

**Pronunciation:**
AYJ uv LEJ-endz

**Notes:**
All standard English. "Age" rhymes with "cage." Included here because it is the only formally named historical era — do not abbreviate to "the Age" alone in official contexts without context to identify which age is meant.

## Ancient Legends

---

### Aurelia Sunstrider
**Type:** Ancient Legend (full name)

**Pronunciation:**
aw-REEL-ee-uh SUN-stry-der

**Notes — Aurelia:**
Four syllables. Stress falls on the second syllable ("REEL"). The first syllable sounds like "aw" (as in "awning"). The third and fourth syllables ("ee-uh") flow together lightly. Do not stress "aw" — it is a soft lead-in.

**Notes — Sunstrider:**
Two parts: SUN (standard English, rhymes with "run") + STRY-der ("stry" rhymes with "try"). Stress falls on "SUN." The compound reads naturally as one word.

**Alternate:** "aw-REEL-yuh" (compressing the final two syllables into one) is acceptable in casual use.

> Example: *"The stories say __aw-REEL-ee-uh SUN-stry-der__ chose courage over survival."*

---

### Valen Ashfall
**Type:** Ancient Legend (full name)

**Pronunciation:**
VAY-len ASH-fall

**Notes — Valen:**
Two syllables. Stress on the first. "VAY" rhymes with "day." The second syllable is soft and short ("len" as in "lenient" clipped short). Do not pronounce as "VAL-en" (short 'a') — the long "ay" vowel is canonical.

**Notes — Ashfall:**
Two parts: ASH (standard English) + FALL (standard English). Equal weight on both parts; this is a compound noun, not a sentence. No special stress needed.

**Alternate:** "val-en" with a short 'a' sound is common but not preferred for formal or recorded contexts.

> Example: *"__VAY-len ASH-fall__ did not retreat. That is what the songs remember."*

## Playable Characters

---

### Talos
**Type:** Playable Character

**Pronunciation:**
TAY-loss

**Notes:**
Two syllables. Strong stress on the first. "TAY" rhymes with "day." The second syllable is "loss" — a clean, closed sound. Do not swallow the second syllable into a schwa ("TAY-luss") in formal or recorded contexts; "TAY-loss" is cleaner and more deliberate.

**Alternate:** "TAH-loss" (with a short 'a') is acceptable.

> Example: *"__TAY-loss__ does not abandon people. That is not who he is."*

---

### Eleanor
**Type:** Playable Character

**Pronunciation:**
EL-uh-nor

**Notes:**
Three syllables. Stress on the first. Standard English name. The middle syllable is a short, unstressed "uh" — do not over-pronounce it as "el-AY-nor" or "el-ee-nor." The final syllable is "nor" (rhymes with "more").

> Example: *"__EL-uh-nor__ says the spell is already destabilizing."*

---

### Ragash
**Type:** Playable Character

**Pronunciation:**
rah-GASH

**Notes:**
Two syllables. Stress falls on the **second** syllable — this is the key distinction from an English-default reading. The first syllable "rah" is short and low (like "rah" in "rahrah"). The second syllable "GASH" is hit cleanly and firmly (rhymes with "clash").

Do not stress the first syllable ("RAH-gash") — the name sounds diminished. The force belongs on "GASH."

> Example: *"__rah-GASH__ called the hound, and it came."*

---

### Ronan
**Type:** Playable Character

**Pronunciation:**
ROH-nan

**Notes:**
Two syllables. Stress on the first. Irish name origin. "ROH" has a long 'o' sound (rhymes with "go"). The second syllable "nan" is soft and unstressed. Do not shorten the first vowel to "RON-an" — the long 'o' is correct.

> Example: *"__ROH-nan__ has not told them what happens when the moon is full."*

---

### Steven
**Type:** Playable Character

**Pronunciation:**
STEE-ven

**Notes:**
Two syllables. Standard English name. Stress on the first. Included here because Steven is a rare companion — his name should be spoken with the same weight and care as any other party member, not treated as an afterthought.

> Example: *"Nobody noticed __STEE-ven__ until his pickaxe found the passage."*

## Organizations

---

### Mages Guild
**Type:** Organization

**Pronunciation:**
MAY-jez GILD

**Notes:**
Two words. "Mages" is two syllables: MAY (rhymes with "day") + jez (soft 'z' sound, like the end of "wages"). "Guild" rhymes with "build." Stress "MAY" and "GILD" roughly equally — both words carry weight in the title.

Do not pronounce as "MAJ-ez" (short 'a') — the long vowel in "Mages" is standard.

> Example: *"The __MAY-jez GILD__ does not forgive students who crack the binding."*

## Enemies & Creatures

---

### Ashen Guardian
**Type:** Boss Enemy

**Pronunciation:**
ASH-en GAR-dee-an

**Notes:**
Four syllables total across two words. "Ashen" is two syllables: ASH (standard English) + en (soft, unstressed). "Guardian" is three syllables: GAR + dee + an (stress on "GAR"). The middle syllable "dee" is short and light.

**Alternate:** "GARD-yan" (compressing "Guardian" to two syllables) is acceptable in casual speech but not preferred for recorded content.

> Example: *"The __ASH-en GAR-dee-an__ does not move until you cross the threshold."*
