# Hollow Game — Corrupted Animal Encounters

> **Document Status:** Canonical as of July 2026.
> **Authority:** This document defines what "hollow game" animals are in play — behavior, example archetypes, encounter structure, and campaign-stage escalation. All corrupted-wildlife encounters in the Frontier and Great Forest must align with this document and with the Mossroot Corruption Manifestation in [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../world/goblin_tribes.md), which establishes the phenomenon and its name.

## What Hollow Game Is

The Mossroot named it first, and their formulation remains the best one in canon: *things that should be alive in one way are alive in a different and wrong way.*

A hollow animal looks like what it was. It smells like what it was. It moves like what it was — mostly. What has changed is the decision layer: the animal no longer runs on the logic of hunger, fear, and survival that every hunter, every farmer, and every predator on the continent has spent a lifetime learning to predict. Canonical observed behaviors: they do not flee from fire; they double back on hunters who have lost the trail; some return to a kill site repeatedly after the kill is gone, circling without purpose. Two Mossroot hunters — people who knew exactly what to expect from the species they tracked — have been killed by animals that should have run.

**The design intent writers must preserve:** a hollow animal is not a monster. It is something familiar rendered wrong. The horror is not fangs or size — it is a deer that stands in a burning thicket watching you, a wolf that behaves like it has read the book on wolves and is doing the opposite. Encounters should be built on violated expectations, not on spectacle. The moment a hollow animal is described as visibly rotten, glowing, or obviously "a corruption monster," the phenomenon's storytelling value is gone.

**What hollow game is not:**
- Not rabid or sick in any mundane sense — a healer or hunter who examines a carcass finds a physically healthy animal
- Not coordinated servants of a will. Hollow animals do not take orders, gather into armies, or pursue goals. Their wrongness is directionless — which is, in its own way, worse
- Not exclusive to Mossroot territory. The Mossroot noticed first because they read the land better than anyone, but the phenomenon belongs to the Frontier and Great Forest broadly, wherever the Corruption's spread reaches the natural world

**Where it appears:** the Great Forest margins and interior (the canonical "portions of the forest becoming twisted," per [`docs/world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`](../world/WORLD_BIBLE.md)) and the Frontier's wooded and open country. The "corrupted wildlife" entry in the travel-encounter combat table is this phenomenon; this document supersedes improvisation for it.

## Three Archetypes

Writers should treat these as templates: the species can be swapped for regional equivalents (an elk in the north, a boar in the deep forest), but each archetype's *behavioral signature* is the reusable content.

### 1. The Watcher — hollow deer

**The wrongness:** prey that has stopped being prey. A hollow deer does not graze — it stands. It watches the party at distances a healthy deer would never tolerate, holds ground when approached, does not startle at noise, fire, or thrown stones. It follows. A party that makes camp may find it at the edge of the firelight at midnight, in the same posture it held at noon.

**Threat profile:** low direct threat, high dread. A hollow deer attacks only if physically cornered or touched — and then with total commitment, all hooves and weight and no self-preservation. The real function of the Watcher in an encounter is informational and psychological: it marks a hollow zone, it unnerves the party, and its behavior is the cleanest teaching example of what "hollow" means.

**Tells for an observant party:** it never eats. Over hours of observation, it never grazes, never drinks, never beds down. Trackers notice its trail doesn't behave — no feeding stops, no water stops, long stretches of standing still in open ground no deer would choose.

### 2. The Doubler — hollow wolf

**The wrongness:** a predator that hunts without hunger. Healthy wolves are economical — they test, they select the weak, they disengage when the cost is too high. A hollow wolf does none of this. It doubles back on pursuers who think they've lost it (the canonical behavior that killed a Mossroot hunter). It selects the *strongest*-looking target, not the weakest. It continues attacking through wounds that would drive off any natural predator. And it does not eat what it kills.

**Threat profile:** the most dangerous common hollow encounter, and the archetype closest to a straight combat threat. Use standard wolf-tier stats with the elite modifier (+10 hp, +3 damage — the engine's elite convention, as applied in [`docs/encounters/krath_the_unyielding.md`](krath_the_unyielding.md)); the hollow difference is behavioral, not statistical: it never flees (no morale break, no retreat at low hp) and it re-engages a party that withdraws, shadowing them for miles.

**Special problem — the hollow pack:** if one wolf in a pack is hollow, the healthy wolves *avoid it*. A pack encounter where the other wolves keep visible distance from one of their own is a readable tell — and a merciful design opening: the healthy pack disengages normally, leaving the party facing only the hollow one.

**Tells:** kills along its range that are unfed-upon. Wolf sign that revisits the same kill site over and over. Ragash's hounds (see Companion Hooks) refuse the trail outright.

### 3. The Returner — hollow carrion bird

**The wrongness:** a scavenger that has stopped scavenging and started *attending*. Hollow crows and carrion birds circle sites — kill sites, corruption-touched ground, sometimes the party itself — in slow, patient rotation, for hours or days, without ever landing to feed. Where several gather, they hold formation wrong: evenly spaced, silent, turning against the wind.

**Threat profile:** almost none physically — and that is the point. The Returner is the phenomenon's *map*. Hollow birds concentrate over hollow zones, over shrine-touched ground, over places where something has died wrongly. A party that learns to read them gains a genuine navigation tool for Corruption spread — and a party that notices the birds circling *them* has learned something they will not enjoy knowing. Writers should use the Returner as atmosphere, foreshadowing, and a breadcrumb system, not as combat.

**Tells:** silence. Carrion birds are loud; hollow ones make no sound at all.

## Encounter Structure

Hollow-game encounters follow a three-beat structure. The AI DM should let the party set the pace through the beats rather than forcing all three.

### Beat 1 — The Wrong Detail

The party's first contact is never "a corrupted animal attacks." It is one detail that doesn't fit, delivered without commentary: a deer that doesn't run. Birds circling with no carcass below. Wolf tracks doubling back across the party's own trail from an hour ago. A skilled party member (tracker, hunter background, Ragash) may register the wrongness immediately; others may need the detail to repeat before the unease lands. The narrator must not label it — no "you sense corruption." The players should be the ones to say *that's not right*.

### Beat 2 — Investigation

What looking closer reveals, layered by effort:

| Investigation | What it yields |
|---|---|
| **Observation from distance** | The behavioral signature in full (never eats / doesn't flee / holds formation); confirms this is not a sick or injured animal behaving strangely |
| **Tracking / trail analysis** | The zone's shape: where the animal ranges, how long it has been wrong, whether there are others — and whether the trail intersects places the party knows |
| **Examining a carcass or kill site** | A physically healthy animal; unfed-upon kills; no disease, no wounds that explain anything. This is the finding that eliminates every mundane explanation |
| **Local knowledge** | Frontier settlers and hunters have fragments ("the game's gone strange past the ridge"); the Mossroot, if the party has any standing with them, have the whole framework — the name, the silent zones, the pattern ([`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../world/goblin_tribes.md)) |

A carcass or thorough documentation is also **evidence**: canonically, a clearly Corrupted animal specimen is one of the things that can unlock the Mossroot council's impasse. Writers should keep that door open in any hollow-game content — the encounter's proceeds have a diplomatic use.

### Beat 3 — Resolution Options

Combat is available and sometimes right. It is never the only option:

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Kill it** | Removes this animal; yields the specimen/evidence; does nothing about the zone. Killing hollow game carries no moral weight the way killing the named or healthy game does — but a party that kills a *named* Mossroot animal gone hollow, and tells the Mossroot, triggers the tribe's genuine cultural grief (canon: the naming tradition meeting a hollow animal is real distress) — handled respectfully, this *builds* standing |
| **Avoid / withdraw** | Valid, especially for Watchers and Returners. The zone persists and, per the world's rule that ignored threats grow, will be wider on the return trip |
| **Observe and document** | The information play: behavioral records, zone mapping, a specimen. Valuable to the Mossroot, to Grove Keepers and the Old Ways, and to the Mages Guild's Corruption research |
| **Track it backward** | The bravest option: follow the wrongness toward where it started. This leads toward corruption-touched ground — and the AI DM should route it toward established content (a corruption shrine, a silent zone) rather than inventing a new source; what specifically corrupts a given animal population stays unauthored at the individual-animal level |
| **Warn someone** | Carrying word to the nearest settlement, Mossroot camp, or grove is a small act with real reputation and world-state weight; hollow game near a farmstead is a family's livelihood and possibly their lives |

**Companion hooks:** Ragash's hounds are the party's built-in detector — canonically, animals avoid what is wrong (the Fang-Hold hounds refuse Krath's tent), and her hounds' refusal of a trail or a direction is reliable, wordless information. Ronan's unexplained sensitivity to distant howls can register a hollow wolf before anyone sees it. Both beats belong in Beat 1.

## Escalation Across the Campaign

Hollow game tracks the Corruption's spread through the natural world. The AI DM should scale the phenomenon with campaign stage:

### Early campaign — Anomalies

Hollow animals are rare, solitary, and ambiguous. A single Watcher; one strange kill site; birds behaving oddly once. Locals have no framework and mundane explanations still feel plausible. Encounters at this stage are seeds: the party should leave with a wrong detail they cannot yet explain — and the Mossroot's phrase, if they hear it ("the game is hollow"), should land as a riddle to be understood later.

### Mid campaign — Zones

The phenomenon has geography. Silent zones — the canonical hunting grounds gone quiet, where the *absence* of normal animal behavior is itself the encounter — are findable and mappable. Hollow animals appear in twos and threes within a zone (still not coordinated — parallel wrongness, not pack behavior). The edges are readable: a party can stand at a treeline and notice where the birdsong stops. Mossroot intelligence about zone locations and spread rates becomes the valuable commodity canon says it is, and the council's paralysis over what the phenomenon means is at its most acute — this is the stage where party evidence matters most.

### Late campaign — The Silence Moving

Zones are merging and expanding faster than anything can adapt. Healthy game flees ahead of the spread, creating refugee-wildlife pressure at the edges — over-grazed margins, predators pushed into settled land, hunts failing — so the Corruption's damage arrives at farms and camps *before* the hollow animals do. Inside old zones, a harder wrongness appears: hollow animals that were prey and predator in life standing in mixed, motionless groups. The Watcher's behavior spreads to species that never showed it. At this stage hollow game stops being an encounter type and becomes an environmental condition of Frontier and Great Forest travel — and the regional consequences (Mossroot displacement into contested territory, settlement abandonment) engage the world-state systems.

**Escalation does not rewind.** A zone the party mapped early is larger when they return, unless campaign events have genuinely pushed the Corruption back.

## Boundaries for Writers and the AI DM

- **No cause at animal level.** *Why* a given animal went hollow is never authored or narrated as fact. The correlation with corruption-touched ground may be observed; mechanism stays with the campaign's central mysteries.
- **No cure content.** Nothing in canon restores a hollow animal. Do not stage one recovering.
- **No coordination.** Hollow animals never act in concert, follow orders, or serve the Burned Claw, the cults, or anyone else. (Whether some *faction* tries to exploit them is fair game — the animals themselves stay directionless.)
- **The Mossroot keep their primacy.** They are the continent's early-warning system for this phenomenon (canon). Content that hands another faction a superior understanding of hollow game undercuts an authored role; outsiders can hold fragments, not the framework.
- **Stats stay light.** Hollow game uses base-species stats, with the standard elite modifier for the dangerous archetypes; `crit_chance` values are percentages (1–100) per the engine convention. Behavioral rules (never flees, re-engages, targets the strongest) are AI-DM overlay guidance unless and until the engine supports them.
