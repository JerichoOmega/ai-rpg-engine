# Mossroot First Contact — Ragash Leads the Approach

> **Canon Status:** [CONFIRMED — established July 2026]  
> **Authority:** This document defines the specific authored scene for Mossroot first contact when Ragash leads the approach. It is the AI Director's primary reference for this encounter. All content must remain consistent with [`docs/world/goblin_tribes.md`](../world/goblin_tribes.md) (Mossroot section) and [`docs/heroes/RAGASH.md`](../heroes/RAGASH.md) (Companion Events — Goblin Trail Detection, Event 2).  
> **Prerequisite:** This scene follows The Quiet Passage companion event (RAGASH.md, Event 2), in which Ragash identified a Mossroot hunter trail and offered to approach first.

---

## Scene Overview

**What this scene is:** The party has accepted Ragash's offer to lead the approach into Mossroot territory. This document covers what happens from the moment Ragash goes ahead through the two possible outcomes — a successful first contact with Elder Speaker Vess, or a failed contact caused by the party following Ragash before being invited.

**What this scene is not:** This is not the first Mossroot quest, a formal alliance negotiation, or a guaranteed diplomatic success. It is a first meeting. The outcome, at best, is permission — permission to move through Mossroot territory without being treated as a threat, and perhaps a thin first thread of something that might eventually grow into more.

**Who appears:**
- **Ragash** — leads the approach; the scene's primary actor
- **Thorn** (War Leader) — the first Mossroot to make contact; tests Ragash before Vess appears
- **Elder Speaker Vess** — arrives only if the first contact goes correctly; makes the scene's primary assessment
- **Two to three unnamed Mossroot hunters** — present throughout; never speak; their stillness is the measure of how the encounter is going

---

## Before the Scene — Setting

Ragash follows the hunter trail she identified into a section of forest that is denser than the margin zone the party has been moving through. The canopy closes overhead. The undergrowth thickens on both sides of a narrow natural path.

The party cannot see her after approximately thirty seconds of movement. This is intentional — she went far enough ahead that the Mossroot could not watch her and the party simultaneously.

**AI Director note:** The party has a choice here. They can wait, as Ragash asked, or they can follow. This choice is the branch. Do not resolve it yet — play out what Ragash does first, then let the player decide.

---

## Ragash's Approach — What She Does

Ragash follows the trail for two minutes, long enough to be clearly inside Mossroot range. Then she stops.

She brings her hounds to a sit — a hand signal, no spoken command — and steps off the trail into a small clearing to her left. She is deliberate about this: she is not hiding, she is presenting herself. She stands facing the denser forest with nothing in her hands, no weapon drawn, no aggression in her posture.

She does not call out. She does not perform patience. She simply waits, because she knows she is already being watched.

This is the behavior of a tracker who knows when she has walked into someone else's range. It is not surrender. It is acknowledgment. In Mossroot terms — though Ragash does not know this — it is something very close to the opening posture of The Quiet Passage.

---

## First Contact — Thorn

After roughly a minute of silence, a sound comes from the left — deliberate, not accidental. The snap of a twig that did not snap by itself. Not a threat. A question.

**Ragash's response:** She does not move. She does not tense. She looks toward the sound once, acknowledges it with a slight tilt of her chin, and looks forward again.

This is the correct answer. The sound was a test. Thorn was checking whether she would flinch, reach for a weapon, or bolt. She did none of these things.

Thorn emerges — not from where the sound came from, but from a different direction entirely. He is younger than Ragash expected, compact and precise in his movement. He carries a short spear held loosely at his side, not raised. His expression is neutral and completely unreadable.

He looks at Ragash for a long moment. Then he looks at the hounds.

The hounds hold their sit, watching him with the careful, contained attention they give to unknown persons — not alert, not relaxed, waiting on Ragash. Thorn notices this. He does not comment on it.

He says, in Common, accented but clear:

> "You're not from the Frontier towns."

It is not a question.

**Ragash:** 

> "No."

> "Orc."

> "I know whose ground this is."

Another silence. Thorn reads her. What he sees: an orc with trained hounds who followed the trail on purpose, who stopped at the edge of their range rather than walking through, who is standing in the open without weapons drawn, who has not lied or performed anything in the three sentences she has spoken.

He says:

> "Who is behind you?"

**Ragash:**

> "My party. I told them to stay."

> "They stayed."

*(This last line is only accurate if the party actually stayed. If the party has followed Ragash in — see Branch B below.)*

Thorn looks past her toward the forest margin, long enough to verify. Then he looks at the hounds again, and something shifts in his expression — not warmth, but a specific kind of attention that was not there a moment ago.

> "Your animals. They are not working dogs."

He does not frame it as a question. He is observing aloud, the way the Mossroot observe prey they have been watching long enough to know.

**Ragash:**

> "No."

> "They're mine."

A beat. Ragash almost never elaborates unprompted. Here, for reasons she would not explain, she does:

> "Raised from pups. All three."

Thorn is quiet for a moment. Then he steps back into the forest without another word.

---

## Vess Arrives

Two minutes pass. The hounds do not move. Ragash does not move.

Elder Speaker Vess emerges from a different direction than Thorn departed — she was already there, somewhere Ragash did not locate, which Ragash will register and file away. Vess carries her tracking staff. She is smaller than Thorn, older than the forest seems like it should be able to hold.

She looks at Ragash the way she looks at everything she has been watching for a long time before approaching: with complete assessment already complete, and only the confirmation remaining.

Her first words:

> "The orc who tracks with hounds."

Not a greeting. An identification. She has already been told what to expect — or she has been watching since Ragash entered her range.

> "Thorn says you raised them yourself."

**Ragash:**

> "Yes."

> "From pups. Each one."

Vess looks at the hounds directly. The hounds look back at her. This is, again, not something working dogs typically do with strangers — they would look to Ragash for guidance. These look at Vess the way individuals look at someone they are assessing, not the way tools wait for instruction.

**Vess:**

> "We name the animals we hunt."

She says this as information, not as an invitation to respond.

> "Not because we are sentimental. Because they are real. Because ignoring that would make us worse at finding them."

A pause.

> "I did not expect to say something true to an outsider today."

This is the closest Vess comes, in a first meeting, to a compliment. She does not explain it further.

## What Vess Assesses — Ragash Specifically

The following is the AI Director's internal reference for Vess's read on Ragash. It does not appear in dialogue unless the scene continues into a longer conversation.

**What Vess sees:**
- An orc who is not from the Capital Province political structure — the civilization that has historically contracted against the Mossroot is human-institutional; Ragash sits outside that in a way no human party member could
- Someone who understands that you do not walk into another tracker's range and pretend you didn't know; the approach was correct in ways that most outsiders, even sympathetic ones, get wrong
- A person whose relationship with her animals is not instrumental — working dogs do not behave the way Ragash's hounds behave; these animals have individual attention from someone who considers them worth it

**What Vess does not know yet:**
- Why Ragash is traveling with human adventurers
- What the party wants from the Mossroot
- Whether Ragash's apparent competence reflects her actual values or is a performance for the sake of the contact

**What Vess is willing to give in this meeting:** A conversation. Not trust, not alliance, not access to the Deep Warren, not a guide, not a quest. A conversation, in which she will evaluate whether the party's stated purpose is consistent with behavior she can observe.

---

## The Question Vess Asks

Vess's first substantive question, after the opening exchange:

> "What does your party want from this territory?"

This is the only question that matters in this meeting. How Ragash answers — and how accurately she represents the party's actual purposes — determines the outcome.

**Accurate answer (party wants passage through, information, or contact):** Vess listens. Asks one or two specific clarifying questions. Gives nothing that she has not verified can be given safely. Grants passage through current Mossroot range without interference and, if the stated purpose seems genuine, an oblique instruction about where not to go — which functions as a map of the places the Mossroot do not want outsiders and also, read correctly, a map of the places the party might actually want to find.

**Evasive or dishonest answer:** Vess does not visibly react. She listens. She thanks Ragash for the contact and departs. The hunters melt back. The party is not threatened, but the window has closed — they are not barred from Mossroot territory, but they are not acknowledged in it either. Any future contact attempt requires rebuilding from zero.

**Ragash's role in the answer:** Ragash speaks for the party here because she led the approach. She is not a diplomat. She will not construct a careful political framing of what the party wants. She will say what is true, the way she always says what is true. If the party's actual purpose is something she cannot say honestly, this is a problem she will raise with the party afterward, not paper over in the moment.

> "She asked what we wanted. I told her what we wanted. That's either enough or it isn't."

## Branch A — Party Stays Back (Correct)

The party holds position. Thorn verifies this when he looks past Ragash toward the margin. The scene plays out as described above.

**Outcome of Branch A:**

Vess grants the party permission to move through current Mossroot range. She does not come to meet the party — this first meeting is Ragash's, not the party's. She will be aware of the party's presence. She will not be actively hostile.

If the answer to the question was accurate and the stated purpose seems consistent with what she can observe, she adds one thing as Ragash turns to go:

> "The trail your hounds almost missed — there is a second one, two hours north. It is not ours."

This is information about a threat in the area — possibly a Stonefang scout party, possibly something else. It is given without explanation or obligation. It is the beginning of something that is not yet trust.

**Reputation effect:** Mossroot reputation increases by a small increment. The party is now acknowledged — not allies, not favored, but known to Vess personally and not to be treated as uncleared outsiders within this range.

## Branch B — Party Follows Ragash In (Incorrect)

The party does not stay. They follow Ragash within the two minutes that she is visible, entering Mossroot territory without having been invited.

Thorn is mid-conversation with Ragash when he registers the party's approach — not because they are loud, but because Mossroot hunters are watching the margin and the hunters have a signal language. Ragash sees the change in Thorn's expression before she hears anything.

Thorn does not say anything further. He steps back into the forest. The other hunters, who were present throughout in positions the party could not identify, are simply no longer there.

Ragash is left standing in the clearing with her hounds and the sound of the forest reasserting itself around her.

**What Ragash says when she returns to the party:**

> "I said stay back."

She does not raise her voice. She does not elaborate. She looks at whoever is closest to her, then turns and sets a direction of travel that moves the party away from where she was and away from any Mossroot range marker she identified.

**Can this be recovered?**

Yes, but not soon and not easily. The Mossroot will not pursue the party for having blundered in — that is not their way. But the next attempt at contact requires demonstrating that the first failure was a mistake rather than a deliberate test of their patience. The most effective recovery path: finding a Mossroot hunting trail that the party could have exploited (information about the tribe's movements, the Deep Warren's general area) and demonstrably not exploiting it. Vess will hear about this. She notices behavior.

**Reputation effect:** None, positive or negative. The Mossroot have not registered the party as a threat. They have registered the party as not yet safe enough to talk to. The window is closed, not locked.

## AI Director Guidance

**On running Vess:**  
Vess does not perform warmth or coldness. She performs nothing. She is a person who has spent decades learning to read the Frontier and the people moving through it. She is not hostile to outsiders — she is skeptical of them, which is different. The difference is that skepticism can be addressed with evidence.

Her speech pattern: short. No decoration. Says the meaningful thing and stops. When she asks a question, she is not making conversation — she is collecting information. The AI Director should run her as someone who always knows more than she says and is evaluating whether the party is worth the cost of saying it.

**On the hound detail:**  
The moment where Vess says *"We name the animals we hunt"* is this scene's most important beat. The Mossroot tradition of naming prey animals (documented in goblin_tribes.md) is a near-unique cultural practice. Meeting an outsider who has given individual names and individual attention to her working animals is not common. It does not create trust — but it creates a category that Vess does not often have for outsiders: someone who might understand something.

Writers should not overplay this. Vess does not embrace Ragash. She acknowledges, once, that something true was said. That is the extent of it. The scene that follows belongs to the party, not to that moment of recognition.

**On future meetings:**  
First contact establishes that contact is possible. It does not establish relationship. The Mossroot's trust is earned through behavior over time — returning to Mossroot territory without violating what Vess told them, acting on the information she gave them about the second trail, and not treating her eventual willingness to talk again as guaranteed because the first conversation went well.
